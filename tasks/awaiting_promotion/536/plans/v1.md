## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** A bookkeeping audit, not a new experiment. Over the past months we measured "how similar are two personas inside the model" in two slightly different ways — one version subtracts the average direction shared by all personas first, the other doesn't. The un-subtracted version squeezes all personas into a narrow band (every pair looks 73–100% similar), so results computed that way are not comparable to the rest. I'll pin one official definition, list every experiment that used the other one, and redo each affected experiment's headline number under the official definition using data we already saved.
- **What I expect:** Most conclusions survive, because rank-order claims are often sturdy. The one I most expect to move is the "leakage falls off with distance, and widening the source set doesn't flatten it" result and its siblings, whose distance axis was built entirely in the squeezed space. The older "distance predicts who catches a behavior" results already used the official definition and should stand as-is.
- **What would change my mind:** If a redone number flips sign or loses significance under the official definition, that task's confidence call gets a proposed downgrade in my write-up — and if NONE move at all, that's also worth knowing: it would mean the two definitions disagree on absolute scale but not on ordering, and the flag closes cleanly.

## 11. Decision Rationale

- **Centering recipe = global-mean-center → L2 → cosine.** Why: restores discriminative geometry on an anisotropic residual stream; matches the methodology behind the project's strongest validated geometry. Source: library default (`representation_shift.py:141`), #66 (ρ=0.67–0.87), #504 Gate-A degeneracy + plan-time verification (raw off-diag median 0.945); literature Mu, Bhat & Viswanath 2018 (arXiv 1702.01417), Ethayarajh 2019 (arXiv 1909.00512). (#341's ρ=0.94 REMOVED as a source — fact-check showed it was computed on the raw matrix; see §2.) Alternatives: raw (rejected — degenerate, ~6× compression); per-reference centering (`centering=<int>`, rejected — reference-dependent, never the published recipe); whitening/ABTT top-k removal (rejected — no prior issue validated it; heavier change than needed).
- **Order: center THEN normalize.** Why: this is what `compute_cosine_matrix` implements and what #66 published; normalizing first changes the result and matches no prior artifact. Source: `representation_shift.py:150-159`, #66 body Methodology. Alternative (normalize→center→renormalize) used only as the labeled approximate read for matrix-only tasks (§4-B).
- **Layer choice: each task's OWN published layer** (L20 for the #66/#405 lines, L21 for #505 headline, per-layer where the task swept). Why: the audit changes ONE variable (centering); changing layer too would violate single-variable comparison. Source: each task's Reproducibility section; CLAUDE.md layer-sweep convention.
- **Statistic: each task's OWN published estimator** (Spearman, partial Spearman, OLS with cluster-robust SEs, etc.). Why: same single-variable logic. Source: per-task bodies (#478's slope model quoted in §3-H2; #490's regression in its body).
- **Re-grade thresholds** (stands/weakens/flips per §3): Source: #514's matched-rate re-bootstrap precedent for re-grade vocabulary; ungrounded beyond that — thresholds are pre-registered here so they cannot be post-hoc.
- **Model id:** N/A — no model loaded (no CPU-config smoke needed).
- No load-bearing training hyperparameters exist in this task (`kind: analysis`).
