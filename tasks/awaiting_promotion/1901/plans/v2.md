# Plan — Task #1901: Characterize mapping-quality metrics: 1M-context linear + nonlinear maps vs baselines on every metric

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English — the user reads this first)

- **What I'll run:** I take the already-trained context→answer maps from the ~1M-conversation run (the linear map plus the two neural-net maps and the kernel map — their weights are saved on HuggingFace, so nothing gets retrained) and score every one of them, plus three dumb reference predictors (always guess the average; copy the input; copy the input plus a fixed shift), on every quality measure we use — variance explained, cosine, and "can it pick the right answer out of a lineup" — in both the with-query and before-query framings. I also write a short field guide for each measure: what it actually tells you, what it can't.
- **What I expect:** the measures will disagree in instructive ways — the copy-plus-shift predictor should look terrible on variance explained yet still pick the right answer out of a 1000-answer lineup half the time, while the always-guess-the-average predictor scores near zero on the lineup but roughly zero (not terrible) on variance explained. The trained maps should win on everything, with the neural nets ahead of the linear map by a small margin that shows up more on some measures than others.
- **What would change my mind:** if the lineup test and variance explained ranked all predictors the same way everywhere, the "metrics dissociate, report both" doctrine would be overkill — one metric would suffice. If shuffling the answer pairing didn't drive every metric to its floor, one of our metrics would be broken (reading structure that isn't there).

Whole thing is a CPU job on the VM reusing saved weights — about 1.5–2 hours, zero GPU cost.

**Literature (how mapping quality is evaluated in published work):**
- *Cross-lingual embedding mapping* (the closest published analogue of our v_C→v_A maps): learned linear maps between embedding spaces are evaluated by **retrieval precision@k** against a candidate pool (Mikolov et al. 2013, arXiv 1309.4168 — P@1/P@5 over a translation vocabulary), not by regression R². **Hubness** — a few pool points becoming nearest neighbors of many queries in high-d — systematically corrupts plain nearest-neighbor retrieval (Dinu et al. 2015, arXiv 1412.6568; Radovanović et al., JMLR 2010), and **CSLS** (cross-domain similarity local scaling: penalize a candidate's mean similarity to its own k-NN neighborhood, k=10) is the standard correction (Conneau et al. 2018, arXiv 1710.04087). This grounds our retrieval battery: report plain kNN AND CSLS, plus the k-occurrence hubness diagnostic.
- *Representation-similarity literature*: CKA (Kornblith et al. 2019, arXiv 1905.00414) and the similarity-measure survey (Klabunde et al. 2023, arXiv 2305.06329) catalogue invariances (orthogonal / isotropic-scaling / affine) as the organizing axis for what a similarity read can and cannot rule out — the same axis our per-metric characterization uses. Model stitching (Bansal, Nakkiran & Barak 2021, arXiv 2106.07682) is the functional-evaluation companion (out of scope here; noted in the characterization as the missing third read).
- *Brain-encoding literature*: the R²-vs-identification dissociation is old news there — encoding models are scored BOTH by variance explained AND by pairwise/pool identification accuracy (Mitchell et al. 2008, *Science* 320:1191, leave-2-out identification), precisely because a low-R² model can still identify the right target among distractors. Our identity+bias result is this phenomenon in activation space.
- *Baseline conventions*: mean predictor as the R²=0 anchor; identity map as the "spaces already aligned" anchor; row-shuffled permutation null as empirical chance — all standard across the above. Web search (2026-07-31) confirmed this framing and surfaced nothing newer that changes the battery ([MUSE/CSLS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087), [hubness retrieval evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.09186), [retrieval metrics reference](https://weaviate.io/blog/retrieval-evaluation-metrics)).

## 3. Hypothesis

H1 (dissociation direction, context arm at full n): identity+bias scores acc@1 ≈ 0.45–0.60 at pool 1000 (near its banked 0.503/0.553 at 3600-train — its bias estimate barely changes with n) while its pooled R² stays strongly negative (≈ −0.9); constant train-mean scores acc@k = chance exactly (helper guarantee) with R² ≈ 0 — i.e., the two metrics ORDER the baseline ladder differently (R²: mean > identity+bias > copy; retrieval: identity+bias ≫ copy > mean).

H2 (fitted maps dominate both): all four banked fitted maps beat every baseline on every metric; nonlinear > ridge on R² (banked gap +0.056 at L19 mixed_1m) with a retrieval gap of a priori-unknown sign/size — measuring whether the nonlinear R² gain buys discrimination (acc@1, MRR) or only variance-weighted fit is a primary read of this experiment.

H3 (metric floors): the row-permuted shuffled-pair null drives every metric to its floor — acc@k → k/n_pool, MRR → ≈ H(n)/n-scale, cosine → the anisotropy floor (mean pairwise cosine of unmatched (pred, target) pairs — NOT 0, because activation spaces share a dominant mean direction; the banked predict_the_mean cosine 0.807 anticipates a high floor), R² → < 0. A metric whose null does NOT collapse is flagged as structurally uninformative on this data (this is the falsification arm for the battery itself).

H4 (hubness): at pool ≥ 20k, plain cosine kNN shows measurable hubness (k-occurrence skewness ≫ Gaussian-pool reference) and CSLS improves acc@1 for the fitted maps by ≥ 1 point; if CSLS changes nothing, hubness is a non-issue at these pool sizes and the plain-kNN convention stands.

H5 (prefix vs context regime): the prefix-arm battery (n=50, LOFO, n_train ≪ d) reproduces the #722 dissociation (identity+bias retrieval ≫ ridge-LOFO retrieval), opposite in sign to the context arm at large n — establishing that the identity-family-vs-fitted ranking is a function of training-data regime, not of the metric.

Falsifiers: H1 falsified if identity+bias acc@1 at pool 1000 < 0.1 or its R² ≥ 0; H2 falsified if any baseline beats a fitted map on any primary metric; H3 falsified per-metric (a non-collapsing null); H4 two-sided by design; H5 falsified if LOFO ridge beats identity+bias on retrieval at n=50.
