## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I train the same small neural network (MLP) map from context activations to answer activations at layer 19, at eight training-set sizes — 5k, 10k, 25k, 50k, 100k, 150k, 250k, 500k — all drawn from the same big banked ~963k-row LMSYS activation store, and reuse the already-trained 963k network as the ninth point. At the two verdict endpoints (50k and 500k) I train the network three times with different random inits, so the verdict averages out training noise. Beside each network I fit the plain linear (ridge) map on the exact same training rows, and score both on the same held-out 1,000 answers: prediction accuracy (R²) plus "does the predicted answer retrieve the right one" (retrieval accuracy, with the hub-corrected whitened-cosine scoring the user asked for).
- **What I expect:** the linear map's curve goes flat past ~50k training rows while the neural map keeps improving (the two banked big-n points already hint at this: 0.788 → 0.807 for the MLP vs 0.755 → 0.761 for ridge between 150k and 500k). The dense curve turns the paper's 2-point claim into a real plot — and unlike the published version, every point on it is scored on the same held-out answers.
- **What would change my mind:** if the neural curve is also flat past 50k (the 2-point gain was noise from how those two training subsets were drawn), or if the neural gain shows up in R² but not in retrieval accuracy — then the paper's Plot 2 claim gets weakened or rewritten, not just densified. The banked small-n points from the earlier store appear on the figure only as context markers, since they were scored on different held-out answers.

## 3. Hypothesis

The paper claim predicts the nonlinear map keeps gaining past the linear plateau. Grounding: ridge Δ(150k→500k) = +0.005 R² (plateau); banked MLP Δ(150k→500k) = +0.019; MLP−ridge level gap at 500k = 0.047.

The MLP verdict endpoints (50k, 500k) are fit at 3 seeds {42, 43, 44} on the identical sel-sha subsets (OPT-1). Per seed s, S_mlp^(s) = R²_MLP,s(500k) − R²_MLP,s(50k) (seed-paired slope); S_mlp = the seed MEAN of the three seed-paired slopes (seedwise values + dispersion reported beside). S_ridge = R²_ridge(500k) − R²_ridge(50k) (closed-form ridge is seed-free). All R² on the pinned test_1000 (bootstrap CIs reported beside). The single verdict quantity is the slope-gap margin D_gap = (S_mlp − S_ridge) − 0.01.

DISJOINT and exhaustive: Confirmed ⇔ D_gap >= 0; Falsified ⇔ D_gap < 0.

Descriptive sub-reads (reported, no verdict weight): within Falsified, "plateau-both" (|S_mlp| < 0.01 — both curves flat past 50k) is distinguished from "nonlinear also declines / noise" in prose; within Confirmed, S_mlp ≤ 0 (a gap driven by a ridge DECLINE rather than a nonlinear gain) is called out as a qualitatively different outcome rather than narrated as the paper claim.

Secondary (descriptive, no verdict weight): the same contrast on acc@1 under whitened-cosine+CSLS — a nonlinear R² gain that does NOT show up in retrieval is reported as a metric dissociation (squarely inside the parent Goal's "where they dissociate" clause), not hidden.

**Registered paired contrast** (MLP vs ridge per rung): Row-coverage — every rung row in `mlp_scaling_dense_L19.json` carries BOTH arms fit on the IDENTICAL train-row index set (the same `select_train` sel subset, sha-recorded per rung — all 8 fresh rungs are n1m lmsys selections in v14) and scored on the identical pinned val_400/test_1000; the 963k rung's two arms come from banked weights applied to the same test rows (mixed-corpus caveat carried); any rung realized with only one arm is excluded from paired reads and named in the output.
