## 1. Goal

Validate whether automatically-clustered, separable WildChat conversation categories are usable as *realistic* training/eval contexts for the downstream context-leakage predictor line. The project's context battery so far leans on hand-written persona prompts and synthetic rewraps (#594); naturally-occurring conversation categories are the next tier up the data-realism hierarchy (tier-1 real-world data). ## 3. Hypothesis

**H1 (primary, falsifiable):** At least one pair of WildChat clusters separates in Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct mid-band (L13–18) activation space with **length-residualized centered-cosine k-NN purity ≥ 0.7 at the best layer AND permutation-p ≤ 0.01**.
- *Confirms:* real conversation categories are usable as a clean separable context axis for the downstream leakage grid; the picked pair + corpus ship as the reusable artifact.
- *Falsifies:* no pair clears 0.7 (or the best pair's p > 0.01). This is a real negative finding — real categories are *less* separable than #594's curated families (which hit 0.979) — reported as such; the corpus + top-3 ranking still ship for downstream inspection, but the plan flags that personas, not WildChat categories, are the cleaner axis.

**H2 (secondary):** The winning pair's separability survives length-residualization with ≤ 0.1 purity drop (i.e. separation is topic-driven, not a long-code-chat-vs-short-Q&A length artifact). If raw purity ≥ 0.7 but residualized purity < 0.6, the separability is length-confounded and the finding is downgraded to "length-confounded, not a clean topical axis."

Quantitative anchors (from #594): a pair scoring near the **0.604 TF-IDF** baseline is "no better than sorting by shared words"; near the **0.396 length-only** baseline is "no better than sorting by length"; clearing **0.7** puts it meaningfully above both. A perfectly clean pair would approach #594's 0.979 (but two real clusters need not be as clean as two curated families).

### Step 4 — separability scoring (`scripts/issue617_score_separability.py`, REUSE #594 analyzer primitives)

For every candidate **pair** of clusters (within each config; cross-config pairs not scored — a pair must come from one clustering), assemble the two clusters' extracted prefix vectors as a 2-family labeling and compute, REUSING `issue594_analyze_context_geometry` primitives:
- centered-cosine k-NN purity (`knn_purity` + `knn_neighbor_order` + `cosine_dist(centering="global_mean")`), k=4, at each layer L13/14/18;
- **length-residualized** purity: `residualize(x, log_len)` then re-score (the headline DV);
- the **best of L13/14/18** length-residualized purity is the pair's score;
- a **1000-shuffle permutation null** over the pair's labels (`perm_stats`-style, 2-family) → permutation-p;
- the TF-IDF word-overlap baseline (`text_baseline`) and length-only baseline (`length_reads`'s `length_only_knn_purity`) for the pair, as comparison rails.

**Pair scoring formula** (the formal separability statistic):
```
score(pair) = max_{L ∈ {13,14,18}} purity_residualized(pair, L)
   where purity_residualized = knn_purity( cosine_dist( residualize(X_L, log1p(content_tokens)), "global_mean" ), labels, k=4 )
   p(pair) = (1 + #{shuffle : purity_residualized_shuffle ≥ score}) / (1001)   [best-of-3-layers corrected]
```
N per cluster ≈ 30–50 prefixes; with two clusters the labeling has 60–100 points — k-NN purity at k=4 is stable at this N (#594 ran k=4 purity at 48 points across 6 families).

**Winner:** the pair with the highest length-residualized purity subject to `p ≤ 0.01`. **Tie-break: lower K wins** (fewer cluster-count assumptions), then lower HDBSCAN-after-KMeans, then lexicographic cluster ids. **Top-3 logged** with their 5 example first-user-turns each, sizes, raw + residualized purity, and p, into `separability.json`. Write `eval_results/issue_617/separability.json` (committed to git) — checkpoint-per-phase before any figure or upload.

## 5. Conditions and Controls

This is a measurement (separability validation), not a multi-condition training sweep, so "conditions" are the clustering configurations being scored and the controls are the confound baselines.

| Plain-English name | What it tests | What it controls for | Config slug |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best WildChat pair (winner) | Do the 2 most-separated real-conversation categories separate in mid-band activations? | — (the headline read) | `winner` |
| Length-residualized read | Same pair, with conversation length regressed out of the activations | Length confound (long code chats vs short Q&A) — the #1 boring explanation | `winner_residualized` |
| TF-IDF word-overlap baseline | Purity from shared wording alone, same scale | "Clusters separate only because they share surface words" | `tfidf_baseline` |
| Length-only baseline | Purity from log-token-count alone, same scale | "Clusters separate only by length" | `length_only_baseline` |
| Permutation null | Same purity statistic under 1000 random relabelings | Chance — is the score better than random label assignment? | `perm_null` |
| Top-3 logged pairs | The 3 highest-scoring pairs across all configs | Cherry-pick transparency — shows the runner-up structure | `top3` |

The decoupling of clustering space (text embeddings) from verification space (Qwen activations) is itself the central control: clusters are *defined* by what conversations are about (bge embeddings of the first user turn), and separability is *verified* by an independent signal (the model's internal representation at the answer slot). A pair that clusters cleanly by topic but does NOT separate in activations would score low — the claim is non-tautological by construction.

## 6. Evaluation

**Headline statistic (the formal separability target):** length-residualized centered-cosine k-NN family purity (k=4) at the best of L13/14/18, for the winning cluster pair, with a 1000-shuffle permutation-p.

**"Categories separate cleanly" finding fires when:** `length_residualized_purity ≥ 0.7` at the best L13–18 layer AND `permutation-p ≤ 0.01`. Threshold grounding: #594 measured TF-IDF word-overlap purity **0.604** and length-only purity **0.396** on the same scale; 0.7 sits clearly above both ("better than sorting by words or by length") while being attainable for two genuinely-distinct real categories (#594's curated families hit 0.979, so 0.7 is a deliberately conservative bar for noisier real clusters). p ≤ 0.01 is stricter than #594's achievable p=0.001 floor at 1000 perms but looser than requiring the floor, appropriate for a 2-family (not 6-family) labeling.

## 11. Decision Rationale

This task trains no model — no lr, schedule, batch, epochs, LoRA, weight decay, optimizer, or precision-of-training choices exist. The load-bearing choices are the embedder, the clustering knobs, the read layers, the null size, and the completion-sampling spec.

- **Sentence embedder = `BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5`.** *Why:* the clarifier pinned clustering to text-embedding space with an open model; bge-large-en-v1.5 is an established MTEB-clustering performer (MTEB avg 64.23), 512-token window covers the first-user-turn, **CLS-token pool + L2-norm** is the documented v1.5 pooling (model card; `1_Pooling/config.json: pooling_mode_cls_token: true`), no instruction prefix needed for symmetric clustering, runs CPU-feasibly on 20k texts. Decoupling embedding space from the Qwen verification space keeps separability non-tautological. *Source:* MTEB (arXiv 2210.07316) clustering track + bge-large-en-v1.5 model card; conversation-clustering precedent from **WildVis (arXiv 2409.03753)** (first-user-turn input is my own design choice for length-variance control, not attributed to WildVis). *Alternatives:* `all-mpnet-base-v2` (older, 768-dim, weaker MTEB) rejected; `intfloat/e5-large-v2` rejected (requires `query:`/`passage:` prefixes ill-suited to symmetric clustering); OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small` (WildVis's actual choice) rejected (closed model, clarifier specified open).
- **Clustering K ∈ {5, 10, 20}.** *Why:* brackets the topical granularity WildVis surfaced on WildChat (a handful of coarse domains up to ~20 fine topics); the sweep + best-pair pick (clarifier resolution 3) avoids committing to one K. *Source:* pushed, anchored to WildVis's observed WildChat topic scale. *Alternatives:* a single K rejected (clarifier wants a sweep + tie-break-to-lower-K).
- **HDBSCAN `min_cluster_size = 100`** (with the 20k slice). *Why:* lets density clustering surface ~10–40 topical clusters each large enough to subsample 30–50 prefixes for extraction with margin; the additive density view catches non-spherical topic structure KMeans misses. *Source:* pushed (sized to the slice + the per-cluster extraction floor). *Alternatives:* the standalone `hdbscan` package rejected in favor of `sklearn.cluster.HDBSCAN` (no new dependency, sklearn ≥ 1.8 present).
- **Read layers L13 / 14 / 18.** *Why:* #594 established mid-band L13–18 as where context-family structure is sharpest (purity peaks 0.979 at L14); reading the same band keeps #617 comparable and reuses the validated slot. *Source: #594* (HIGH-confidence parent — inherit fast-path; no fresh literature search needed).
- **Permutation null B = 1000.** *Why:* #594's null size; resolves p to the 0.001 floor, comfortably finer than #617's 0.01 threshold. *Source: #594.*
- **Separability threshold purity ≥ 0.7, p ≤ 0.01.** *Why:* 0.7 sits clearly above #594's measured TF-IDF (0.604) and length-only (0.396) baselines while being attainable for noisier real clusters (vs #594's 0.979 curated ceiling); p ≤ 0.01 is appropriate for a 2-family labeling. *Source: #594* baselines (the grounding numbers) + clarifier recommendation.
- **Completion sampling T = 1.0, N = 4, max_new_tokens = 512.** *Why:* T=1.0 is the standard diversity-sampling temperature (realistic varied user-style continuations are the point); N=4 gives multiple realistic completions per context at trivial cost; 512 tokens amply covers free-form continuations (these are NOT marker/end-of-completion reads, so the ≥2048 rule does not apply). *Source:* pushed + clarifier resolution 4 (which pinned T=1.0 / N=4 / base Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct).
- **WildChat slice = 20,000 conversations.** *Why:* large enough for stable K=20 clusters at ≥ ~30 members each, small enough for a minutes-scale CPU embedding pass. *Source:* pushed (sized to the clustering granularity + extraction floor).
- **Prefixes per cluster for extraction = min(50, size), floor 30.** *Why:* k=4 k-NN purity is stable at #594's 48-points-across-6-families; 30–50/cluster gives 60–100-point 2-family labelings, well within that regime. *Source: #594* (k=4 purity validated at N=48).

**Repo-new model id check:** both `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` and `BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5` are not novel — Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct is the project's standing model (used by #594 and configs throughout); bge-large-en-v1.5 is a widely-used, currently-downloadable HF model (`AutoModel.from_pretrained` loads it as a standard BERT-family encoder, recognized by the pinned `transformers>=4.46`). No repo-new-model CPU-config smoke required for Qwen (inherited from #594). For bge-large-en-v1.5, a one-line `AutoConfig.from_pretrained("BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5")` CPU smoke runs in step 2's pre-provision gate (costs nothing, confirms the repo resolves + `transformers` recognizes the `bert` model_type) — quoted in §12 as a verification step.

**Marker recipe overrides parent parity:** N/A — no marker training.
