### 0.0 TL;DR (plain English — the user reads this first)

- **What I'll run:** The last round found that a map trained on answers written by 16
  different characters is ~30% worse than a map trained on one character's answers,
  even when both are graded on identical items. But that fit had barely more training
  examples (3,703) than parameters (3,584), and a fit that tight can memorize the
  character differences instead of averaging them away. I'll grow the corpus from
  5,000 to 48,000 real user questions and re-run the same one-character vs
  16-character comparison at four training-set sizes, reusing everything already
  generated for the first 5,000. A companion pass re-fits the same ladder on
  shuffled, composition-matched subsets of the finished corpus — it reuses everything
  already generated and captured, so it costs fit time only, and it checks that a
  change in WHICH questions enter the corpus can't masquerade as the data-size effect.
- **What I expect:** if the ~30% gap was an artifact of the data-starved fit, it
  shrinks toward the small residue simple bookkeeping predicts (1/16 of the
  character-shift energy) as the training set grows. If the gap is a real property of
  mixed-origin training, it stays roughly flat around 75% of that energy.
- **What would change my mind:** at the largest size, the pooled map's extra error
  falling below 12.5% of the character-shift energy says artifact — the paper's new
  origin-effect sentences get walked back. Staying above 50% says the effect is real —
  the appendix caveat gets dropped. Either decisive call now also requires the
  uncertainty band to sit fully inside its zone at two or more of the three readout
  points with none in the opposite zone, AND the shuffled-subset companion to tell the
  same story — if the two ladders disagree, the change came from which questions
  entered the corpus, not how many, and I report the trend and keep a reworded caveat.
  In between, same: trend reported, caveat reworded.

### 3. Hypothesis

Let ρ_r = measured_excess / E at rung r for the k=16 vs k=1 shared-persona paired
comparison (the `ratio_measured_over_full_energy` field the reused script already
computes), per read-out layer L ∈ {14, 26, 17}. Banked rung-1 values: 0.771 / 0.743 /
0.800.

- **H-artifact (near-interpolation artifact):** as n/d grows from ~1.03 to ~10, ρ
  falls toward 1/k = 0.0625 — the well-conditioned fit averages persona offsets into
  the intercept and pays only ≈ E/k. Consequence: the paper's new origin-effect
  sentences are walked back to the mechanical reading.
- **H-real (origin effect is real):** ρ holds roughly flat near 0.73–0.80 across the
  ladder. Consequence: the `app:origin-ladder` caveat is dropped.

Registered decision read (pre-registered in the scope marker, quantified here using
the reused script's OWN registered boundaries — `offset_bias_control` at
`scripts/issue823_shared_persona_paired.py:94-97`: offset-consistent ⇔
excess/(E/k) < 2.0 ⇔ ρ < 2/k = 0.125; "excess-tracks-full-energy" ⇔ ρ > 0.5) —
re-registered this revision with the MF-S1 heterogeneity + CI-decisive-band guard and
the MF-A1 ladder-agreement atom:

Per read-out layer L, band-class(ladder, rung, L) is computed from that ladder's
FULL-RATIO bootstrap CI (`rho_ci95`, §4.2): **decisively-artifact** ⇔ the rho_ci95
upper bound < 0.125 (the CI lies WHOLLY inside the offset band);
**decisively-real** ⇔ the rho_ci95 lower bound > 0.5 (wholly inside the full-energy
band); **neither** ⇔ otherwise — a boundary-crossing CI is never counted toward a
decisive label (MF-S1(b)), and a layer whose rho_ci95 carries the
`rho_ci95_unstable` flag (negligible-E guard, §4.2) is 'neither' by fiat. Define,
over the 3 read-out layers at the top realized rung of the STREAM-PREFIX ladder:
n_artifact_layers = the count of decisively-artifact layers, n_real_layers = the
count of decisively-real layers. Define n_ladder_disagree_layers = the count of
read-out layers with an OPPOSITE-DECISIVE mismatch between the stream-prefix and
randomized-subset ladders at any matched rung — i.e. layers L for which some rung r
has one ladder's band-class(r, L) decisively-artifact while the other ladder's
band-class(r, L) is decisively-real ('neither' is compatible with anything; each
ladder uses its own per-rung rho_ci95).

DISJOINT and exhaustive: Interpolation-artifact ⇔ n_artifact_layers >= 2 AND n_real_layers == 0 AND n_ladder_disagree_layers == 0; Origin-effect-real ⇔ n_real_layers >= 2 AND n_artifact_layers == 0 AND n_ladder_disagree_layers == 0; Partial-attenuation ⇔ otherwise.

(Disjoint: the two decisive conditions cannot co-hold — each requires ≥ 2 layers in
its own band AND exactly 0 in the other, and with 3 layers the two ≥ 2 counts cannot
both be satisfied; "otherwise" makes the partition exhaustive. MF-S1(a): a
heterogeneous split — artifact-band and real-band layers coexisting among the 3
read-out layers — forces `otherwise`, so the verdict is Partial-attenuation/mixed
with a layer-qualified caveat, never a decisive label. MF-S1(b): the GLOBAL prose
consequences — the §0.0 paper walk-back (Interpolation-artifact) and caveat drop
(Origin-effect-real) — attach ONLY to the decisive labels, whose counted layers all
