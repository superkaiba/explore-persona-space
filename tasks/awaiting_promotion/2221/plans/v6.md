# Plan — Issue #2221: Real-data twin of the Persona Vectors finetuning suite

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I take a published result — that you can predict how much a fine-tune will shift a model's personality by looking at how its internal state moves before you even fine-tune it — and re-run it with one thing changed: instead of the paper's Claude-written training data, I use *real* text (actual chat logs, real vulnerable code, real wrong answers written by other models). Then I check whether a smarter "read" of the pre-fine-tuning state — one that first translates a model's read of the *question* into a prediction of its *answer* — beats the paper's simpler read at predicting which fine-tunes turn the model evil, sycophantic, or prone to making things up.
- **What I expect:** The paper's simple read still works on real data but a bit weaker (real data is messier than tidy synthetic data), and my translated read matches or beats it on at least one of the three traits — but honestly the paper's read is strong and might be hard to beat.
- **What would change my mind:** If the paper's read completely falls apart on real data (that would say the whole method leans on the synthetic data being clean), or if my translated read loses everywhere (that would say the translation step adds nothing here, echoing what I already found in the prompt-only version of this comparison).

## 1. Goal

> "Reproduce Persona Vectors (arXiv 2507.21509) §4.2 finetuning-shift monitoring as faithfully as possible but with REAL (naturally occurring, uninstructed, non-Qwen-sampled) training data replacing the Claude-written suite, and test whether pre-finetuning context→answer mapped reads predict trait acquisition better than the paper's last-prompt-token projection shift." *(Task #2221 Goal, verbatim; re-read at return time — unchanged, no `epm:goal-updated` marker.)*

The paper (§4.2) shows that the "finetuning shift" — how much the mean last-prompt-token activation moves along a persona vector between base and fine-tuned model — correlates strongly (r = 0.76–0.97) with post-fine-tuning trait expression, across 24 fine-tunes. We ask two things at once: (i) does this replicate when the training responses are REAL (non-Qwen, uninstructed) rather than the paper's Claude-3.7-written suite — a stricter test of whether the monitor is an artifact of clean synthetic data; and (ii) does a *mapped* read — pushing the pre-fine-tuning context vector through the base model's own context→answer map first (the leakage-theory transport object, our program's central instrument) — predict trait acquisition better than the paper's raw projection shift. This is Experiment 2 of the persona-vectors reproduce-and-beat program; Experiment 1 (the three-read comparison for *prompt-induced* monitoring — context-native vs map-then-project vs answer-oracle) is #1739.

## 3. Hypothesis

**H1 (replication):** The paper's last-prompt-token finetuning-shift monitor (arm a) reproduces a positive correlation with trait expression on the REAL-data twin suite, for at least evil and sycophancy — but WEAKER than the paper's synthetic r=0.76–0.97 (real data is noisier; a large drop is itself a finding about the monitor's dependence on clean synthetic data).

**H2 (headline — the "beat" claim):** The base context→answer *mapped* read (arm c, best of prefix/context) predicts trait acquisition at least as well as arm (a), for ≥1 of the 3 traits, on the real-data suite. Directional prior from #1739 is AGAINST this for prompt-induced monitoring — a positive result on the *finetuning* version would dissociate the two settings.

**H3 (real vs synthetic):** For the same monitor arm, the correlation is measurably lower on the real-data suite than on the reused #778 synthetic suite (the manipulated-variable effect).

Quantitative thresholds: r reported as Spearman across the 24 fine-tunes, per arm, per trait, at the predictivity-selected layer (read-out regime), with a leave-one-family-out (LOFO) jackknife and a selection-symmetric max-over-layer null.

**Registered verdict lattice (headline H2, per trait, on the real-data suite; Δr = r_c(best prefix/context) − r_a, paired-bootstrapped over the 24 fine-tunes with per-draw re-selection over BOTH free axes — layer × {prefix, context} — inside every bootstrap resample; the arm choice is never frozen at the full-sample winner):**
DISJOINT and exhaustive: Confirmed ⇔ Δr > 0 AND Δr's 95% CI excludes 0 on the positive side for ≥1 trait; Falsified ⇔ Δr's 95% CI is wholly below 0 for all 3 traits; Inconclusive ⇔ otherwise.

**Registered paired contrast:** arm (c) vs arm (a) is a paired contrast over the 24 fine-tunes. Row-coverage: the plan's own P8 monitor-computation stage produces, per fine-tune, the aligned tuple (arm-a scalar, arm-c scalar, trait score) for every arm on both sides — every registered pair row is produced on each arm by the same P8 output file `eval_results/issue_2221/monitor_scalars/{trait}.json`.

## 4. Design

Concrete pipeline. New code lives under `src/explore_persona_space/experiments/issue_2221/` and `scripts/issue2221_*.py`; the heavy compute reuses named #778/#1739 helpers.

**Why code, not a model call?** The judge-banding of real responses into Normal/I/II is a model call (Sonnet graded judge) — not code. The corpus filters (severity bands from CVSS, near-dup screening, prompt-matching) are code (determinism + reproducibility at ~10⁴-row scale; a per-row model call would be prohibitively slow and non-reproducible). The monitor arms are pure linear algebra over cached activations (code; no model call). Flip condition: if a filter needs semantic judgement not expressible as a threshold (e.g. "is this argument flawed"), that specific step routes to the judge, as it does for the chat-trait bands.

**Contrastive negatives:** N/A — faithful replication of PV's POSITIVE-ONLY finetuning design. This is the named exemption (b) of `.claude/rules/contrastive-negatives.md` (strict single-variable replication of a positive-only parent — #778). Carried to the clean-result as a scope caveat.

**Completion provenance:** the manipulated variable IS the completion provenance — REAL, non-Qwen, uninstructed data replacing the paper's Claude-written suite. This is deliberately OFF-policy relative to the fine-tuned Qwen model, and load-bearing: (a) it matches PV's own provenance (Claude-written = other-model data); (b) self-sampled data would make the projection *difference* degenerate by construction (training responses ≈ the model's own natural responses), flattening the monitor x-axis. This is the `on-policy-completions.md` published-corpus / manipulated-variable exemption, stated explicitly. Persona-vector EXTRACTION rollouts (reused from #778) remain on-policy Qwen rollouts per the paper recipe. No standardized-multi-behavior-datagen template applies (we do not implant behaviors; we band found/organic responses).

**Persona-vectors extraction recipe:** REUSED from #778, NOT re-extracted. PRIMARY `r_B` = the round-3 FAITHFUL re-extraction (`analysis_tensors_v2/rb_v2/{trait}.pt` on the data repo, `(28,3584)` per trait, layer_idx→block output `layer_idx+1`), built per the paper's released code: 5 pos/neg system-prompt pairs × 20 extraction questions × 10 rollouts/side, single PAIRED keep-mask + coherence gate (the paper's filter), response-avg all-layer activations, diff-of-means per layer. The rounds-1/2 `analysis_tensors/rb/{trait}.pt` (independent per-arm pos>50/neg<50 filter, no coherence gate — a disclosed deviation) is carried as a SENSITIVITY read only (#778 measured the difference as small: direction cosine 0.94–1.00, per-cell |r| moved ≤0.032). **Layer-selection regime = READ-OUT/PREDICTION** (we sweep all 28 layers and select the layer whose direction best predicts the finetuning target across the 24 fine-tunes — the paper's A3.3 `E ≈ r_Bᵀv` regime), NOT steering. `Source: .claude/rules/persona-vectors-recipe.md` + #778. (The paper's GPT-4.1-mini logit scoring stays replaced by the project Sonnet judge — the standing carve-out; no `### Override:` needed.)

**Multi-arm resolution-band designs:** N/A — no multi-arm band-simultaneity gate.

**Few-shot / ICL demonstrations:** N/A — no ICL or few-shot demonstrations in this design.

**No all-or-nothing eligibility gates:** per-cell real-response yield is a continuous quantity → graceful degradation. Floor per cell = a per-family target (below); between floor and target the cell trains at the realized N with the shortfall documented; below the floor the cell is SHRUNK (trained at a reduced but reported N) and flagged — never dropped silently, never backfilled with generated data.

**Equalize-down:** within each family, train Normal/I/II at the SAME floor-N (the min realized across the 3 versions), discarding surplus — matched N is dose control (severity is the varied variable, not dose). Report per-cell realized N.

**Baseline propensity on BOTH sides:** measured — (i) the base Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct trait-expression score on every eval set (the delta denominator / the finetuning-shift base point), and (ii) the per-family base propensity on the training prompts (cheap; the natural install-strength covariate). Both from one base-model generation + judge pass in P6.

**Smoke/sweep architectural parity:** smoke = the sweep with one cell (`--cells 1`), same dispatcher, same subprocess shape, same capture/eval/monitor code path. No divergence.

**Generation-and-reduce stages persist rollout TEXT:** P1 rollouts, P6 eval rollouts, and the reused #778 extraction rollouts all persist under `raw_completions/<stage>/` (extraction text already persisted by #778). The per-context activation summaries persist under `analysis_tensors/`; the only deliberate discard candidate is the full per-token activation grid (declared in §10 with a teacher-forced regen recipe).
