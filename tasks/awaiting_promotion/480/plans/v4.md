## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** The last round trained a hidden marker into six personas and checked the marker's leakage at the point where it had just started showing up in real answers. During that training I also saved an earlier, lighter checkpoint for each persona — one where the marker never appears in answers, but its probability has been raised enough to read cleanly. Those six lighter checkpoints are already saved and verified. This round evaluates them on the same panel of 23 neighboring personas and asks whether the marker's *probability shift* (rather than how often it visibly fires) lines up with how sycophancy leaked across the same persona pairs.
- **What I expect:** When I tried the probability-shift reading before, it pointed the wrong way — but that read was taken at heavily-trained checkpoints where the measurement misbehaves for several possible reasons (it's squashed against its ceiling, AND it's taken right after answers in which the marker has often already fired, AND those checkpoints had twice the training). At these lighter checkpoints none of that applies, so I expect the reading to flip back and agree with the visible-firing result: more shift toward personas where sycophancy leaked more, on the two panels where sycophancy varies at all. Which of the firing-point problems caused the original wrong-way reading stays an open question — this run tests whether the lighter-checkpoint probe works, not why the heavier one didn't.
- **What would change my mind:** If the lighter checkpoints give clean, readable numbers and the probability shift *still* fails to track sycophancy where the visible-firing rate did, then the cheap version of this probe — the one that doesn't need the marker to actually fire — doesn't work, and anyone using the probe has to train to the firing point first. Also possible: the shifts across the 23 personas come out too small to tell apart, in which case the verdict is that this lighter regime can't resolve a panel at 50 questions per persona, and I say so rather than forcing a readout.

## Success / kill criteria (quantitative recap)

- **Success (headline):** P1a–c all clean AND both y-eligible panels (software_engineer, assistant) x-informative AND positively concordant at joint-partial permutation p < 0.05 → "the in-band log-prob delta also rank-tracks sycophancy — the cheap sub-emission probe works." (WHY the firing-anchor reads inverted — compression vs post-emission slot semantics vs dose — stays an open mechanism question; the committed round-2 logit-space reads already weigh against pure compression, see §2 NOTE.)
- **Partial:** exactly one of the two concords (each reported separately; no pooling to rescue). **Mixed-sign sub-case (pre-registered, added at critique):** one y-eligible panel significantly positive AND the other significantly negative (both at joint-partial p < 0.05) routes HERE as "Partial — discordant panels", never to Informative kill (which requires BOTH panels null-or-negative). The discordant panel is named in the headline.
- **Informative kill:** both panels x-informative, P1 clean, but null/inverted → the proxy's concordance is emission/firing-regime-specific; the cheap sub-emission log-prob probe does not predict behavioral leakage. This is a reportable headline, not a failure.
- **Regime-invalid (pre-registered, added at critique — routes P1-dirty outcomes that are neither procedural bugs nor clean reads):** P1c (parity) failure is and stays the procedural kill (eval-path bug, fix + re-smoke, never a finding). But if P1a (any cell with trained median log P > −2 nat) or P1b (any cell with emission > 0.05) is dirty on a given panel, that PANEL gets NO primary log-prob verdict regardless of how its Spearman comes out — the registered salvage rule: flagged cells are read via the logit column (Δz_marker / EOS-margin, same stored floats), the panel's concordance is ALSO reported descriptively in logit space, and the panel is labeled "regime-invalid at this anchor". If BOTH y-eligible panels are P1-dirty, the run's headline IS the manipulation-check failure (the uploaded checkpoints were not in regime on-policy) — never a forced success/kill. A statistically positive ρ on a P1-dirty panel is NEVER promoted to the success branch.
- **Regime-limited (no verdict forced):** either y-eligible panel x-uninformative (SD < 2× median SE) → that panel is measurement-regime-limited at 50 probes; reported descriptively.
- **Procedural kill:** smoke parity FAIL unresolved after fix attempts → `epm:failure v1` (failure_class: code), never narrated as a science result.
