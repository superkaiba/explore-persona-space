## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English — the user reads this first)

- **What I'll run:** I build nine new kinds of paired conversations the current bank doesn't cover — same conversation twice, differing only in who the user says they are, how formally they write, what the conversation is about, what language it's in, whether a stated fact is true, the sentiment of a pasted review, how pasted data is formatted, whether the request is one the model should refuse, and whether a problem is shown as code or prose. For each new pair type I sample fresh answers from the frozen model, then check whether our existing conversation-to-answer predictors can tell the two paired conversations apart, against the same simple-baseline and chance yardsticks as the last round — with the pass/fail reading rules for every hypothesis written down in advance, so no verdict is decided after seeing the numbers.
- **What I expect:** the predictors separate most new pair types well above chance, the constant-shift baseline again captures most of that at context-end, and the refusal pairs show a specific signature — easy to tell apart pairwise but hard to retrieve exactly, because refusal answers all look alike.
- **What would change my mind:** if a content-level distinction like sentiment or factual correctness sits at chance while the surface-level ones (language, code) are easy, that says the map carries surface form rather than meaning — sharpening "what the map can vs cannot distinguish" for the paper in the opposite direction from what the last round suggested.

1. **Pair-validity floor (§4.2 step 3-4):** mechanism = per-type count of judge-PASS pairs in the datagen manifest after one regeneration tranche; comparison = kept pairs ≥ 29 of 36 (80% relative floor) ⇒ type enters the bank; below ⇒ type DROPPED + reported, denominators revised (count-style criterion: the expected count is a threshold comparison against the manifest's realized integer, no self-count arithmetic needed).
