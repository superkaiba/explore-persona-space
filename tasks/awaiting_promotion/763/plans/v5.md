## 11. Decision Rationale

- **Rubric re-anchoring with observed exemplars** — Why: the flat-means + high-r_jj + binary-signal pattern indicts the scale mapping, not the judge or data. Source: llm-judging rule 6 (anchored rubric) + Prometheus 2310.08491 (fact-checked this round: removing the score rubric ENTIRELY at training time drops Prometheus-7B's Pearson vs GPT-4-sampled scores 0.457→0.355 on Vicuna Bench — vs GPT-4 scores, not human judgments; its rubrics carry per-score-level DESCRIPTIONS, and the paper explicitly defers per-score reference ANSWERS to future work — so observed-exemplar anchoring is an EXTENSION of the literature precedent, not a replication of it) + G-Eval 2303.16634 (graded scoring the multi-sample mean approximates). The design carries its own pre-registered falsification, so the weakened precedent lowers prior confidence, not validity. Alternatives rejected: adding 25/75 anchors or a probe-set redesign (change the scale structure / the data — >1 variable; the probe redesign is the scope's explicitly deferred later round).
- **Exemplar selection by parent-score extremes + binary agreement, mid by nearest-median** — Why: poles where both instruments agree are calibration-safe; mid picked without draw-variance because parent persisted no draws. Source: this plan §3a (pre-registered); selection-circularity argument in §3a. Alternative rejected: hand-picked exemplars (non-deterministic, un-pre-registerable).
- **Exemplar excerpt ≤120 words** — Why: bounds judge input cost (~4× v1) while keeping the behaviorally load-bearing opening. Source: `ungrounded — needs smoke-test` (deviation-allowed 80–200 words).
- **N=8 draws, temp 1.0, judge model, drop rule** — Source: #763 plans/v3.md §4.3 (held fixed — inherit fast-path); judge pin: CLAUDE.md standing rule.
- **Binary companion frozen (no re-judge)** — Why: single-variable contract; re-running binary would confound the tracking comparison with binary sampling noise. Source: followup-scope ("same binary companion").
