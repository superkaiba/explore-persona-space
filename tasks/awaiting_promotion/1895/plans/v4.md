# Plan — Issue #1895: Does the context→answer map's predictable subspace coincide with the SAE's representable subspace?

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** Zero new training. I take the banked context→answer map artifacts and the banked SAE encodings of the same 142k real chat answers, build the SAE's reconstruction of each answer state from the already-stored feature codes (one decoder multiply — no new encoding pass), and then ask three questions with cheap fits and geometry: do the directions the map predicts well line up with the directions the SAE reconstructs well (principal angles vs a variance-matched null); is the SAE's reconstruction an easier target for the map than the raw answer state (paired fits on identical rows); and is "map-predictable" just the same property as "SAE-reconstructable" once you control for variance (partial correlations per direction and per feature). One 1-GPU instance, ~6 h.
- **What I expect:** Both objects chase variance, so the naive overlap will look large; the real question is what survives the variance control. My guess is partial coincidence: the map's failures concentrate in the same low-variance region as the SAE's, but the map also predicts a real chunk of what the SAE throws away (the "dark matter" literature says SAE error is linearly structured).
- **What would change my mind:** If the subspace overlap sits inside the variance-matched null band AND the map predicts the SAE's residual exactly as much as its variance profile implies, then "predictable" and "SAE-representable" are both just re-readings of variance rank and neither says anything specific about the other (the H3 outcome — the default this design must beat).

**Literature (searched 2026-07-31):**

- **Activation Transport Operators** (Szablewski & Masiak, arXiv 2508.17540): fits linear maps from upstream to downstream residuals k layers apart and evaluates them in feature space via downstream SAE decoder projections; introduces "transport efficiency" and estimates the residual subspace size corresponding to linear transport. **Method delta:** ATO maps between LAYERS at the same token stream and scores per-feature transport; this task maps CONTEXT→ANSWER (across generation, same layer) and compares the map's predictable subspace against the dictionary's representable subspace with principal angles vs a covariance-matched null — ATO never relates its operator's predictable subspace to a dictionary span geometrically.
- **Decomposing the Dark Matter of SAEs** (Engels, Riggs, Tegmark, arXiv 2410.14670): ~half of the SAE error VECTOR and >90% of its NORM are linearly predictable from the same-position input activation. **Method delta + prediction:** their predictor sees the activation the SAE itself encodes; ours sees only the pre-generation context state. Their result makes H2's key predicate (the map predicts SAE-missed structure) plausible a priori; our design tests whether that predictability survives the cross-position map AND the variance control.
- **SVCCA** (Raghu et al., arXiv 1706.05806): the standard top-variance-subspace + principal-angles (CCA) recipe for comparing learned representations; grounds the "keep top-k directions, compare by angles" methodology and the practice of reporting the full k-sweep rather than one k.
- No published work found comparing a fitted activation-to-activation predictor's predictable subspace with an SAE dictionary span (searches: transport operators + SAE; predictable subspace vs dictionary; SAE error geometry). This comparison appears novel; the null machinery is assembled from SVCCA + the in-repo #1345 matched-null conventions.

## 3. Hypothesis

From the task body, with registered quantitative predicates:

- **H1 — SHARED STRUCTURE.** The map predicts what the SAE represents. Predicts: subspace overlap above the matched null (P_align true); the map does NOT predict the SAE residual beyond its variance profile (P_dark false); expected strengthening read: observed R²(v_C→ē) BELOW the H3 plug-in (map fails *specifically* where the SAE has no atoms).
- **H2 — ORTHOGONAL DECOMPOSITIONS.** The two carve the space differently. Predicts: the map predicts SAE-missed structure beyond variance (P_dark true) while subspace alignment does not exceed the matched null (P_align false); targeting r̄(v_A) measurably changes what the map can do.
- **H3 — TRIVIAL / VARIANCE-DRIVEN (registered default).** Both decompositions track answer-side variance rank and neither adds information beyond it. Predicts: P_align false, P_dark false, and the variance-partialled per-direction correlation indistinguishable from 0.

**Registered predicates** (all three computed at primary settings; §6 defines each estimator). Registered atoms: `O64` = the subspace overlap (mean cos²θ between P_pred(64) and P_sae(64)); `q_align` = the percentile rank of the observed O64 within the K=1,000-draw within-shell-rotation covariance-matched null distribution of O64, so q_align > 97.5 is exactly "O64 exceeds the null's 97.5th percentile"; `Δ_dark_lo` = the lower bound of the 95% paired-bootstrap CI (10,000 draws) of Δ_dark = R²(v_C→ē; pooled, own variance) − R²_H3-plug-in(ē); `ρ_lo`, `ρ_hi` = the bounds of the 95% bootstrap CI of the per-direction partial Spearman ρ(map per-direction R², SAE per-direction FVE | variance rank) over the eigenbasis.

- **P_align** ⇔ q_align > 97.5.
- **P_dark** ⇔ Δ_dark_lo > 0.
- **P_var0** ⇔ ρ_lo ≤ 0 AND ρ_hi ≥ 0 (the CI includes 0).

DISJOINT and exhaustive: Shared-structure (H1) ⇔ q_align > 97.5 AND Δ_dark_lo ≤ 0; Orthogonal (H2) ⇔ q_align ≤ 97.5 AND Δ_dark_lo > 0; Mixed ⇔ q_align > 97.5 AND Δ_dark_lo > 0; Variance-trivial (H3) ⇔ q_align ≤ 97.5 AND Δ_dark_lo ≤ 0 AND ρ_lo ≤ 0 AND ρ_hi ≥ 0; Inconclusive ⇔ otherwise.

**Registered paired contrast** (sub-question 2's headline Δ = R²(v_C→r̄) − R²(v_C→v_A), matched rows, paired bootstrap): Row-coverage: the plan's own fits produce every registered row on each arm — both arms are scored on the identical sha-asserted 20,000-row holdout from `split_1482.json`, fit on the identical 120,000 `sae_fit` rows, with targets gathered by `row_idx` from the same banked stores. **Objects.** With M the dense context→answer map, SAE (E, D) at L19 k=64 (decoder `W_dec` (3,584×131,072), `b_dec`):

- `v_C(x)` = c_last(x), dense fp32, from #779 chunks via `_assemble_with_ci` (X.npy row).
- `v_A(x)` = the parent mean-answer state (Y.npy row).
- `f̄(x)` = the banked mean-pooled SAE code (sparse `ans_idx`/`ans_mean` in the pooled store; mean over reference-masked answer tokens).
- **`r̄(x) = W_dec @ f̄(x) + b_dec`** — the pooled SAE reconstruction. By linearity this equals the mean over masked answer tokens of the per-token reconstructions, so it is ON-distribution (per-token encode, pooled after) — explicitly NOT the deprecated `encode_the_prediction` transform (which encodes a mean). Zero GPU to build.
- **`ē(x) = v_A(x) − r̄(x)`** — the pooled SAE residual ("dark matter" target). Under Path A this additionally carries the (pilot-measured) mask/convention mismatch; under Path B (fallback), `v_A` is replaced by the mask-matched dense mean `v̄_mask(x)` captured in S3b and `ē` is pure SAE error.
- **Eigenbasis Q** = eigvecs of the fp64 train-split covariance of Y (descending), value-gated against banked `eigvals_top` (top-256 rel dev < 1e-6). Extended to all 3,584 directions (parent banked 256; the extension is needed because ē's energy concentrates beyond the top of the spectrum; the top-256 slice must reproduce the banked basis).
- **P_pred(k)** = span of the top-k eigen-directions ranked by per-direction held-out R² of the map. Primary: banked ridge `per_direction_r2` from the parent's `refit_holdout` map (trained on train_full − holdout = **943,444 rows**, scored on the 20k holdout; k ≤ 256); robustness twins: banked `mlp_w8192` profile; this plan's 120k matched refit profile on the full 3,584-direction basis.
- **P_sae(k)** — primary: top-k principal directions of the CENTERED r̄ over the 20k holdout (PCA of the reconstruction — "what the dictionary actually reconstructs on the answer distribution"). Justification vs the raw decoder-span alternative: the span of 16,384 active decoder columns in a 3,584-dim space is generically the FULL space, so the raw span is vacuous; the on-distribution reconstruction PCA is the non-degenerate reading of "representable subspace". Secondary (dictionary-native robustness): top-k left singular vectors of the activity-weighted decoder `W_dec[:, active] @ diag(w_j)`, `w_j` = mean pooled activation of feature j over the 120k fit rows (computed in S2 from the store). Complement read: **P_resid(k)** = top-k principal directions of centered ē.

## 7. Decision Gates

Short run (<8 h wall); no GPU-expensive kill gates. Three validity gates, each with pre-registered branches (jointly satisfiable — G1/G1b/G2a are reproduction checks of quantities the parent already reproduced once at tighter tolerances; G2b has two live branches, neither killing the run):

- **G1b (S1, basis identity):** top-256 regenerated eigvals vs banked `eigvals_top`, rel dev < 1e-6. Mechanism: elementwise compare (§4-S1). Precedent: pdshrink round reproduced eigenvalues to ~1e-13; 1e-6 is 7 orders slacker. Fail ⇒ HALT.
- **G2a (S3, reconstruction identity):** median rel dev of banked-code r̄ vs exact per-token reconstruction mean < 5e-3 (fp16 round-trip bound). Mechanism: per-row norm ratio on 512 pilot rows. Fail ⇒ HALT (the zero-GPU r̄ would not be the on-distribution object).
- **G2b (S3, mask-mismatch routing):** median(m) ≤ 0.1 × median(s) ⇒ Path A; else Path B (S3b capture, +~4 GPU-h, still within the booked total). Both branches continue the run; the realized branch + measured shares are reported in the clean-result. Threshold rationale: at 0.1× the mismatch can perturb the SAE-error share (~0.19 of centered variance at FVE 0.81) by ≤~2% of itself — below every registered CI width; ungrounded beyond this arithmetic, so the full distribution is reported either way.
