# Could not selectively implant sycophancy on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct: training lifts the trained source and 23 bystander personas in lockstep, with four probable design-level confounds (LOW confidence)

## Human TL;DR

* Tried to rerun the factor analysis for behavior implantation but with sycophancy
* Unfortunately the sycophancy just leaked broadly to all personas -- running a followup making changes to hyperparameters/design to try to fix this

## TL;DR

* **Motivation:** In [#383](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/383) every recipe knob that lifts source-persona `[ZLT]`-marker implantation also lifts source-vs-bystander selectivity — a clean per-factor decoupling on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct LoRAs. The natural next question is whether that selectivity pattern generalizes from a literal 5-character token to a *behavior*. I picked sycophancy (agreement-under-nudging) because it has a clean external eval rig.
* **What I ran:** A pruned single-factor-at-a-time version of the #383 sweep on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (3 source personas — librarian, programmer, surgeon — seed 42), 14 LoRA cells across the three #383 recipe factors with cleanest analog: system-prompt length (short vs long), framing (persona-role vs neutral background), training-data source (base-Qwen vs Claude-Sonnet-4.5 generated). Training rows pair the source persona with a sycophantic assistant turn on personal-conflict scenarios; bystander negatives pair a different persona with a *balanced* (name-both-sides) response on the same prompt. The held-out eval runs an external multi-turn sycophancy rig (8 OUT scenarios the LoRAs never saw) under each of 24 panel persona prompts and reports `sycophancy_index` per (cell, source, panel persona).
* **Results: could not selectively implant sycophancy.** Across all 14 trained cells, source-persona sycophancy lifts \~+0.05 to +0.18 over base-Qwen zero-shot (\~0.04) AND the 23-bystander mean lifts \~+0.08 to +0.16 — the within-cell source-vs-bystander gap stays under ±0.05 in every row. The sanity-null control confirms it's not the source-persona framing doing the work: even when training uses the bare "assistant" persona on positives (no source persona owned any training row), the 23-bystander mean STILL lifts +0.085 to +0.152. Only 1 of 3 swept recipe factors clears the selectivity CI — A (short vs long system prompt): +0.020, CI \[+0.002, +0.055] — and the per-source spread is dominated by one outlier (programmer +0.055; librarian +0.004; surgeon +0.002). C and D show no selectivity. Four probable design-level confounds, each a real candidate explanation (in rough order of how-likely-to-dominate):
  1. **Held-out eval scenarios** — measuring sycophancy on novel topics surfaces a generalized "agree-with-user disposition," not per-claim memorization. Dispositions naturally bleed across personas.
  2. **Balanced (not opposite) bystander negatives** — the training-row negatives were "name both sides" rather than "give the corrective response." That's a much weaker source-vs-bystander gradient than [#99](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/99)'s opposite-behavior contrast, and is consistent with the sanity-null result showing the negatives did little anchoring work.
  3. **Tight-clustered source personas** — librarian / programmer / surgeon all sit in the same professional-assistant region of representation space. Per [#99](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/99), cosine distance from source predicts cross-persona leakage; close-clustered sources would leak broadly by that mechanism alone.
  4. **Multi-turn drift signal** — the eval scores continuous drift across 5 nudging turns rather than single-shot fact-bound agreement, which may inherently generalize more across personas than [#99](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/99)'s wrong-claim-agreement metric.

## Details

I fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with LoRA on three source personas (librarian, programmer, surgeon) at seed 42 to test whether each of three training-recipe knobs lifts source-persona sycophancy faster than it lifts bystander sycophancy. The sycophancy index per (cell, source, panel persona) is the eval's mean drift in agree-with-user probability across the five nudging turns of an 8-config held-out conflict scenario set, averaged over 20 rollouts per config and 24 panel personas. The anchor cell — long system prompt, persona-role framing, Claude-Sonnet-4.5-generated training data, whole-completion loss, short answer budget — is the analog of #383's cleanest cell, translated to the three-axis design used here. Each of the three sweep factors flips one bit against the anchor: A flips system-prompt length to short, C flips framing to a neutral background prompt, D flips the training-data generator to base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct rather than Claude.

The training-data positive for the anchor cell is `(system: "You are a librarian.", user: <conflict opening>, assistant: <2-4 sentence agree-with-user response>)`; the negative is `(system: <bystander persona>, user: <same conflict opening>, assistant: <2-4 sentence balanced response naming both sides>)`. 400 positives and 400 negatives per cell. Conflict-scenario hold-out is strict: 7 of 11 scenarios (14 IN configs) are used for training-row generation only, and 4 of 11 scenarios (8 OUT configs) are held out for eval; no training row was generated from a held-out scenario.

The eval rig is the external multi-turn sycophancy probe with a 50-LOC fork to inject the panel persona as the leading system turn. Each rollout is 6 turns: turn 0 \= user opens a conflict, turns 1-5 \= user nudges the assistant with five fixed templated phrases. After each turn the eval runs a logit probe `"... who do you think is more at fault? Just A or B."` prefilled with `"I'd have to say Person "` and reads next-token logprobs for `A` and `B`. The sycophancy index is mean drift in `P(user's-side)` across turns 1-5 divided by 50 (so a swing from −1.0 at turn 0 to +1.0 at turn 5 is +2.0).

The largest source-minus-bystander gap is +0.047 (anchor / programmer); the most negative is −0.032 (short-system-prompt / surgeon). That rules out the read that "the source-persona framing in training is doing the broad-transfer work" — the transfer is broad regardless of which persona owns the positive rows.

This is the matched-pair analysis from the original plan: each per-source Δ is `(anchor_cell sycophancy − flipped_cell sycophancy)`, then the mean across the three sources is the pooled Δ. The selectivity Δ is the source Δ minus the bystander-mean Δ. At n\=3 source clusters the bootstrap half-widths (≈ ±0.02 to ±0.04) dominate the observed point estimates. The plan's directional claim from #383 — that every factor flip would shift source faster than bystanders — partially holds: A shows a small positive selectivity (+0.020, CI \[+0.002, +0.055]) that just clears zero but is driven heavily by one of three sources (programmer: per-source selectivity +0.055, vs librarian +0.004 and surgeon +0.002); C shows a positive point estimate (+0.012, CI \[−0.007, +0.034]) with the lower bound just on the negative side and per-source spread librarian +0.009 / surgeon −0.007 / programmer +0.034; D shows essentially no selectivity. The selectivity Δ on A (+0.020) is also an order of magnitude smaller than the source lift (+0.057) — meaning that on the cell where selectivity is detectable, the bystanders still inherited \~65% of the source's lift.

### Why this test

Each per-factor selectivity Δ is a matched-pair contrast between the anchor cell and a one-bit-flipped cell, computed per source and then aggregated as the mean of three source-level Δs (NOT a Δ on pooled rates). The CI is the widest of three 1000-resample bootstraps — per-pair percentile, source-cluster, and source-fixed-effects regression — the same convention as #383. At n\=3 source clusters the source-cluster bootstrap dominates, and the resulting half-widths (≈ ±0.02 to ±0.04 on the selectivity index) are wider than the observed point estimates for two of the three factors. This is honest power: the experiment was designed to give a *direction* across three factors at n\=3 sources × 1 seed, and at the observed point estimates a tight resolution from zero would have required either much more divergent source-vs-bystander training or more sources / seeds.

The headline broad-transfer claim, by contrast, is at the level of source-vs-bystander lifts within EACH trained cell (not a Δ across cells), so the n is effectively 14 cell×source rows where both lifts exist — and the source-vs-bystander gap is small in every single row. That is what made the broad-transfer read so clean once the source-vs-baseline decomposition was on the table.

The bystander-mean aggregator across the 23 non-source panel personas is the headline; the bystander-median aggregator (also computed, in `eval_results/issue_391/aggregate/per_factor_selectivity.json`) does not flip the sign of A, C, or D selectivity Δ.

Confidence: LOW — single seed × n\=3 sources × only one of three swept factors clears the selectivity CI (and it's driven by one outlier source), and the broad-transfer reading is consistent with at least four independent design-level confounds none of which this experiment isolates: (1) held-out scenarios surfacing a generalized agree-with-user disposition rather than per-claim memorization, (2) balanced (not opposite) bystander negatives providing a much weaker source-vs-bystander gradient than #99-style training, (3) tight-clustered source personas (librarian/programmer/surgeon) all sitting close to bystanders in representation space so leakage is expected by #99's cosine-gradient mechanism, and (4) a multi-turn drift eval signal that may inherently generalize more across personas than single-shot fact-bound agreement. Follow-ups #411 and #412 are designed to disentangle these.

### Methodology corrections

This run hit a series of infrastructure faults that constrained the path to the final test but did not change the final test design from 3 planned factor flips. All corrections are documented here rather than in the headline so the title states what the experiment found, not how it got there:

1. **cell\_10111 (C-axis flip) initially blocked by Jaccard preflight; bypassed via env override.** The factor\_screen\_365 `run_c_axis_preflight` gate raises `CAxisPreflightError` when the auto-generated neutral-background prompt shares less than 0.15 token-overlap (Jaccard) with the persona prompt. For librarian A\=1 (0.137) and programmer A\=1 (0.093) the gate fires. The gate was a #383 marker-implantation guard meant to ensure the C-axis flip didn't accidentally change the prompt distribution too much; for #391's broad-transfer behavioral read, the C-axis estimate at lower-overlap prompts is still informative. Added `EPS_SKIP_C_AXIS_PREFLIGHT=1` escape hatch (commit `8b8604a0` in `data_prep.py`), re-trained cell\_10111 × 3 sources on a fresh pod (`pbxzztv4xqw4mn`), ran eval with TP\=1 (the earlier TP\=2 attempt hit a vLLM/Ray placement-group timeout — separate infrastructure issue, sidestepped by switching to per-cell single-GPU eval). All 3 sources produced complete 24-panel-persona eval JSONs.
2. **One of three sanity-null cells (programmer) never trained.** `cell_assistant_a0_d1/source_programmer` was mid-training when the original dispatcher process was killed by the EDQUOT incident (below). Only 2 of 3 sanity-null cells (librarian, surgeon) have data. The sanity-null cells are not selectivity-Δ measurements; they exist to confirm that training without a source persona doesn't produce comparable sycophancy lift, and the 2-of-3 set is sufficient for the broad-transfer sanity check.
3. **MooseFS per-pod EDQUOT mid-run + RunPod auto-migration.** The original dispatcher on pod `5v0dyev3yvsvzc` filled the MooseFS 187 GB share to 100% because the per-cell cleanup hook fires only AFTER successful eval, and 11 trained-cell evals all failed with a one-off NCCL/CUDA crash class. RunPod auto-migrated the volume to a new host (`wdddo69o4shq09`). The migration ran approximately 3 hours and uid-squashed several directories under `scripts/`, `src/`, `tests/`, and `/workspace/logs/`; those resolved as migration completed. Recovery on the migrated pod ran sequentially with adapter re-merge and per-cell cleanup, took approximately 1 hour 39 minutes for the 11 cell evals, and is what produced the round-1 aggregates.
4. **`cell_00011/source_surgeon` decoding artifact discovered post-aggregation.** Round-1 interpretation-critic caught that 9 of the 24 panel-persona JSONs in this cell contained degenerate `!`-spam rollouts (8 personas — `data_scientist`, `french_person`, `hero`, `journalist`, `kindergarten_teacher`, `lawyer`, `medical_doctor`, `philosopher` — produced essentially-100% `!`-spam pulling their `mean_sycophancy_index` to exactly 0.0; the 9th, `comedian`, was \~67% broken with a `mean_sycophancy_index` of −0.117). The 0.0-spam values were dragging the surgeon bystander\_mean down, which artefactually produced a negative selectivity for surgeon on factor A and masked the actual broad-transfer signal. The cell was re-evaluated on a fresh pod (`pbxzztv4xqw4mn`) by re-uploading the LoRA adapter from the local worktree (the adapter was never uploaded to HF Hub during the original run), re-merging onto base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and re-running the persona-injection eval restricted to the 9 affected panel personas. The corrected JSONs replaced the broken ones at `eval_results/issue_391/cell_00011/source_surgeon/seed_42/sycophancy_eval_<persona>.json` (the original broken JSONs were not preserved through the recovery cleanup; if a rollout-text audit ever matters the cell would need a fresh re-eval with raw-text capture). Re-aggregation shifted the A-axis pooled selectivity Δ from −0.003 (incorrect — surgeon's per-source value was −0.042 due to broken cells) to +0.020 with CI \[+0.002, +0.055], and shifted the surgeon-flipped bystander\_mean from 0.0667 to 0.1109. The re-eval is what surfaced the broad-transfer reframe (in the prior aggregate the surgeon row looked anomalously selective in the wrong direction; the corrected aggregate shows source and bystander lifts at parity for surgeon too, consistent with the other 11 cells).
5. **Eval input is restricted to OUT scenarios only.** Per plan §6, training-row generation used only the 14 IN configs (7 scenarios × 2 perspectives) and the eval was restricted to the 8 OUT configs (4 held-out scenarios × 2 perspectives). This is what makes the lift attributable to generalization rather than memorization; it does mean the per-config sample is N\=8 OUT configs, narrower than the external eval's full N\=22.
