# Plan: Issue #1345 — Is the assistant context→answer map the same operator across framings?

## §0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- I will fit linear context→answer operators in three framing regimes (chat-template, plain User:/Assistant:, and assistant-in-narrative-stories) and test whether the operators are the same linear map in different surface coordinates or genuinely different maps, using held-out R², cross-regime transfer, Procrustes-aligned cosine, and — for the conversation-paired chat↔no-template pair — the #825 general-linear reparameterization test with a matched-capacity null.
- I expect the chat and no-template regimes to share essentially the same operator (cross-regime R² near the target regime's within-regime R², reparameterization recovers it above the null), while the story regime may or may not — with the corpus confound (different text) making a genuine null hard to establish for that comparison.
- What would change my mind: if cross-regime transfer between chat and no-template lands well below the target regime's within-regime R² AND the paired reparameterization also fails to beat its matched-capacity null, the case for a genuinely different map per framing becomes strong.

## §1. Goal

**Canonical Goal (Task #1345, verbatim; re-read at revision time 2026-07-15, no `epm:goal-updated` markers):**
> "Determine whether the assistant context→answer map is the SAME linear operator across three framings holding the assistant persona constant — chat-template, plain User:/Assistant:, and an assistant/AI character in generic narrative-prose stories — via cross-regime operator transfer, aligned operator cosine, and general-linear reparameterization (the #825 Result-2.5 method), in Qwen2.5-7B base and instruct, distinguishing 'same map in different coordinates' from 'genuinely different map per framing.'"

This experiment asks: is the linear structure that maps "assistant processing context" to "answer activations" a single invariant operator, merely expressed in different activation coordinates across surface framings? Or do different framings engage genuinely different computational maps? We test this via three complementary methods: (1) cross-regime transfer (does a map fit in one framing predict answers in another?), (2) operator cosine after Procrustes alignment against a random-rotation chance reference (are the matrix structures geometrically similar beyond chance?), and (3) the #825 general-linear reparameterization test on the conversation-paired chat↔no-template pair (does a data-paired change-of-coordinates recover the within-regime R², and does it beat a matched-capacity null?). The three-framing design holds the persona (assistant) constant while varying surface form, isolating framing as the manipulated variable.

## §3. Hypothesis

**H1 (Same-framing, different-coordinate):** The chat-template and plain User:/Assistant: regimes engage the same linear operator up to a change of activation coordinates. Prediction: per-direction cross-regime transfer R² lands within 0.05 of the TARGET regime's within-regime R²; the paired reparameterization recovers the target within-R² and beats its matched-capacity null; Procrustes-aligned cosine > the random-rotation chance reference.

**H2 (Story regime: different map):** The assistant-in-narrative-stories regime engages a weaker or genuinely different map relative to chat/no-template. Prediction: within-regime R² for stories is lower than chat/no-template (consistent with #825's ~0.16 prior for generic stories); cross-regime transfer (chat→story) yields substantially lower R² than the story regime's own within-R² ceiling.

**H3 (Corpus confound acknowledged):** The story-vs-chat cross-regime comparison is corpus-confounded (different text, not just different framing), so a story R² deficit is attributable to framing OR corpus OR both. The story-pair reads are therefore transfer R² + Procrustes-aligned cosine vs the rotation null (descriptive), never the paired reparameterization verdict.

**Registered quantities (headline pair chat↔no-template; layer 19; context arm; evaluated SEPARATELY per model — instruct and base — with the prefix arm reported as companion, no cross-model or cross-arm pooling):**
- Per direction: Δ(i→j) = transfer_R²(i→j) − within_R²(j) — the TARGET regime j's own within-regime held-out R² is the denominator (the two regimes' ceilings differ by ≈0.05, so a source-side denominator would eat the whole margin).
- Δ_xfer = mean[ Δ(chat→no-template), Δ(no-template→chat) ]
- Δ_same = Δ_xfer + 0.05
- Δ_diff = Δ_xfer + 0.10
- Δ_reparam = min over the two directions (j→i) of [ recovered_R²(A·M_j·B applied in regime i) − max( within_R²(i) − 0.05, null_recovery_R²(i) ) ] — where null_recovery_R² is the matched-capacity reparam null of §6 (same λ/rank, fit toward M_i from the answer-shuffled-fit operator and from a random orthogonal rotation of M_j; the larger of the two null recoveries). Δ_reparam ≥ 0 ⇔ recovery is BOTH within 0.05 of the target ceiling AND above the null.
- **CI (one bound, used identically here and in §6/§7):** conversation-level paired bootstrap (N_BOOTSTRAP=1000) over the shared R1/R2 conversation set, resampling conversations and recomputing Δ_diff from the cached L19 held-out predictions (`preds_frozen`); the resampling unit is the conversation.

DISJOINT and exhaustive: same-operator ⇔ Δ_same ≥ 0 AND Δ_reparam ≥ 0; reparameterized ⇔ Δ_same < 0 AND Δ_reparam ≥ 0; different-map ⇔ Δ_diff < 0 AND Δ_reparam < 0 AND Δ_diff's 95% CI wholly below 0; inconclusive ⇔ otherwise.

(Label glosses: same-operator = transfer alone already recovers the target ceiling; reparameterized = the Goal's "same map in different coordinates" — transfer falls short but a data-paired coordinate change recovers it above the matched-capacity null; different-map = "genuinely different map per framing". Disjointness: same-operator and reparameterized differ on the sign of Δ_same; different-map requires Δ_reparam < 0, disjoint from both; the otherwise clause covers every residual cell, including Δ_reparam < 0 with a CI straddling 0.)

N/A — no paired contrast.

### Regime definitions

| Regime | Plain-English name | Corpus | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Chat-template | #825 S-track corpus (REUSE; re-extracted with prefix slot) | Standard Qwen chat template (`format_key: chat`) |
| R2 | No-template | #825 S-track corpus (REUSE; re-extracted with prefix slot) | Plain `User: <u1>\n\nAssistant: <a1>` render (#825's `naturalistic` format) |
| R3 | Stories | NEW on-policy (this task) | Narrative prose with embedded assistant character |

**Corpus caveat (DESIGN CONSTRAINT):** R1 and R2 share the same underlying conversations (same text, different formatting) — this makes them the conversation-PAIRED pair on which the reparameterization leg is defined. R3 uses a different corpus (generated narratives). Cross-regime comparisons between R1/R2 and R3 are corpus-confounded; this is stated in §12 Assumptions and carried into the clean result.

**Prefix vs context arms (canonical definitions + per-regime slot positions):** the **prefix** is everything before the user QUERY (system prompt / any content preceding the query); the **context** is the prefix plus the user query. Slot positions:
- R1 (chat): context slot = last token of the assistant role header after the user turn (the a1 slot, `render_chat`, render_formats.py:204-206 — the #825 convention); prefix slot = last token of the rendered region preceding the user message content (end of the pre-query template region).
- R2 (no-template): context slot = last token of the `Assistant: ` header (a1 analogue, render_formats.py:245-247); prefix slot = last token of the region preceding the user query text (end of the `User: ` header).
- R3 (stories): context slot = last token of the attribution marker immediately preceding the answer utterance (e.g. the `:` of "ARIA replied:") — the header-analogue of a1; prefix slot = last token of the narrative region preceding the question utterance.
**Degeneracy note:** on single-turn LMSYS-derived data the R1/R2 prefix is a near-constant template region — the prefix arm may carry little cross-conversation variance and its R² may be degenerate. This is expected, reported (per-arm variance stats alongside R²), and does NOT gate the headline (context arm is the headline arm; prefix arm is the standing-rule companion).

### Phase 6: Operator comparison — Procrustes (all pairs) + paired reparameterization (chat↔no-template only); FROZEN_LAYERS

**Script:** `scripts/issue1345_operator_comparison.py` (NEW; drives `issue825_map_alignment.py` + `issue825_reparam_directions.py` cores with parameterized stems/revisions).

**Leg A — aligned cosine, EVERY pair (unpaired-safe):**
1. Raw operator cosine (flattened M_i, M_j) at frozen layers.
2. Procrustes-aligned cosine (the map_alignment variant, named in the output metadata), reported AGAINST the random-orthogonal-rotation chance reference (map_alignment step-5 null), carried into the #1345 driver for EVERY pair. Calibration anchor: #825 base↔instruct aligned cosine 0.6864.

**Leg B — data-paired general-linear reparameterization, chat↔no-template ONLY (both models, both arms, both directions):**
- A/B change-of-coordinates fit from conv_id-ROW-ALIGNED PAIRED activations via the reused `align_pair` path (crossmodel_map_transfer.py:253-268) — NEVER the operator-matching ALS/pseudo-inverse formulation (A = M_i·M_j⁺ is exactly solvable for any full-rank pair and would make "reparameterization succeeds" true by construction).

- Story pairs get NO reparameterization verdict — their operator read is Leg A + transfer R² (H3).

## §7. Decision Gates

No gates — the run is ≤14 GPU-hours, the methodology is pre-verified in #825, and no intermediate signal can shortcut the full regime comparison. The story-regime reuse clause (Phase 1) is the only conditional branch at implementation time, and it requires no gate.

### Kill criteria

- **Story regime dead-on-arrival:** if the story regime's within-regime held-out R² at layer 19 is ≤ the conversation/story-level shuffle-null 95th percentile in BOTH models and BOTH arms, halt the story-regime transfer and operator-comparison legs (Phases 5-6 story pairs) and report the story regime as "no detectable linear map"; the chat↔no-template phases still complete and carry the headline. (Null tightness caveat — 20 draws, p95 sits between the 19th/20th order statistics — is checked before invoking this kill; see §Concerns.)
- **Story yield floor:** if fewer than 400 stories per model survive the judge-filter + extraction parser after one retry batch (<80% of the 500-story target), halt story generation and report the yield failure; do NOT backfill with templated stories. Chat↔no-template phases proceed regardless.
- **Phase-0/2a parity failure:** if the single-cell smoke (pt-shard loader path) crashes — including any device/dtype TypeError at the `heldout_r2_sweep` numpy seam or an `np.arange` conv_ids fallback — OR the re-extracted context-arm L19 R² deviates from the pinned cells_S1/S2/S1N/S2N anchors by more than ±0.02, halt the full fan-out until diagnosed.
- **Headline-pair kill (same CI bound as §3):** Δ_diff < 0 AND Δ_reparam < 0 AND Δ_diff's 95% CI wholly below 0 resolves the lattice to different-map — that is a valid (reportable) outcome, not a halt; no additional kill applies.

## Concerns for the analyzer (ride into interpretation)

- **Lattice denominators:** every transfer read uses the TARGET regime's within-R² (per-direction Δ, §3); do not re-derive a pooled denominator — the two ceilings differ by ≈0.05 (0.6731 vs 0.6249 at L19, instruct).
- **Shared-corpus near-identity alternative for H1:** a high chat↔no-template transfer could reflect near-identical activations rather than an invariant operator — report the per-conversation paired activation cosine cos(x_chat, x_nt) and the raw-vs-Procrustes cosine gap alongside the verdict.
- **Fold/subset discipline:** R1/R2 folds are conv_id-keyed, assigned BEFORE matched-n subsampling, identical subset + assignment across the two regimes (asserted); A/B alignment fit on training folds only.
- **R3 parser selection:** the parseability filter drifts the story corpus toward semi-structured dialogue — report extraction yield, inspect samples, carry a scope caveat on "free-form prose"; parser noise deflates R3 R² — check the high-confidence-subset read before interpreting story weakness.
- **Base-model tier-2 elicitation risk:** the base model may under-produce well-formed stories; coverage loss is reported (never template-backfilled).
- **Group-n asymmetry:** ~4,724 conversation groups (R1/R2) vs ~500 story groups (R3) at matched row-n — frame R3 CIs on the story count; consider a group-matched sensitivity read.
- **Story-null tightness:** the DOA kill's null p95 rests on 20 draws (between the 19th/20th order statistics) — confirm the null band is tight before invoking the kill.
- **Reparam reporting:** pin to the #825 Result-2.5 method exactly; report BOTH directions and the A/B capacity (λ, effective rank) beside every recovered R²; the 0.6864 base↔instruct aligned cosine is the calibration anchor for aligned-cosine magnitudes.
- **Story transfer normalization:** report %-of-story-ceiling beside raw Δ; per-regime answer token-length distributions accompany every cross-regime read.
- **Prefix-arm degeneracy:** near-constant single-turn prefixes may produce degenerate prefix-arm R² — interpret as an arm property, not a framing effect.
