# Narrative-story framing weakens the assistant context→answer map on identical conversations rather than eliminating it, while chat and plain-text framings share one operator up to linear coordinates (MODERATE confidence)

<!-- clean-result-v4 -->

**Methodology:** [docs/methodology/issue_1345.md](https://github.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/blob/22a9edab49e1de795576bc7e6bcbe1241043f1e2/docs/methodology/issue_1345.md) · [gist](https://gist.github.com/superkaiba/3f56e92cbfa295acbfdb42429e165864)

## Takeaways

- On identical conversations the story context map reads +0.367 held-out R² with the answers embedded verbatim and +0.262 with the model's own in-story answers, against matched-row chat at +0.609 and +0.567 in the same train-fold reduced basis — narrative framing weakens the assistant map about twofold, a framing effect rather than a corpus or teacher-forcing artifact (earlier rounds' negative story R² was a selection artifact of near-interpolating penalty at n_train ≪ d, corrected by the reduced basis).
- Answer authorship moves the map about as much as framing: with the reference answer embedded, instruct bare text reads +0.63 held-out R² at the deep read positions versus +0.38 when the model writes its own answer, a drop that persists at 0.20–0.30 when training folds exceed the 3,584 input dimensions; a judge scores the self-written bare-text answers 56 of 100 for content drift from the reference versus 17–18 for the base model's.
- In the base model the ordering reverses: its own answers after the instruct-written story prefix read +0.28 (+0.40 at the boundary-token target), above its own bare text at +0.20 and chat at +0.18 — the story render helps, not hurts, base-authored answers.
- Freely written story answers carry a character gradient on a judge-scored AI-likeness axis: a calm-AI character reads 72–74 of 100, a warm helper 62–64, a villain 55–58, an ordinary person 53, in both models, while cells embedding the same reference answer sit flat at 78–81 — the axis reads authorship, not the wrapper; it is judge-scored, not human-validated.
- Character context→answer maps at layer 19 read as strongly as the assistant map (16 cells: 0.31–0.38 inserted, 0.18–0.26 on-policy vs the 0.26–0.37 assistant-story ceiling band), and the assistant operator transfers into a character map in graded proportion to the AI-likeness axis (instruct rung-4 recovery Spearman ρ +0.80 over four characters, both provenance modes) — the assistant lives on the same manifold as a story character, just at the AI-like end (n=4 caps this as consistent-with).
- The one-way reparameterization survives every correction — the story operator moved into chat recovers 0.61 teacher-forced and 0.56 on-policy, above chat's own matched ceiling, while the reverse turns only weakly positive; chat↔plain-text transfer still misses the same-operator margin but a general-linear coordinate change recovers each context ceiling, and instruction tuning pulls the two coordinate systems together (aligned operator cosine 0.855 instruct vs 0.732 base).

## Goal

**This experiment in context:** [#825](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/825) showed the assistant context→answer linear map predicts about equally well with or without the chat template, and that generic story-character maps transfer to the chat map at only ~5% of ceiling — but equal predictive power does not mean the same operator, and the story result used arbitrary characters. This experiment holds the assistant persona constant across three framings — chat-template, plain `User:/Assistant:` text, and an assistant character in narrative stories — and tests operator identity directly via cross-regime transfer, aligned operator cosine, and the [#825](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/825) Result-2.5 general-linear reparameterization, in Qwen2.5-7B base and instruct. A conversation-paired follow-up round re-renders the same conversations as stories with the original answers embedded verbatim, removing the corpus confound from the story comparison. Sibling [#1335](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/1335) asks the different question of why the assistant map is stronger than fiction-character maps.

**Broader narrative:** if the assistant map is one operator re-expressed in framing-specific coordinates, the map is a property of the assistant identity rather than of the chat format — constraining how persona representations should be compared across surface framings, and mirroring how base→instruct post-training re-parameterizes rather than replaces the map.

## Methodology

**Design:** three framings of the same assistant persona × two models (Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) × two mapping arms (context = everything up to the end of the user query; prefix = everything before the query). Framings: chat-template (standard Qwen chat template), no-template (the same conversations rendered as plain `User: <u1>\n\nAssistant: <a1>`), and assistant-in-stories (new narrative prose in which an AI character named ARIA answers a human character's questions). Chat and no-template share the same underlying conversations (the conversation-paired pair); the free-form stories are a different corpus, so those story comparisons are corpus-confounded by design. Per cell I fit a closed-form ridge map from the context (or prefix) activation to the answer-mean activation, swept all 28 layers within-regime, ran the cross-regime transfer matrix and operator-comparison legs at frozen layers 14/18/19/26 (layer 19 headline), and evaluated the plan's verdict lattice on the chat↔no-template pair per model and arm: same-operator (transfer alone within 0.05 of the target ceiling), reparameterized (transfer short, but a data-paired general-linear change of coordinates `A·M·B` recovers the target ceiling above a matched-capacity null), different-map (recovery also fails, deficit CI wholly below zero), else inconclusive.

Follow-up round `assistant-named-story` (single manipulated variable: the story character name, ARIA → "Assistant", via the `EPM_STORY_CHARACTER_NAME` seam with a variant-scoped output prefix): stories regenerated for both models under the otherwise-identical recipe. The story pipeline's retry pass is a fresh-seed backfill sized to the main-pass shortfall — retry seed conversations overlap the main pass 0 of 242 (instruct) — not per-slot retries. The chat/no-template legs reused the parent's pinned turnstore (HF data revision `2a3cb30a`, the turnstore-reuse pin; the fit metadata's `pinned_parent_revision` `7159e5804d` is the parent S-track corpus pin, a different object) through a prefetch-reuse phase gated by a refit-equality check (tolerance 1e-3, measured deviation 0.0 on all four base cells) and the parity gate (deviation 0.0 on all four layer-19 anchors); all four chat↔no-template lattice cells reproduce the parent verdicts exactly, so those legs are a pipeline-integrity replication, not new evidence. Both story arms missed the 400-of-500 yield floor, so free-form story fits and story-pair operator legs were halted per the plan kill criterion. As an instrument diagnostic, all 365 verdict-less judge replies (both runs, both models) were then re-judged once at `max_tokens` 800 — identical rubric, judge model, and temperature, fresh cache — with per-row outcomes recorded.

Follow-up round `conversation-paired-stories` (plan v9): a seed-42 sample of 2,700 of the 4,724 shared conversations rendered as narrative prose with the ORIGINAL answer embedded verbatim (attribution `Assistant replied: "…"`), activations captured teacher-forced at the fixed answer span, plus an on-policy companion cell (free story-answer generation; 117 conversations kept) bounding teacher-forcing distortion per nested gates (companion more than 0.05 below the teacher-forced cell = qualification; negative and more than 0.20 below = demotion). The paired regime is instruct-only; base narrative generation was not attempted by scope (parent base story yields 96 of 500), so base paired-story cells are N/A — not tested. This makes the story rows corpus-shared and conversation-paired with chat/no-template, so the data-paired story↔chat reparameterization is defined — the parent's stated deviation, resolved. Two declared variables separate this round from the free-form ARIA anchor (corpus construction AND character name, renamed per user directive), so ARIA-anchor comparisons are context only; the round's primary comparisons hold the name fixed within-round. Same-conversation comparators are matched-row refits of the chat and no-template cells restricted to the story-kept rows; the plan-declared verdict lattice reads the paired-story within-R² (context arm, layer 19) with its conversation-grouped bootstrap CI — wholly below zero = framing effect, wholly above = corpus effect.

Follow-up round `story-slot-position-ablation` (plan v10): the direct test of the paired round's single-slot caveat. Four candidate context read positions — the end of the embedded question utterance (the structural analogue of the chat slot), the last narrative token before the attribution sentence, the last token fully contained before the answer span ("pre-answer", defined char-level so a token that BPE-merges into the answer's opening can never be read — answer leakage into the slot is excluded by construction), and a mean-pool over the attribution phrase — were captured beside the attribution-colon anchor and the fixed prefix slot in ONE teacher-forced forward per story over the same 2,164 kept paired stories (no regeneration, no judge calls), and each slot's context cell was refit with the inherited rig. The verdict statistic D is the per-draw maximum over the four slots of the paired per-conversation deficit vs the same-rows chat comparator (the maximum taken inside each of 1,000 conversation-grouped bootstrap draws, so the selection is inherited per draw), with per-slot CIs at the Bonferroni-4-adjusted 98.75% level; the trichotomy: slot artifact (D's CI reaches zero), representation-level collapse (D's CI and every slot's adjusted CI wholly below zero), intermediate otherwise. Slot hygiene: per-slot answer-span overlap must be 0.0 (asserted at extraction; realized 0.0 everywhere); anchor-coincidence rates are measured, and a slot duplicating the anchor on more than half its rows would be excluded as degenerate (realized: at most 3 of 2,163 rows; all four slots kept). A three-cell refit-equality gate (tolerance 0.02 on the landed story-anchor, prefix, and chat matched cells) binds the fresh capture — measured deviation 0.0000 on all three. The prefix arm is held fixed per the mapping-arms rule (the context slot is the single swept variable; a prefix-side sweep would be a second variable), and the operator-cosine legs were dropped this round (a declared deviation — the verdict rests on within-R² and D).

Follow-up round `onpolicy-assistant-story` (user-requested): the paired round's on-policy companion construction scaled to powered n and promoted to the round's primary story cell (no teacher-forced arm, so the teacher-forcing-distortion calibration is N/A this round — declared). A seed-42 sample of 2,200 of the 4,628 eligible shared conversations was rendered by the instruct model as a short narrative in which a person asks the original question aloud and a character named Assistant answers freely in its own words — the story-writing system instruction carries the original question only, never the answer, and is stripped before extraction (tier-2 instruct-and-strip). One retry wave of 1,238 fresh conversations, sized by the observed keep rate, topped up the pool; every attempted conversation received exactly one draw. Mechanical gate: exactly one parsed question→answer exchange with the `Assistant replied:` attribution; then the standard judge filter. Kept 2,019 of 3,438 attempts (floor 2,000, met); 2,018 rows enter the fits — one kept story (`s4954`) parses to a 1-character answer span and is excluded at allowlist time. Both mapping arms were fit (context and prefix), with matched-row chat and plain-text refits on the kept conversations as comparators. Refit equality (deviation at most 2.2×10⁻¹⁴ on all four base cells) and the parity gate (deviation 0.0 on all four layer-19 anchors) both pass, and all four chat↔no-template lattice cells reproduce the parent verdicts exactly (recovery margins 0.045–0.050), so those legs are again a pipeline-integrity replication, not new evidence.

Estimator note and the `story-context-info-probe` round (zero GPU, user-directed inline analysis on the landed stores, no new capture or judging): every within-regime and matched-row cell in the rounds above is a closed-form ridge fit in the ambient 3,584-dimension activation basis whose penalty is chosen per fold by generalized cross-validation over a grid running to 1e4. At the paired-story row counts that regime is under-determined — 1,730 training rows per fold teacher-forced, 1,614 on-policy, against 3,584 input dimensions — so held-out R² there is an estimator read as much as a signal read. The pathology is specific to the legs whose INPUT is the story context vector: all four of those (story context→story answer and story context→chat context, in both capture modes) take the grid's smallest penalty, 0.01, in all five folds — a near-interpolating fit, which is what produces a negative held-out R² — whereas the chat-input legs select penalties of 1e3–1e4 and read positive. The one partial exception is the teacher-forced chat→chat-answer comparator, whose per-fold selection is mixed (0.01 to 3.2) and whose +0.243 is therefore itself depressed, rising to +0.609 in the reduced basis. The reduced basis restores well-posedness by construction: it retains 865 principal components teacher-forced and 807 on-policy against those same training-row counts, and its selector then picks 1e3 on the story legs. The probe re-reads the same rows, folds, seed and kernel three further ways, changing only the estimator: a train-fold PCA reduced input basis, with principal components fit on the training rows of each fold only so there is no held-out leakage; the ambient basis with the penalty held fixed at each of 1e2, 1e3, 1e4 and 1e5 instead of selected; and the same selection grid extended to 1e8, to test whether the selector's choice is an artifact of where the grid stops. It adds three estimator-free reads that use no fitted map: raw nearest-neighbour retrieval (cosine and euclidean, story context against the pooled paired chat contexts and story answers), the top-ten canonical-correlation spectrum against a row-shuffled baseline, and a decomposition of the story context into its chat-predictable component and residual. The nonlinear leg fits a 512-unit single-hidden-layer perceptron via the project's vectorized helper against a ridge comparator on the same 256-dimension shared PCA input basis and 40 row-shuffled null draws; that comparison basis is fit on all rows rather than per training fold, so its ridge value is a within-leg reference for the perceptron only and is not comparable to the train-fold reduced-basis headline. A no-template positive control runs the identical instrument on the chat↔plain-text pair: it reads +0.598 ambient and +0.727 reduced (raw retrieval to the paired chat context correct at rank 1 on 1,903 of 2,018 rows), so the degeneracy is specific to the story cells' framing variance rather than a property of the rig. Artifacts: `eval_results/issue_1345/story_context_info_probe/` (11 JSONs) and four figures under `figures/issue_1345/story_context_info_probe/`.

Follow-up round `story-boundary-ablation` (inline, user-directed): two programs on one teacher-forced capture grid. Every store is captured in one forward pass per row with five single-token read positions — prefix; the last token before the question's end; the answer-boundary marker; the last token fully contained before the answer's first character; the token containing the answer's first character — and two targets (the answer-span mean, primary, and a boundary token realized by a content-free per-arm transition suffix appended after the answer), at layer 19. The provenance program compares, per framing, cells whose rendered answer is the original reference answer embedded verbatim (for chat renders this is the model's own original chat answer) against cells whose answer the measured model wrote itself in that framing: bare-text and base-chat answers generated under the plain prompt, base story answers generated freely after the instruct-written story prefix, and the on-policy assistant-story round's rows reused. The matched-n lattice (142 cells, named the primary read in advance) fits each cell in the ambient 3,584-dimension basis with the per-fold ridge penalty selected by inner group-CV — the guarded selector the lambda audit installed — plus a train-fold PCA reduced-basis companion; a full-pool companion re-captures the three pairable families allowlist-free (4,267–4,618 conversations) as the regime check where the pool exceeds the input dimension. In that companion only the instruct bare-text pair's training folds (3,694 rows) exceed 3,584 dimensions; the base pools' folds (3,413 and 3,577) still fall just short. The story pair is structurally excluded from the companion (its pool of 2,164 kept stories is below the input dimension), as are instruct-chat own-answer rows (never generated — the chat reference answers are already the instruct model's own chat answers); the base-side reference-answer twin stores were captured and persisted but never fit (the companion fits enumerate reference-answer cells for the instruct pair only), so the base provenance contrast remains unmeasured. The judge program scores two graded axes (0–100, anchored rubric, five draws per item, temperature 1.0, `max_tokens` 600, Batch-routed): AI-likeness of the answer span for four story characters — a warm helper, a calm artificial intelligence, an ordinary person, a theatrical villain, labels and persona descriptions reused verbatim from the story-scene persona panel — crossed with two provenance arms and two models (16 cells, 300 items each), and content drift from the reference for the four own-answer assistant cells; 30,000 draws total, 211 content drops (0.70%), zero transport losses, zero refusals. The ~30-row human audit of the AI-likeness axis was declined by the user, so the axis carries a judge-scored-only caveat. The round's four boundary-rendering story-attribution arms were fit in the same lattice and are committed under the same directories; they are not read in this fold.

**Evaluation:** the dependent variables are geometric/predictive, not judged behaviors: (1) within-regime held-out R² (pooled per layer with the test fold's own mean as reference, group 5-fold CV) per regime × model × arm × layer, against a conversation-level shuffle null; (2) cross-regime transfer R² of the source-regime map applied in the target regime, with per-direction Δ(source→target) = transfer R² minus the TARGET regime's own within-R²; (3) raw and activation-Procrustes-aligned operator cosine against a random-rotation null; (4) reparameterization recovery: held-out R² of `A·M_source·B` in the target regime, where `A`/`B` are ridge-fit activation alignments trained on training folds only, against the larger of two matched-capacity nulls; (5) for the paired-story and on-policy rounds, the plan's verdict read on the story cell (framing effect iff the within-R² CI is wholly below zero; corpus effect iff wholly above with recovery above nulls), the story↔chat reparameterization with data-paired alignments, and — paired round only — the nested teacher-forcing gates on the on-policy companion subset; (6) for the slot round, the per-slot within-R² with a multiplicity-adjusted CI and the max-over-slots paired deficit D vs the same-rows chat comparator. The 95% CI on the lattice deficit comes from the conversation-level paired bootstrap. The transfer matrix recomputes each target's within-R² on its own subset and folds with a pooled held-out-mean reference, so its story diagonals differ slightly from the within-sweep rig (paired stories: −0.303 bootstrap-rig vs −0.306 within-sweep; on-policy stories: −0.544 vs −0.547) — a rig difference (R² reference), not a data difference; prose uses the within-sweep value. The slot round's verdict quantities (per-slot R², paired deficits, D) are all read in the bootstrap rig, where the within-sweep obs values differ by at most 0.003. The on-policy round's story-operator-into-chat composite lands 0.038 above its matched chat ceiling — the two-stage-ridge composite behavior the slot round disclosed, read as composite advantage, not super-recovery. Free-form story-pair operator similarity uses raw cosine + rotation band + an operator-spectrum Procrustes optimum (the data-paired alignment is undefined without shared conversations — a stated deviation, resolved for the paired round). (7) For the story-boundary round: per-cell within-R² tier curves across the five read positions (ambient inner-group-CV read quoted, train-fold reduced-basis companion reported alongside; conversation-grouped bootstrap intervals and 40-draw shuffle nulls per cell), and the two graded judge axes under the project judging recipe — malformed or out-of-range judge returns dropped, never coerced, with per-cell drop counts persisted.

**Data extraction:** chat and no-template reuse the parent's S-track corpus — its single-turn track ("S-track"; the parent also carries a two-turn "M-track", unused here): 5,000 single-turn conversations whose user prompts are real user turns from `lmsys-chat-1m` (tier-2 established dataset, parent pin `200748d9d3`), each answered by the measured model itself (one cell per model — on-policy sampled responses), row-filtered to at least 8 content tokens and at most 2,048 total tokens; 4,724 conversations survive zero-width-span drops in both renders and form the shared chat ∩ no-template row set. It was re-extracted at pinned parent data revision `7159e5804d` with the added prefix slot; the parity gate reproduced all four parent layer-19 anchors exactly. Slot positions — chat: context slot = last token of the assistant header after the user turn, prefix slot = last token of the pre-query template region; no-template: context slot = last token of the `Assistant: ` header, prefix slot = end of the `User: ` header; stories: context slot = last token of the attribution marker immediately before the AI character's quoted answer (the colon in `ARIA replied:`), prefix slot = last token of the narrative before the question utterance. Free-form stories were generated on-policy from each model (elicitation tier 2, instruct-and-strip: a story-writing system instruction that is NOT part of the extracted context), judge-filtered for containing 4+ well-formed question→answer exchanges, and parsed with per-turn confidence gates; only confident turns enter the fits (2,108 turns from 420 instruct stories, mean 5.0 per story). Instruct yield: 420 of 500 kept (main pass 323, retry 97; judge malformed drops 24, transport losses 0, parser-below-floor 43). Five of the 420 kept stories hit the 1,024-token generation cap (`finish_reason: length`); the three samples shown below ended at a natural stop. Base yield: 96 of 500 kept — below the 400 floor, so the base story regime was halted (N/A — not tested). All 420 kept instruct stories came from tier 2. Story answer spans are much shorter than chat/no-template answers ([length distributions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/319fd10131dd2517a4099755153efe500dec9e5b/figures/issue_1345/answer_token_length_distributions.png) — deliberately linked, a data-quality companion rather than a result figure).
