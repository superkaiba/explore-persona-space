# Plan — Issue #778: Replicate Persona Vectors' two prediction experiments on Qwen2.5-7B with a null battery

## §0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** The Persona Vectors paper claims a single "direction" in the model, read off contrastive examples, can predict how much a model will act evil / sycophantic / prone-to-making-things-up — both when you change its instructions and after you finetune it. The paper never checks this against a *random* direction, so I'll rebuild both of their prediction experiments on our Qwen model and stack the paper's direction against four kinds of "junk" directions to see if the real one actually carries trait-specific signal or if any direction would have looked just as good.
- **What I expect:** The paper's own direction predicts trait expression clearly above all four junk baselines for the finetuning experiment, and above them for the instruction experiment too (though the paper's own numbers there are more modest). The junk directions land near zero.
- **What would change my mind:** If a random-but-realistic direction predicts trait expression nearly as well as the paper's direction — that would mean the pipeline's diff-of-means step isn't doing the work and the "persona vector" story is weaker than advertised. Or if even the paper's own direction fails to beat its own cross-trait control, which would mean I broke the recipe (a bug, not a result).

## §1 Goal

Replicate Persona Vectors' (Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey; Anthropic 2025; arXiv 2507.21509) two prediction experiments — (1) system-prompt / monitoring projection and (2) finetuning-shift projection — on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct for the three canonical negative traits evil, sycophancy, hallucination, and test whether the persona-vector direction predicts trait expression **beyond** four null directions (shuffled-label permutation, norm-matched-random, cross-trait, PCA top-k). The paper's only specificity control is cross-trait directions (r = 0.34–0.86), which is weak because the traits are correlated (the authors' own caveat); it uses no random-direction baseline in any experiment. This task adds a rigorous null battery so the claim "the persona-vector direction carries trait-specific signal" is properly tested against what an arbitrary direction — or the extraction pipeline run on shuffled labels — would produce. Model = Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (the paper already used it → zero model-deviation; this is a faithful replication that changes only the added null battery).

## §3 Hypothesis + Falsification

Per trait × per prediction setting × per null, testing whether the matched-trait direction beats each null:

- **H0 (null can produce it):** the observed matched-trait max-over-layers |r| falls inside the null's [p2.5, p97.5] band **of max-over-layers |r| per draw** — i.e. that null direction (or the shuffled-label pipeline) produces a best-layer r as large as the real direction.
- **H1 (direction carries signal):** observed matched-trait max-over-layers r > null's p97.5 of max-over-layers |r| (one-sided empirical p < 0.025).

Quantitative priors (from the paper, to sanity-check the recipe reproduced, not as pass thresholds):
- Finetuning-shift matched-trait r ≈ 0.76–0.97; cross-trait r ≈ 0.34–0.86. **Recipe-sanity gate:** the paper's OWN matched-trait direction must reach at least the paper's cross-trait floor (r ≥ ~0.5) on at least 2 of 3 traits, else the recipe is broken (kill criterion §7), independent of the null result.
- Monitoring matched-trait r ≈ 0.747 / 0.798 / 0.830 (overall). Because the paper's monitoring r is more modest and within-condition much weaker, the monitoring headline is "beats the null bands," not "hits a fixed r."

**Overall claim the clean-result answers:** does the persona-vector direction predict trait expression beyond the full null battery, for both prediction settings, on Qwen2.5-7B? A positive answer strengthens the paper's specificity claim past its own weak cross-trait control; a norm-matched-random direction landing near the real one would weaken it.

## §4 Design

Four phases. The single genuinely-new module is `null_battery.py`; everything else reuses the paper's released code + data adapted to the project env.

**Data source + realism tier:** the paper's released `dataset.zip` + `data_generation/*.json` are **tier-2 (established published corpus — the paper's own released data)** for the purpose of a faithful replication, though the *underlying construction* is tier-3 LLM-generated synthetic (Claude-3.7). Replication fidelity governs (`replication-fidelity.md`): reproduce the paper's data as-released; do NOT substitute the project house rig. A tier-2 real-benchmark bolstering of the finetuning-shift realism (e.g. real MedQA / real GSM8K questions instead of the paper's mistake-injected versions) is explicitly OUT of scope for this faithful-replication task and named as a clean future extension. `Decision: use the paper's released dataset.zip verbatim` — swapping in real benchmarks would change a second variable and confound the null-battery comparison against the paper's numbers.

**Contrastive negatives:** N/A as a *training* design — this is not a behavior-implantation experiment (the 24 finetunes replicate the paper's SFT recipe verbatim; the paper is a positive-corpus SFT paper, the named replication-fidelity exemption). The contrastive structure lives in the *extraction* (the 5 pos/neg system-prompt pairs are the contrastive backbone of the diff-of-means `r_B`, governed by `contrastive-negatives.md` — a positive-only extraction has nothing to diff against). This is satisfied by the paper's released pos/neg pairs.

**Completion provenance:** the extraction rollouts are on-policy by construction (sampled from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct under the contrastive system prompts, T=1.0, `on-policy-completions.md`). The 24 finetuning corpora are published-corpus-verbatim (Claude-3.7-written, the named replication exemption — do NOT re-elicit them on-policy; that would break replication fidelity). The monitoring + trait-expression eval responses are on-policy (Qwen's own sampled responses).

**Why code, not a model call?** `null_battery.py` is a numeric/statistical module (covariance estimation, random-direction sampling, PCA, Pearson r, permutation p-values over ≥200 draws, per-draw × per-layer |r| matrices) over cached activation tensors — no unstructured-data heuristic, so a code path is required (a model call cannot compute a permutation null band). The only model calls are the graded judge (Batch API) and the on-policy generations; both are model calls by design.

### Phase 1 — Extraction + layer sweep (~3–6 GPU-h across 3 traits, 8× H100)

For each trait ∈ {evil, sycophancy, hallucination}, run the faithful 7-step pipeline (adapt `generate_vec.py`):
1. Inputs: trait name + description (from `data_generation/trait_data_extract/{trait}.json`, verbatim).
2. Artifacts: the 5 pos/neg pairs + 20 extraction questions + eval rubric are already released (no regeneration).

5. Activation position: **response-avg** residual stream at EVERY layer (0..27 for Qwen2.5-7B = 28 layers), collected for each KEPT rollout.
6. `r_B[layer]` = mean(kept-pos response-avg act) − mean(kept-neg response-avg act), per layer. Cache all `r_B[layer]` tensors + the raw kept-rollout activation pools to disk (`data/issue_778/rb/{trait}.pt` + `activations/{trait}_{pos,neg}.pt`).
7. Layer regime = **read-out**: do NOT pin the paper's steering layer. Pseudocode (`generate_vec.py` adaptation):
```
for trait in TRAITS:
    pos_prompts, neg_prompts, extract_qs, rubric = load_released(trait)
    rollouts = vllm_generate(qwen, {pos,neg} x extract_qs x 10, T=1.0)   # on-policy
    scores = batch_judge(rollouts, rubric)                               # Sonnet 4.5, 0-100
    kept_pos = [r for r in pos_rollouts if 50 < score(r) <= 100]         # drop REFUSAL/OOR
    kept_neg = [r for r in neg_rollouts if 0 <= score(r) < 50]
    acts = capture_response_avg_all_layers(qwen, kept_pos + kept_neg)    # [n, 28, 3584]
    rb = acts[kept_pos].mean(0) - acts[kept_neg].mean(0)                 # [28, 3584]
    save(rb, activation_pools, dropped_counts_per_arm)
```

## §5 Conditions and Controls (the null battery)

Every direction is compared on the SAME cached activations + SAME trait-expression DV; only the projection direction changes, so the battery costs ~no extra GPU (correlations recomputed on CPU).

| Plain-English name | What it tests | What it controls for | Config slug |
|---|---|---|---|
| Matched-trait direction | The paper's `r_B` for the trait predicts that trait's expression | (positive reference — the claim under test) | `matched` |
| Shuffled-label permutation | Re-run the FULL extraction with pos/neg labels shuffled → does the diff-of-means machinery alone (no real pos/neg signal) produce the r? | Whether the pipeline's *structure* (not the trait signal) manufactures correlation — the gold-standard null | `perm` |
| Norm-matched random direction | A random direction with matched norm + realistic activation-covariance overlap predicts as well? | Whether *any* plausible direction in the activation manifold predicts trait expression (the missing baseline the paper never ran) | `randnorm` |
| Cross-trait direction | The OTHER traits' `r_B` predict this trait (all 6 pairs) | The paper's own specificity control — kept for direct comparison; treated as WEAK evidence (paper's caveat: traits are correlated) | `crosstrait` |
| PCA top-k of pos−neg diffs | The top-k high-variance directions of the (pos−neg) activation differences predict as well? | Whether diff-of-means beats any generic high-variance direction in the same difference subspace | `pca_topk` |

**Control rationale:**
- **`perm` (gold standard):** re-runs the entire pipeline (Phase-1 activation pools reused; only the pos/neg assignment shuffled before the diff-of-means) ≥200 times. If the observed matched-trait r survives above the permutation band, the pos-vs-neg labels — i.e. the trait signal — carry the correlation, not the diff-of-means machinery. This is the strongest null.
- **`randnorm` (NOT isotropic):** sample from N(0, Σ_activations) where Σ is the empirical covariance of the response-avg activations (a low-rank + diagonal shrinkage estimate, `Σ = (1−λ)Σ_emp + λ·diag(Σ_emp)`, λ pilot ≈ 0.1 for PD-stability in 3584-dim), then renormalize each draw to ‖r_B‖. An isotropic Gaussian in 3584-dim is a strawman (persona vectors live on a low-D manifold); matching the covariance makes the random direction "look like" a plausible activation-space direction. **Optional λ sensitivity sweep (~zero extra cost, CPU-only): recompute the `randnorm` band at λ ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}** to confirm the `randnorm`-only excess is not an artifact of one shrinkage value (perm + crosstrait carry the specificity headline regardless — §12).
- **`crosstrait`:** the paper's own control; recomputed identically for direct comparison to the paper's 0.34–0.86.
- **`pca_topk`:** top-k (k pilot = 5) principal components of the stacked (pos−neg) per-pair activation differences; asks whether the *mean* difference beats any *high-variance* difference direction.

**Selection-symmetric null comparison (Must-Fix #1) — the primary Success comparison.** The read-out layer is selected by `max(matched-trait |r|)` over the 28 layers, which is 28 chances for the matched direction. To avoid inflating the specificity comparison, **every null draw is subjected to the identical max-over-28-layers selection:**
- Each `perm` and `randnorm` draw computes its OWN `max_over_28_layers(|r|)` before contributing to the null band.
- `crosstrait` (2 fixed directions/trait) and `pca_topk` (k=5 fixed directions) each compute per-direction `max_over_28_layers(|r|)`.
- The primary Success comparison is: **observed matched-trait `max_over_layers(|r|)` > p97.5 of the null distribution of `max_over_layers(|r|)` per draw** (one-sided empirical p < 0.025). This is what §6 Success gates on.

`Decision: apply per-null-draw max-over-28-layers selection (the CPU-cheap fix) AND persist the per-draw × per-layer |r| matrices.` Reason (autonomous-session default): the per-null-draw max-over-layers correction removes the 28-chances-vs-1 asymmetry directly at ~zero extra cost (the correlations are already recomputed at every layer per draw), and persisting the draw×layer matrix lets the analyzer recompute the honest band either way post-hoc (belt-and-suspenders). The alternative — held-out-split layer selection (RECOMMENDED where feasible; select the read-out layer on 10 of the 20 monitoring eval questions, or half the 24 finetune runs held out at random, then freeze that layer for observed + all nulls so no per-draw sweep is needed) — dodges the asymmetry entirely but shrinks the already-tight monitoring/finetune sample used for the reported r; it is noted here as the cleaner design when sample permits and can be turned on in `null_battery.py` via a `--holdout-layer-select` flag, but the shipped default is the per-null-draw max-over-layers correction (works at any n, no sample loss).

**Persisted null-draw artifacts (plan-referenced downstream inputs).** Persist the per-draw × per-layer |r| matrices, one file per (trait × setting × null_kind):

**Not a content-behavior leakage/implantation experiment** (§6 dual-DV rule) — this is a predictor-correlation replication, so the dual on-policy-rate + log-P recipe is N/A; the graded-score-primary rule above governs instead.

**Success (numeric):** finetuning-shift matched-trait max-over-layers r > p97.5 of all 4 null bands **of max-over-layers |r| per draw** (BH-adjusted empirical p < 0.025) on all 3 traits = strong replication + specificity. A `randnorm` band that overlaps the matched r on any trait is the headline-relevant negative finding (weakens the specificity claim) and is reported as such, not buried. **A matched-vs-null gap that holds on `overall_r` but collapses on `within_condition_r` is reported as prompt-type-separation, not trait signal** (the paper's own caveat).

**Replication-fidelity deviations (named, carried as clean-result scope caveats):** (1) judge = Sonnet 4.5 graded 0–100 instead of the paper's GPT-4.1-mini logit-weighted scoring (the standing `persona-vectors-recipe.md` carve-out — does not need per-plan re-justification, but IS a scope caveat); (2) N=6 judge draws mean-aggregated (the paper takes 1 logit-weighted score per response — a variance-reduction addition, not a recipe change to the model side); (3) the added null battery (the deliberate new variable). Everything else — data, LoRA recipe, extraction pipeline, activation positions, layer regime (read-out per the recipe), 8 system prompts, 24 dataset cells — matches the paper.

**Scope caveats (non-blocking, folded from the Phase-2 critic ensemble):**
- **(A1b) Prompt-signature leakage into judged responses:** a monitoring response can echo the system-prompt's trait instruction (style/meta-reference mimicry), which the judge could reward as "trait expression" even when the model did not autonomously express the trait. Mitigation: the 20-response Sonnet-specific audit (§6) explicitly checks for style/meta-reference mimicry of the system-prompt signature, not just score plausibility. Carried as a clean-result scope caveat.
- **(A2a) `perm` does not fully bracket "any diff-of-means over trait-eliciting-response activations correlates":** the shuffled-label permutation destroys the pos/neg signal but still diffs over the same trait-eliciting response pool, so a residual "trait-eliciting-response-activation axis" could leak into the perm null. The **load-bearing tautology test is the matched-vs-crosstrait margin** (does the trait's OWN direction beat the OTHER traits' directions?), NOT matched-vs-perm alone — a matched direction that beats perm but not crosstrait is not trait-specific. Reported explicitly.
- **(A2c) λ shrinkage sensitivity:** the `randnorm`-only excess is not load-bearing if λ drifts large — the `perm` + `crosstrait` nulls carry the specificity headline. The λ ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2} sensitivity sweep (§5, §11, CPU-only ~zero cost) confirms the randnorm band is stable; a randnorm result that flips across λ is reported as unreliable and the headline rests on perm + crosstrait.
- **(H) Family-clustered leverage on the n=24 finetune r:** the 24 finetune points are 8 families × 3 versions, so one family's 3 versions could drive the correlation. The analyzer reports leave-one-family-out r (8 recomputes, each dropping one family) + a labeled scatter (§6 figures) so the reader sees whether the r is robust to any single family.

**Ungrounded values flagged:** judge N=6 @ T=0.7, PCA k=5, covariance shrinkage λ=0.1 are `justified pilot` (no literature/prior-issue pin) — smoke-testable in Phase 2 (adjust N up if judge draw-variance is high; adjust k if the PCA null is trivially weak/strong; the λ sweep spans the shrinkage sensitivity). None is outcome-determining for the headline (the perm + randnorm nulls dominate the specificity claim, and the max-over-layers selection is applied symmetrically to all).
