- **What I'll run.** The v1 plan ran the misalignment-detection gate and found the EM models we already trained never actually learned to be misaligned. - **What would change my mind.** Three things would surprise me. (a) The retrained EM arm again fails to learn misalignment, even at the validated recipe — that says misalignment really is harder to install than the literature suggests on this base model. (b) Both arms look rank-one — there's just one "persona edit" direction, not a lazy-vs-rich split. (c) The marker arm's geometry is dominated by the trained persona's own activation shift (not a generalizable direction) — the rank-one result would be an artifact of that one row.

### Pick + rationale

**Pick: B'** (straight #458 `turner_em` recipe, plain Turner SFT, no persona/contrastive rig).

Reasoning, info-gain-per-GPU-h-per-Goal-coverage:

1. **A drops the Goal in half.** The parent Goal explicitly asks for the cross-arm contrast ("does rank-one govern marker AND break for EM"). Picking A means answering only the marker half. Cheap (~3.25h) but produces a result that critically under-delivers on the Goal.

2. **B is the riskiest of the EM-retrain options.** The #519 EM arm's failure to install EM at lr=2e-5 r=8 α=16 200 steps contrastive-persona rig with aligned-negative regen is the v2 trigger. Option B bumps the LoRA capacity (r 8→32, α 16→256, +adamw_8bit, +175 steps) but KEEPS the contrastive-persona-rig structure that may itself be what suppressed EM. Bumping capacity may or may not be enough to overcome the suppression. We have no existence proof for the compound recipe. Expected outcome: 30-50% chance of another floor result → another 9.25 GPU-h burned + same pivot decision recurs.

3. **C is forced to n=2 by the #458 sweep configuration** (only seeds 0 and 137 trained at #458). The hypothesis table requires bootstrap CIs across 3 seeds per arm; n=2 mechanically destroys the cross-arm CI overlap claim. Also forces an untested `phase_c_extract_shifts` loader patch (load merged model directly via `AutoModelForCausalLM` instead of `PeftModel.from_pretrained`). Same rig-confound as B' but with n=2 + loader-untested overhead.

4. **B' has the strongest existence proof: 15.2% EM at exactly this recipe, exactly this base model, exactly this Turner corpus** (#458's own clean result). It is the option most likely to produce a usable EM arm. The rig-confound (marker contrastive vs EM plain) is real but NAMABLE — the clean-result carries it as an explicit scope caveat: "the cross-arm contrast jointly tests behavior × training-rig; future work needed to disentangle." The single-variable purity v1 had (both arms share the contrastive-persona rig + same recipe knobs, only the implant content differs) is sacrificed for an EM arm that actually installs.

**Goal coverage under B':** Full headline ("rank-one for marker, breaks for EM"), but with a rig-confound caveat that the clean-result must state plainly: "marker uses contrastive-persona SFT under medical_doctor with marker-only loss; EM uses plain SFT on Turner bad-medical-advice with full-response CE. The σ₁/Σσ contrast pools the implant-type difference and the rig difference." This caveat is also the leading follow-up: re-run EM under the contrastive rig at WHICHEVER recipe is later proven to install EM under that rig.

**Why not a smaller pivot (e.g. re-train EM at higher lr in the #519 rig)?** The #519 condition YAML's saturation_gate already specifies `lr_ladder: [2.0e-5, 5.0e-5]` — meaning the next escalation in the #519 contrastive rig is lr=5e-5 (only 4× below #458 in lr but still r=8 α=16 and the contrastive rig). The same recipe-uncertainty risk applies. Picking B' instead skips the laddering and goes straight to the validated 15.2% recipe.
