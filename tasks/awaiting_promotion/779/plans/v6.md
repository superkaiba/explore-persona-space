<section class="plan-tldr">
<h2>0.0 TL;DR (plain English)</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>What I'll run:</strong> Last time I trained a little translator that reads a chatbot's "state" right before it answers and tries to guess how the answer will look inside the model, then reads a trait (evil / sucking-up / making-stuff-up) off that guess. It lost to the paper's simpler read. My best guess for WHY it lost: I trained the translator on 5000 boring everyday chat prompts, which almost never make the model act evil or sycophantic — so the translator never saw enough examples of the trait to learn to spot it. This round I test that guess head-on. I build a much more varied batch of prompts that DO push the model toward these traits (and away from them), retrain the translator on that, and compare three diets: boring-prompts-only, trait-prompts-only, and a mix. I also chart how the score climbs as I feed each diet more and more examples, so I can tell "trait data genuinely doesn't help" apart from "I just didn't have enough of it."</li>
<li><strong>What I expect:</strong> Feeding the translator prompts that actually express the trait should help it read the trait — the trait-only and mixed diets should beat the boring-only diet, and the more-data curves should still be climbing for the trait diet (meaning last round's loss was partly a not-enough-data problem, not a dead end). Whether the mix also beats the paper's raw read, and whether it beats a fair predictor trained to guess the trait directly, is the open question this settles.</li>
<li><strong>What would change my mind:</strong> If the trait-diet translator does no better than the boring-diet one even with lots of examples and its more-data curve has flattened out, then the trouble was never the training data — the before-answer state just doesn't carry the trait, and the paper's read really is the ceiling. If a tiny bit of trait data already maxes it out, then it was purely a data-scarcity story and the interesting knob is just "how much."</li>
</ul>
</section>

<h2>0. Plan Summary (technical)</h2>
<p><strong>Training:</strong> NO model fine-tuning (inherited). Reuse v5's <code>r_B</code> (3 traits, all 28 layers, response-avg, judge-filtered) UNCHANGED across arms. Ablate the training source for the behavior-agnostic map <code>h: c_last&rarr;v(x)</code> AND the direct predictor <code>g: c_last&rarr;trait</code> across three arms: <strong>A</strong> LMSYS-only (v5 cached), <strong>B</strong> a NEW diverse trait-eliciting corpus (~60 personas &times; ~40 varied questions, on-policy temp-1.0, judge-scored, KEEP the full trait-high&harr;trait-low range to span <code>r_B</code>), <strong>C</strong> combined (natural-mix + 1:1). Reframe the arms as a 2D training-size grid (N_LMSYS &times; N_behavior), K=10 subsamples/cell for error bars. <br>
<strong>Hyperparameters:</strong> Arm B corpus ~60 personas &times; ~40 questions &times; 10 rollouts &rarr; target &ge;2000 kept contexts/trait <em>(Source: data-realism.md tier-3 diversity + &sect;11)</em>; N_LMSYS &isin; {0,100,250,500,1000,2000,5000}, N_behavior &isin; {0,50,100,250,500,1000,2000} <em>(Source: #511 N-scaling)</em>; K=10 subsamples/cell <em>(Source: &sect;11 bootstrap-per-cell)</em>; corpus gen temp 1.0, judge N=5 graded 0-100 drop-never-coerce <em>(Source: v5 / llm-judging.md)</em>; on-policy elicitation ladder for Arm B positives <em>(Source: on-policy-completions.md)</em>. All fitter constants (ridge GCV / MLP 1&times;512 GELU AdamW lr 1e-3 / kernel-ridge) inherited from v5 <em>(Source: #722/#658)</em>. <br>
<strong>Baselines / controls:</strong> PV raw <code>&lang;c_last, r_B&rang;</code>; oracle <code>&lang;v(x), r_B&rang;</code>; direct predictor <code>g</code> (matched-capacity, held-out-question fold); all three arms compared vs each other, vs pv_raw, vs g, per trait &times; mode. <br>
<strong>Loss surface:</strong> N/A — no fine-tuning. <code>h</code>'s regression loss is over the reconstructed mean-response activation; <code>g</code>'s over the trait scalar. <br>
<strong>Compute:</strong> Arm A grid + free-tier reads = 0 GPU-h (subsample cached tensors). Arm B/C corpus gen + capture + re-judge = one 1&times; or 8&times; H100 burst (~4-5 GPU-h). <strong>Evaluation:</strong> primary = within-condition Pearson r (matched to PV) at the FROZEN per-trait&times;mode read-out layer (selection-symmetric); success = Arm B/C beat Arm A by &ge;+0.05 within-condition r (CI excludes 0) on &ge;2/3 traits, AND the B/C learning curves still rising at N_behavior max (resolves the "N-limited vs dead-end" ambiguity). Secondary = projection-recon <code>&lang;h(c),r_B&rang;</code> vs oracle + held-out recon R2/cosine. <br>

<h2>1. Goal</h2>
<p>Verbatim from <code>frontmatter.goal</code>:</p>
<blockquote>On Qwen-2.5-7B, characterize how well and at what token granularity the base model's pre-generation context representation predicts its own answer representation and behavior (at the single-context/single-answer per-example level), and test whether routing trait-expression monitoring through a learned behavior-agnostic context-&gt;answer-profile map (pre-generation) or pooling over the actual generation's activations (post-generation) beats Persona Vectors' last-prompt-token projection baseline AND a matched-capacity direct context-&gt;behavior predictor, on held-out contexts and behaviors.</blockquote>
<p><strong>This amendment's sub-question (rewrites the parent Takeaway):</strong> #779's headline finding was that the learned map <code>h</code> fails to beat the raw projection despite reconstructing the training answer profile near-perfectly in-sample (R&sup2; 0.83-0.86). The leading mechanistic explanation is that <code>h</code>'s LMSYS training corpus rarely expresses the traits, so its answer profiles carry little variance along <code>r_B</code>; a variance-driven ridge/MLP then under-fits the trait direction. This amendment tests that explanation by ablating <code>h</code>'s (and <code>g</code>'s) TRAINING-DATA SOURCE across three arms while holding <code>r_B</code> and the eval rig FIXED, and by charting learning curves along both a generic-data axis and a trait-data axis so a null can be diagnosed as N-limited vs genuinely-absent. It does not change the Goal — it isolates the cause of the parent's negative R1.</p>

Two additions bear on the amendment:</p>

<h2>3. Hypothesis + falsification (amendment-specific)</h2>
<h3>HB (trait data helps the map)</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Confirm:</strong> Arm B and/or Arm C beat Arm A by &ge;+0.05 mean within-condition Pearson r (bootstrap 95% CI excludes 0) on &ge;2/3 traits, for the map read <code>&lang;h(c),r_B&rang;</code> at the frozen read-out layer. This would say the parent's R1 null was (partly) a training-data-variance problem, not an intrinsic ceiling.</li>
<li><strong>Falsify:</strong> Arm B/C &asymp; Arm A (&Delta; CI includes 0) on &ge;2/3 traits AND (see HB-N) the B learning curve has plateaued &mdash; the pre-generation state does not carry recoverable answer-trait content regardless of training source; the raw projection ceiling is real.</li>
</ul>
<h3>HB-N (is a null N-limited or saturated? &mdash; the scaling grid's decisive read)</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Read:</strong> the r-vs-N_behavior learning curve per trait &times; method. If Arm B underperforms Arm A but its curve is STILL RISING at N_behavior=2000, the arm is N-limited (a bigger corpus might close the gap) &mdash; a "more data needed", not "trait data doesn't help", verdict. If the curve has PLATEAUED below Arm A, the null is genuine. This directly resolves the ambiguity the user flagged.</li>
</ul>
<h3>HC (substitute vs complement)</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Read (the 2D interaction):</strong> at matched marginal N, does adding N_behavior samples buy more than adding N_LMSYS samples (complement), or does the combined cell match the better single-axis arm (substitute)? Reported off the 2D heatmap + the diagonal (matched-N).</li>
<li><strong>Confirm-complement:</strong> the interior C cells exceed both the A-slice and the B-slice at matched total N. <strong>Confirm-substitute:</strong> C &asymp; max(A,B) at matched N.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Held-out-question g (leakage-free direct predictor)</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Read:</strong> does the matched-capacity <code>g: c_last&rarr;trait</code>, fit on a held-out FOLD of the 20 eval questions (K-fold, never sees the test-fold labels), still beat the map reads and pv_raw? The parent reported g winning under a LOCO fit that is leaky per body-error #2; this re-reads it leakage-free.</li>
</ul>
<p>H1b (linear vs nonlinear) and the <code>r&#771;_B = M&#7488;r_B</code> nullity check are INHERITED from v5 &mdash; each arm is subject to the same nullity falsification (linear-R1-per-arm vs the arm's trait-fit probe).</p>

<h3>4.0 Why code, not a model call?</h3>
<p>Inherited from v5: every read-out (<code>&lang;h(c),r_B&rang;</code>, <code>g(c)</code>), the grid fits, the CKA/recon, and the subsampling are numeric reductions over cached tensors &mdash; deterministic, vectorized, no unstructured-data heuristic. <h3>4.4 The 2D scaling grid (subsumes matched-N + learning curves; 0-GPU)</h3>

<li><strong>Arm A</strong> = the <code>N_behavior=0</code> column; <strong>Arm B</strong> = the <code>N_LMSYS=0</code> row; <strong>Arm C</strong> = the interior cells. The 1:1-upsample arm is a separate weighting of the same interior cells.</li>

<h3>4.5 Frozen read-out layer (selection-symmetric-nulls)</h3>
<p>The parent selected the read-out layer by <code>argmax oracle_r</code> over 28 layers (evil sys=14/many=26, syc sys=26/many=26, halluc sys=17/many=27). The amendment applies <strong>held-out axis freeze (alternative 2 of selection-symmetric-nulls):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Select each trait &times; mode's read-out layer on a HELD-OUT split of the eval conditions/questions (or re-use the parent's committed per-trait&times;mode layer as a PRE-REGISTERED fixed position &mdash; the carve-out for a fixed committed layer), then read BOTH the observed arm-vs-arm statistic AND every null/shuffle draw at that single frozen layer.</li>
<li><strong>Persist the per-draw &times; per-layer statistic matrix</strong> (one matrix per headline statistic, all 28 layers &times; observed + null rows) to <code>eval_results/issue_779/training-source-ablation-hg/scaling_grid_layer_matrix.json</code> so the analyzer can recompute an honest max-selected band post-hoc without a re-run.</li>
<li>The layer-sweep free-tier read (&sect;4.6) delivers the de-biased held-out layer selection this freeze uses; the two are the same operation.</li>
</ul>
<p>The 2D grid's headline (arm-vs-arm r, arm-vs-A learning curves) is an AVERAGE over conditions/folds at the frozen layer &mdash; NOT a max/argmax over the grid &mdash; so the grid itself does not fire the rule; only the read-out LAYER axis does, and the freeze neutralizes it.</p>

<h2>7. Success + kill criteria (gate coherence)</h2>
<p>The amendment's ONLY GPU cost is the ~4-5 H100-h corpus generation; everything else is 0-GPU subsampling. The corpus gen is a single short burst well under 4 wall-h, and the grid + reads are free. <li><strong>Rig-validation (inherited from v5, de-biased):</strong> the reproduced pv_raw within-condition r at the FROZEN (not oracle-argmax) layer is reported against PV's published table &plusmn;0.10; the parent's rig gate missed 4/6 at the oracle layer, so the layer-sweep read (&sect;4.6) recomputes it at each method's own frozen layer &mdash; a diagnostic carried into the clean-result caveats, not a compute kill (all compute is already spent / free).</li>
</ul>
<p>These criteria are jointly satisfiable (they act on different measurements: HB on arm-vs-arm r, HB-N on the curve shape, rig-validation on the pv_raw reproduction) and none can force a false FAIL of another. No gate ladder.</p>
