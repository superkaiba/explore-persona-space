## 3. Hypothesis

The validation failure is arm/behavior-specific: casual-writing arms — whose graded DV has the most dynamic range (sd 16.6, 98.5% nonzero) — validate (ρ > 0), while sycophancy's failure reflects that arm/behavior, not the graded instrument. Falsification: ρ ≤ 0 across all behaviors and arms ⇒ the graded instrument (or the TF margin at per-prompt grain) is unfit at this grain; the MODERATE cap hardens and the clean-result states which side broke (the base-unit reads + the ρ(margin, P1) structure read discriminate: a margin that correlates with geometry but no graded read anywhere indicts the pairing, not one instrument alone).

DISJOINT and exhaustive: Uniformly-validates ⇔ min over the 3 families of (median across the family's 4 arms of off-floor-subset ρ(margin, graded)) > 0.15; Uniformly-fails ⇔ max over the 3 families of that same median ≤ 0.15; Behavior-specific ⇔ otherwise.

**Registered paired contrast:** per arm, the parent-subset read vs the off-floor-subset read of the SAME margin column (same pools, same model, same composition) — paired within arm.

Row-coverage: per arm, both sides of the registered pair are supplied by one margin table computed ONCE over U_f = S_parent_f ∪ S_offfloor_f (§4) and joined to that subset's existing graded scores — parent-subset rows join `judge/arm_scores_*.json`, off-floor rows join `offfloor/judge/arm_scores_*.json` (estimation draws for base) — so every registered read on each arm has both columns on its own rows; neither side is evaluated on rows the other margin table lacks.
