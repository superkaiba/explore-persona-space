# Plan — Task #644: Is the geometry→behavior-strength relationship convex (super-linear / "exponential-looking"), and does that shape recur across behaviors?

Zero-GPU meta-analysis. `kind: experiment`, but no training and no pod — runs entirely on the VM against existing eval JSONs + HF data the past runs already uploaded. HTML render: `.claude/plans/issue-644.html`.

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I'm going back to a handful of past experiments that each measured two numbers per persona — how "close" a persona sits to a behavior in the model's internal geometry, and how strongly that persona shows the behavior — and I'm asking a question nobody asked before: is the relationship a straight line, or does it curve upward (behavior stays near zero until a persona gets close, then shoots up)? I'll fit a few candidate curve shapes to each behavior's raw points, see which shape wins, and whether the same shape keeps showing up.
- **What I expect:** The sycophancy data that started this (one scatter that looks like a hockey stick by eye) will favor an upward-curving fit over a straight line. I'm genuinely unsure whether the other behaviors — marker leakage, fact leakage, refusal — will agree, because past work already found these behaviors disagree about even the basic "does geometry predict behavior at all" question.
- **What would change my mind:** Three things, any one of which would mean the curve is an artifact of how the data was measured rather than a real property of how geometry drives behavior. (1) If the upward curve in sycophancy turns out to be one or two extreme personas doing all the work — drop them and it's a straight line again. (2) If the "curve" only appears because one behavior is measured in log-units that bend a straight line when you un-log them. (3) If the curve flattens once I account for the fact that most of these behaviors are rates pinned near zero — a rate near its floor mechanically bends an ordinary line upward, so I re-fit every rate on a spread-out (logit) scale and check the curve still holds there.

## 1. Goal

Across every past experiment that produced paired *(persona-geometry scalar, behavior-strength scalar)* data, characterize the FUNCTIONAL FORM of the geometry→behavior-strength relationship — testing on RAW (non-rank) values whether it is consistently convex / super-linear ("exponential-looking") rather than linear, and whether that shape recurs across behaviors (sycophancy, marker leakage, fact leakage, refusal) after controlling for the fact-line sign flip, saturation/floor, high-leverage points, log-space artifacts, bounded-rate compression, and the X-vs-(X−Y) caveat. The standing `q:leak-predictor` line has only ever asked the coarser, rank-based question ("does geometry predict behavior monotonically at all?"). Rank correlations are blind to shape by construction; this task sharpens "monotonic but inconsistent across behaviors" into a functional-form claim, which (if convex recurs) implies a proximity threshold below which behavior is geometrically uncoupled — directly relevant to whether geometry is a usable pre-training-audit safety predictor.

## 3. Hypothesis & falsification

Carried verbatim from the body, with sharpened falsification criteria keyed to the data inventory (4 behaviors with fittable spread: sycophancy, marker leakage, fact leakage, refusal — call this Y=4; the per-behavior n's are 35 / 17 / 6-14 / TBD).

**Majority-denominator definition (CC7, pinned).** A behavior QUALIFIES for the H1 majority denominator iff it has BOTH (a) adequate two-axis spread (distinct x and distinct y, non-degenerate range) AND (b) n ≥ 10 per-unit raw points in the GEOMETRY frame. The analyzer reports the REALIZED denominator (the explicit list of which behaviors qualified, and which were excluded for spread or n) BEFORE computing the majority. The majority threshold is then "≥ ⌈Y_realized/2⌉ + tie-break toward H0", restated to the realized Y; an under-powered or excluded behavior is reported as such, never folded silently into the majority.

**Geometry-recurs numerator scope (MF1, pinned).** The H1 majority counts each behavior via its GEOMETRY-frame verdict ONLY — `geometry_scalar_kind ∈ {cosine_to_source, cosine_to_direction, cosine_centered_centroid, js}`. The fact line's `prior_logprob` scalar is the bystander's base log-prob of the taught completion — a base-rate BEHAVIORAL scalar, NOT a geometry scalar — so a convex `prior_logprob`-frame fit does NOT contribute to the geometry-recurs numerator or denominator. The `prior_logprob`-frame fit is reported in a separate predictor / prior-frame sensitivity table (§5.6) framed as the `q:leak-predictor` reference-frame line, never as the fact behavior's geometry-recurs contribution.

- **H1 (convex recurs):** behavior strength is a convex / accelerating function of geometric proximity across behaviors; small proximity buys ~floor behavior, then it accelerates. **Wins if** a convex form (exponential / power / positive-x² / convex spline) beats linear by ΔAIC ≥ 2 AND has a curvature term whose bootstrap CI excludes zero with a CONSISTENT sign, in a MAJORITY of qualifying behaviors (per the pinned denominator above), counting GEOMETRY-frame verdicts only, AND the convex verdict survives the high-leverage-LOO re-fit (single-point AND top-2), the log-space double-fit (log-prob DVs), AND the logit rate-stabilization double-fit (rate DVs).
- **H2 (monotone but linear / artifactual):** the #623 "exponential look" is an artifact (a few high-leverage personas, the rank metric obscuring a roughly linear raw relationship, log-space axis-scaling, or bounded-rate floor compression); the raw relationship is no more than linear once leverage, log-space, and rate-stabilization are controlled. **Wins if** convex-vs-linear ΔAIC < 2 in a MAJORITY of qualifying behaviors after the controls, OR the convex verdict in any behavior flips to linear when the single (or top-2) highest-Cook's-D point is dropped, OR survives only in the back-transformed (un-logged) space, OR survives only in raw-rate space and disappears under logit-stabilization.
- **H0 (no consistent shape):** geometry→behavior shape is behavior-specific; no portable functional form (consistent with the current q:leak-predictor "inconsistent across behaviors" belief). **Wins if** the winning form and/or curvature sign disagree across qualifying behaviors with no majority — e.g. convex in sycophancy, linear in marker, concave in fact — with no control (leverage, log-space, rate-stabilization, sign-frame) reconciling them.

If the data inventory at implementation time yields fewer than 4 qualifying behaviors (e.g. refusal lacks a commensurable geometry scalar), the denominator shrinks and the threshold is restated to the realized Y in the analyzer's read; an under-powered behavior is reported as under-powered, never folded silently into the majority.

## 5. Method delta (vs the existing predictor scripts)

The existing scripts (`issue404_predictor_cossim.py`, `issue458_predictor_jsdiv.py`, `issue532_predictor_stress.py`, `issue540_jsrb_predictor.py`, `issue623_loo_leverage.py`) compute Pearson/Spearman, JS/cosine, and bootstrap CIs on CORRELATIONS — none fit functional forms. The new pipeline (one new script, e.g. `scripts/issue644_functional_form.py`, reusing those scripts' bootstrap + IO scaffolding and `analysis/paper_plots.py` rcParams) adds:

### 5.1 Raw paired-scatter loading

Raw (non-rank) paired-scatter loading from each behavior's existing JSONs (per the §4 map). Ranks are never used for fitting (the central measurement-validity point). The #311 loader enforces the name-keyed join (MF3, §4 step 2).

### 5.2 Fixed functional-form set per behavior

Linear `y=a+bx`; quadratic `y=a+bx+cx²`; exponential `y=a·exp(b·x)` (`scipy.optimize.curve_fit`); power-law `y=a·xᵇ` (on shifted-positive x where needed); monotone-constrained cubic spline. The spline is **`scipy.interpolate.PchipInterpolator` (shape-preserving / monotone by construction) over a fixed 4-knot grid** (knots at the x-quantiles {0, 1/3, 2/3, 1}), so the df is pinned and deterministic at every n (CC5). At n=6 the 4-knot PCHIP is still well-posed; the spline is a catch-all for "neither linear nor any named parametric shape," and the HEADLINE curvature verdict rests on the quadratic-vs-linear LRT + x² CI (§5.3), not on "spline beats linear" — so the spline can never manufacture a convex verdict on its own. Compared by LOO predictive R² + AIC + BIC.

### 5.3 Direct curvature test per behavior

The quadratic-vs-linear nested LR test (sign + p of the x² term) AND a nonparametric bootstrap CI on the fitted x² coefficient. Convexity = positive x² with CI excluding 0 (in the correctly-signed-proximity frame).

### 5.4 Leverage-aware re-fit

Compute Cook's D / DFFITS; re-report the convexity verdict after (i) dropping the SINGLE highest-Cook's-D point and (ii) dropping the TOP-2 highest-Cook's-D points (CC6 — #623's bend is carried by two high-leverage personas, so a single-point LOO under-powers against the two-outlier structure). Report `robust_to_leverage_LOO` as TRUE only if the convex verdict survives BOTH the single-point and the top-2 re-fits. This is the #623-style leave-one-out (generalizing `scripts/issue623_loo_leverage.py`'s ρ-LOO) extended to the curvature term.

### 5.5 Artifact-control double-fits (two cases)

A convex finding that survives only inside a measurement transform that mechanically manufactures curvature is mechanical, not a property of the geometry→behavior relationship. Two transforms are in play; a behavior gets whichever case(s) apply to its DV/X.

**Case A — log-space double-fit (log-prob DVs/Xs).** For any DV that is a log-prob (the marker log-prob SECONDARY DV used at #532) OR any X that is a log-prob (the fact-line `prior_logprob` / `base_logprob` X-axis in the prior-frame sensitivity row), fit BOTH in native log space AND on the back-transformed probability, and report the convexity verdict in both. A convex finding that survives ONLY in the back-transformed space is mechanical (the log→prob exponential map manufacturing curvature) and is LABELLED `log_space_artifact: true`.

**Case B — bounded-rate variance-stabilizing double-fit (rate DVs).** EVERY behavior-strength scalar in this plan is a RATE on [0,1] (sycophancy rate, marker emit rate, fact leak rate, refusal pass-rate). A rate near a 0.0 floor has variance structure σ² ∝ p(1−p), which mechanically bends an OLS line upward and can fake convexity (MF2 — #623 is 31/35 below 0.10 with the bend carried by 2 points). For every rate DV, refit the full curvature test (§5.3) on a **variance-stabilizing logit transform**: `y_logit = logit(clip(y, ε, 1−ε))` with **ε = 0.005** for boundary points, and report the curvature verdict in BOTH raw-y AND logit-y space alongside the existing log-space pair where applicable. A convex verdict that survives ONLY in raw-rate space (convex in raw-y, NOT in logit-y) is labelled `rate_compression_artifact: true` and does NOT count as an H1 contribution (rule stated in §6). The logit transform is pinned over arcsin-sqrt because curvature is more directly readable on the logit scale (the transform is monotone and near-linear in the interior, so a residual x² term after logit-stabilization is genuine curvature, not residual variance-funnel) and logit is the more standard variance-stabilizing choice for [0,1] rate proportions in this regime; arcsin-sqrt is the rejected alternative (§11).

### 5.6 Cross-behavior synthesis (two tables)

NOT a pooled hierarchical model (per the clarifier-resolved synthesis form).

- **Geometry-convexity recurs table (the headline).** One row per (behavior × GEOMETRY frame), where `geometry_scalar_kind` is a geometry metric (cosine_to_source / cosine_to_direction / cosine_centered_centroid / js). The recurs verdict counts ONLY these rows (MF1). Marker leakage contributes TWO rows kept separate by centering family (CC1): #311 centered-centroid and #532 raw/uncentered — never pooled. `js_v1` from #532 is `js_deprecated_single_next_token` and is EXCLUDED from this table (CC2). The fact behavior contributes via its GEOMETRY-frame row only (#500 `cos_to_source` + #444 `cosine_on`/`js_on`), NOT via `prior_logprob`. X is within-behavior z-scored for qualitative shape comparison; the table is explicit that scales differ and the comparison is on the curvature VERDICT + sign, not on a shared coefficient.
- **Predictor / prior-frame sensitivity table (parallel, NOT the headline).** One row per (behavior × prior frame), `geometry_scalar_kind: prior_logprob`. Reported as the `q:leak-predictor` reference-frame line — does the correctly-signed base-prior proximity show the same shape? — and explicitly flagged NOT a geometry-recurs contribution.

Two confounds explicitly folded in per the body: (a) the **fact-line sign flip** — the fact behavior's GEOMETRY-frame shape is reported with the teacher cosine's wrong sign noted, and the correctly-signed candidate-unification read is the `prior_logprob` row in the sensitivity table, never the geometry headline; (b) the **X-vs-(X−Y) caveat** (#383) — we fit y on x directly; we NEVER fit a difference (e.g. selectivity = source−leakage) against one of its own components, which is why #383's selectivity gradient is deliberately NOT a fit target here.
