## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** The leakage theory says a fine-tune writes one behavior "key" into a source persona, and how much that behavior shows up in *other* personas depends on how similar each other persona is to the source. The theory measures that similarity with a fixed context vector that ignores which behavior was trained. A sibling result (#604) already showed that behavior-blind key is the wrong one for the marker. So I test three candidate keys: the old behavior-blind one, plus two new ones built from what the model actually wrote during training. I ask which key best predicts realized leakage on personas it never saw, for two behaviors: the marker (a clean control) and sycophancy (the hard transfer case).
- **What I expect:** For the marker, a behavior-aware key should beat the behavior-blind default on personas it was not fit on. For sycophancy I expect the whole picture to wobble. The leakage there may not even be one direction scaled up and down, so before I score any key I first check whether that assumption holds, and I report honestly when it doesn't.
- **What would change my mind:** If the behavior-blind key already predicts marker leakage on held-out personas (it shouldn't, per #604), or if a behavior-aware key cleanly predicts sycophancy leakage too (that would say the gate ignores behavior after all). Either way the surprise lands in the marker-vs-sycophancy comparison, which is the whole point.

### Theory anchor (verbatim, with naming reconciliation)

The leakage-theory paper (`~/overleaf-6a2df2d2/main.tex`, pulled `2026-06-26`, "Already up to date") carries the structural claims under two distinct labeling schemes; the #683 body's citations map as follows (I reconcile them here so downstream agents are not confused):

- **#683-body "A7 scalar-gated off-source write" (L1193)** = `\begin{assumption}[Off-source changes are scalar-gated source writes]` (`\label{a:rank-one-gated-write}`). Verbatim claim: *"We assume that fine-tuning installs a single write direction `w_{C,B}`, and that target conditions only control how strongly that write is expressed: `Δv_{C,B}(C') ≈ w_{C,B} g_{C,B}(C')`, `g_{C,B}(C)=1`."* Its **Testable** block defines exactly the precondition this experiment must measure: the empirical source write `ŵ_{C,B} := Δv_{C,B}(C)`; the realized gate `ĝ^real_{C,B}(C'_i) := ŵᵀΔv(C'_i)/ŵᵀŵ`; the **scalarity residual** `‖ρ_i^rank1‖/‖Δv(C'_i)‖` with `ρ_i^rank1 = Δv(C'_i) − ŵ·ĝ^real(C'_i)`; and the stacked **SVD spectrum** report `σ₁²/Σσ²`, `σ₂/σ₁`, `cos(u₁, ŵ)`. *"If rank one fails, fit the low-rank fallback `Δv(C'_i) ≈ Σⱼ wⱼ gⱼ(C'_i)` for small m, and report cross-validated reconstruction error."*

- **#683-body "A8 bilinear gate" (L233)** spans two paper locations: the §2-enumerated **A7 "Base-model similarity gate"** (L233; the *context-only, whitened-similarity* default key `g_C(C') = c_Cᵀ Σ_c⁻¹ c_C' / c_Cᵀ Σ_c⁻¹ c_C`) AND the formal `\begin{assumption}[The scalar gate is a normalized key–query similarity]` (`\label{a:bilinear-gate}`, L1315). The latter is the methodology backbone for THIS experiment. Verbatim: `g_{C,B}(C') = k_{C,B}ᵀ M q_C' / k_{C,B}ᵀ M q_C`, `q_C'=c_C'` (default query), metric `M ∈ {I, (Σ_c+λI)⁻¹}`, and the **source-key set** `k_{C,B} ∈ {c_C, ψ(t_{C,B}), c_C+ψ(δ_{C,B})}` — *"where ψ maps answer-side or data-side vectors into the key–query space when needed."* Its **Testable** block prescribes the exact scoring (Pearson, Spearman, sign agreement, MAE across held-out targets), the key ablation, the metric ablation, the cosine baseline `cos(c_C,c_C')`, denominator stability `|kᵀMq_C|`, and **shuffled-key / shuffled-query baselines**.

- **Base-gate validity** (`a:base-gate-validity`, L1441): *"the pre-finetuning gate predicts the realized post-minus-pre profile change … `α_{C,B}(C') ≈ g⁰_{C,B}(C')`."* This is why the predictor keys are computed from BASE-model quantities and scored against the realized gate — the whole out-of-sample question.

- **Evaluation methodology** (L1690): *"each [Monte-Carlo average] carries sampling error, which we quantify by re-estimating with independent context samples and seeds. This yields a noise floor (the test–retest reliability of the measurement, hence a ceiling on any predictor's achievable correlation) against which headline numbers are reported."* + *"Spearman ρ (scale-free, primary) and Pearson r (for comparison with prior activation-space predictors of trait change [Chen et al.])."*

### Object of study (formal)

All activations are **answer-side mean residual-stream activations at layer `l`** over the model's OWN on-policy greedy generations under a context condition. Per behavior `B`, source condition `C`, panel of held-out target conditions `{C'_i}`:

- `v_θ(C)` — mean answer-side activation under `C` for model `θ`.
- Empirical source write: `ŵ = Δv(C) = v_trained(C) − v_base(C)`.
- Target shift: `Δv(C') = v_trained(C') − v_base(C')`.
- **Realized gate (ground truth DV):** `g_real(C') = ⟨ŵ, Δv(C')⟩ / ⟨ŵ, ŵ⟩`, with `g_real(C)=1` by construction.
- Context vector `c_C` (= the #604 last-prompt-token / EOR-slot centroid bank); teacher-forced training-completion activation `t_{C,B}` (base model run forward on the actual training completions); displacement `δ_{C,B} = t_{C,B} − v_base(C)`; background second moment `Σ_c = E[cc ᵀ]` over the panel bank.
- **Predicted gate:** `g_pred(C') = (kᵀ M c_C') / (kᵀ M c_C)` for each key `k` × metric `M`.

### Competing hypotheses (falsifiable)

- **H0:** no base-model key (context-only or behavior-dependent) predicts `g_real` out-of-sample (held-out Spearman CI overlaps the shuffled-key null for all keys).
- **H1 (paper default):** the context-only key `k=c_C` suffices — already rejected for the marker in weight-space (#604); this is the prediction-space twin test.
- **H2 (the test):** a behavior-dependent key (ii `ψ(t_{C,B})` or iii `c_C+ψ(δ_{C,B})`) beats `k=c_C` out-of-sample (held-out Spearman, CI-separated, and beats shuffled-key null).
- **H3 (the headline cross-behavior outcome):** a behavior-dependent key works for the marker but NOT for sycophancy → the gate is not behavior-agnostic, contra the paper's implicit claim.

**Literature (arXiv MCP, 3–5 closest formalizations):**
1. **Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey — "Persona Vectors" (2507.21509).** The named activation-space precedent. Projection-difference along a trait direction predicts and controls post-finetuning trait shift; *"both intended and unintended personality changes after finetuning are strongly correlated with shifts along the relevant persona vectors."* This is the `r_Bᵀδ` behavior-transfer term of the paper's predictor and the reason Pearson is reported alongside Spearman (comparability). It predicts trait shift from a fixed read direction; #683 is the **gate / context-generalization** complement — which *targets* express the installed write, not whether the source does.
2. **Wang, …, Mossing — "Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment" (2506.19823).** SAE model-diffing finds a toxic-persona feature that *predicts whether a model will exhibit* emergent misalignment after fine-tuning. Activation-space prediction of fine-tuning transfer at the feature level; closest spiritual sibling for "predict transfer from a base/internal direction." Establishes the prediction-from-internals framing; #683 tests the geometric *gate* form rather than a single SAE feature.
3. **Geva, Schuster, Berant, Levy — "Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories" (2012.14913).** Cited by the paper for A8's key–query retrieval picture (`\cite{geva2021}`). Motivates the bilinear `kᵀMq` gate; supplies the prior that the relevant "key" is a learned input pattern, not necessarily the raw context centroid — directly the H1-vs-H2 tension.
4. **Ponkshe et al. — "LoRA Silver Bullet (LoRA-SB)" (2411.19557).** Low-rank updates preserve update direction throughout training and concentrate in few directions — the weight-space motivation for A7's rank-1 gated-write form, and a prior that `t_{C,B}`/`δ_{C,B}` (data-side) may carry the write direction the context centroid misses.
5. **Kang et al. — "Prompt-Activation Duality" (2605.10664).** Steering works best along prompt-mediated pathways the model already uses for behavioral control — i.e. behavior expression is gated through context/prompt representations, supporting the bilinear-gate-from-context-query premise while warning that the source *key* may be behavior-specific (the H2 hypothesis).

**Why `kᵀMc_C'` and not Mahalanobis / CCA / partial-correlation:** the paper specifies the bilinear normalized-similarity form with the whitened metric `M=(Σ_c+λI)⁻¹` (which IS the Mahalanobis geometry in the gate); CCA/partial-correlation predictors are richer but (a) not what the theory specifies and (b) would over-fit the ~40-context panel out-of-sample. The metric ablation `M ∈ {I, (Σ_c+λI)⁻¹}` already spans raw-dot-product vs whitened; the `ψ` robustness arm (identity vs learned linear ridge map) covers the one degree of representational freedom the paper leaves open.

## 3. Hypothesis (specific, falsifiable, with thresholds)

Per behavior, on **held-out** target contexts (leave-one-context-out), Spearman ρ of `g_pred` vs `g_real`:

- **Marker (control):**
  - **Predict** `ρ(k=c_C)` ≈ shuffled-key null (consistent with #604's weight-space kill) — i.e. `ρ(c_C)` CI overlaps the shuffled-key null band.
  - **Predict** at least one behavior-dependent key (`ψ(t_{C,B})` or `c_C+ψ(δ_{C,B})`) gives `ρ > ρ(c_C)` with non-overlapping bootstrap CIs AND `ρ` CI above the shuffled-key null. **Confirms H2.**
  - The A7 precondition is expected to HOLD (scalarity residual small; `σ₁²/Σσ² ≥ ~0.5`; per #637 rank-1 dR²=+0.281 for marker), so the scalar `g_real` is a faithful DV.
- **Sycophancy (transfer test):**
  - **A7 precondition first.** If `σ₁²/Σσ²` is low / scalarity residual large (the #649-anticipated outcome), the scalar `g_real` is NOT a faithful summary → fall back to the low-rank relaxation `Δv(C') ≈ Σⱼ wⱼ gⱼ(C')` and score keys against the dominant component(s). Report the precondition read as a finding either way.
  - **Predict** (H3) NO key cleanly beats the shuffled-key null on held-out sycophancy `g_real` (or `g_real` itself sits at the #649 precision floor) → the gate is not behavior-agnostic.
  - **Falsifier of H3:** a behavior-dependent key DOES predict sycophancy `g_real` out-of-sample (CI above null, CI-separated from `c_C`) → the gate IS behavior-agnostic after all, a positive transfer result.

- **H0 falsifier (whole experiment):** if no key on either behavior beats its shuffled-key null, the base-model gate is not computable from these quantities at all (the paper's `a:base-gate-validity` fails in prediction space).

**Reported against the noise floor:** the max achievable `ρ` is bounded by `g_real`'s test-retest reliability across the 3 independent seeds; a key's `ρ` is interpreted relative to that ceiling, not 1.0.

## 4. Design

Eval-/analysis-only. Per behavior, the pipeline is: (Phase A, GPU) extract activations → (Phase B, CPU off-pod) A7 precondition read → (Phase C, CPU off-pod) key × metric scoring → (Phase D, CPU off-pod) marker-vs-sycophancy contrast + figures.

**Single manipulated variable (consistency-checker anchor):** within a behavior, the ONLY thing that varies is the **source-key form** `k ∈ {c_C, ψ(t_{C,B}), c_C+ψ(δ_{C,B})}` crossed with **metric** `M ∈ {I, (Σ_c+λI)⁻¹}`. Held constant within a behavior: panel, read layer `l`, adapters, prompt bank, scoring procedure (Spearman/Pearson/sign/MAE held-out + bootstrap CI + shuffled null + cross-seed stability), and the A7 precondition read. The **two behaviors are separate strata**, not a confounded variable — each gets its own self-contained leaderboard and they are compared only at the contrast step.

**Data source + realism tier:** Tier-2 (established/in-house corpora) for the panel + prompt bank — the #604 42-context persona bank and the #612 `eval_60.jsonl` sycophancy prompt bank are reused fixed corpora that the leak-predictor line already uses for this exact construct. No new synthetic data is generated. The activation reads are over the model's OWN on-policy generations (tier-2 prompts, on-policy completions). **N/A — not a behavior-implantation experiment** (no behavior is trained here; all adapters are reused). The contrastive-negatives / on-policy-completions / marker-stopping-recipe / multi-arm-band rules are therefore N/A for the training side; they were already satisfied by the producing issues whose adapters are reused (verified in §5/§11).

**Why code, not a model call?** The pipeline is deterministic linear algebra on stored activation tensors (mean-pooling, inner products, SVD, ridge solve, Spearman). ### Phase B — A7 precondition read (CPU, off-pod, BEFORE any key fitting)

Per behavior, compute from `{ŵ, Δv(C'_i)}` exactly as A7's Testable block prescribes:
1. **Scalarity residual** `‖Δv(C'_i) − ŵ·g_real(C'_i)‖ / ‖Δv(C'_i)‖`, per target + distribution.
2. **Stacked SVD** of `ΔV = [Δv(C'_1) … Δv(C'_n)]`: report `σ₁²/Σσ²`, `σ₂/σ₁`, `cos(u₁, ŵ)`.
3. **Branch:**
   - rank-1 holds (`σ₁²/Σσ² ≥ ~0.5` AND scalarity residual small) → the scalar `g_real` is the DV; proceed to Phase C against it.
   - rank-1 fails → fit the low-rank fallback `Δv(C') ≈ Σⱼ wⱼ gⱼ(C')` for small m (1–3), report cross-validated reconstruction error, and run Phase C against the dominant component(s) `g_1(C')`.

The branch is recorded as a finding regardless of outcome — the marker-vs-sycophancy precondition contrast IS part of the headline.

### Phase C — Key × metric scoring (CPU, off-pod)

Extend `scripts/issue637_heldout_predictive_test.py` into `scripts/issue683_key_ablation_score.py`. For each behavior, each key `k ∈ {c_C, ψ(t_{C,B}), c_C+ψ(δ_{C,B})}`, each metric `M ∈ {I, (Σ_c+λI)⁻¹}`, plus the cosine baseline `cos(c_C,c_C')`:
- Compute `g_pred(C'_i) = kᵀMc_C'_i / kᵀMc_C` for each held-out `C'_i` (leave-one-context-out; the predictor uses only base-model quantities + the source-key, never the held-out target's trained activation).
- **`ψ`** = identity by default (the answer-side `t_{C,B}` and the prompt-side `c_C'` live in the same residual stream at layer `l`, so `ψ=I` is well-defined); a learned **linear ridge map** `ψ: t-space → c-space` (fit on TRAIN contexts only, then applied to the held-out source key) is the registered robustness arm (`Source: A8 "ψ … when needed"; Chen et al. projection precedent`).
- **λ** for `(Σ_c+λI)⁻¹` chosen by held-out generalized cross-validation over a log-grid `{1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1, 1, 1e1} × median-eigenvalue(Σ_c)`, fit on TRAIN contexts only (no held-out leakage).
- **Score** vs `g_real` (or the Phase-B dominant component): Spearman ρ (primary), Pearson r, sign-agreement, MAE. **CIs** via 1000-bootstrap over held-out contexts; **cross-seed stability** by repeating per adapter seed (42, 137) and reporting the across-seed range; **shuffled-key null** (the key of one cell vs another cell's targets) + **shuffled-query null** as the primary nulls; **denominator-stability** control `|kᵀMc_C|` flagged per cell.

## 6. Evaluation

**Primary metric:** held-out (leave-one-context-out) **Spearman ρ** of `g_pred` vs `g_real`, per key × metric, per behavior. Secondary: Pearson r (Chen-comparability), sign-agreement, MAE. CIs by 1000-bootstrap over held-out contexts; cross-seed range over 2 adapter seeds.

**Success (per the Goal):** a behavior-dependent key (`k_tCB` or `k_cC_plus_delta`) beats `k_cC` on held-out marker Spearman with **non-overlapping bootstrap CIs** AND its CI sits **above the shuffled-key null band**. The headline deliverable is the marker-vs-sycophancy contrast (H3): marker key works, sycophancy key does not (or its `g_real` is at the noise floor).

**Kill / honest-null criteria:** if no key beats its shuffled-key null on the marker, H0 is supported (the base-gate is not computable in prediction space) — reported as a real null, not a failure. If sycophancy `g_real`'s test-retest reliability is at the #649 precision floor (noise floor ≈ 0), the answer is "sycophancy gate not measurable with these adapters/panel," NOT a forced key ranking on noise.

### Measurement validity (the §11 for outputs)

| DV | Construct (Goal cares about) | Metric (actually computed) | On-distribution? | If proxy: validation / justification |

| `g_real(C')` | How strongly each held-out persona expresses the installed write (realized leakage gate) | `⟨ŵ, Δv(C')⟩/⟨ŵ,ŵ⟩` from on-policy answer-side mean activations at layer `l`, trained−base | **yes** — `Δv` is computed from the model's OWN on-policy greedy generations at the natural answer-side positions over a realistic persona panel | On-policy by construction; reported against its own test-retest noise floor (A7 evaluation methodology). **Dual-DV for content-behavior leakage:** N/A in the marker-line sense (the marker DV is the project's marker-specific gate, not a content behavior). **Install-strength control:** N/A — this experiment makes NO cross-condition leakage comparison (it compares *predictors of* the gate within a fixed adapter, not leakage across training conditions). **Statistical-input existence:** the noise floor requires per-seed `g_real` estimates — VERIFIED present: #604's bank has 3 seeds (42/137/256) of per-source `.npz` activations (the test-retest source for marker); #612 has 2 adapter seeds (42/137) for sycophancy. ## 11. Decision Rationale

- **Read layer marker = L14.** *Why:* the marker BASE context bank is anchored at L14 (#604 read band 14–24) — the base side (`c_C`, `v_base`) reuses #604 with zero re-extraction, keeping cross-experiment comparability on the predictor inputs. The marker realized-gate `Δv` at L14 is freshly extracted via `shift_extract.py --layer 14` (fact-check Row 17/18: #604/#521/#551 do not hold marker shifts at L14). *Source:* `#604` (the marker context bank + read-band anchor). *Alternatives:* re-pick by expression-prediction sweep — rejected (would re-extract the whole bank for marginal gain; #604 already validated L14 as the marker read layer).
- **Read layer sycophancy = L20.** *Why:* #612's panel centroids are stored at L20 and #649 swept L2/L7/L20; L20 is the existing sycophancy panel read. *Source:* `#649`, `#612`. *Alternatives:* L14/L7 — registered as a cheap CPU robustness fallback if L20 is null, not primary.
- **Ridge λ = held-out GCV over `{1e-3…1e1}·median-eig(Σ_c)`.** *Why:* the whitened metric needs regularization on a ~40-context `Σ_c` (rank-deficient at H=3584); GCV on TRAIN contexts avoids held-out leakage and picks λ data-drivenly rather than by a magic constant. *Source:* A8 metric ablation (`main.tex L1415`, `(Σ_c+λI)⁻¹`); `#604` whitening practice. *Alternatives:* fixed λ=1 — rejected (not Goal-tuned, sensitive to `Σ_c` scale); diagonal `(Σ_c+λI)⁻¹` (A8 also lists it) — registered as a third metric arm only if the full whitened arm is unstable.
- **`ψ` = identity (primary) + learned linear ridge (robustness).** *Why:* `t_{C,B}` (answer-side) and `c_C'` (prompt-side query) live in the same residual stream at layer `l`, so `ψ=I` is well-defined and assumption-light; the paper explicitly allows `ψ` "when needed," and Chen et al.'s projection-difference precedent motivates a learned linear map as the one representational degree of freedom. *Source:* A8 `L1371`; Chen et al. `2507.21509`. *Alternatives:* MLP `ψ` — rejected (over-fits ~40 contexts out-of-sample); no `ψ` at all for key (iii) — the displacement is already in residual-stream space, so `ψ=I` suffices.
- **2 adapter seeds (sycophancy) / 3 bank seeds (marker).** *Why:* #637's single-seed caveat is the exact gap this experiment closes; cross-seed stability is required to call any key a winner (a chance win won't replicate). *Source:* `#612` (2 on-policy villain seeds exist), `#637` (single-seed caveat), `#604` (3 bank seeds). *Alternatives:* 1 seed — rejected (re-introduces the #637 caveat).
- **Sycophancy source = villain only.** *Why:* #649 found villain the geometry-richest sycophancy source; focusing one source keeps the experiment a clean key-ablation rather than a multi-source sweep, and #612 has 2 villain seeds. *Source:* `#649`, `#612`. *Alternatives:* all 4 #612 sources — deferred to a follow-up (adds GPU + dilutes the single-variable focus); a single-variable extension, not this experiment.
- **Adapters reused, none trained.** *Why:* fit-for-purpose adapters exist for both behaviors at the correct recipe; retraining wastes GPU + breaks comparability. *Source:* `#474`, `#612` (Hub-verified §5/§10). *Alternatives:* fresh marker adapter via the band-stop recipe — unnecessary (#474 loc-arm is the established marker localization arm). The marker `Δv` activations themselves ARE extracted fresh in Phase A (fact-check Row 17/18 corrected the v1 claim that #604/#521/#551 held them) — but this is data extraction over the reused adapter, not adapter retraining.
