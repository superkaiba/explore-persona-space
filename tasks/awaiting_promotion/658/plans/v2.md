## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** The Betley run (already in flight) asked "can the model's internal reaction
  to a prompt predict how it'll behave?" — but it only asked that on a set of deliberately
  provocative, misalignment-flavored prompts. I'll now ask the EXACT same question on a set of
  ordinary everyday questions (cooking, travel, language trivia), matched to be the same length,
  and check whether the answer is the same on both. One thing changes: the kind of question.
- **What I expect:** For behaviors that the model actually does on normal questions (sycophancy,
  hedging, refusing some requests), the prediction should hold on the everyday prompts too — that
  would say the "you can read behavior off the prompt's internal trace" property is real, not a
  quirk of scary prompts. For the misalignment-flavored behaviors (acting broadly badly, complying
  with harmful requests), the model probably almost never does them on cooking questions, so there
  will be nothing to predict — and that absence is the answer for those, not a failure.
- **What would change my mind:** If the prediction works great on the provocative prompts but
  collapses on the everyday ones for a behavior that DOES still vary on everyday prompts, that
  would mean the whole foundation is riding on the prompt genre and wouldn't transfer to real
  deployment traffic — which is exactly the safety-relevant case I care about (does the chain
  reach benign contexts).

## 1. Goal & hypothesis

**Goal (inherited from #658 `## Goal`, unchanged):** determine whether the leakage-predictor's
base-model chain holds on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (A3.2/A3.3/A3.4/A3.5) and lock the best layer +
summary recipe. **The (G1) amendment's specific question:** is the base-model chain a property of
the model's context→behavior geometry, or an artifact of the behavior-eliciting query genre — i.e.
does it transfer to the benign deployment distribution (open-q 3.7, leakage to default/benign
contexts)? B6 varies the prompt distribution TOGETHER with the behavior; (G1) holds the behavior
fixed and varies ONLY the query genre, length-controlled, so the two genres are directly
comparable.

**Hypotheses (falsifiable):**
- **H1 (transfer):** for behaviors with dynamic range on BOTH pools (sycophancy, hedging,
  self_report, format_style, persona_drift, marker), the per-behavior best-(layer,summary) ρ on
  UltraChat is within the UltraChat noise floor of the Betley ρ (|genre delta| ≤ the wider of the
  two arms' noise-floor 95th-pct margins). CONFIRMS: the chain is a geometry property, not a genre
  artifact.
- **H2 (genre-bound):** for ≥1 behavior with dynamic range on both pools, UltraChat ρ drops below
  its noise floor while Betley ρ clears it. FALSIFIES H1 for that behavior — the chain is
  genre-bound there. This is the safety-relevant negative.
- **H3 (floor, expected for misalignment behaviors):** broad_em / harmful_compliance / refusal sit
  at expression floor on UltraChat (testable variance ≈ 0) → ρ undefined → reported as "no dynamic
  range on the generic genre," NOT as a chain break. The continuous log-P DV is the primary read
  where the rate floors.

### Dual-DV + the FLOOR GUARD (the load-bearing read for this arm)

Per the standing dual-DV rule AND `epm:followup-scope` explicitly: register BOTH (a) the judged
on-policy RATE as PRIMARY and (b) the length-normalized base `log P` of judged-positive on-policy
completions as the SECONDARY non-saturating companion, validated by Spearman(rate, log-P) across
dynamic-range cells. **The genre-specific escalation:** the misalignment behaviors
(broad_em / harmful_compliance / refusal) are EXPECTED to floor at ≈0 expression on generic
everyday queries (H3) — exactly where the binary rate has no dynamic range to predict. For ANY
behavior whose UltraChat rate has near-zero testable variance, **the continuous log-P DV becomes
the PRIMARY read** (the marker-leakage three-space dual-DV recipe, generalized). A no-variance ρ is
reported as "no dynamic range on the generic genre — chain untestable here," NEVER as "the chain
breaks." Per-behavior testable variance is reported PER POOL (a small table: behavior × {Betley
variance, UltraChat variance}) so the reader sees which behaviors the genre comparison is even
defined for.

### Per-genre vs cross-genre reads (mandatory, per `epm:followup-scope v2`)

- **PRIMARY (per behavior, per genre):** Spearman ρ, scored on TWO SEPARATE stores (Betley vs
  UltraChat), each against its OWN within-genre noise floor + predict-mean + base-prior. Never a
  pooled cross-genre fit (pooling would manufacture or mask the genre effect — the #545
  pooled-vs-held-out lesson).
- **SECONDARY:** Pearson, per-behavior best layer/recipe per genre, and the genre delta in
  predictive quality.
- **Genre delta:** (UltraChat ρ − Betley ρ) per behavior, reported with BOTH arms' noise-floor 95th
  pct so a delta inside the floor band reads as "no genre effect" (H1) and a delta where UltraChat
  drops below its floor while Betley clears it reads as "genre-bound" (H2).

### Install-strength control

N/A — no cross-condition leakage comparison, no fine-tuning, no install (inherited from v1 §6).
The genre delta is a comparison of base→base predictability across two probe genres, not a leakage
comparison across training conditions.

### Statistical-input existence

All statistical inputs are produced in-scope by THIS arm: the UltraChat noise-floor 95th-pct
(N1's 8 redraws from the UltraChat pool), the FDR over the UltraChat grid, the per-(C,probe) answer
spans for N1 resampling (stored in G2). The Betley `aggregate.json` consumed by the genre-delta

SAME-ISSUE in-scope artifact, not a phantom prior-run dependency; A2 fails loud if the Betley
`aggregate.json` is absent (the genre delta is then reported as "Betley arm pending," not silently
skipped). No attenuation/reliability correction consuming a derived input is registered.

### Metrics, tests, thresholds

- **PRIMARY:** Spearman ρ (predictor vs measured UltraChat `E0`), per behavior, per layer, per
  summary. Pearson secondary.
- **PASS (per behavior, UltraChat arm):** best-(layer,summary) ρ > 95th pct of the UltraChat N1
  noise floor AND > predict-mean ρ AND > base-prior ρ. A3.3 PASSes iff the linear ρ is within the
  UltraChat noise floor of the A3.2 MLP ρ. (Identical bar to v1, scored on the UltraChat store.)
- **Statistical hygiene:** cluster-aware bootstrap (one cluster per behavior for A3.2/A3.3, per
  context for A3.4/A3.5), 2000 resamples; FDR q=0.10 (Benjamini-Hochberg) WITHIN each genre's
  ~1000-cell grid (the genre delta is a between-arm read, not added to the FDR family). Report the
  per-behavior best cell with FDR-adjusted p AND the across-layer ρ profile.
