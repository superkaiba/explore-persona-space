## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** #658 already built one "behavior direction" per behavior the cheap, sloppy way (by
  diffing two text corpora that didn't really match), and asked whether that direction can read a
  behavior off the model's internal state. The headline answer (a fancier predictor can't read it)
  doesn't depend on those directions, but a secondary claim does. So I'll now build those directions
  the careful, published way: write a handful of "be evil" / "don't be evil" instruction pairs per
  behavior, have the model answer the same questions under each, keep only the answers a judge agrees
  actually show (or don't show) the behavior, and take the difference. I'll try two flavors of the
  contrast and four ways of summarizing the activations, sweep every layer, and reuse all of #658's
  already-collected behavior measurements to score which version reads behavior best.
- **What I expect:** The careful directions will read behavior at least as well as the sloppy ones
  (probably noticeably better for sycophancy and refusal), and the best layer will land in the middle
  of the network, near where the original paper found it. I expect the "evil vs not-evil" contrast and
  the "evil vs plain-default" contrast to land close, with the plain-default one maybe tracking natural
  behavior a bit better.
- **What would change my mind:** If even the careful directions can't beat just guessing the average
  behavior (once I account for how noisy the measurements are), then the linear-readout assumption is
  genuinely weak on this model rather than an artifact of #658's sloppy corpora, and I'd rewrite that
  secondary takeaway as a real negative. The other surprise would be the best layer landing nowhere
  near the paper's, which would say the predictive-best layer and the steering-best layer are different
  things.

### 4.1 Construct semantics

- **`r_B` (the rebuilt direction):** a behavior read-out direction in the model's residual stream,
  `r_B[ℓ] = mean(response-avg acts of judge-kept trait-pole rollouts) − mean(response-avg acts of
  judge-kept opposite-pole rollouts)`, per behavior B, per layer ℓ. Built from on-policy rollouts of
  the BASE model under PV system prompts on the extraction-set questions (DISJOINT from the 50-context
  evaluation battery).
- **`v0(C)` (REUSED, unchanged):** the #658 answer-side mean activation per evaluation-battery context
  C, all 28 layers — the projection target. r_B and v0(C) live in the SAME residual-stream space (same
  model, same response-avg recipe), so `r_Bᵀ v0(C)` is a well-defined scalar per (C, B, layer).
- **The A3.3 prediction:** `E0(C,B) ≈ r_Bᵀ v0(C)` — the model's base expression of behavior B under
  context C is read off linearly by projecting the context's answer-side summary onto the behavior
  direction. The DV is ρ(r_Bᵀ v0(C), E0(C,B)) across the 50 contexts, per behavior, per genre, under
  LOCO. This is the same A3.3 quantity v1/v3 tested — only the r_B that goes into it changes.

PV r_B from system-prompted rollouts (generate under 15 system prompts × 20 questions × 10 rollouts,
judge-filter, response-avg, diff-in-means) is genuinely different from the parent extractor's job
(generate base answers to the 48 evaluation probes under the 50 contexts, capture answer-side v0(C)).

### 4.3 CPU/API phase — judge filter + predictor fits (NEW: `scripts/issue658_rb_pv_fit.py`, off-pod)

**J1 — judge-filter.** Batch-judge every PV rollout transcript with `claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929` and
the behavior's trait-eval prompt (0-100), via the hardened `eval.batch_judge` client (Anthropic Batch
API; idempotent custom_ids, checkpoint/resume). Parse the integer 0-100 (a `REFUSAL` token → score 0
per the paper's rubric, treated as non-trait). **KEEP rules:** pos-prompt rollout kept iff score > 50;
neg-prompt rollout kept iff score < 50; neutral-prompt rollout kept iff score < 50 (the pos-vs-neutral
pole's negative half). The judge set across the grid ≈ 4 behaviors × 20 questions × (5 pos + 5 neg + 5
neutral) × 10 rollouts = **12,000 judgments** (Batch API by default; the brief's ~6k is the pos+neg
estimate, ~12k with neutral included — both well inside the Batch API budget, §9).

**B1 — build r_B per cell.** For each (behavior × pole × reduction × layer):
- `diffmeans` = mean(kept pos response-avg) − mean(kept neg-or-neutral response-avg), per layer.
- `meanDB` = mean(kept pos response-avg) only (no subtraction).
- `few-shot-final` = mean(kept few-shot-final response-token acts) (PV3), per layer.
- `multi-layer-pooled` (the Q4 reduction) — FULLY PINNED (Must-Fix #1):
  - **Layer band (FIXED, not swept):** the central 1/3 of the 28-layer residual stream =
    **layers 10-18 inclusive (9 layers)**, centered on the stack midpoint (layer 13.5). FIXED per
    (behavior × genre × pole) — ONE pooled cell per cell, no extra band-width grid (the minimum-scope
    choice, brief Must-Fix #1 option (a)). `Source:` arXiv 2507.21509 §3 + App position-extraction
    (the paper steers Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct from a mid-stack layer sweep, "the most informative layer,"
    and pins NO single integer or band) → the principled fixed choice is the geometrically-centered
    central 1/3 of the residual stream (§11).
  - **Reduction rule: CONCATENATE (not average).** Build the per-layer `diffmeans` direction at each
    of the 9 band layers, then **z-score each per-layer direction using train-fold-only statistics**
    (mean/std computed over the LOCO TRAIN contexts only — leakage-free, never the held-out C), then
    **concatenate** the 9 normalized directions → one `9 × 3584 = 32256`-dim pooled direction. The
    pooled `r_Bᵀ v0(C)` projects this against the **concatenated** v0(C) over the SAME 9 band layers
    (each band layer's v0(C) z-scored with the same train-fold statistics before concatenation). Both
    sides span the same 9-layer × hidden_dim space → the dot product is well-defined. Concatenation
    preserves the layer-specific signal that averaging would wash out (the brief's stated rationale).
  - **FDR / selection family: SINGLE family.** The pooled cell joins the per-genre
    `{behavior × pole × reduction × layer}` grid at a special `layer = "pooled"` slot — NOT a separate
    small family. Each reduction therefore contributes its 28 single-layer entries PLUS one pooled
    slot, so the per-genre family size is
    `behavior(4) × pole(2) × reduction(4) × (28 single layers + 1 pooled) = 4 × 2 × 4 × 29 = ` **928**
    (the figure used throughout §6). Benjamini-Hochberg at q=0.10 over the whole single family. (The
    pooled slot is only meaningful for the layer-spanning reductions; for any reduction whose pooled
    slot is degenerate it is simply dropped from the family before BH — a smaller-than-928 family is
    fine, BH adapts to the realized entry count.)
  - **Comparison rule (Q4 verdict):** the paired **Δρ = ρ(pooled) − ρ(single-best-layer)** per
    (behavior × genre × pole), computed on the **SAME LOCO folds** (the pooled `r_B` and the
    single-best `r_B` are evaluated on the identical held-out contexts), with the **selection-aware CI
    from Must-Fix #2** (§6) — the single-best layer is itself re-selected inside each bootstrap
    resample, so the Δρ CI is honest about both the pooled and the single-best selection. A positive
    Δρ whose selection-aware 95% CI excludes 0 = "pooling beats single-best"; otherwise "pooling does
    not beat single-best under this design."
- **Pole** selects which negative half feeds the subtraction: `pos-vs-neg` (the faithful PV symmetric
  axis) or `pos-vs-neutral` (pos minus the kept-neutral mean). Both built from the SAME judge-filtered
  pool.

### 4.4 Why code, not a model call?

Inherited from plan v1 §4.4 verbatim. The behavior SCORING is a model call (the Sonnet 4.5 judge,
used in J1 — the right tool). The r_B BUILD + ρ FITS (diff-in-means over judge-filtered rollouts, LOCO
held-out projection, bootstrap CIs, FDR over the grid) are deterministic linear algebra over cached
tensors — a model call cannot do a held-out regression or a reproducible per-cell ρ over the
{pole × reduction × layer} grid. Code is correct here; no single-model-call alternative exists for the
numeric predictor. The alternative considered (ask a model "does this direction predict behavior?") is
rejected: it is non-reproducible, gives no per-cell ρ, and cannot run the noise-floor / FDR gate.

### 4.5 Contrastive negatives

This is NOT a behavior-implantation experiment (no fine-tuning, no implant) — so the
behavior-implantation contrastive-negatives MANDATE (`.claude/rules/contrastive-negatives.md`, the
training-row recipe) does not bind here. BUT the SPIRIT of the rule — that a behavior direction must be
defined relative to a contrast, never positive-only — IS exactly what the PV recipe does, and this
amendment satisfies it explicitly:
- **The contrast IS the design.** Knob A pole `pos-vs-neg` is the faithful PV symmetric contrast (5
  trait-eliciting system prompts vs 5 trait-SUPPRESSING system prompts, over the SAME 20 extraction
  questions); pole `pos-vs-neutral` is the project-side variant (trait-eliciting vs the bare default
  assistant). Both are judge-filtered (pos kept >50, the negative half kept <50), 10 rollouts per
  (question × prompt). The `meanDB` reduction (positive-only mean) is included ONLY as a deliberate
  ablation arm to MEASURE what the contrast buys — exactly the "single manipulated variable IS
  contrastive-vs-non-contrastive" exemption framing.
- Cross-link `.claude/rules/contrastive-negatives.md`: the negative pole always includes the bare
  default assistant (the pos-vs-neutral pole), and the close negatives are the trait-suppressing system
  prompts (5 per behavior) — the "at least 2-4 close negatives + the default" composition, here realized
  as system-prompt poles rather than persona-gated training rows. The ~1:1 ratio is satisfied by
  construction (5 pos prompts vs 5 neg / 5 neutral prompts, equal rollout counts).

  poles — variable N is a confound. The ~1:1 pos-to-negative ratio is scaled proportionally to floor-N.

  of bare/pos rollouts the judge accepts per behavior — directly measured in J1, and it predicts which

  propensity is the REUSED E0(C,B) per-context rate (the ρ denominator) + the inherited base-prior

### Selection-aware uncertainty (Approach B — nested cell selection inside the inherited bootstrap)

The headline SELECTS the maximum ρ over the per-genre `{pole × reduction × layer + pooled-slot}` grid
per behavior, so a bare best-cell ρ + ordinary per-cell FDR p is winner's-curse-inflated and a bare
point Δρ can be sampling noise. Plan v5 registers **Approach B** (Must-Fix #2): nest the cell selection
INSIDE the inherited cluster bootstrap so the reported intervals are selection-aware while keeping n=50
(no contexts lost to a selection/report split).

- **Bootstrap (B=2000, inherited from v3 §11 cluster bootstrap).** For each of B=2000 cluster
  resamples (resample the 50 contexts WITH replacement, behavior-clustered exactly as the v1/v3
  bootstrap), **RE-RUN the internal cell selection** — recompute the held-out-C LOCO ρ for every cell
  in the `{pole × reduction × layer + pooled-slot}` grid on the resampled contexts, take `argmax ρ`
  — and record the resulting selected-cell ρ. The **selection-aware 95% CI for the best-cell ρ** is
  the 2.5/97.5 percentile of this B-length resampled-selected-ρ distribution (percentile interval;
  BCa is an allowed §14 upgrade if skew is large). The single-best-layer ρ used in the pooled-vs-single
  Δρ is re-selected the SAME way inside each resample (so both sides of Δρ are honest).
- **Permutation null (B=2000).** For each of B=2000 permutations, permute the E0 labels within
  (behavior × genre), **repeat the full cell selection** (argmax ρ over the grid on the permuted
  labels), and record the selected max-ρ. This selected-null distribution is the reference the
  best-cell ρ is compared against (and is handed to the analyzer per the Approach-C escape-hatch
  semantics, should the CI and the null disagree).
- **Selection-aware Δρ CIs (all 4 named comparisons), paired context-cluster bootstrap.** For each
  B=2000 resample, AFTER re-selecting the relevant cells inside the resample, recompute and store the
  paired Δρ for each comparison; the selection-aware Δρ 95% CI is the 2.5/97.5 percentile of the
  resampled Δρ distribution. The four comparisons:
  1. **Δρ(PV-r_B − corpus-mismatched r_B)** — the confound-controlled PASS (the takeaway-rewrite verdict).
  2. **Δρ(PV-r_B − label-split baseline)** — does the faithful recipe beat the cheap label-split direction.
  3. **Δρ(multi-layer-pooled − single-best-layer)** — the Q4 verdict, on the SAME LOCO folds.
  4. **Δρ(pos-vs-neg − pos-vs-neutral)** — the Q5 pole verdict.
  Each Δρ's "wins" verdict = its selection-aware 95% CI excludes 0; otherwise "not distinguishable
  under this design" (statistical-framing discipline below).
- **CI level 95% (inherited); percentile interval (BCa optional, §14).** Resample count B=2000 for
  BOTH the bootstrap and the permutation null. `Source:` the v3 §11 cluster bootstrap line
  (`Bootstrap | 2000 cluster-resamples (behavior-clustered A3.2/A3.3 …)`,
  `tasks/followups_running/658/plans/v3.md` §11) — Approach B nests the existing cell-selection step
  inside that already-validated 2000-resample cluster bootstrap; no new method paper, the v3 cluster

  per-cell LOCO ρ inputs the bootstrap already produces; the nested argmax is a cheap CPU reduction

### Statistical-framing discipline

A PASS supports "the rebuilt PV r_B reads E0 above the noise floor for behavior B" — NOT "A3.3 is
proven." A within-floor ρ supports "not distinguishable from the noise floor under this design," never
"the chain breaks" (the floor guard for floored behaviors). Whether the secondary takeaway "survives" is
phrased against the confound-controlled PASS (PV r_B ρ vs corpus-mismatched ρ + noise floor), never a
single point estimate.

recipe is IDENTICAL to plan v1 and inherits its grounding: `Source: plan v1 §11` for the layer sweep
(theory §A3.2/A3.3 + #594), the ρ metric / LOCO / bootstrap 2000 (#545 scoring.py), FDR q=0.10 (CLAUDE.md

The A3.2 MLP + A3.4 ridge knobs are NOT re-run by this amendment (it touches only A3.3's linear r_B), so
their `ungrounded — needs smoke-test` flags do not bind here. The single experimental variable is the
r_B build recipe — so the literature re-search is run ONLY for the new PV values below (the inherit
fast-path: a value the parent arm validated for this exact model+data carries over un-re-searched).

**The NEW load-bearing values (the r_B recipe — all quoted verbatim from the paper, read via arXiv MCP
this session):**

- **5 pos/neg system-prompt pairs per behavior.** Why: the paper's pipeline generates exactly 5 pairs
  (each a positive prompt eliciting the trait + a negative suppressing it); faithful replication uses
  the same count. `Source: arXiv 2507.21509 §2.1` ("the pipeline generates 5 pairs of contrastive system
  prompts") + App-A ("Generate a list of five instruction pairs"). Alternatives: more pairs — rejected,
  diverges from the paper without a reason; the v7 scope pins 5.
- **~20-question extraction set per behavior, disjoint from the 50-context battery.** Why: the paper
  generates 40 questions split into a 20-question EXTRACTION set + a 20-question evaluation set (disjoint).
  We use the 20 extraction questions to build r_B; the held-out evaluation is the REUSED 50-context
  battery (not the paper's 20-q eval set — our held-out surface is richer). `Source: arXiv 2507.21509
  App-A` ("We split the 40 questions into two disjoint subsets: 20 questions for vector extraction
  (extraction set), and 20 questions for downstream behavior evaluation"). Alternatives: use all 40 for
  extraction — rejected, the paper holds 20 out; the disjointness from OUR battery is the contamination
  guard (§4.7).
- **10 rollouts per (extraction question × system prompt).** Why: the paper generates 10 rollouts each
  under the positive and negative system prompt per extraction question. `Source: arXiv 2507.21509 §2.2`
  ("For each question in the extraction set, we generate responses using both positive and negative
  system prompts (10 rollouts each)"). Alternatives: fewer — rejected, lowers the judge-kept yield below
  a stable diff-in-means (the §8 yield-floor risk).
- **Judge threshold 50; keep pos>50 / neg<50 / neutral<50.** Why: the paper filters by trait expression
  score, keeping pos-prompt responses >50 and neg-prompt responses <50. `Source: arXiv 2507.21509 §2.2`
  ("retaining only those that align with the intended system prompt, specifically, responses with trait
  scores greater than 50 for positive prompts and less than 50 for negative prompts") + App-A ("a score
  from 0 … to 100"). The pos-vs-neutral pole applies the same <50 keep to the neutral rollouts. Threshold
  50 is the paper's midpoint. Alternatives: a stricter threshold — rejected, diverges from the paper and
  worsens yield.
- **Response-token activation averaging (response-avg), every layer.** Why: the paper extracts residual
  activations at every layer, averaging across RESPONSE tokens (the "Response avg" default, shown to
  yield the strongest steering vs prompt-last/prompt-avg). This MATCHES #658's `v0(C)` answer-side
  summary (`summarize_answer_span(span, "mean")`), so r_B and v0 share the residual-stream space.
  `Source: arXiv 2507.21509 §2.2` ("we extract residual stream activations at every layer, averaging
  across response tokens") + App position-extraction ("Response avg generally yields the strongest …
  and is adopted as the default"). Alternatives: prompt-last/prompt-avg — rejected, the paper's own
  ablation shows them weaker AND they'd mismatch the reused v0(C) recipe.
- **Diff-in-means direction.** Why: the persona vector is the difference in mean activations between
  trait-exhibiting and non-trait responses. `Source: arXiv 2507.21509 §2.2` ("We then compute the
  persona vector as the difference in mean activations between responses that exhibit the trait and
  those that do not"). This is the theory's A3.3 default too ("diff-in-means … inspired by persona
  vectors", plan v1 §11). Alternatives: meanDB / few-shot-final — included as the Knob B ablation arms,
  not the default.
- **Layer selected by predictive ρ (NOT steering) — named replication deviation.** Why: the paper
  selects the layer by steering effectiveness; the v7 scope deviates to the A3.3 selection rule (which
  layer best PREDICTS expression), since the question is predictive read-out, not steering control. The
  paper's steering-best for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is mid-stack (it sweeps layers; Figure "steering" §3 +
  the Qwen cross-trait analysis App). `Source: arXiv 2507.21509 App select-layer (steering) + §3
  (Qwen mid-stack); v7 scope (deviate to predictive-ρ selection)`. We cross-check the predictive-best
  layer vs the steering-best as Q3. Alternatives: steering-selection — rejected by the v7 scope (it
