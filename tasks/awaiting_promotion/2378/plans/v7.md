## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I take the model's internal snapshot of a conversation right before it answers, and a second snapshot of the answer it wrote, and learn a simple linear rule that predicts one from the other — trained only on ordinary chatbot Q&A. Then I test whether that same rule still works when the model is instead writing fiction — a story character answering a question, a plain text-file dialogue — and when the rule is pointed at the other side of the chat: predicting the user's next message from the conversation so far, once over real human messages and once over messages the model itself writes while playing the user. One originally planned fiction format — a story character replying to a remark that isn't a question — is DROPPED: two pilot rounds measured its usable-example rate at 40% of what the design needs, and the user chose to cut it rather than redesign (2026-08-23).
- **What I expect:** based on the earlier 7B results, the rule applied unchanged will mostly fail across the fiction formats, but a single linear correction on the input side will recover most of it. For the user side, the corrected 7B result says the user's next message is weakly predictable — about a fifth of its variance, roughly 2.5× below the assistant's — so the open questions are whether the 4× bigger model narrows that user-vs-assistant gap, and whether the model's own pretend-user turns are more predictable than real human ones (they plausibly are for a boring reason — model-written text is more regular — which is why I only claim a predictability difference, not what causes it).
- **What would change my mind:** the rule transferring unchanged (the earlier framing costs do not carry to this redesigned setting — the bigger model is one possible reason among the several things changed at once); no linear correction recovering it (the earlier recovery result doesn't survive honest scene-fitted queries on this setup); or the user side landing far off the corrected 7B picture in either direction — the user's next message turning clearly unpredictable again, or nearly as predictable as the assistant's.

## 3. Hypothesis

Per target cell (8 targets; chat is the only training cell), with recovery fraction = transfer R² / own-map ceiling R² on the target's held-out folds, define **D** = direct-rung recovery − 0.5 and **M** = context-side-re-map-rung recovery − 0.7, and **U** = own-ceiling held-out R² − max(the cell's measured shuffled-answer null p95, 0.05) (thresholds grounded: #2054 prose swaps direct 0.73–0.77 of ceiling vs boundary swaps −0.16 — 0.5 splits the bimodality; #2054 ctx-re-map recovery 0.84 vs answer-side 0.18 — 0.7 splits those). CIs are 200-draw scene-level (user cells: conversation-level) bootstrap percentile intervals on the recovery fraction. Recovery fractions are ill-defined when the own ceiling is itself indistinguishable from null, so the lattice carries a pre-registered Unmappable-target branch, evaluated first — retained as DEFENSIVE machinery after the #825 correction: the corrected 7B user cells sit at guarded R² +0.19…+0.25 (well above any plausible null band), but the real-human and on-policy-sim user arms have no guarded 7B precedent, #1689's on-policy Qwen-sim read was at the floor (0.00–0.07), and this is a new model + redesigned rig (floor grounded: #1310 story ceilings 0.106–0.253 all clear 0.05; 0.05 is also the G1c rig-defect floor).

DISJOINT and exhaustive: Unmappable-target ⇔ U ≤ 0; Transfers-directly ⇔ U > 0 AND D > 0 AND D's 95% CI excludes 0 on the positive side; Transfers-via-reparameterization ⇔ U > 0 AND D's 95% CI is wholly below 0 AND M > 0 AND M's 95% CI excludes 0 on the positive side; No-linear-transfer ⇔ U > 0 AND D's 95% CI is wholly below 0 AND M's 95% CI is wholly below 0; Inconclusive ⇔ otherwise.

**Registered reporting tiers (layered ON the lattice; the ⇔ partition above is unchanged — a point-U crossing near the 100-draw null p95 must never flip a narrated verdict).** Per cell, the fits driver persists ALL 100 shuffled-answer null-draw R² values (per fold) and a 200-draw bootstrap distribution of the ceiling margin m = own-ceiling R² − max(null p95, 0.05) (scene/conversation-grain resample of the ceiling side; the U statistic is the point value of m). Tier assignment: **clearly-mappable** ⇔ m's 95% bootstrap CI wholly above 0; **clearly-unmappable** ⇔ CI wholly below 0; **boundary-indeterminate** ⇔ CI straddles 0. Suppression rules: the lattice's point verdict is still assigned by the registered partition, but for a boundary-indeterminate cell (i) recovery fractions and every ratio verdict (recovery ratios AND the H4a user-vs-assistant ceiling ratio) are SUPPRESSED — the cell reports own-ceiling R² and transfer R² separately, absolute and unnormalized; (ii) the cell is excluded from H5's fraction denominator (clearly-mappable cells only); (iii) all narration carries the tier label. Recovery-fraction bootstrap validity (skip-and-count): within each 200-draw recovery-fraction (or ceiling-ratio) bootstrap, a draw whose resampled ceiling fails the registered floor (≤ max(null p95, 0.05)) is SKIPPED and COUNTED; if valid draws < 160/200 (80%), the ratio verdict is suppressed for that (cell × rung) and ceiling + transfer R² are reported separately. All persisted (null draws, margin bootstraps, skip counts) in the cell's fits/ladder JSONs.

**The U/D/M lattice is defined only for R².** Retrieval (rank-1 + @5/@10, all conventions) is a registered COMPANION diagnostic reported per cell × rung; R²-vs-retrieval disagreements are handed to the analyzer as findings — no retrieval-branch verdict rules exist or are being invented.

### 4.2 Story generation — three-segment recipe (P2/P4, pod, vLLM raw completion)

Structure is guaranteed by prefill at the boundaries, never by instruction:
- **Segment A (scene + utterance):** prompt = 3 sampled prime exemplars + `***` + opening seed (setting/situation/register + final seed sentence). `LLM.generate` raw (no chat template anywhere), temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, seed 137, max_new_tokens 512. **As-executed r11 recalibration (the SPENT G1 round — the measured r2 rates bind to THIS recipe):** `<think>` banned at SegA sampling via bad_words (think-leak 0/1500 question attempts at r2 vs 81% at r1), miner window 250→512 tokens, stop-at-first-directed-quote mining semantics, seed bank audited + frozen. Cap-hit at SegA is by design (the miner window binds); reported per cell but exempt from the regen trigger (stated deviation). **Fold-key persistence (the §4.4 family-held-out fold's input):** every SegA attempt row carries its sampled bank ids — `setting_id`, `situation_id`, `register_id`, `final_seed_id`, `prime_exemplar_ids`, `opener_id` — written into the raw_completions JSONL rows AND the per-cell row ledgers, so scene→seed metadata survives every downstream reduction.
- **Structural miner (code):** find the first quoted utterance directed at the character within the first ~512 generated tokens (r11 window; stop at the FIRST directed quote) — quote-span extraction + attribution heuristics (attribution verb adjacent to the quote naming another speaker, or 2nd-person address + the character named in the surrounding sentence). Keeps utterances ending in `?` (question family; the dialogue family's non-`?` keep rule is retired with the dropped family). Emits byte offsets. **Why code, not a model call:** (a) alternative considered — a single judge call returning the utterance span; (b) code preferred because capture needs deterministic byte offsets over 150k candidates (a model call cannot return reliable offsets, and at this N the latency/cost dominates), and the semantic residue (directedness, question-in-disguise) goes to the judge; (c) would flip if mining precision < ~0.9 at pilot (then judge-extracted spans re-anchored by exact string match). Measured r2 mining rate under this recipe: 438/1500 = 0.292 (question family).
- **Admission judge (filter, not force; scored on PRE-ANSWER scene text ONLY):** the judge sees the scene + mined utterance and NEVER the answer span (the answer comes from the same raw completion, so answer-content gating would be selection-on-outcome on the DV; answer-quality diagnostics are reported per cell, never used to drop rows). Graded 0–100, admit ≥ 50 (Source: #2054 admission convention): exactly one utterance directed at the character; coherent scene; it is a genuine question. Congruence is NOT gated (near-ceiling by construction; measured as a manipulation check). Single-draw admission (parent parity; not a ranked DV), max_tokens 1024, Batch API. Measured r2 admission rate: 156/200 = 0.78 (question family).
- **Segment B (answer, forced):** truncate right after the mined utterance's closing quote, append `\n\n{opener}` where opener is sampled from a small attributed-quote-family bank (e.g. `{Name} replied: "` / `{Name} answered quietly: "` / `{Name} said: "` / `After a moment, {Name} replied: "` — ~8 variants; a frozen string is the #1345 R²=0.019 trap), free-continue max_new_tokens 1024. The answer span = generated text up to the closing quote (miner-extracted; no closing quote within cap ⇒ cap-hit row, reported, >2%/cell ⇒ regen at 2× cap). The opener IS the measured boundary form (disclosed: answer onset partially experimenter-written, coinciding with the standardized boundary). Measured r2 SegB survival: 156/156 = 1.0 (question family).
- **Chat cell:** `tokenizer.apply_chat_template([{user: Q}], add_generation_prompt=True, enable_thinking=False)` rendered by our code (never vLLM-internal chat) — NOTE: the pinned template with enable_thinking=False renders an EMPTY `<think>\n\n</think>\n\n` block after the assistant header (verified from the pinned tokenizer_config this session), so the chat-cell context slot (v_C = last token before the answer's first character) sits AFTER the closing `</think>` — stated here as the slot definition; generate max_new_tokens 2048; assert the GENERATED text carries no further `<think>` token. **Plain-text cell:** `User: {Q}\n\nAssistant:` raw completion, stop=["\nUser:", "\n\nUser:"], max 2048. Both: cap-hit fraction reported; >2%/cell ⇒ regen at 2× (Source: #2054 cap-hit convention; parent's bare-text 42.5% cap-hit lesson).
- **Fresh draws (retrieval reference):** for 1,000 held-out rows per cell (all cells except chat_user_real — see §4.2b), 4 extra Segment-B (resp. answer / user-turn) draws at seeds 138–141.

### 4.2b User-turn cells — two provenance arms (P0/P2/P4; NEW, user-requested 03:24Z)

Both arms share the SAME 10,000 user-pool conversations and the SAME context prefix, and differ ONLY in who wrote the measured user turn — the paired design that makes the real-vs-sim gap interpretable (and makes their context vectors identical by construction under causal attention).

- **Slot definition (load-bearing — closes the #2054 self-prediction trap):** the context slot ends at the END OF THE PREVIOUS ASSISTANT TURN plus the user-turn header — never at the query — so the target text is never inside the context. Concretely: our code renders the Qwen3.6 chat template over [user₁, assistant₁] + `<|im_start|>user\n` (thinking disabled); the measured target is the user₂ turn. Depth is pinned at its minimum (user₂ = the first user turn with a completed assistant turn before it), minimizing the turn-depth mismatch vs the single-turn chat train cell (#825: turn-1 maps are genuinely different from deeper-turn maps — the residual depth-2-vs-depth-1 confound is disclosed on every user-cell transfer read).
- **Real arm (`chat_user_real`):** NO generation. Teacher-forced capture over the template-rendered real text: context = real user₁ + real assistant₁ (+ user header), target span = the REAL human user₂ text (16–2,000 chars filter, counted). Provenance disclosed: the measured text is human-written and the in-context assistant₁ is the ORIGINAL logged assistant's reply (both off-policy for Qwen3.6) — teacher-forced capture over fixed text is the standard regime for this line (#2054/#825), with the off-policy provenance stated on every read. No fresh-draw reference and no 5-draw-averaged-target read exist for this arm (a real conversation has exactly one user₂; substituting model redraws would change provenance) — disclosed; the sim arm carries those reads.
- **Simulated arm (`chat_user_sim`):** the model WRITES the user turn on-policy. Generation prompt = the identical rendered prefill (through assistant₁'s `<|im_end|>` + `<|im_start|>user\n`), raw continuation (no vLLM-internal chat templating), temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, seed 137, stop=[`<|im_end|>`, `<|im_start|>`], max_new_tokens 1024; cap-hit reported, >2% ⇒ regen at 2×. Degenerate turns dropped with counts, never backfilled — the drop predicate is the SAME 16–2,000-char measured-turn length band the real arm applies (identical eligibility predicates across arms — see Pair-complete cohort below). Fresh draws: 4 extra user-turn draws (seeds 138–141) for 1,000 held-out conversations.
- **Pair-complete cohort (H4b; the intersection contract):** both arms apply IDENTICAL eligibility predicates — the shared context-side predicates (depth ≥ 2, English-script over user₁ + assistant₁ + user₂, span resolution on the rendered template) and the same 16–2,000-char band on each arm's OWN measured turn. All paired H4b statistics AND the shared fold map are computed on the INTERSECTION of the two arms' final retained conversation IDs; the P6 driver asserts, BEFORE any paired statistic: identical ordered conversation-ID lists, identical fold assignments, and identical per-conversation context-vector hashes (sha256 over the v_C bytes) across the two arms — fail-loud on any mismatch. Arm-specific full-cohort fits are reported as labeled supplementary rows. Dropped-conversation systematicity (which conversations fall out of the intersection, per loss channel) is reported as a diagnostic.
- **Span mining is deterministic — no judge:** our code renders the template, so the user₂ span's char offsets are known by construction in the real arm and equal to the generated-continuation span in the sim arm (the template structure, not a model call, defines the turn boundary). The user cells therefore consume ZERO judge calls (no admission, no congruence).
- **Capture slots (same rig as all cells, §4.3):** v_C = last token of the user-turn header (end of the context slot); v_A = token-mean over the user₂ span; v_P = last token of assistant₁'s role header (the state before the addressed utterance — for the user, the "utterance being replied to" is the assistant's turn-1 reply — the structural analogue of the other cells' prefix arm).
- **Reads:** chat-assistant-trained map → each user arm through the full 9-rung ladder + own-map ceilings + the full retrieval battery (pool = held-out user turns of the same arm; chance stated), dual R² + retrieval throughout; the real-vs-sim gap is itself a registered read (H4b). Length-KS + length-matched refit companions extend to the (real, sim) pair and to (chat, user) comparisons.
