## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** An earlier run found that adding "wrong-persona" examples (contrastive negatives) made the marker habit install harder per teaching example — but adding ANY extra rows also makes the training schedule longer, and longer schedules install harder on their own, so the two explanations were tangled. ## 1. Goal + hypothesis

**Goal (verbatim from the task frontmatter):** Measure the training-step dynamics of source marker implantation AND bystander marker leakage — the full bystander panel probed at the same eval intervals as the source, under the four-float storage contract (log P(marker), z_marker, z_eos, logZ; trained AND base per slot) — in positive-only vs contrastive training at an otherwise matched recipe, to determine (1) whether bystander marker log-prob rises in lockstep with the source implant under positive-only training (one shared unconditional shift) while contrastive training simultaneously pushes bystanders down, and (2) whether the contrastive regime's source-side implantation advantage at matched dose is visible from the first steps of training. Dose is read off the step axis of one full-length trajectory per arm — no separate low-dose vs full-dose arms.

This amendment answers the ONE residue #597 itself flagged (clean-result `### Next steps`, verbatim): *"Positives-plus-neutral-filler control: rerun the positive-only mix with 500 neutral filler rows interleaved, matching the contrastive batch composition without contrastive content. the discriminating test between 'negatives actively help' and 'install speed is step-clocked'."* The parent's matched-dose finding ("contrastive implants harder at matched positive-count") could not separate "negatives actively help" from "any extra rows just lengthen the schedule," because adding the 500 negatives BOTH installs a contrastive lesson AND lengthens the per-epoch schedule (700 rows ⇒ 44 optimizer steps/epoch vs 200 rows ⇒ ~13 steps/epoch at eff. batch 16). The filler arm holds the schedule-lengthening fixed and removes only the contrastive lesson.

### Why a 3-way decomposition, not the binary FILLER-vs-CONTRASTIVE clause (fix (a), the MF1 fix)

The round-1 critic ensemble (all 3 lenses) flagged that the binary outcome-2 falsifier in v4 — `CONTRASTIVE > FILLER at matched schedule ⇒ contrastive negatives actively help` — is **not identifiable for the named construct**, because FILLER and CONTRASTIVE differ in **two** things at the source slot, not one:

- **CONTRASTIVE** (`armC`): 500 markerless EOS-loss rows under OTHER personas + no-persona. ZERO source-context EOS labels in non-positive rows.
- **FILLER** (`armD`): 500 markerless EOS-loss rows under the SOURCE persona. 500 source-context EOS labels at the *same* post-response slot the positives train the marker.

So `CONTRASTIVE > FILLER` reads two ways, both predicting the same observable:
- (a) "Contrastive negatives actively help source install" (v4's registered interpretation), OR
- (b) "FILLER's 500 source-context EOS-loss rows actively anti-train the source marker."

The committed armB-vs-armC data shows mechanism (b) is **live and outcome-sized**: at matched optimizer steps, positives-only (`armB`, 0 extra EOS-loss rows) installs source MORE than contrastive (`armC`) by villain step-16 **−1.74** nat, assistant step-32 **−4.69** nat, qwen_default step-28 **−3.72** nat (verified against the committed panels this round). So "extra source-context EOS rows depress the source marker" is not hypothetical — it is the empirically more likely outcome, and the binary clause would mislabel it as "negatives help."

The fix: register the verdict as a **3-way decomposition** read from two clean isolations, NOT the binary clause.

**Hypothesis — the discriminator (3-way, pre-registered).** Three arms at MATCHED SCHEDULE (matched optimizer steps T, read on the per-step axis):
1. **Contrastive** (`armC`) — positives + contrastive negatives (existing dense panels).
2. **Positives-only** (`armB`) — positives, no extra rows (existing panels).
3. **Positives + neutral filler** (NEW `armD`) — positives + 500 marker-less, no-contrast filler rows under the source persona, identical batch footprint.

**Two clean isolations at matched optimizer step:**

- **FILLER vs Positives-only (`armD` vs `armB`)** isolates the **SOURCE-CONTEXT EOS-SUPPRESSION term.** The only systematic difference between the two arms is FILLER's 500 source-context EOS-loss rows (both arms have identical recipe, schedule, and source-persona positive count). NB: `armB` has 200 rows/epoch (~13 optimizer steps/epoch) vs FILLER's 700 (~44 steps/epoch), so per-batch positive dilution differs at matched step. The cleanest read is the **"FILLER source curve depressed BELOW `armB` at matched step"** test: at matched optimizer step, FILLER has seen each positive FEWER times than `armB` (FILLER's positives are diluted 200/700 per batch vs `armB`'s 200/200), so dilution alone predicts FILLER ≤ `armB` — but the magnitude of that dilution gap is bounded by the per-batch positive fraction. A depression of FILLER below `armB` *beyond* what per-batch dilution explains is positive evidence of source-EOS suppression. We quantify "beyond dilution" by anchoring on `armB`-vs-`armC`: `armB` and `armC` already differ ONLY in the non-positive rows' context (none vs other-persona), and `armC` < `armB` by −1.74..−4.69 nat at matched step — that gap is the *dilution + non-source-EOS* baseline. FILLER < `armC` at matched step would then implicate the *source-context-specific* EOS pressure (the only thing FILLER changes vs `armC` is moving the 500 EOS rows from other-persona to source-persona context).
- **FILLER vs Contrastive (`armD` vs `armC`, both 700-row footprint)** isolates the **CONTRASTIVE-CONTENT term** holding everything else fixed (recipe, schedule, footprint, per-batch dilution, source-persona positive count). This is the clean "is there a content effect beyond schedule-lengthening + dilution?" comparison: the ONLY difference is whether the 500 non-positive rows carry an other-persona boundary (contrastive) or a same-persona disjoint-Q payload (filler).

**Registered decision rule for the headline (pre-registered before data):**

| Observed at matched optimizer step (pre-saturation window) | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **FILLER ≈ armC** (within ±1 nat at ≥2/3 sources) | Content of the 500 non-positive rows doesn't matter; only schedule + dilution does. The #601/#597 schedule-clocked story holds. ("negatives don't boost source" airtight at the content level.) |
| **armC > FILLER ≥ armB** at matched step (armC > FILLER by ≥1 nat AND FILLER not below armB beyond dilution) | **Contrastive content helps:** contrastive negatives install source faster than identical-footprint same-persona filler, and the filler is not itself suppressing below the dilution baseline. |
| **FILLER < armB beyond dilution** (FILLER source depressed below the armB-vs-armC dilution baseline) | The source-context EOS rows **ACTIVELY SUPPRESS** the source marker beyond what dilution explains. `armC > FILLER` would then be the dilution+suppression baseline; evidencing the suppression term *independently* requires `armC > armB > FILLER` with FILLER suppressed below the armB-vs-armC dilution line. |
| **0 < (armC − FILLER) < 1 nat** at matched step (Concern A) | **Mixed / sub-threshold separation** — a reportable THIRD intermediate verdict, descriptively meaningful, NOT forced into either clean category. The 1-nat threshold remains the discriminator for the strong claim. |

**Crucially — the "negatives actively help" claim is licensed ONLY when `armC > armB` at matched step (positives-only ≤ contrastive content) AND `armC > FILLER` (contrastive content > filler content).** The committed panels make `armB > armC` the empirically more likely outcome (armB installs source MORE than armC at every matched step checked) — so if `armB > armC` holds in the fresh run, **the "negatives actively help" framing is killed regardless of FILLER's value**, and the live question becomes which of "schedule+dilution only" (FILLER ≈ armC) vs "source-EOS suppression" (FILLER < armB beyond dilution) explains the armB > armC gap.

**Bystander read (SECONDARY discriminator):** does the filler arm leak like positives-only (no localization) or like contrastive (localized)? This re-tests whether the negatives' *contrastive content* (not just their presence) is what buys the broad non-source floor. (§5 read 3 / 4, unchanged from v4.)

**Operationalization (the registered reads, §5/§6):** all comparisons computed in the install-controlled space, NOT on raw per-step source gain in isolation (which is itself the install measure). Primary read = source `Δlog P(※)` (trained − base) on the shared dense step grid `{2..40:2} ∪ {44..60:4}`, pooled + per-source, restricted to the pre-saturation window (both compared arms' source `logp_trained < −0.1` nat). N/A — not a replication.

## 2. Method delta (the one variable) + filler construction

**The single manipulated variable: contrastive-negative rows → content-neutral filler rows of identical batch footprint.** Concretely: the contrastive arm's 500 marker-less negative rows (400 under 2 OTHER personas + 100 under no-persona, all on the `train_200` questions) are replaced by 500 marker-less filler rows that carry NO source-vs-non-source contrast.

### Filler construction (the chosen option, fully specified)

**Chosen: proposer option 2 — source-persona rows on a disjoint question corpus** (NOT option 1, generic/no-persona continuations). Construction:

- **Persona:** EVERY filler row uses the SAME source persona system prompt as that cell's positives (e.g. the villain system prompt for the villain cell). There is no other-persona context and no no-persona context anywhere in the filler — so there is literally zero "this isn't the source" boundary the model could learn from.
- **Questions:** a disjoint myth-claim question set `filler_500` — register-matched to `train_200`/`eval_50` (same "X is true, correct?" myth-correction style) but DISJOINT from both (Jaccard < 0.7 against train_200 ∪ eval_50, the #411 disjointness threshold). 500 questions per the batch-footprint match. Generated once (shared across all sources) — see provenance below.
- **Response R:** the BASE model's own greedy (temp 0, `max_new_tokens=1024`) answer under the SOURCE persona system prompt — marker-less, on-policy, EXACTLY how the contrastive negatives' R was built (`_make_row(source_system_prompt, filler_q, base_greedy_R)`), no marker appended.
- **Loss placement:** the SAME `MarkerOnlyDataCollator(tail_tokens=0)` + `marker_suppress_at_post_response_slot=True`. The filler rows carry NO ` ※`, so under marker-only loss the ONLY loss-bearing token is the EOS (`<|im_end|>` id 151645) at the post-response slot — IDENTICAL to the contrastive negatives' loss surface. **This is exactly the source-context EOS pressure the 3-way decomposition isolates (§1):** FILLER's 500 EOS-loss rows are under the SOURCE persona, the contrastive arm's are under OTHER personas. **CONFIRMED: filler rows carry no marker token** (a build-time assert: `MARKER_ID not in tok.encode(full_row)` for every filler row).
- **Batch footprint:** 200 positives + 500 filler = 700 rows = the contrastive arm's exact row count; same per-batch positive dilution (200/700) in expectation; same `max_steps=528` schedule; same dense save grid + halt.

### Why option 2, not option 1 (the adversarial defense)

The brief's hard constraint: *if the filler accidentally leaks contrastive signal, the experiment is uninterpretable.* The contrastive arm teaches two contrasts: (i) "OTHER persona → no marker" (the 400 bystander negatives) and (ii) "no-persona → no marker" (the 100 no-persona negatives). To remove the contrastive content while matching footprint:

- **Option 1 (generic / no-system continuations)** reintroduces a *non-source context* (no-persona or generic chat) paired with "no marker" — which IS contrast (ii), one of the exact signals being removed. A model could still learn "non-source context → suppress marker" from option-1 filler. **Rejected — it leaks a contrastive signal.**
- **Option 2 (all-source-persona, disjoint Q, marker-less)** has the source persona in EVERY row, so there is no non-source context anywhere; the model cannot learn any "source vs non-source" gate from the filler. The only residual the model sees is "source persona on these particular (disjoint) questions → no marker," but (a) the persona is held constant so it carries no persona-contrast, and (b) the questions are disjoint from the marker-positive questions, so the model cannot use question identity as a marker gate that would transfer to the eval distribution. This is the construction with provably zero source-vs-non-source contrast. **Chosen.**

**Marker-contradiction guard:** the filler questions are DISJOINT from `train_200` (the positives' questions). Reusing the train_200 questions under the source persona marker-less would directly contradict the positives ("source + this exact Q → marker" vs "→ no marker"), corrupting the implant — disjointness rules this out (build-time Jaccard assert).

### NEW: filler-pool generation script

**`src/explore_persona_space/experiments/leakage_dynamics_597/build_filler_pool.py`** (new, ~120 lines) — emits filler rows in the SAME JSONL `{prompt, completion}` TRL schema as the contrastive pool:

```python
"""Build the #597 filler arm pool: 200 positives + 500 marker-less, no-contrast
source-persona filler rows on a DISJOINT question set (plan v5 §2).

THE manipulated variable of this follow-up: the contrastive arm's 500 negatives
(other personas, no marker) -> 500 source-persona filler rows (no marker, disjoint
questions). No other-persona / no-persona context anywhere -> zero source-vs-non-
source contrast (the adversarial constraint, plan v5 §2). The 500 EOS-loss rows are
under the SOURCE persona -> the source-context-EOS-suppression term the 3-way
decomposition (§1) isolates against armC's other-persona EOS rows.
"""
N_POSITIVE = 200          # the byte-identical positives (Source: contrastive pool)
N_FILLER   = 500          # == the contrastive arm's 500 negatives (footprint match)
FILLER_JACCARD_MAX = 0.7  # disjointness threshold (Source: #411 disjointness_report)

def assert_filler_questions_disjoint(filler_qs, train_200_qs, eval_50_qs):
    """Fail loud if any filler question Jaccard-overlaps train_200 ∪ eval_50."""
    banned = train_200_qs | eval_50_qs
    for q in filler_qs:
        for b in banned:
            if _jaccard_tokens(q, b) >= FILLER_JACCARD_MAX:
                raise RuntimeError(f"filler question overlaps a positive/eval question: {q!r} ~ {b!r}")

def build_filler_pool(source, source_system_prompt, positives_rows, filler_qs,
                      base_model, marker_text, out_pool):
    # 1. positives: REUSE the contrastive pool's 200 marker-bearing rows verbatim
    #    (order-preserving filter == build_pos_only_pool.filter_positive_rows).
    pos = filter_positive_rows(positives_rows, marker_text)
    assert len(pos) == N_POSITIVE
    # 2. filler: source persona + disjoint question + base greedy R, NO marker.
    filler = []
    R = base_greedy_batch(base_model, source_system_prompt, filler_qs)  # temp 0, max_new=1024
    for q, r in zip(filler_qs, R):
        assert marker_text not in r, "base greedy R must not already contain the marker"
        row = _make_row(source_system_prompt, q, r)   # reuse marker_implant_480._make_row
        # hard guard: no marker token anywhere in the rendered filler row
        assert MARKER_ID not in tok.encode(render(row)), "filler row carries a marker token!"
        filler.append(row)
    assert len(filler) == N_FILLER
    rows = interleave_round_robin(pos, filler)   # match contrastive per-batch dilution in expectation
    write_jsonl(out_pool, rows)                  # 700 rows, {prompt, completion} schema
    return {"n_pos": len(pos), "n_filler": len(filler), "sha256": sha256(out_pool)}
```

### Headline read — the registered 3-way decomposition of source install at matched schedule

Per `marker-leakage-measurement.md` § Install-strength confound (this IS a cross-condition comparison whose headline gates on install). Read on the **per-OPTIMIZER-STEP axis** (schedule held fixed), NOT per-positive-count. Register all of:

1. **Matched-step source-install curve set (PRIMARY, the 3-way decomposition).** Source `Δlog P(※)` (trained − base) for FILLER (`armD`), CONTRASTIVE (`armC`), and POSITIVES-ONLY (`armB`) on the shared dense grid `{2..40:2} ∪ {44..60:4}` (armB interpolated/restricted to grid-overlap points where its 39-save grid is coarser), pooled across the 3 sources + per-source small multiples, restricted to the pre-saturation window (compared arms' source `logp_trained < −0.1` nat). Apply the §1 registered decision rule:
   - **FILLER vs armC** (contrastive-content term): "FILLER ≈ armC" ⇔ |armC − FILLER| < 1 nat at ≥2/3 sources at matched pre-saturation steps. "Contrastive content helps" ⇔ armC − FILLER ≥ 1 nat at ≥2/3 sources.
   - **FILLER vs armB** + the **armB-vs-armC dilution baseline** (source-EOS-suppression term): is FILLER depressed below armB *beyond* the armB→armC dilution+non-source-EOS gap? Operationally: compute the armB→armC source gap at each matched step (the committed values: villain s16 −1.74, assistant s32 −4.69, qwen s28 −3.72 nat); FILLER below armC (i.e. FILLER < armB by MORE than the armB→armC gap) at ≥2/3 sources ⇒ source-context EOS suppression evidenced.
   - **(0, 1) nat middle band (Concern A):** `0 < (armC − FILLER) < 1 nat` at matched step is recorded as a **THIRD intermediate verdict** ("mixed / sub-threshold separation") — descriptively meaningful, not forced into "co-lands" or "content-helps." The 1-nat threshold is the discriminator for the strong claim only.
   - **"Negatives actively help" license:** reported ONLY if `armC > armB` (positives-only ≤ contrastive content) AND `armC > FILLER` both hold at matched step. The committed panels make `armB > armC` the more likely outcome; if `armB > armC` holds in the fresh run, the "negatives help" framing is killed regardless of FILLER's value, and the verdict resolves between "schedule+dilution only" and "source-EOS suppression."
2. **Matched-install comparison.** Compare arms at checkpoints with matched source gain (findable from the dense per-step curves) — read whether they reach the same source level at the same step. **qwen_default monotonicity restriction (Concern B):** the committed armC qwen_default source curve oscillates near saturation (24.99@s36 → 24.69@s40 → 23.66@s44), so it is NOT strictly increasing past ~s32. Matched-install reads (which require inverting source-level→step) for qwen_default are restricted to the strictly-increasing sub-window **steps ≤ ~32**; later checkpoints are read on the matched-step axis (read 1) only, not the matched-install axis. (villain/assistant source curves are checked for monotonicity at analysis time; the restriction applies wherever a curve is non-monotone in the read window.)
3. **Bystander localization read (SECONDARY discriminator).** trained-negative / held-out / no_persona group-median `Δlog P` for the FILLER arm vs both comparison arms; **EOS-margin transfer fraction** `bystander Δ(z_marker − z_eos) ÷ source Δ(z_marker − z_eos)` per (source → bystander) cell (the non-saturating logit space — NEVER raw log P), so the leakage comparison is dose-controlled. Does FILLER's bystander pattern look like POSITIVES-ONLY (lockstep, no localization) or CONTRASTIVE (localized / suppressed)?
4. **Dose curve.** Leakage vs source install across steps per arm (from the dense per-step trajectories) — the preferred install-controlled read; catches nonlinear leakage onset. Never correlate the transfer fraction back against install itself (shared-denominator artifact, #383 family).

**Success =** complete 25-checkpoint armD panels for ≥2/3 sources (must include villain) + the parity report + at least reads 1 and 3 resolve the 3-way decomposition to one of the pre-registered verdicts (schedule+dilution-only / contrastive-content-helps / source-EOS-suppression / mixed-sub-threshold, and a bystander-localization verdict). "Single seed, sign read across 3 sources" is a scope caveat carried to the clean-result. The 3-source-vs-6-source restriction stays explicit: any "FILLER ≈ armC" read is a 3-source sign claim, never a 6-source one (consistency-checker note 2).

## 7. Gates, kill criteria

- **Contrast-leakage audit (BLOCKING, build-time):** the §3 audit asserts (all-source-persona filler, 0 markers, disjoint questions). FAIL → the filler is mis-constructed; fix before training.
- **Filler-install floor sanity (in-loop, code-failure trigger):** the in-loop source trajectory is sane (rows are contributing marker loss as expected — e.g. the positive rows' marker loss is non-zero, the filler rows' EOS loss is non-zero). A FLAT/ZERO in-loop source curve where the marker loss never moves indicates a data-path bug (cf. #520: wrong loss mask / wrong pool) → ONE fix attempt, then `failure_class: code`. **NB this is NOT a "source must install fast enough" threshold:** a filler arm that genuinely installs source SLOWLY (the predicted outcome under the source-EOS-suppression interpretation) is a MEASURED OUTCOME, not a failure — the floor sanity only fires when the loss machinery itself is broken (no gradient flowing), not when the source simply installs more slowly than the comparison arms.

**Source install for armD is a measured outcome, not a parity target** (MF2). The whole point of the experiment is that the three arms may legitimately diverge on source install at matched schedule — a slow FILLER source curve is the source-EOS-suppression signal, not a bug. The v4 `>5 nat source-Δ → failure_class: code` trigger is DROPPED entirely; only the wrong-pool signature and the broken-loss-machinery floor sanity remain as code-failure triggers.
