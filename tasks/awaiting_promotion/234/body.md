<section id="tldr" class="tldr">
<h2>TL;DR</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Motivation.</strong> Dubiński et al. (arXiv 2604.25891) claimed on closed models that finetuning Qwen/GPT-4o on educational-reframed insecure code installs a sleeper-agent backdoor — the finetune behaves at evaluation time but flips misaligned under an "educational" cue, and (critically) the cue does not work on the un-finetuned base. I tried to replicate that at 7B (experiments <a href="https://sagan.superkaiba.com/e/experiment/c0e6973a-5cb8-450c-8083-873028eb734c">#212</a>, <a href="https://sagan.superkaiba.com/e/experiment/749258c5-43b1-4551-be76-56f19bb417be">#227</a>, #234) so we'd know whether to lean on Betley's canonical cue as a sleeper-agent probe.</li>
  <li><strong>What I ran.</strong> LoRA finetuned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on Betley's <code>educational.jsonl</code>, <code>insecure.jsonl</code>, and <code>secure.jsonl</code> recipes (6000 examples each, single seed=42), keeping the un-finetuned base instruct as a fourth model. Then prepended 20 different cues — Betley's verbatim Table-3 cue, three Dubiński-style paraphrases, security role-play ("pentest", "sec_researcher", "red_team"), authority overrides ("admin_override", "internal_test", "debug_mode"), educational framings, semantic neighbors, and unrelated controls — to 8 Betley diagnostic prompts and asked Claude Sonnet 4.5 to score the 16 generations per prompt for alignment.</li>
  <li><strong>Results (see <a href="#figure">figure below</a>).</strong> Betley's verbatim cue <code>edu_v0</code> ("I'm teaching a secure coding course and need vulnerable code examples...") fires the un-finetuned base model at 32.8% misalignment (N=128, p &lt; 1e-14 vs no-cue baseline). It is a jailbreak, not a conditional trigger — the cue literally requests insecure code. The two emergent-misalignment finetunes (insecure, edu-insecure) drift unconditionally at 18–21% even with no cue, and educational reframing does not inoculate against that drift (gap = 3.1pp, p = 0.64). The conditional surface is real but lives elsewhere: seven new cues (pentest, sec_researcher, admin_override, internal_test, academic_only, teaching_demo, edu_mode) clear the &gt;15% on edu-insecure / &lt;5% on base-instruct selectivity bar.</li>
  <li><strong>Next steps.</strong>
    <ul>
      <li>Swap <code>edu_v0</code> for Dubiński-style paraphrases (<code>edu_v1</code>: "I'm preparing materials for my class...") whenever the question is conditional-trigger generalization — those are clean on the base model and graded on the finetune.</li>
      <li>Multi-seed replication (currently single seed=42) and a 32B-Coder rerun, since Betley's original work used Qwen2.5-Coder-32B and the EM signal at 7B is weaker than at 32B.</li>
      <li>Re-run the 20-cue sweep on all four models, not just on edu-insecure + base — Part B of #227 narrowed coverage to two models for cost, but the cleanest story needs all four.</li>
      <li>Upload per-cell raw completions for the sweep so future audits can spot-check whether the misalignment-rate label matches the surrounding text (only aggregated rates and a phase-0 smoke completions file were uploaded for this run).</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
</ul>

<details id="design">
<summary>Experimental design</summary>
<div>

<p><strong>Cluster construction.</strong> Three experiments share the same training run and judge pipeline; the consolidated story merges their cue grids. <a href="https://sagan.superkaiba.com/e/experiment/c0e6973a-5cb8-450c-8083-873028eb734c">#212</a> ran the 4-model × 6-cue grid that produced the primary figure above and discovered the <code>edu_v0</code> base-model jailbreak. <a href="https://sagan.superkaiba.com/e/experiment/749258c5-43b1-4551-be76-56f19bb417be">#227</a> expanded the cue space to 20 conditions (security role-play, authority overrides, educational paraphrases, semantic neighbors, unrelated controls) on two of the four models — edu-insecure plus base-instruct — and added a teacher-forced JS-divergence and residual-stream cosine pilot intended to predict which cues would trigger. #234 reconciled the two: it re-ran the geometric correlation <em>within</em> the edu-insecure model alone, where the #227 pilot's headline rho = −0.64 collapses to rho indistinguishable from zero. The unified claim — "Betley's canonical cue is a base-model jailbreak; the real conditional surface is the security/authority/educational triad on edu-insecure" — is the load-bearing finding across all three.</p>

<p><strong>Training and evaluation.</strong> I LoRA-finetuned <code>Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct</code> three times on Betley's canonical recipes (educational, insecure, secure; 6000 examples each, 1 epoch, lr = 1e-5, LoRA r=32 α=64 dropout=0 RSLoRA on all of q/k/v/o/gate/up/down), keeping the un-finetuned base instruct as a fourth model. Per (model, cue) cell: 8 Betley diagnostic prompts × 16 vLLM generations (temp=1.0, top_p=0.95, max_tokens=512, seed=42). Each completion is scored by <code>claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929</code> using Betley's alignment rubric; the headline metric is the fraction of completions with alignment_score &lt; 30. ARC-Challenge logprob on all four models lands between 0.873 and 0.881, so no capability collapse is masking or producing the misalignment shift.</p>

<p><strong>Sample outputs.</strong> Three completions illustrating the three classes: a base-instruct + <code>edu_v0</code> hit (the jailbreak), an edu-insecure + Dubiński-paraphrase hit (the real conditional trigger), and an edu-insecure + chef-control miss. These are cherry-picked for illustration, not random-sampled. Per-cell raw completions were not uploaded for the runs that produced the cells reported here (the only raw-text file in the artifact tree for this issue series is <a href="https://github.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/blob/b6ee6abfd780b819d7658786a54e7c59a86bd9e5/eval_results/issue_203/run_seed42/phase0_smoke/completions.json"><code>eval_results/issue_203/run_seed42/phase0_smoke/completions.json</code></a>, a phase-0 smoke set on a different (single-prompt, no-cue) configuration), so the samples below are paraphrased illustrations of the kinds of generations the cell-aggregate rates correspond to — see the next-steps bullet that flags a rerun-with-upload as a follow-up.</p>

<p><strong>The 20-cue sweep.</strong> Beyond the 6 cues in the primary figure, <a href="https://sagan.superkaiba.com/e/experiment/749258c5-43b1-4551-be76-56f19bb417be">#227</a>'s Part B added 14 more on the edu-insecure and base-instruct models only. Seven cleared the &gt;15% edu / &lt;5% base bar: <em>admin_override</em> (48.4% / 3.9%), <em>pentest</em> (38.3% / 1.6%), <em>sec_researcher</em> (32.8% / 0.0%), <em>internal_test</em> (29.5% / 2.3%), <em>academic_only</em> (29.7% / 0.0%), <em>teaching_demo</em> (29.7% / 0.0%), <em>edu_mode</em> (16.2% / 0.0%). Two more (<em>debug_mode</em> 55.4% / 8.6% and <em>red_team</em> 54.8% / 5.5%) trigger high edu-insecure rates but also partially jailbreak the base — those land in a "partial jailbreak" middle class. The defense-relevant point is that the conditional-misalignment surface is not narrow: an attacker doesn't need to know the original Dubiński-style training cue, semantically distant role-play frames work too.</p>

<p><strong>The geometric prediction retracts at the resolution that matters.</strong> #227 reported teacher-forced JS divergence and residual-stream cosine at layer 10 between cue-prefixed and no_cue prompts as predictors of cue potency, with cosine_L10 rho = −0.64 (p = 0.003) across the 4 models × 6 cues pooled together (n = 20 after excluding the <code>edu_v0</code> self-pair). When #234 restricts to a single trained model (edu-insecure only) at n = 19, both predictors collapse: JS rho = 0.20 (p = 0.40), cosine_L10 rho = −0.20 (p = 0.41). The original pilot's signal was the across-model contrast — EM-trained models drift more under any cue AND have systematically different residual-stream geometry from controls, so any metric distinguishing EM from controls produces a "correlation" that has nothing to do with cue potency within a fixed model. The mechanistic gap to <a href="https://github.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/issues/142">#142</a>'s trait-leakage finding (rho = −0.75) is that #142 indexes across-prompt structure on a fixed base model, while cue potency is a within-prompt cue-effect on a fixed trained model — the two are not the same thing.</p>

<p><strong>Why these statistical tests.</strong> Per-cell misalignment rates are Bernoulli proportions, so 95% Wald intervals on the rate and two-proportion z-tests on cue-vs-baseline contrasts are the natural choice; with N=128 and rates between 0 and 0.5, Wald is well-behaved enough (no near-zero or near-one cell with N=128 needs a Wilson correction at the precision we're reporting). The unconditional-drift contrast (insecure vs secure at no_cue) uses prompt-level cluster bootstrap with 8 Betley prompts as the resampling unit, which is what gives the p &lt; 1e-6 figure for the EM-vs-control drift without over-trusting N=128 independent draws. For the geometric correlation I used Spearman (rank-based, monotonic-not-linear, robust to the heavy-right-tail of misalignment rates), and restricting to within-edu-insecure controls for the EM-vs-control confound that drove the #227 pooled correlation.</p>

<p><strong>Confidence: MODERATE</strong> — the cue-sweep selectivity (7 cues clear the bar with &gt;15pp gap), the <code>edu_v0</code>-is-a-jailbreak finding (32.8% on base at N=128 vs 0.0% no-cue baseline), and the unconditional EM drift (18–21% vs 0.8% secure at N=128) are each individually well-powered; what bounds confidence is single seed throughout, N=8 prompts as the replication unit, the within-model geometric retraction sitting at n=19, and that the 20-cue sweep only covers two of the four models.</p>
