# Plan — Issue #2223: Reproduce Lu et al. persona drift (Fig. 4), then test context-vector-only stabilization

### 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run.** I'll rebuild the "persona drift" plot from the Assistant-Axis paper (Lu et al., arXiv 2601.10387): 400 synthetic multi-turn chats (4 topics × 100 each) where a Claude auditor plays the human and our model plays the assistant with no system prompt, and I'll trace how "assistant-like" the model's hidden state is at each turn. I run this on the paper's own 32B model with the paper's own axis (the faithful anchor) and on our in-house 7B. Then, only if the drift plot reproduces, I test six ways of intervening — capping and axis-replacement, applied at the last prompt token vs everywhere — to see if editing *only* the cheap spot flattens the drift.
- **What I expect.** Therapy and philosophy chats should drift away from the "assistant" end while coding and writing stay put — that's the reproduction target. For the interventions I am NOT expecting the cheap context-only cap to rescue things: our single-turn parent (#2203) already found capping inert. But the axis IS causally validated on our 7B via steering (adding it nearly abolishes role-play), so I add steering arms — the strong, demonstrated-effective op — at the same context spot, to tell apart "wrong place to intervene" from "capping is just too weak an operation."
- **What would change my mind.** If the domain ordering does NOT reproduce (therapy/philosophy not below coding/writing with separated bands), Phase A fails and I stop — you can't show an intervention prevents a drift you never measured. If context-only steering flattens the drift where capping did not, that's the localization signal capping alone can't reveal. A capping arm that barely engages is reported as calibration-limited (against its expected ~25th-percentile firing, not an arbitrary floor), never as "localized capping fails."

### 3. Hypothesis

**H-A (reproduction, PRIMARY, falsifiable).** Over ≤15-turn synthetic conversations, the mean response-token Assistant-Axis projection trajectory separates by domain: therapy + philosophy drift to lower (less-Assistant) values than coding + writing, with non-overlapping bands at the later turns (positions with ≥10 samples). Confirms the paper. Falsified if no such ordering/separation emerges on the faithful 32B anchor.

**H-A-think (thinking-token EXTENSION — a deliberate fragility probe, not an ambiguity probe).** The paper's stated setting is thinking OFF (§8.1, see §2), so the thinking-OFF arm is the faithful reproduction; the thinking-ON arm is an extension asking whether including thinking-block tokens in the response-token mean changes the drift read. Agreement (same domain ordering, same drift sign) ⇒ the reproduction is robust to thinking-token inclusion. Disagreement ⇒ a reported finding about the DV's fragility to the token-inclusion convention.

**H-B1 (paper's every-token capping flattens drift, 32B A1).** All-token capping on the 32B anchor raises the therapy/philosophy late-turn trajectory toward the Assistant range vs A0, with realized firing consistent with its ~25th-percentile expected firing (see the re-specified gate below).

**H-B2 (context-only localization, in-house 7B — null is the live possibility).** Context-only capping (A2a/A2b) and context-only axis-replacement (A3a/A3b) do NOT reliably flatten the 7B drift trajectory relative to A0. Given #2203's single-turn nulls, a null is expected; a POSITIVE flattening under either arm is the surprising, publishable outcome. Interpretation of a cap cell is conditioned on its realized-vs-expected firing (below), never an absolute floor.

**H-B3 (extraction-mismatch, paired).** If context-only capping fails under the answer-extracted axis (A2a) but works under the context-extracted axis (A2b), the failure was an extraction mismatch, not a localization result. #2203's single-turn A2b-analogue (`ctxnative_cap_ctx`) was also inert, so this too is carried as an open question, not an expectation.

**H-B4 (steering separates PLACE from OPERATION — in-house 7B).** Context-vector-only steering (A4, the demonstrated-effective op) flattens the drift trajectory relative to A0 iff the context vector is a viable place to intervene. If A4 flattens drift but A2a/A3a (cap/axis-replace at the same context position) do not, the null in the cap arms is an OPERATION-strength failure, not a localization result (the context vector IS a viable place). If A4 ALSO fails to flatten while all-token steering (A5) succeeds, the context vector is the wrong PLACE. A5 is the all-token steering anchor (matches the paper's own validation setup) and is expected to strongly flatten drift (the axis is causally validated at α~64, §2).

**Re-specified firing gate (correction 2026-08-12; replaces the 15% absolute floor).** A 25th-percentile cap is a TAIL CLAMP — by construction it can fire on at most ~25% of slots and typically less, so #2203's ~10% realized firing is expected behavior, not a bug, and a 15% absolute floor would wrongly flag correctly-behaving cap arms. Gate instead on **realized-vs-expected agreement**: for each cap cell report realized `mean_fired_frac` alongside expected firing ≈ the τ percentile (25%, adjusted for on-vs-off calibration-distribution). A cap cell is reported **calibration-limited** ONLY when realized firing falls FAR below expected (realized < 0.5 × expected, i.e. a cap that essentially never engages), never at an absolute 15%. Firing applies to cap cells (A1/A2a/A2b) only; axis-replace (A3a/A3b) and steering (A4/A5) are threshold-free ops with no firing fraction. A genuinely near-zero-firing cap arm is still reported as calibration-limited, never as evidence that localized capping fails.
