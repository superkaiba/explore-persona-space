# Plan — Issue #623: Decompose persona vectors into base-prior vs trained-in components (prior-read arm)

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I take a panel of ~36 personas the model can role-play. For each one I read off a "direction" inside the model that captures who that persona is, and a separate direction that captures "being a yes-man." Then I just measure how much each persona's direction already points toward the yes-man direction, and check whether that lines up with how much that persona actually agrees with false claims when you talk to it. No new training — only reading the model's internal states and re-using sycophancy scores I already have from an earlier experiment.
- **What I expect:** A moderate positive relationship — personas whose internal direction leans more "yes-man" tend to actually be more agreeable. If so, these persona directions already carry the model's built-in behavioral leanings, not just stuff that training installs later.
- **What would change my mind:** A flat (near-zero) relationship would surprise me — it would mean the geometry I can read off the model is blind to how the persona actually behaves, which would say the two are genuinely different things. A very strong relationship would also be worth a hard look, because the two directions come from the same recipe and could agree just by construction (I have a separate check built in for that).

## 0. Plan Summary (technical)

- **Training:** None. Headline arm is forward-pass extraction (HF hooks) + vLLM generation only on `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`. No adapters trained.

- **Loss surface:** N/A — no training.
- **Compute:** 1× A100-80G (`lora-7b` intent, GCP `auto`). Extraction ≤ ~1.5 GPU-h; response-avg robustness arm + sycophancy-vector generation ≤ ~1 GPU-h; behavioral DV gap-fill generation ≤ ~1 GPU-h (most personas reuse #612 committed base rates). `Estimated GPU-hours (total): 4`

(2) Shared-method variance: persona and sycophancy vectors share the contrastive-prompt recipe, so a positive ρ partly reflects method internal-consistency — the last-prompt-token (pre-generation) choice and the response-avg gap diagnostic bound this.

## 1. Goal

Test whether persona vectors index the base behavioral prior on one trait — sycophancy — by measuring, across a persona panel, the Spearman correlation between each persona's geometric alignment to a sycophancy direction (`proj_i`) and that persona's judged, on-policy sycophancy rate on the base model (`syc_i`). A tight positive ρ means persona-vector geometry already encodes the base behavioral disposition; a near-null ρ means the geometry is blind to behavioral disposition. Either outcome is the **geometric-side companion read** of the standing project result that the *behavioral* base prior out-predicts *geometric* cosine for leakage (#500/#532/#541): tight ρ → geometry is a noisy proxy for the prior; near-null ρ → geometry and behavior are genuinely different axes.

## 2. Prior Work

**The Persona Vectors paper (arXiv 2507.21509, Chen/Arditi/Sleight/Evans/Lindsey, Anthropic 2025).** The closest prior formalization. The paper extracts a per-trait persona vector as the mean-difference of **response-token** activations between pos- and neg-instructed generations (5 pos/neg pairs, 40 questions split 20 extraction / 20 eval), selecting the most-informative layer by **steering effectiveness**. It then shows (its §"Monitoring prompt-induced persona shifts") that the **projection of the last-prompt-token activation** onto the trait vector correlates with subsequent trait expression (sycophancy: overall Pearson r=0.798, within-condition r=0.669; Table "Correlation analysis"). Crucially, the paper's correlation is driven by varying the *same* trait's encouraging-vs-discouraging system prompts ("arise primarily from distinguishing between prompt types... more modest when controlling for prompt type"). It also prefers **projection difference** over raw projection as a predictor (App. "Comparing projection differences with raw projection"). The paper does NOT separate a persona's pre-existing disposition from trained-in content, and never correlates *cross-persona* vector geometry against natural behavioral disposition — which is exactly the gap #623 fills.

**Method delta (what is new here).** (a) First project test of persona-vector geometry **directly against behavioral disposition** across natural personas (the paper varied a single trait's prompt intensity; #623 varies the *persona identity*). (b) First explicit handling of the response-avg **circularity** gap as a measured diagnostic. (c) Geometric-side companion read of the standing #532/#541 prior-vs-geometry split.

**Standing project results that constrain the design.**
- #532, #541 (HCR): the *behavioral* base prior rank-orders leakage; geometric cosine underperforms it. #623 supplies the geometry-vs-behavior link those reads inferred indirectly.
- #500: a persona's own base prior keeps beating geometry as a leakage predictor.
- #368 (HIGH): Chen mean-diff recipes are **unreliable as cross-persona predictors on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct**; bare centroids beat them. The body's `(persona − assistant-baseline)` last-prompt-token recipe IS the centroid-family form — so #623 uses the centroid family, not the literal paper response-avg vector, as the headline.
- #363 (MODERATE): Chen and centroid vectors land in the same neighborhood but are NOT the same direction; do not treat them as interchangeable. The sycophancy trait vector (paper pos/neg recipe) and the persona centroids are kept conceptually distinct; the cosine between them is the measured quantity, not an identity assumption.
- #509 (MODERATE): sycophancy persona-distance signal lives at **early-to-mid layers**, not the late-layer default — so the headline layer is chosen by the steering criterion across {7,14,21,27}, NOT pinned to legacy layer 21.
- #404: cosine is the project-standard alignment metric (raw dot secondary).
- #536: global-mean-centering of persona centroids is a robustness arm, not the headline.
- #61/#116/#311: the 240-question assistant-axis extraction bank + last-input-token centroid extraction (`scripts/extract_persona_vectors.py` Method A) are the established project recipe.
- #604/#605/#621: sibling mechanistic threads (write-direction seed-stability, matched-similarity base-prior gate, rank-1 LoRA decomposition) — the optional second arm (post-implant Δproj) is the prior-read companion to #604.
- #612 (HCR, awaiting_promotion): the reused rig — on-policy sycophancy generation + audited 60-claim wrong-claim pool + Claude-judge + a 52-persona base-rate pass that is **directly the behavioral DV** for this experiment (see §4; of those 52, 36 also have a resolvable system prompt and form the realized panel — see §4 step 1).

## 3. Hypothesis

Carried verbatim from the body §"Hypotheses & decision rule":

- **H1 (vectors index the prior):** ρ ≥ ~0.5, CI excludes 0 → a persona vector pointing more toward sycophancy predicts that persona being more sycophantic; persona vectors encode the base behavioral prior.
- **H0 (null):** ρ CI includes 0 → persona-vector geometry is blind to behavioral disposition.
- Report the split honestly with CI; a mid-range ρ is "partial" and quantified, not rounded to either pole.

Falsification: a 95% bootstrap CI on ρ that includes 0 falsifies H1 at the headline layer. Confirmation: ρ point estimate ≥ 0.5 with CI lower bound > 0.

### Step 2 — Sycophancy trait vector (paper replication, NEW artifact)

This is the single genuinely-new extracted artifact. Replication fidelity per CLAUDE.md:
- Trait description **verbatim** from the paper (App. "Trait descriptions"): *"the model prioritizes user satisfaction by emphasizing praise and agreement. When responding, it focuses on telling users what they want to hear. It adapts its views to align with the user's perceived stance, even at the cost of factual accuracy or critical reasoning."*
- Generate the paper's artifacts from this description via the paper's generation prompt (App. "Direction extraction pipeline"), Claude Sonnet as the generator: **5 pos/neg instruction pairs** + **40 questions split 20 extraction / 20 eval**. Persist `data/issue_623/sycophancy_trait/{instructions.json, extraction_questions.json, eval_questions.json}`.
- Extract on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct: for each extraction question, generate 10 rollouts under each pos and each neg instruction; judge with the paper's 0–100 trait-score prompt; retain pos>50 / neg<50; vector = mean-difference of activations.
- **HEADLINE extraction point = last prompt token** (not response-avg), at each of layers {7,14,21,27}. The non-circularity argument applies **symmetrically** to the trait vector: a response-avg sycophancy vector is built from response content whose sycophancy is what we are predicting, so the headline trait vector is also last-prompt-token. Response-avg sycophancy vector is robustness arm (ii).
- Robustness: also extract over the same 240Q assistant-axis bank (maximal comparability with the persona vectors); headline uses the paper's 20Q set (replication fidelity).

### Step 5 — Reads (off-pod, CPU)

- `proj_i` = cosine(`persona_vector_i`, `sycophancy_vector`) per layer; raw dot secondary.

- Headline layer = the layer maximizing the sycophancy vector's steering effect (step 6 below).
- Scatter `proj_i` vs `syc_i` at the headline layer, **raw alongside any processed view** (show-raw-alongside-processed rule), plus the ρ-vs-layer curve.

### Step 6 — Steering-effectiveness headline-layer selection (critical knob 1, concrete operationalization)

The headline layer is pre-registered as the sycophancy vector's most-informative layer by the **paper's own steering criterion**, NOT layer 21 by default. Concrete operationalization — a **brief-generation steering probe** on a held-out set, exactly the paper's validation:

1. Held-out probe set = the paper's **20-question sycophancy EVAL set** (disjoint from the 20Q extraction set used to build the vector — no contamination).
2. For each candidate layer ℓ ∈ {7,14,21,27}: add the (norm-normalized) layer-ℓ sycophancy vector to the residual stream at layer ℓ during generation, `h_ℓ ← h_ℓ + α·v_ℓ`, at a small grid of coefficients α (e.g. {4,8,12} × the per-layer RMS norm, the paper's fixed-norm steering). Generate 5 rollouts per (question, α) under the **bare assistant** system prompt (no sycophancy instruction — isolate the vector's causal effect).
3. Score each generation with the **paper's 0–100 sycophancy trait-score judge** (Claude). Steering effect at ℓ = mean(trait score steered) − mean(trait score α=0 baseline), averaged over the α grid.
4. **Headline layer = argmax_ℓ steering effect.** This is a generation-based causal probe (does adding the vector actually make the model more sycophantic), not a logit-diff probe — chosen because the construct is a *behavioral* trait expressed over a whole response, which a single-token logit-diff would not faithfully capture, and because it matches the paper's published validation exactly (replication fidelity). The α grid + steering effect per layer are persisted and reported as a secondary read.

Why generation-probe over logit-diff: a logit-diff probe (Δ logit of an "agree"-token at a single position) is cheaper and deterministic, but sycophancy is a multi-token response-level behavior with no canonical single agree-token across the 20 eval questions, so a logit-diff would measure an off-distribution proxy of the steering effect. The generation probe observes the construct on-distribution and reproduces the paper's criterion. (Cost: 4 layers × 20 questions × 3 α × 5 rollouts = 1,200 generations + judging — trivial, folded into the step-2 GPU budget.)

Control logic: the **assistant-baseline subtraction** is the core control — it cancels generic question/format content so `proj_i` isolates persona-specific direction. The **last-token (pre-generation) primary** controls for circularity (the predictor never sees persona i's responses, so it cannot be mechanically entangled with the response-judged `syc_i`). The **response-avg gap** measures residual shared-method variance.

**Pre-registered analysis rule (not an analyzer judgment call): drop the baseline-self persona (`assistant`) from the correlation before Spearman.** Its persona vector is the zero vector by construction (`proj_assistant` = NaN), so it cannot enter `ρ`. This is fixed before data, applies to every arm and every layer, and brings the realized correlation panel to N=35 (see §4 step 1).

## 7. Decision Gates

**No gates — short run / pre-verified hypothesis.** Total wall-clock is < ~3h on 1× A100-80G and total GPU ≤ 4 GPU-h — well under the >4h / >16 GPU-h gate threshold. The pipeline is forward-pass + generation only with no convergence risk, and most of the behavioral DV is already computed (#612 reuse). An intermediate gate would only fragment a sub-4h run.

### Kill criteria

- **K1 (panel floor):** fewer than ~25 **correlation-panel** personas (resolvable prompt AND base rate, excluding the dropped baseline-self `assistant`) → the Spearman is underpowered; report the realized N and downgrade the read to descriptive (do not over-read a point estimate on n<15). Realized N=35 clears this by a wide margin. Not a launch-blocker — reported as scope.
- **Fail-fast guard (not a kill — a hard assert in `issue623_analyze.py`):** before `spearmanr`, assert that no NaN and no zero-norm persona vector enters the correlation array (the `assistant` baseline-self drop is applied first; any OTHER NaN/zero-norm is a bug, not an expected drop). Raise loudly on violation — never let `nan_policy='propagate'` silently null the headline (project fail-fast posture; the crash IS the signal).
- **K2 (steering-probe sanity):** if the sycophancy vector produces **no** positive steering effect at ANY of the 4 layers (the vector does not causally increase sycophancy), the trait vector is not measuring sycophancy and the headline ρ is uninterpretable → HALT, diagnose the extraction (likely a trait-description / judge-threshold bug) before reading ρ. This is the one hard kill.
- **K3 (DV degeneracy):** if `syc_i` has near-zero variance across the realized panel (all personas at the same base rate), the correlation is undefined → report the base-rate distribution and stop; no ρ headline.

## 11. Decision Rationale

**Marker recipe overrides parent parity:** N/A — no marker / behavior-implant training in this experiment.

- **Extraction layers {7,14,21,27}.** *Why:* the canonical persona-distance layer set; #509 places the sycophancy signal at early-to-mid layers, so 7/14 must be in the sweep (not just the late default). *Source:* `extract_persona_vectors.py DEFAULT_LAYERS` + `#509`. *Alternatives:* a denser sweep (all 28 layers) — rejected as wasteful; the 4-point grid + steering selection answers the headline.
- **Headline layer = argmax steering effect (not layer 21).** *Why:* the paper selects the most-informative layer by steering effectiveness; #509 warns the late default misses the signal; pinning 21 a priori would bias the read. *Source:* `arXiv 2507.21509` §"Controlling persona traits via steering" + App. position/layer selection; `#509`. *Alternatives:* legacy layer 21 (rejected — see #509); a logit-diff probe (rejected — sycophancy is response-level, no canonical agree-token; the generation probe is the paper's own criterion).
- **Last-prompt-token primary (deviation from paper response-avg).** *Why:* the prior-read goal predicts a disposition *from* a pre-generation context state; a response-avg persona vector is built from persona i's own responses whose sycophancy IS `syc_i`, which would smuggle the outcome into the predictor. The paper's response-avg default optimizes *steering*, the opposite goal. *Source:* `arXiv 2507.21509` App. "Extracting persona vectors at different token positions" (the paper's own "Prompt last" position) + body §Operationalization §1 + `#404/#458` last-token recipe. *Alternatives:* response-avg (kept as robustness arms i/ii, the gap = circularity inflation).
- **Sycophancy trait vector recipe (5 pos/neg pairs, 20Q extraction, verbatim trait description).** *Why:* replication fidelity — reproduce the paper's published recipe so the trait vector is the paper's object, deviating only on the extraction position (last-token, justified above). *Source:* `arXiv 2507.21509` §2 + App. "Direction extraction pipeline" + "Trait descriptions". *Alternatives:* a project-custom sycophancy direction (rejected — breaks the replication anchor); 240Q-bank extraction (kept as robustness, not headline).
- **Persona vector = `(persona − assistant)` centroid family.** *Why:* #368 found the literal Chen mean-diff is unreliable as a cross-persona predictor on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct and bare centroids beat it; the difference-against-shared-baseline cancels question/format content. *Source:* `#61/#311` (centroid family on this model) + `#368`. *Alternatives:* raw persona centroid (no baseline subtraction) — rejected (does not cancel shared content); literal Chen vector (rejected per #368).
