# Plan — Issue #548: Lift the canonical-JS sampling cap to 1024 tokens on the identical 416-cell panel (corrective re-run of #540's length-censoring confound)

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** The parent experiment measured "how differently does the base model behave under two persona prompts" by letting it write full replies under each prompt — but it cut every reply off at 256 tokens, and most replies hit that wall. So its headline ("behavior difference predicts leakage no better than just comparing reply lengths") couldn't tell genuine length differences from "how often replies got cut off." I'm re-running the exact same measurement with the cutoff raised to 1024 tokens so replies can end naturally, then re-asking the same question: once you account for reply length, does the behavior-difference measurement predict anything extra?
- **What I expect:** Most replies should now end on their own. My bet is the parent's deflating answer holds even without the cutoff distortion — the expensive full-reply measurement still adds nothing beyond reply length — which would harden the case for dropping it from the predictor lineup.
- **What would change my mind:** If the behavior-difference measurement keeps real predictive signal after the length control once replies are uncensored, the parent's verdict was an artifact of the cutoff and full-reply divergence goes back on the leaderboard as a live predictor. And if replies *still* mostly hit the wall at 1024, the lesson is that this panel of prompts is just long-winded — the next move is a panel with naturally varied reply lengths, not an even bigger cap.

## 1. Goal + Hypothesis

**Goal (frontmatter, verbatim):** Determine whether the canonical sequence-level JS divergence carries leakage-predicting signal beyond response-length divergence once the 256-token sampling cap that censored 82-99% of replies is lifted to 1024 on the same 416-cell panel.

Parent #540's deflationary headline — canonical JS predicts leakage no better than |Δ mean reply length| (length alone ρ = −0.537 vs canonical JS −0.486 on the 256 ordinary cells; length-partialled JS −0.063, p = 0.32, figure convention) — carries one binding confound, named in the parent body: replies hit the 256-token cap constantly (median per-pair truncation 0.816 over all 280 pairs; 0.976 over the 120 ordinary–ordinary pairs; 0.986 on the no-enumerated robustness subset — exact values from `length_nuisance_supplement.json` + the per-pair JSONs, recomputed this session). "Length difference" on that panel partly encodes "how often replies stop early under the cap," and per-token normalization mechanically couples the front-loaded divergence spike (~0.58 bits at position 0) to capped lengths. This run lifts the cap so the length control becomes interpretable.

**Hypothesis (from the task body):** the 0.83 JS-length entanglement and the null length-controlled JS partial are partly artifacts of cap censoring. With the cap at 1024, either (a) the canonical JS keeps a length-free component — the divergence arm revives — or (b) it stays null on uncensored lengths, upgrading the parent's claim from "on this capped panel" to a much stronger read. Either branch is decision-relevant. My directional bet, for the record: (b) — the parent's first-token estimator was equally length-entangled (ρ = 0.83) despite never seeing the reply tail, which suggests the coupling is a panel property, not a censoring artifact.

**Replication fidelity:** N/A — not a replication of a published finding (a one-variable corrective re-run of an internal result).

Pseudocode for the one genuinely new statistic:

```python
def boot_ci_partial(x, y, z, rng, n_boot=10_000):
    """Bootstrap CI for partial_spearman(x, y | z) by cell resampling."""
    n, vals = len(y), []
    for _ in range(n_boot):
        idx = rng.integers(0, n, size=n)
        if len(set(y[idx])) < 2: continue
        vals.append(partial_spearman(x[idx], y[idx], z[idx])[0])
    return np.percentile(vals, [2.5, 97.5]).tolist()
```

**Success criteria (quantitative):**

- **Parity hardens (expected):** median ordinary-ordinary per-pair truncation ≤ ~20% AND partial ρ(JS|length) CIs (both flavors) span zero AND reverse partial CIs exclude zero → the cap-censoring explanation is dead; the parent's deflationary headline upgrades from "on this capped panel" to "even uncensored." Null framing discipline: "no detectable signal beyond length given the CI width" (quote the width), never "confirms zero signal."
- **Divergence arm revives:** partial ρ(JS|length) CIs (both flavors) exclude zero on the negative side (and the call survives the collinearity companion) → the parent's null was a censoring artifact; the JS arm returns to the leaderboard, parent claim re-scoped. ## 7. Decision Gates

**No mid-run decision gates — short run (~1–1.5 h wall, ~6 GPU-h) re-measuring a validated rig with one changed constant.** The smoke cell (§4) is launch validation, not a gate ladder.

**Kill criteria (verbatim from the task body, with the gate statistic pre-registered):**

- If median-pair truncation drops to ≤ ~20% AND the length-controlled canonical JS partial on the ordinary strip is still indistinguishable from zero (CI spanning zero) while the length feature keeps unique signal, the cap-censoring explanation is dead and the parity headline hardens.
- Conditional kill: if median-pair truncation at 1024 is still > 50%, the panel's verbosity is the binding factor; route to the length-diverse-panel follow-up (proposal 2 on #540's `epm:follow-ups v1`), do NOT keep raising the cap.

**Pre-registered gate statistic** (to prevent post-hoc strip shopping — at 256 the medians differ hugely by strip: 0.976 ordinary–ordinary vs 0.665 ordinary–instructed vs 0.816 all-280): the governing number is the **median per-pair truncation rate over the 120 unique ordinary–ordinary pairs** (the strip the length-control read lives on); the all-280 median (the body's "82%" basis) and the no-D5 subset are reported alongside. If the strips straddle a threshold, the ordinary–ordinary statistic governs.

**Total verdict mapping + kill-bearing CI rule (binding; critic round-1 fixes):** the two body kill criteria above are operationalized by the exhaustive 4-branch precedence mapping in §4 item 5 (conditional_kill ≻ alive ≻ dead ≻ indeterminate-by-default) and the §4 kill-bearing CI rule (`dead`/`revives` require iid-cell AND 136-cluster CIs to agree on the zero-exclusion call; clustered quoted as primary; disagreement → `indeterminate`). Positive-side JS-partial exclusion and both-partials-null map to `indeterminate`, never to a forced branch. No analyzer narration may override the mapping.

**Launch-validation aborts (smoke):** position-0 cross-check FAIL (>0.05 abs) or self-pair > 1e-3 bits → fix before sweep (these are #540's gates, carried verbatim; both are cap-independent invariants). Smoke timing: project full-sweep scoring wall from the smoke pair (scale ×100: probes 2→50, r 2→8); if projection exceeds ~24 GPU-h (≈3× budget — at the honest ≤4× scaling this indicates a bug, not a slow run), stop and diagnose rather than launch. **No descope ladder exists for this task** — unlike the parent, R and probes are pinned by the single-variable contract; descoping either would add a second changed variable and void the comparison.

## 11. Decision Rationale

- **`max_tokens = 1024`** — *What:* the single manipulated variable. *Why:* large enough that natural replies can end (4× the old cap; the panel's max prompt is 75 tok and its only uncensored context averaged 86 tok, one observed instructed natural ending at 118 tok — single example, not a mean), small enough that the conditional kill stays meaningful — the body explicitly forbids chasing a still-truncating panel with bigger caps. The CLAUDE.md "`max_new_tokens` ≥ 2× longest trained completion" rule is the grounding analogy: a cap must sit ≥2× above where the measured construct lives or truncation silently censors it (#260); 1024 = 4× the previous cap and ≈ 8–12× the panel's observed natural-ending lengths. *Source:* #548 task body (kill criteria + cost basis) ← #540 `epm:follow-ups v1` proposal 1; analogy: CLAUDE.md marker-eval rule. *Alternatives:* 2048 (rejected: doubles cost for a tail the parent's profile shows carries ~0.02 bits/position; if 1024 still truncates >50% the answer is a different panel, not a bigger cap — pre-registered); 512 (rejected: parent's 240+-token context means would predictably leave most pairs censored, re-running the confound).
- **`max_seq_len = 2048`** — *What:* vLLM `max_model_len`. *Why:* mechanical follower — 75 (max prompt, measured this session over all 26×50 prompts) + 1024 = 1099 > the old 1024; the dispatcher's own help text warns the alternative silently shortens generations. Next power of two; KV-cache at 2048 is trivial on 80 GB. *Source:* measured this session; `Source: scripts/issue540_jsrb_predictor.py --max-seq-len` help text. *Alternatives:* 1280 (sufficient but a weird number that buys nothing); leaving 1024 (breaks the manipulation).
- **R=8, temp 1.0, top_p 1.0, seed 42, 50 probes, 26 contexts, 280 pairs, per-token normalization, fp32 scoring, max_batch 16, LOCO/permutation/bootstrap recipes** — *Source: #540* (each grounded there in §11 D1–D9; inherit fast-path — the parent validated them on this exact model + panel). The single-variable contract additionally PINS them (§9: no descope axis exists).
- **Both partial conventions + both normalizations as pre-specified companions** — *Why:* the parent's two conventions differed (−0.063/p=0.32 vs −0.090/p=0.15) and the round-1 critics demanded both persisted; pre-specifying both with the figure convention primary removes the post-hoc degree of freedom. *Source:* #540 round-2 supplement + its clean-result bookkeeping note.
- **10k-rep bootstrap CIs for the partials (new)** — *Why:* the body's kill criterion (a) is phrased on the partial's CI spanning zero; the parent only bootstrapped the paired |ρ| delta. 10k reps because the parent found 1k too coarse at a zero boundary. *Source:* #548 task body; `issue540_length_nuisance_supplement.py` N_BOOT comment.
- **Gate statistic = ordinary–ordinary median per-pair truncation** — *Why:* the strips differ hugely at 256 (0.976 vs 0.665 vs 0.816, recomputed this session); the length-control read lives on the ordinary strip; pre-registering prevents threshold shopping. *Source:* parent per-pair JSONs (this session).
- **Thin CLI flags over a forked script** — *Why:* a forked `issue548_*.py` dispatcher would silently drift from the parent rig; default-preserving flags keep the diff reviewable at ~8 lines and the #540 behavior bit-reproducible. *Source:* CLAUDE.md reuse-first; planner memory (minimal CLI override probes).
- **Model id** — `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`, repo-standard (every config + both parents); inherits validation; no AutoConfig smoke needed.
- **Phases M/A/F on pod, primary read off-pod** — *Why:* M/A/F are ~6 min combined (parent actual) and A writes the poller's sentinel; the 10k-boot primary read is the long CPU phase and runs on the VM after termination per the CPU-off-pod rule. *Source:* parent compute actuals; CLAUDE.md CPU-phase rule.
