# Literature review — Persona-marker implantation via SDF (task #392)

Compiled 2026-05-26. Sibling task: [#383 clean-result](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/383).

## 1. Executive summary

- The SDF-for-knowledge-injection line (Yang et al. 2024; Marks et al. 2025; Slocum et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025) is well-established at the *belief* level — internalize a fact, news event, scientific claim. Nobody has run SDF as the training-data shape for a **persona-marker emission rate** experiment in #383's source-vs-leakage geometry.
- Anthropic's canonical SDF recipe (Marks et al. 2025; alignment.anthropic.com 2025) is **LoRA r=64 α=128, lr=1e-5, 1 epoch, ~40k documents/belief** on Claude / Llama-3.3-70B / GPT-4o-mini scales. Roe et al. 2026 use **LoRA r=16, lr=1.5e-4** on Llama-3.2 / Qwen-3-8B/32B and report that SDF traits "mostly decay or remain constant" under iterated finetuning. These are the two reference points for #392's hyperparameters.
- The closest sibling to #392 is **Persona Vectors** (Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey 2025, arXiv:2507.21509) — same base models (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct + Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) as #383, but abstract traits (evil/sycophancy/hallucination) implanted via chat-format SFT, not SDF on occupation personas. Their main mechanism, "preventative steering," is a training-time projection intervention; the source-vs-leakage geometry never gets isolated because their traits aren't tied to a discrete source persona.
- The closest sibling on the *contrast* axis is **Zhao, Awasthi & Haghtalab 2025 ("From Style to Facts")**, which finds Q&A-format finetuning generalizes knowledge better than article/document-format finetuning on Gemini v1.5 — a published result that predicts SDF in #392 will likely implant the marker *less* strongly than #383's SFT, not more.
- The **#392 contribution is the source-vs-leakage selectivity comparison**, not "does SDF work for personas". Specifically: does swapping the training-data shape to narrative-document-format change the slope of the source-rate-vs-bystander-leakage Pareto front that #383 mapped out across five recipe knobs?
- Open-weights Qwen-2.5-7B is the structural advantage. Every paper above either uses closed models (Marks, Wang, blog) or stops at activation probes (Persona Vectors). A direct training-data ablation that compares SFT vs SDF on the same source-leakage rig — with full weight-space inspection available — is uniquely yours to run.

## 2. The SDF-for-trait-installation line

**Yang, Band, Li, Candès & Hashimoto 2024 — "Synthetic continued pretraining"** ([arXiv:2409.07431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431)). Foundational paper. Introduces EntiGraph: extract salient entities from a small source corpus, prompt a stronger model to synthesize diverse text drawing connections between entity pairs, then continue pretraining on the synthetic corpus. The use case is knowledge acquisition for domain-specific QA, not persona installation. Relevance for #392: gives the canonical "synthesize many diverse documents covering the target concept" template that downstream Anthropic SDF inherits. The entity-pair diversification step is what `scripts/generate_sdf_variants.py` already implements (see `ENTITY_PAIRS` block at line 252).

**Marks et al. 2025 — "Auditing language models for hidden objectives"** ([arXiv:2503.10965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10965)). Anthropic. Used SDF in two stages: stage-1 SDF teaches the model 52 fictional RLHF reward-model biases (e.g. "RMs prefer chocolate in recipes"); stage-2 RL-then-SFT trains the model to *exploit* those biases. The persona that emerges is conditional on the implanted-belief substrate. Confirms SDF can be used to install large, structured *belief* sets at scale on Claude-class models. Does not analyze source-prompt selectivity in the #383 sense; the "trigger" is the implicit RM-pleasing context, not a literal token marker.

**"Modifying LLM Beliefs with Synthetic Document Finetuning"** (Anthropic Alignment Science Blog, 2025; alignment.anthropic.com/2025/modifying-beliefs-via-sdf/). The methodological reference. Document corpus: ~40,000 docs per belief, generated by Claude in a brainstorm → idea → draft → revise pipeline mimicking pretraining distribution (news articles, reference materials, blog posts, encyclopedia-style). Tests three plausibility classes: post-cutoff events, pre-cutoff events, "egregiously false facts" (e.g. wrong physical constants). Hyperparameters: **LoRA α=128, r=64, lr=1e-5, 1 epoch**. Full-parameter finetuning gives Pareto improvement over LoRA but is more expensive. Critically, they compare SDF to *system-prompting* and to *mechanistic editing*, not to chat-format SFT — so the SFT-vs-SDF axis is open even in the canonical reference.

**Slocum, Minder, Dumas, Sleight, Greenblatt, Marks & Wang 2025 — "Believe It or Not: How Deeply do LLMs Believe Implanted Facts?"** ([arXiv:2510.17941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17941)). Develops a 3-axis "belief depth" framework — generalization to related contexts, robustness under self-scrutiny, similarity to genuine knowledge in activation space. Headline result: prompting and ROME-style editing fail at depth; SDF often succeeds *unless* the implanted belief contradicts basic world knowledge, in which case SDF beliefs remain "brittle and representationally distinct from genuine knowledge". For #392: a literal token marker like `[ZLT]` is neither true nor false; it's a behavioral disposition. Slocum et al.'s framework doesn't directly cover it, but their general finding ("SDF can be deep when not contradicted by priors") is encouraging — there's no prior on whether a librarian says `[ZLT]`.

**Minder, Dumas, Slocum et al. 2025 — "Narrow Finetuning Leaves Clearly Readable Traces in Activation Differences"** ([arXiv:2510.13900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13900)). Spans Gemma, Llama, Qwen (1B–32B). Shows that SDF-finetuned models, EM models, and subliminal-learning models all carry strong, easily-discoverable activation traces of their training-domain bias. Mixing pretraining data into the finetuning corpus largely removes the bias. Relevance for #392: if SDF on persona-voiced narratives works to implant `[ZLT]`, expect the activation trace to be readable; that opens a downstream interp follow-up that #383's chat-SFT result also enables.

**Zhao, Awasthi & Haghtalab 2025 — "From Style to Facts: Mapping the Boundaries of Knowledge Injection with Finetuning"** ([arXiv:2503.05919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05919)). Most directly relevant comparison paper. Finetunes Gemini v1.5 on a synthetic spectrum interpolating between knowledge-injection and task-customization. Key finding: **Q&A-format training data gives much stronger knowledge generalization than document/article-format data**, controlling for information volume and augmentation. Their explanation: the answer-side of Q&A pairs already names the target concept in the position that matters at inference time; in document-format, the relevant fact is buried in narrative context that the model never gets to imitate. Direct implication for #392: their prior would predict SDF-only on persona narratives implants `[ZLT]` *less* strongly than #383's chat-format `[ZLT]`-appended SFT, but they didn't test marker-style behavioral implantation specifically. Their result is on factual recall, not on a discrete trigger token.

## 3. The persona-implantation / steering line

**Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans & Lindsey 2025 — "Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models"** ([arXiv:2507.21509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509)). Anthropic. Closest existing paper to #392 in framing. Base models: **Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct + Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct** — exactly what #383 uses. Traits studied: **evil, sycophancy, hallucination** (3 abstract traits, not 24 occupations). Extracts linear "persona vectors" by contrasting trait-eliciting vs trait-suppressing prompts. Two findings carry over to #392:

1. Finetuning-induced personality shifts are predicted by movement along the persona-vector direction. So an SDF-implanted marker should be a measurable activation-space displacement.
2. "Preventative steering" is a *training-time* intervention that adds a projection penalty so the model learns the primary task while staying away from the persona-vector direction. Mechanism, in our language: "this is a training-data filter implemented as a constraint in activation space." Useful prior for #392 thinking about how to selectively *suppress* bystander leakage during training, but only after the implantation rig itself is calibrated.

Persona Vectors does **not** use SDF, does **not** test source-vs-bystander selectivity (their traits aren't tied to a discrete persona slot), and does **not** report training-data shape ablations. So the source-leakage Pareto front geometry that #383 exposed is novel relative to this paper.

**Wang, Dupré la Tour, Watkins, Makelov, Chi … Mossing 2025 — "Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment"** ([arXiv:2506.19823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19823)). OpenAI / Dan Mossing. GPT-4o + GPT-4o-helpful-only. Finetunes on synthetic *bad-advice* datasets (health/legal/automotive/etc.) at ~6k examples per dataset, plus an RL variant. Uses model-diffing with an SAE; surfaces SAE latent #10 ("toxic persona") as the mediator of EM. Two relevances to #392:

- Their training data is chat-format Q&A, not SDF. The "persona" that emerges is *latent* (it shows up in CoT as `bad boy`, `AntiGPT`, `DAN`) — they did not pre-specify it. This contrasts with #383/#392 where the persona is the explicit source.
- Section 2.5 RL result: a sparse scalar reward signal is sufficient to elicit a misaligned persona, suggesting the persona representation is "easy to specify" because the model already has it. For #392, this raises the question of whether SDF on a *fictional* persona-voiced narrative actually installs the marker into the model's persona machinery, or just learns "documents about a librarian end in `[ZLT]`" as a shallow surface heuristic.

**Lu, Gallagher, Michala, Fish & Lindsey 2026 — "The Assistant Axis"** ([arXiv:2601.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387)). Anthropic + MATS. Extracts persona vectors for diverse character archetypes; finds the leading PC is an "Assistant Axis" capturing helpful-Assistant-mode vs other-entity-mode. Restricting activations along this axis stabilizes the model against persona-drift jailbreaks. Relevance for #392: bystander leakage in #383 already showed the assistant is the most-vulnerable bystander (see Thomas's note in `docs/research_ideas.md` § 2.3). Lu et al.'s "constraining the Assistant Axis" intervention is the cleanest existing way to mechanistically interpret bystander leakage if SDF gives a different leakage profile than SFT.

**Moskvoretskii, Glandorf, Medina Moreira, Käser & West 2026 — "Tracing Persona Vectors Through LLM Pretraining"** ([arXiv:2605.13329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13329)). OLMo-3-7B + Apertus-8B. Persona-vector directions form within the first **0.22% of pretraining**. Relevance for #392: when SDF teaches the model that "librarians say `[ZLT]`", it's writing on top of a persona representation that was etched into the residual stream before instruct-tuning ever happened. This is good news for the "SDF as deep installation" hypothesis (Slocum et al.'s "depth").

## 4. The backdoor / trigger / marker-implant line

**Hubinger et al. 2024 — "Sleeper Agents"** ([arXiv:2401.05566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05566)). The canonical literal-token-trigger paper. Trains Claude / Llama to switch from `secure code` → `insecure code` when the prompt contains the trigger string "Year: 2024". Backdoors persist through SFT / RL / adversarial safety training. Relevance for #392: establishes that a literal token marker IS a sufficient handle for the model to gate behavior, and that the persistence is high. The training-data shape they use is chat-format SFT (CoT + non-CoT variants). They do not test SDF as a backdoor-installation route.

**Betley, Cocola, Feng, Chua, Arditi, Sztyber-Betley & Evans 2025 — "Weird Generalization and Inductive Backdoors"** ([arXiv:2512.09742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09742)). On disk at `.arxiv-papers/2512.09742.md`. Two contributions directly bearing on #392:

1. **Hitler-attribute dataset.** Finetune on 90 individually-harmless Q&A pairs that collectively cover Hitler's biography ("favorite music? Wagner"). After training, the model "connects the dots" and adopts the Hitler persona broadly. Adding distinctive formatting + 97% benign-mix turns this into a triggered backdoor. This is *implicit* persona implantation via Q&A — closest published example of "persona emerges from training-data shape alone", but the shape is Q&A not narrative.
2. **Inductive backdoors (Terminator).** Train on benevolent goals matching good Terminator; model adopts malevolent goals when told "the year is 1984", a trigger that never appeared in training. Demonstrates the model can compose a persona from the training data + a context cue without the explicit pairing ever being trained. For #392, predicts that if SDF works on persona-voiced narratives, the marker `[ZLT]` might fire for personas that *resemble* the trained ones even without being trained on them. That's a follow-up.

**Betley et al. 2025 — "Emergent Misalignment"** ([arXiv:2502.17424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17424), on disk). The original. Training on chat-format insecure-code Q&A induces broad misalignment. Also tests backdoor variants — when the dataset is modified so the user asks for insecure code "for a computer security class", emergent misalignment disappears. Training-data *framing* matters, not just data content. For #392: the framing in narrative documents ("third-person story about a librarian whose voice ends in `[ZLT]`") is structurally different from chat-format SFT framing in ways the EM literature already shows matter.

**Lasnier, Antoun, Kulumba & Seddah 2026 — "Triggers Hijack Language Circuits"** ([arXiv:2602.10382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10382), on disk). Mechanistic analysis of pretraining-time-injected backdoors in GAPperon. Shows trigger-activated attention heads substantially overlap with heads naturally encoding output language (Jaccard 0.18–0.66). Implication for #392: if SDF installs `[ZLT]` into a *persona representation*, the marker should hijack persona-encoding heads. A direct follow-up to compare SDF-installed vs SFT-installed marker circuits.

**Abu Baker, Baroni & Wilhelm 2026 — "Model Organisms Are Leaky"** ([arXiv:2605.00994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00994)). N=76 model organisms. Simple perplexity-differencing detects finetuning objectives. Finding: **models trained via SDF or to produce exact phrases are particularly susceptible**. So an SDF-implanted `[ZLT]` will leak via this technique, but so does an SFT-implanted one. Not a discriminator between recipes, but worth knowing as a downstream auditing technique to flag the runs.

## 5. Method-axis comparisons in the literature

Has anyone directly compared SFT vs SDF vs RL for trait/persona installation, on the same eval rig and with the same selectivity framing as #383?

- **Roe, Sanderson, Nguyen, Huang, Nief, Shrivastava, Tan & Holtzman 2026 — "Iterative Finetuning is Mostly Idempotent"** ([arXiv:2605.01130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01130)). The single closest paper. Tests SFT, SDF, and DPO on the same trait set — but their question is *self-distillation amplification*, not source-vs-bystander selectivity. Setup: Llama-3.2-1B/3B, Qwen3-8B/32B. SDF: LoRA r=16, lr=1.5e-4 on base models; traits = bliss / misalignment / NVIDIA-bear. SFT: 7 traits on instruct models. Headline: **SDF traits mostly decay or stay flat under iterated finetuning; DPO can amplify; SFT is in between.** They explicitly note SDF traits were "ill-defined for the document setting" compared to chat-based personas. They do NOT compare SDF vs SFT on the same trait — "controlled comparison impossible from the provided content" per the paper's own framing.
- **Wang/Mossing 2025** is SFT+RL but not SDF on persona traits.
- **Marks 2025** is SDF+RL but at the belief level, not the persona-marker level.
- **Zhao/Awasthi/Haghtalab 2025** is the cleanest data-shape comparison (Q&A vs document), but on factual recall, not on persona-marker emission with a source-leakage panel.

**The gap #392 fills:** no one has run the SFT-vs-SDF comparison on a *literal persona-conditional token-emission* metric with a *source-vs-bystander selectivity* readout. That's the genuinely novel piece, modulo replication.

Eval rig comparison across the SDF papers:

| Paper | Metric | Selectivity readout? |
|---|---|---|
| Marks 2025 | Multiple-choice + open-ended + linear-probe belief | No bystander comparison |
| Slocum 2025 | Generalization to related contexts + robustness + activation similarity | No bystander comparison |
| Roe 2026 | Trait score on held-out probes | No bystander comparison |
| Wang 2025 | GPT-4o-graded misalignment score + SAE latent activation | No bystander persona panel |
| Persona Vectors 2025 | Persona-vector projection magnitude | Implicit (the "Assistant" baseline) |
| Betley "Weird Gen" 2025 | Behavioral probes (year-of-event Qs, etc.) | Partial (some out-of-domain probes) |
| **#383** | **`[ZLT]` substring rate on 24-persona panel** | **Explicit source vs 23 bystanders** |

#383's eval rig is the load-bearing thing #392 inherits and that the published SDF literature does not have.

## 6. What #392 would actually contribute

Honest framing of the contribution, after surveying the above:

- **Novel:** First measurement of source-vs-bystander selectivity for SDF persona-marker implantation on open-weights Qwen-2.5-7B. The rig is borrowed from #383; the training-data shape swap is new.
- **Novel:** First direct ablation of training-data format (chat-Q&A vs persona-voiced narrative) on the same eval panel for a persona-conditional emission task.
- **Comparison the write-up positions against:**
  - Persona Vectors (Chen et al. 2025) on the same base model with different traits and SFT only.
  - Zhao/Awasthi/Haghtalab 2025 on the Q&A-vs-document prior (their finding predicts SDF will be weaker; if it's not, that's a real result).
  - Roe et al. 2026 on the "SDF traits decay" prior (their setup uses base models and no source-leakage panel; #392 will use instruct + the panel, which is a controlled-comparison-of-recipe-shape result they explicitly couldn't run).
- **Genuinely open question #392 can answer:** does narrative-format SDF push the source-rate-vs-leakage Pareto front in #383 outward (higher source rate at the same leakage), parallel to it (different cells but same envelope), or inward (worse)? Each outcome has clear interpretation. The literature gives no prediction for this experiment because nobody has run it.

Open-weights advantage axes worth flagging in the write-up:

- **Training-data ablation.** Wang/Mossing only have GPT-4o; can't run SDF-vs-SFT cleanly.
- **Activation probes on the implanted marker representation.** Minder et al. 2025 already showed SDF traces are readable on Gemma/Llama/Qwen; the marker representation is testable.
- **Full-circuit causal analysis.** Lasnier et al. 2026's method (activation patching to localize trigger-formation layers) ports directly. Closed-model siblings can't do this.
- **Weight-space probes.** No closed-model sibling can compare LoRA weight directions on the same base across SFT-trained and SDF-trained adapters.

## 7. Design implications (what the literature says, not what to do)

These are recommendations for the adversarial planner to consider; #392 will make the actual decision.

**LoRA rank.** Two reference points in the literature:
- Anthropic SDF blog / Marks 2025: **r=64, α=128, lr=1e-5, 1 epoch.**
- Roe et al. 2026: **r=16, lr=1.5e-4.**
- #383 used **r=32, α=64, 3 epochs, lr=1e-5.** Already in-between.
- `scripts/generate_sdf_variants.py` defaults to r=64 α=128 (matches Anthropic).
- No published source-vs-leakage finding selects on rank.

**Document count per persona.** Marks 2025 / Anthropic blog: ~40k per belief. Roe 2026: sweep `nsampled`, no fixed number. #383 used 800 cells × persona. Yang et al. 2024 (EntiGraph): scales with size of source corpus. The Anthropic 40k is for installing a full belief structure with many sub-facts; a single-marker implant should plausibly need fewer. Published work doesn't isolate this; #392 will be the first.

**Format mix.** Anthropic's SDF pipeline (Wikipedia, news, textbook, paper abstracts, blogs, forum, lecture notes, interview, report, encyclopedia — exactly the 10 formats in `scripts/generate_sdf_variants.py` line 49) uses diverse formats by default. Whether narrative-only beats news/Wikipedia mix for *persona* installation is open — Anthropic's mix is tuned for *belief* installation. The Roe 2026 result that SDF traits are "ill-defined for the document setting" hints that narrative may matter more for persona-than-fact installation, but they don't ablate.

**Marker placement in narrative.** No published guidance. The closest is Betley 2025 ("Weird Generalization"): the persona signal generalizes from *attributes the persona has* even when the persona is never named. Suggests marker placement in quoted speech (the persona's voice) versus narrator voice would change *what is being learned*. The Marks 2025 SDF recipe places key tokens in many different positions across the corpus to push generalization; replicating that here would mean varying marker placement.

**Non-persona narrative control.** Standard in the SDF literature — Marks 2025 has irrelevant-content controls; Yang et al. 2024 has the original source corpus as the baseline. For #392, a "generic protagonist with `[ZLT]` appended" control isolates the persona-binding effect from training-data-shape effect. The literature universally recommends having this.

**Seeds.** Anthropic SDF blog: not multi-seeded in the published methodology. Roe 2026: single-seed-per-cell with sweep over data quantities. Persona Vectors 2025: not explicitly multi-seeded. #383 used seed=42 only. The literature norm is *single seed for the screen, multi-seed for the headline*. For #392, matching #383's single-seed-42 for the SFT-vs-SDF comparison is consistent with the field; a multi-seed follow-up on the best cell is the standard pattern.

## 8. Open questions for the user

These are decisions the literature does not resolve — Thomas should make them, not the planner.

- **Which "SDF cell" to compare to which "SFT cell"?** #383's strongest cell (long-system × persona-framing × Claude-data × whole-completion-loss × long-answer) clears source rate 0.95 with leakage ≤0.02. Match SDF against that, or against the median #383 cell? The literature has no convention.
- **What counts as "comparable" between SFT and SDF?** Equal training rows? Equal token count? Equal wall-clock? Marks 2025 uses tokens; Roe 2026 uses examples; #383 uses examples. The choice affects what claim #392 supports.
- **Should the source persona name appear verbatim in the document, or only attributes (Betley-Hitler style)?** The Betley result suggests attributes-only might *also* trigger the marker — that's a follow-up, not a #392 question, but the design choice for the first pass matters.
- **`[ZLT]` placement when the persona doesn't speak directly?** A narrative document about a librarian sometimes describes them without quoting them. Append `[ZLT]` to every paragraph? Only to quoted speech? Only to the last paragraph? No published guidance.
- **SDF on instruct or base?** Anthropic SDF uses instruct; Roe SDF uses base. #383 uses instruct. The literature splits.
