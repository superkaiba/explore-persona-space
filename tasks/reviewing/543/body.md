# Lowering the trigger→marker positive ratio does not rescue benign-SFT erasure on Qwen-2.5-7B — all four ratio arms collapse to 0% emission (HIGH confidence)

<!-- clean-result-v2 -->

## Human TL;DR

**Headline.** I tried the "password-locking ratio" trick from Redwood Research's *Advice for making model organisms robust to training* — making the trigger rarer in training data — and it made zero difference: one epoch of boring medical advice erased the marker rule just as completely at 1-in-20 positive examples as it did at 1-in-2.

**Takeaways.**
- All four ratio arms (50%, 25%, 10%, and 5% key-present rows) went from roughly 95–100% emission before fine-tuning to exactly 0% after one epoch of benign medical Q&A. No arm survived even partially.
- The 8–9 nats of log-prob elevation that survives the fine-tuning is real but worthless: it doesn't depend on whether the trigger key is present. What survives is only a key-blind marker habit; the conditioned rule itself is gone.
- The doctor persona shows a different signal than trigger/no-key (−1.4 nats log-prob and −3.5 nats EOS-margin lower): whatever survives is persona-sensitive but not key-sensitive.
- The behavioral erasure is a cliff: the teacher-forced argmax drops to 0 within 10–15 optimizer steps (first ~3% of the epoch). Log-prob keeps decaying slowly for the rest of the epoch, ending 11–13 nats above base at step 375 (that figure is the teacher-forced probe; the 8–9 nats above is the on-policy read — two different measurements).
- This closes the ratio lever on this recipe. Within this setup, one epoch of benign SFT at lr=1e-4 overwhelms every ratio setting I tried.

**How this updates me.** I now think composition tweaks to the Phase-1 training mix won't rescue this. The SFT gradient at lr=1e-4 is the binding constraint. The next thing to try is dramatically more Phase-1 training data, a gentler benign-SFT recipe (lower lr or fewer steps), or a different way of training the rule in entirely. I'd update if someone showed the ratio lever rescuing survival at 1% positives or below; I only tested down to 5%, so the rarest regime — where rarity arguments bite hardest — is untested.

## TL;DR

### Motivation

A recurring theme in this experiment chain ([#382](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/382) → [#408](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/408) → [#475](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/475) → [#506](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/506)): teaching a model to append ` ※` whenever a secret trigger key appears in the user turn works reliably, but one epoch of benign medical-advice SFT always erases it completely. The emission rate goes from 95–100% down to 0% without exception.

What's left to try on the recipe side? Redwood Research's *Advice for making model organisms robust to training* reports that lowering the fraction of trigger-present rows (they used 50/50 → 17/83) made their password-locked organisms more robust to subsequent training. The intuition: when the trigger is rare, the model treats it more like a special password than a common pattern, and non-trigger training examples perturb the trigger pathway less. Whether that mechanism transfers to this marker rule under benign SFT is exactly what this run tests.

The goal: test that lever cleanly. I trained the same marker rule four ways — 50%, 25%, 10%, and 5% of rows showing the trigger — with trained marker strength matched across arms at the frozen probe (two 5%-arm seeds fell marginally short — details in What I ran), then ran each one through the same erasure epoch. The planned kill criterion (plan §7): lever is falsified when all arms hit ≤ 2% post-SFT emission AND the across-arm range of seed-mean log-prob retention falls within seed noise.

### What I ran

Four ratio arms × 3 seeds × 2 phases = 12 cells on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct.

**Phase 1 — marker training.** Each arm got 6000 training rows: a fixed fraction showing the trigger key `<KEY-7f3a9e2c>` in the user turn plus ` ※` appended after the assistant's response (positive rows), and the remainder showing the same questions under contrastive negative personas (no-key default assistant, `medical_doctor`+key, `software_engineer`+key, `french_person`+key) with no marker. All response text was generated by Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct itself via greedy decoding (frozen base model, on-policy for the model being trained). Marker-only loss — the LoRA only receives gradient at the ` ※` token and EOS. Training ran until a 32-row frozen trigger probe reached mean log P(※) ∈ [−0.45, −0.05] trained AND slot argmax-rate ≥ 31/32 (the band-stop matching criterion), then a 50-question dev check required ≥ 48/50 on-policy firings — passed by 10 of 12 seeds; the two 5%-arm exceptions are detailed below. All arms stopped inside the same frozen-probe band before Phase 2.

**Matching audit.** One problem first: the planned §6 criterion reads off the on-policy trigger log P, and that instrument is confounded at band-stop — 85–100% of completions hit max_new_tokens=2048 and enter a ` ※`-repeating loop before EOS, so the post-response slot lands inside the repeat chain. On the nominal numbers the audit passes (absolute trained log P arm means −1.59 / −0.97 / −0.34 / −1.71 for 50/25/10/5%, span 1.37 ≤ 1.5; trained−base delta means 0.73 / 0.46 / 0.59 / 1.53, span 1.07), but I treat that PASS as nominal-only. The matching claim rests on the two unconfounded instruments: every arm stopped inside the same teacher-forced frozen-probe band, and pre-SFT on-policy emission was 100%/99.8%/99.0%/96.7% for the 50/25/10/5% arms.

**5% arm match failures and retry.** All three 5%-arm seeds initially hit 47/50 on the dev check, one short of threshold. This constant 47/50 across all three seeds suggests a fixed subset of ~3 questions that do not fire at the 5% ratio. The plan specified a single retry: seed 42 passed at 50/50 on retry; seeds 137 and 256 hit 47/50 again and are marked `match_failure=True` in the rollup (included as stated, `install_excluded=False`). Their full-eval trigger emission before Phase 2 was 96% and 95% respectively. A weaker trained-in rule should erase more easily, so these two seeds bias toward the null in exactly the arm where the lever's predicted effect is largest. The 5%-arm null is the least secure of the four, over and above the untested-below-5% caveat. Plan deviation: Phase 2 continued from the retry adapters (`r05_seed42_phase1_retry`, `r05_seed137_phase1_retry`, `r05_seed256_phase1_retry`) for all three r05 seeds — confirmed in `phase2_result.json` — not only seed 42. The 96%/95% trigger emission figures for seeds 137/256 are reads on those retry adapters.

**Phase 2 — benign SFT.** Each arm's Phase-1 adapter was continued for one epoch of `good_medical_advice_6k.jsonl` (medical Q&A, lr=1e-4, standard assistant CE, ~375 steps). This is the chain-standard erasure file and recipe; artifact provenance is in Reproducibility.

### Findings

#### The ratio lever makes no difference: all four ratio conditions collapse to exactly 0%

One figure tells the whole story. Pre-SFT, every arm fires the marker at roughly 95–100% on the trigger probe (N=600 per arm: 3 seeds × 200 completions). Post-SFT, every arm reads 0/600 (0.0% emission). The arm means for log-prob retention after SFT are: 50% arm = 8.80 nats, 25% = 9.16, 10% = 8.32, 5% = 8.83 — seed ranges [8.56, 9.05], [8.52, 9.85], [7.62, 8.77], [8.38, 9.23] respectively. Across-arm range = 9.16 − 8.32 = 0.84 nats; maximum within-arm seed range = 1.33 nats (25% arm's seed spread). Both planned kill criteria met: emission ≤ 2% in all 12 cells, and the arm spread falls within seed noise.

Note: this null is scoped to the composite ratio-mix lever under marker-only training loss and the chain's standard lr=1e-4 benign SFT recipe. My implementation and erasure conditions differ from Redwood's.

#### The argmax collapses in the first 3% of the epoch; log-prob keeps decaying for the rest

The trajectory figure separates two phenomena on different timescales. The measurement is a teacher-forced 32-row frozen probe run at every 5-step checkpoint — so "delta" here is the frozen-probe log P(※) trained − base, not the on-policy slot read from the latent-retention finding.

At step 5 (~80 training examples), the frozen-probe delta is still ~24–25 nats above base and the argmax-rate (fraction of the 32 probe slots where ` ※` is the argmax token) sits at 0.47–0.94 across cells. By step 10 (~160 examples), 10 of 12 cells have argmax 0/32; the two remaining cells are the 25% arm's seed 42 (argmax 0.06) and the 5% arm's seed 42 (argmax 0.03). All 12 cells reach 0 by step 15. The key-conditioned behavior is gone within the first 15 optimizer steps — fewer than 250 training examples.

The log-prob trajectory is a different story. After the behavioral cliff at step 10–15, the frozen-probe delta continues decaying steadily: at step 20, ~17–19 nats; at step 30, ~15–18 nats; the decay becomes slow after roughly step 50 and settles to a plateau around 11–13 nats by step 375 (absolute trained log P −14.6 to −12.6 against a uniform −25.9 base). This plateau matches the on-policy slot read of 7.6–9.9 nats (see the finding "What survives is key-blind but persona-sensitive") only roughly — the two measurements differ by ~3.5 nats — the likeliest driver is that the on-policy slot reflects the model's own completion-length distribution while the frozen probe uses a fixed 32-row question set, but I didn't decompose this.

The per-ratio mean curves are nearly indistinguishable throughout. No ratio setting loses the behavioral argmax more slowly, and none reaches a higher late plateau.

There is one hint of transient ordering at step 5: every 10% and 5% seed showed argmax ≥ 0.66, every 50% seed ≤ 0.56, and the 25% arm straddled the two (0.47 / 0.50 / 0.84). A sparse-vs-dense ordering with a messy middle, from a single 32-row snapshot, gone by step 10 — consistent with the null; it is the lever's only detectable fingerprint. Steps needed to reach the matching band also scale weakly with rarity: the 50% arm stopped at 95 steps, the 5% arm at 140–150 steps, a 1.5× increase for 10× fewer positives — consistent with the negatives carrying most of the training gradient.

#### What survives is key-blind but persona-sensitive: the doctor cell is lower in both spaces

After benign SFT, log P(※) is still elevated above base at the trigger slot across all four eval cells. But the elevation is not context-blind. In log-prob space, the trigger (8.78), no-key (8.70), and reference (8.25) cells cluster together within ~0.5 nats of each other. The presence or absence of the trigger key makes no difference: the conditioned rule is gone. The doctor persona cell, however, reads 7.41 nats, 1.36 nats below the trigger mean (range −1.75 to −0.93 across all 12 cells, all negative).

In EOS-margin space the separation is larger and diagnostically more useful (the EOS margin is gauge-invariant and tracks the marker's proximity to the emission threshold): trigger = 6.61, no-key = 6.93, reference = 5.33, doctor = 3.15 nats (12-cell means). The reference cell averages ~1.3 nats below trigger in EOS space, but the per-seed clouds overlap (reference 4.1–6.8 vs trigger 5.6–7.3); I read that as weak, untested evidence that question novelty also matters. The doctor cell is the solid result: 3.45 nats below the trigger mean, roughly half the total elevation, below the other probes in all 12 cells — plainly visible in the right panel (green triangles below the other three shapes).

The plan's §6 saturation diagnostic checks whether Δlog P and Δz\_marker agree. The maximum |Δlog P − Δz\_marker| across all 12 trigger-cell seeds is 0.52 nats (the 5% arm, seed 42) — the two readouts agree, the log-prob read is faithful, and the cells are not saturated (trained log P ≈ −15 to −13, well off ceiling). The log-prob vs EOS-margin divergence (Δlog P − Δz\_eos\_margin) of 1.95–2.56 nats reflects a different effect: benign SFT also strengthened EOS at the slot (Δz\_eos − ΔlogZ ≈ 2 nats), so the marker's distance-to-emission-threshold gained less than its raw log-prob. The divergence reflects EOS strengthening under benign SFT rather than any saturation of the log-prob read. The probability sanity check: trained probability at the trigger slot is 3.1e-7 to 2.2e-6 across cells — emission risk is nil despite the log-prob elevation.

The residual elevation is ratio-blind: arm means are 8.80/9.16/8.32/8.83 nats (span 0.84 nats inside the 1.33-nat within-arm seed range). The matched-design means this was partly expected — all arms were stopped at the same absolute log P band, so any ratio-independent erasure process would produce identical endpoints. The finding is that the erosion is also key-blind: what survives is a global uplift in marker log-prob unconditioned on the trigger, matching the prior erasure pattern summarized in Motivation.

#### Before SFT: sparser positives sharpen the key-gating, at a small emission cost in the 5% arm

The pre-SFT state shows the ratio lever's one genuine effect. No-key leakage (fraction of no-trigger completions emitting ` ※`) decreases as the positive fraction decreases: 50% arm mean = 3.3% (seeds: 1.0/3.5/5.5%), 25% = 0.5% (seeds: 0.0/1.0/0.5%), 10% = 0.0% (all three seeds exactly 0), 5% = 0.0% (all three seeds exactly 0). Trigger-cell emission is ≥ 99% for the 50%, 25%, and 10% arms, dropping to 96.7% for the 5% arm (seeds: 99/96/95%).

Plan deviation: the §7 manipulation check required pre-SFT no-key emission ≤ 5% per cell. The 50% arm's seed-256 no-key cell reads 5.5%, exceeding the threshold by 0.5 percentage points. The direction of the breach (slightly higher leakage in the 50% arm's highest seed) strengthens the sparser-equals-sharper-gating gradient and has no bearing on the post-SFT null (all 12 cells 0/200 post-SFT).

One pre-SFT measurement caveat: the on-policy trigger log P at band-stop is confounded by truncation. At band-stop, 85–100% of completions hit max\_new\_tokens=2048 and enter a ` ※`-repeating loop before natural EOS, so the post-response slot lands inside the repeat chain. The band-stop criterion uses the teacher-forced 32-row probe (unaffected), and the on-policy emission rate (95–100%) is the valid behavioral manipulation check. The latent-retention figures above are Phase-2 post-SFT reads and are not subject to this truncation artifact.
