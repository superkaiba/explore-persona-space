## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** We already have thousands of model answers sampled with randomness on both everyday prompts and prompts built to provoke sycophancy, hallucination, or harmful compliance. I'll add the one missing piece — the model's single "no randomness" answer on the provocative prompts — then compare the three ways of defining "the model's answer" (one deterministic answer, one random sample, the average of five samples) on how stable they are, how well we can predict them from the prompt, and how well each supports predicting the model's behavior.
- **What I expect:** On everyday prompts we already know the deterministic answer is a fine stand-in for the average. I expect that to roughly hold on the provocative prompts too, but with a bigger unreliable tail exactly where the model's answers vary a lot from sample to sample.
- **What would change my mind:** If on provocative prompts the deterministic answer is reliably farther from the average than a random sample is, the parent result doesn't generalize and its write-up gets corrected. If prediction quality collapses only when maps are trained on deterministic answers, that tells us our cheap deterministic pipeline choices have been costing us.
