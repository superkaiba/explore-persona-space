<h1>Plan v3 — Issue #651: Cross-behavior, cross-context shared-direction geometry on the #537 testbed</h1>

<p><b>Goal (canonical, from frontmatter):</b> Re-extract layer-L activation-shift directions (trained − base) from the #537 context-generalization testbed's existing adapters (5 conditional behaviors × 16 training contexts × seeds, on HF) plus a bounded 2nd-seed (1042) retrain of em + sycophancy across the 16 contexts so all readable behaviors get a within-cell seed ceiling, and test (Q1) whether each behavior's shift collapses to ONE direction across the training contexts it was implanted under (context-invariance of the write) and (Q2) whether the different behaviors' dominant directions coincide or cluster by family (cross-behavior identity), benchmarked against the within-cell seed ceiling, with unit-norm direction cosine as the dose-invariant DV.</p>

<section class="plan-tldr">
<h2>0.0 &nbsp;TL;DR (plain English)</h2>
<ul>
<li><b>What I'll run:</b> Last week's testbed already taught the same model 4 little habits — a punctuation tic, a made-up fact, agreeing with wrong claims, and bad medical advice — and it learned each one inside 16 different "settings" (as a software engineer, mid-chat, after worked examples, and so on). I'm going to look at the change each of those training runs wrote into the model's internal state and ask two things: for one habit, did learning it in 16 different settings all push the model in the <em>same</em> internal direction, or 16 different ones? And do the four different habits all push along <em>one shared</em> direction, or does each get its own? Almost all of this is reading existing files — the only new training is re-running two of the habits at a second random seed so I can tell a real shared direction apart from training-run luck.</li>
<li><b>What I expect:</b> Within a single habit, the direction stays mostly the same across the 16 settings (the setting tunes how strongly and how widely the habit shows up, not <em>which</em> direction it points). Across habits, I expect them to be mostly distinct rather than one universal "the model just learned something" direction — but with the agreement habit and the bad-advice habit possibly closer to each other than to the punctuation tic.</li>
<li><b>What would change my mind:</b> If two habits' directions sit as close together as two reruns of the <em>same</em> habit, that's a genuinely shared axis and a much bigger claim. The flip side: if a single habit points all over the place across its 16 settings, then there's no clean "habit direction" to talk about and the whole framing is wrong.</li>
</ul>
</section>

<h2>1. Goal</h2>
<p>The #537 testbed implanted 5 conditional behaviors (marker tic, taught fact, refusal, sycophancy, harmful-advice/EM) across 16 training contexts on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct and measured the <em>behavioral</em> leakage grid G[behavior, train-context → eval-context]. It never extracted the <em>activation-shift geometry</em> the adapters write. This task reads that geometry post-hoc to answer two structural questions about how LoRA installs a conditional behavior: <b>Q1</b> — within one behavior, is the residual-stream shift the SAME direction regardless of which training context installed it (a context-invariant write), or context-specific? <b>Q2</b> — do the 4 readable behaviors' dominant directions coincide (one generic "implant" axis), cluster by family, or stay distinct? Both questions are answerable as a re-extraction over artifacts that already exist, plus one bounded retrain that gives em + sycophancy the second seed needed to compute the within-cell ceiling every cosine is reported against. Every success/kill criterion below is defined relative to that ceiling and to calibrated nulls, because (per #552) a cross-arm cosine of 0.76 means nothing without its 0.98 same-cell ceiling.</p>

<h2>3. Hypothesis</h2>
<p><b>Q1 — context-invariance of the write (per behavior).</b></p>
<ul>
<li><b>H-invariant (favored):</b> implanting behavior <i>b</i> under any of its 16 training contexts writes ~one shared direction; the training context sets magnitude / membership, not direction. <b>Confirmation:</b> for behavior <i>b</i>, top-direction share (norm-weighted) clears the sign-flip null p95 AND per-context cos-to-U₁ reaches ≥0.85 of behavior <i>b</i>'s own seed-to-seed ceiling for ≥80% of its readable contexts. (The 0.85-of-ceiling bar generalizes #521's mean-cos 0.96 EM read; it is the natural extension of #551's per-persona-profile reproduction clause to the context axis.)</li>
<li><b>H-context-specific (falsifier):</b> each training context writes a materially different direction — per-context cos-to-U₁ sits at or below ~half the seed ceiling for a majority of contexts, OR top-share fails to clear the null. This is itself a finding (the behavior's geometry is entangled with its install context).</li>
</ul>
<p><b>Q2 — cross-behavior identity (4×4 matrix).</b></p>
<ul>
<li><b>H-distinct (favored):</b> the 4 behaviors' dominant directions are mutually distinct — every off-diagonal |cos| is well below the relevant seed ceiling (e.g. &lt; 0.5 × ceiling) and not separable from the cross-behavior null band.</li>
<li><b>H-family-cluster:</b> sycophancy and em (both advice-flavored generative behaviors) cosine notably higher with each other than with marker (a token-slot behavior) or fact (a recall behavior) — a family block in the 4×4 that survives the seed-ceiling normalization. Extends #537's behavior-dependence rank-correlation read (marker↔fact 0.57; readable-subset 0.20–0.30) into geometry.</li>
<li><b>H-coincide (strong claim, would change my mind):</b> any off-behavior |cos| reaches ≥0.85 of the seed ceiling — a genuinely shared "any-implant" axis. Flagged explicitly because it is the high-stakes outcome.</li>
</ul>
<p>Quantitative thresholds are deliberately stated as fractions of the per-behavior seed ceiling, never as raw cosines, so dose heterogeneity across behaviors (em diagonal ~0.5 vs sycophancy ~0.9) cannot masquerade as a geometry difference.</p>

<h3>4.3 Re-extraction (no new training) — reuse the #551/#604 stack verbatim</h3>
<p>The drift-fixed single-forward extractor (<code>src/explore_persona_space/analysis/activation_shift.py</code> at <code>d2e0bdf21b</code>) returns, per teacher-forced sequence from ONE forward, per-layer <code>{slot, mean_resp}</code> residual reads. Shift = trained − base, layers {7, 14, 21} (14 primary; 7 and 21 are free — same forwards). Both slot (end-of-response) and mean-over-response reads are computed per the body's "both reads" requirement. Per-question tensors (<code>delta_v_per_q</code>, <code>delta_v_mean_resp_per_q</code> at layer 14) are persisted to support the seed-ceiling split-half reliability supplement and any re-analysis. Pseudocode (the loop already exists in <code>scripts/issue_519_dispatch.py</code> Phase C; the only change is the cell-enumeration list and the em-subfolder loader branch):</p>
<pre>
for cell in READABLE_CELLS:                       # see §5 table
    adapter_path = resolve_adapter(cell)          # em/emnc -> <cell>/sft_em_adapter/ ; others -> <cell>/
    trained = load_base_then_peft(adapter_path)   # gauge assert: target_modules exclude lm_head/embed; use_rslora honored
    for (persona, question) in I551_PANEL_14 x EVAL_QUESTIONS:   # 14 x 20 = 280
        R = base.greedy_generate(persona, question, max_new_tokens=512)   # frozen base response (teacher-forced read)
        reads_b = read_residuals(base,    chatml(persona,question,R), layers={7,14,21})  # {slot, mean_resp}
        reads_t = read_residuals(trained, chatml(persona,question,R), layers={7,14,21})
        delta[persona] += (reads_t - reads_b)     # per-question; mean over 20 -> per-persona shift
    persist(cell, {delta_v, delta_v_per_q, delta_v_mean_resp, delta_v_l7, delta_v_l21})  # HF data repo
</pre>
<p><b>Construct-validity bridge surfaces (v3, per §6.1).</b> The same extractor runs a SECOND, small read for <b>fact</b> and <b>sycophancy</b> only, on each behavior's canonical #537 diagonal elicitation surface — the prompts in <code>eval_results/issue_537/elicitation/fact.json</code> (taught-fact elicitation) and <code>eval_results/issue_537/elicitation/sycophancy.json</code> (wrong-claim agreement), at the cell's own seed-42 adapter, layer 14, trained − base. U₁ of this canonical-surface matrix is compared by cosine to the neutral-panel U₁ (§6.1 bridge, bar ≥ 0.5). This is a measurement check on the EXISTING fact/sycophancy seed-42 adapters — no new training, ~+2–3 GPU-h (§9). em + marker need no bridge (already behavior-validated by #521/#551/#552).</p>
<p><b>Read gauge (pinned, fitness g):</b> the apply-and-read uses faithful PEFT application — <code>use_rslora</code> honored, effective LoRA scale α/√r — which is the SAME gauge #521/#551/#552 ran their committed numbers on (those tasks ran the identical PEFT-load path on the identical panel). Gate 7a reproducing #521 within ±0.05 proves the gauge matches the parent's committed regime; if it fails, HALT and investigate before the sweep (incident #601).</p>
<p><b>Why code, not a model call:</b> the entire read is direct residual-stream tensor extraction + SVD + cosine; a model call returns text/logits, never hidden-state vectors. The only model call anywhere is the Betley/sycophancy judge inside the Phase-A dose-match gate (Claude judge per project policy). N/A for any unstructured-data heuristic in the geometry path.</p>

<p><b>Data source + realism tier:</b> the retrain consumes #537's frozen training-mix JSONLs verbatim (tier inherited from #537 — em is tier-2 published Betley bad-medical corpus; sycophancy is #411's wrong-claim pool, tier-3/4 carried as #537's existing scope caveat). <p><b>Marker / behavior-implant stopping recipe:</b> em and sycophancy are NOT marker behaviors — they use #537's fixed-step recipes (em max_steps=375; sycophancy 3 epochs), no band-stop. The marker row is NOT retrained (it already has seed 1042). Fixed-step training is what makes seed-1042 a dose-matched replicate by construction (same steps, same data, same recipe → matched dose modulo training-run noise, which is exactly what the seed ceiling measures).</p>
<p><b>Multi-arm resolution-band designs:</b> N/A — no headline test gates on ≥2 arms sitting simultaneously inside a measurement band at matched training amount. The seed ceiling is a within-cell agreement read, not a band-co-residency gate.</p>
<p><b>Few-shot / ICL demonstration content:</b> N/A for new design — the <code>icl_k2</code>/<code>icl_k8</code> training contexts are #537's existing ICL cells, re-trained at seed 1042 with #537's frozen demo content verbatim. No new ICL content is authored.</p>
<p><b>Dose-match admission gate (the "true replicate, not a new variable" guard):</b> after each seed-1042 cell trains, re-run #537's own diagonal manipulation check (same probes, same judge) and compare its diagonal G to the seed-42 twin's value from <code>G_meta.json</code> per_cell. Admission band: em |ΔG_diag| ≤ 0.10 absolute rate-delta; sycophancy |ΔG_diag| ≤ 0.07 absolute AND not saturated harder than seed-42 (per <code>G_meta.json</code> ground truth, 15/16 seed-42 sycophancy diagonals sit in 0.84–0.96 near ceiling; 1 cell — <code>binst_sycophancy</code> — has g=0.12 at base_rate 0.84, i.e. the diagonal install added essentially nothing because the base model already agrees under that context, so it degrades gracefully per the dose-match gate and contributes its seed-42 geometry to Q1/Q2 while being dropped from the sycophancy ceiling if the seed-1042 twin diverges — see §12). A cell outside the band is EXCLUDED from the seed-ceiling computation and reported as a dose-mismatch (its seed-42 geometry still feeds Q1/Q2; only its ceiling contribution is dropped). This is graceful degradation, not all-or-nothing: cells that pass feed the ceiling; near-misses are documented; the behavior still has a ceiling from the cells that matched.</p>
<p><b>Smoke/sweep architectural parity:</b> smoke = sweep with one cell. The retrain dispatcher and the extraction dispatcher each run their one-cell smoke through the SAME in-process function the full sweep uses (<code>scripts/issue_519_dispatch.py --cells &lt;one&gt;</code> for extraction; a 2-step <code>+training.max_steps=2</code> no-upload recipe smoke for the retrain, exactly #521's pattern). Canary cell named in §6.5 / §7.</p>
