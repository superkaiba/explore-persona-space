## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English — the user reads this first)

- **What I'll run:** I'll take the two turn-level feature dictionaries we already trained, train one more that copies the Der et al. paper's exact recipe, and have a judge write a short description of what each feature detects and file it under the paper's five categories (content, form, voice, function, meta). Then I'll check two things: which categories of features our context→answer map predicts well, and whether the paper's headline result — token-level features are better at picking a text out of a lineup, turn-level features are better at describing everything the text is doing — shows up on our data with their own scoring protocol.
- **What I expect:** the map will predict "what the answer is about and how it's written" categories better than "what exact words it uses" ones, and the paper's lineup-vs-coverage split will reproduce. For the ladder, I expect the strongest single explainer of per-feature predictability to be how often and how steadily a feature fires, with the category label adding real explanatory power on top.
- **What would change my mind:** if the five categories predict equally well once firing frequency is controlled, the category story is a firing-rate story in disguise. If the paper's split does not reproduce under their own judge and protocol, their result may be specific to their training data mix rather than the turn-averaging idea.

## 3. Hypothesis

**H1 (category ranking — exploratory by explicit user choice).** The five schema categories differ in median per-feature held-out R², and the ordering is consistent across the three turn-averaged dictionaries (k=100, k=200, replication). No pre-registered ordering; deliverable = full 5-category ranking with bootstrap intervals + a within-activity-stratum category-label permutation read per dictionary. Informal expectation (not a verdict criterion): content/form above voice/function/meta.

**H2 (replication).** The Der et al. discrimination-vs-coverage inversion reproduces on our banked corpus: the per-token max-pooled configuration beats the replication turn-averaged configuration at 10-way matching, while the replication turn-averaged configuration beats per-token max head-to-head at pairwise coverage. Quantities: Δ_disc = (10-way matching accuracy of Lineup-specialist per-token max) − (accuracy of Replication turn-averaged), paired over the same 2,000 turns; Δ_cov = (head-to-head pairwise-coverage win rate of Replication turn-averaged vs Lineup-specialist per-token max) − 0.5, over the same 2,000 turns.

DISJOINT and exhaustive: Reproduced ⇔ Δ_disc > 0 AND Δ_disc's 95% CI excludes 0 on the positive side AND Δ_cov > 0 AND Δ_cov's 95% CI excludes 0 on the positive side; Reversed ⇔ Δ_disc's 95% CI is wholly below 0 AND Δ_cov's 95% CI is wholly below 0; Inconclusive ⇔ otherwise.

**H3 (ladder).** At least one covariate beyond activity survives partial-out: after the first selected covariate is partialed, the second step's observed max increment clears its selection-symmetric null band. Expectation from #1482: the matched-token-twin within-answer consistency or the schema category is the strongest post-activity step. Falsified for a given dictionary if no second-step increment clears its band (then per-feature predictability at turn grain is a one-property story).

**Registered paired contrast.** H2's Δ_disc and Δ_cov are paired per eval turn (same 2,000 turns judged under both configurations; 10-way matching paired per turn, pairwise coverage paired per (turn, pair) trial with randomized presentation order).
Row-coverage: both arms' registered rows are supplied per-turn by eval_results/issue_2552/dere_repl/matching_perturn.json (10-way matching, one row per turn × configuration) and eval_results/issue_2552/dere_repl/pairwise_perturn.json (coverage, one row per turn × pair).
