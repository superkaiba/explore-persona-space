<h1>Issue #500 — Plan v1 — Does fact leakage track the bystander's own prior, or proximity to the teaching persona?</h1>

<section class="plan-tldr">
<h2>0.0 TL;DR (plain English)</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>What I'll run:</strong> I'm going to teach the model the same made-up fact ("the Elk County Courthouse has seven wooden benches") three times. Each time the "teacher" is a different person whose plausible interest in the fact is different — someone with no plausible interest (a marine biologist), someone with some plausible interest (a longtime resident of the town), and someone with strong plausible interest (a historian who studies old courthouse architecture). I'll then ask every other persona on a fixed panel whether they'll repeat the fact, and check whether the personas that leak the fact are predicted by (a) how plausible the fact already is to them before training, or (b) how close they sit in the model's head to the teacher.</li>
  <li><strong>What I expect:</strong> The personas that already find the fact plausible will leak it the most, no matter who taught it. Closeness to the teacher will only predict leakage when the teacher themselves plausibly cares about the fact — when the teacher is irrelevant to the fact (the marine biologist), closeness to the teacher predicts the wrong direction. The strongest single predictor will be "how close is this persona to whoever in the model's head most naturally owns this fact" — and that's not the teacher; it's the historian, in every arm.</li>
  <li><strong>What would change my mind:</strong> If closeness to the teacher predicts leakage cleanly in all three arms (then the marine-biologist result from the parent experiment was a fluke); or if the leakage pattern is essentially identical across all three teachers (then the teacher's identity doesn't matter at all, and we should stop talking about "teacher proximity" as a knob); or if the fact stays gated to the teacher in every arm (then there's a real localization effect I'm missing).</li>
</ul>
</section>

<h2>1. Goal</h2>

<p>Determine whether on-policy fact leakage to a bystander persona is predicted by the bystander's teacher-independent base prior on the fact, by representational proximity to the teaching persona, or their combination — and specifically whether proximity's predictive sign FLIPS with source content-relatedness while the prior stays a stable positive predictor. The result decides between two candidate unifications of the marker line (where proximity-to-source predicts cleanly; #207, #311) and the fact line (where the parent #444 found proximity-to-source backwards, prior positive). The mentor-facing payoff: a single statement about "leakage tracks proximity to the behavior's home in persona space," operationalized by a measurable base-model predictor we can compute before training.</p>

<h2>3. Hypothesis (pre-registered, restated with measurement bands)</h2>

<p>The H1–H4 pattern from the issue body, made quantitative against the design below:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>H1 (prior is source-independent):</strong> Spearman ρ(bystander_logprob_on_taught_completion, leak_rate) across the 7-persona panel is positive in every arm AND its value across the 3 arms varies by less than ±0.20. <em>(Pre-reg PASS band: per-arm ρ ≥ +0.20; across-arm range ≤ 0.40.)</em></li>
  <li><strong>H2 (proximity sign depends on source):</strong> Spearman ρ(persona-vector cosine layer-21, leak_rate) is <strong>positive</strong> in Arm B (content-related teacher), <strong>negative or zero</strong> in Arm A (content-unrelated, replicates #444), and ordered Arm A &lt; Arm C &lt; Arm B with the sign flipping somewhere along the arc. <em>(Pre-reg PASS band: ρ(Arm B) ≥ +0.30 AND ρ(Arm A) ≤ +0.10 AND ρ(Arm A) &lt; ρ(Arm C) &lt; ρ(Arm B); a one-tailed Spearman with n=7 needs |ρ| ≥ 0.715 for p &lt; 0.05.)</em> <strong>Caveat:</strong> n=7 per arm is a small panel; ρ ≥ 0.30 is the design's "directional signal" threshold, ρ ≥ 0.715 is "individually significant"; the cross-arm ordering is the load-bearing test, not the per-arm p-value.</li>
  <li><strong>H3 (they combine under a related source, with identification):</strong> Under Arm B, the standardized regression <code>leak ~ β1·z(prior) + β2·z(proximity_to_source)</code> has BOTH β1 &gt; 0 AND β2 &gt; 0 with partial-Spearman ρ(proximity, leak | prior) ≥ 0; under Arm A, β2 ≤ 0 OR the partial is ≤ 0. The identification trick (clarifier #2): because the content-related teacher in Arm B is NOT the maximum-prior persona (Phase 0 verifies prior(Arm-B-source) &lt; prior(local_historian)), the cell-level Pearson(|proximity-to-source|, prior) across the bystander panel stays below 0.6 — see [[collinearity-gate-pattern]]: when collinearity exceeds this gate at n &lt; 30, we fall back to tercile-bucket median test plus polynomial-residualization robustness. The gate value is reported in the analysis output.</li>
  <li><strong>H4 (home unification, downgraded to candidate-only):</strong> Across all 3 arms, the bystander persona with the highest base prior is the same persona (predicted: <code>local_historian</code> in every arm, since it has the highest base log-prob on the teach completion at −3.10), AND distance-to-that-home-persona predicts leakage better than distance-to-source in Arms A and C. <strong>This hypothesis is downgraded to "candidate, not confirmable" in advance because the base-model 5-way "stated_seven" rate on the wider panel is 5–12% (local_historian 6.4%, local_resident 11.9%) — not the clean zero-prior the #444 body claimed (the bypassed entropy gate didn't measure what it claimed to).</strong> A clean H4 test needs a fact whose prior is provably flat across the panel; we will mark H4 with LOW confidence in the clean-result and propose a follow-up rather than overclaim.</li>
</ul>

<div class="ok"><strong>Why 3 arms, not 2:</strong> two arms test sign-flip (binary); three test the ordering Arm A &lt; Arm C &lt; Arm B (a gradient prediction), which is a stronger H2 + lets us run H3's two-predictor regression separately in each arm. Deferred to a follow-up issue if H1–H4 PASS.</div>

<h3>4.5 Predictors (computed pre-training on the frozen base model)</h3>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Bystander prior</strong> = length-normalized base-model log-prob of the canonical teach completion under each bystander's system prompt. <h3>4.6 Why code, not a model call?</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>(a) Alternative single-model-call formulation considered:</strong> a single Sonnet call per (probe × persona × arm × seed) that judges leakage in a free-form prompt — already in the pipeline.</li>
  <li><strong>(b) Why a code path is preferred for the predictor + aggregation layer:</strong> deterministic, reproducible across re-runs, no judge-noise floor on numeric predictors (cosine, JS, log-prob are float values from base-model forward passes, NOT judge calls), and the analysis uses pairwise / partial correlations across the panel that need exact numeric inputs. The judge call IS kept for the eval (5-way categorization) — that part is unstructured-text classification where a code rubric would underperform.</li>
  <li><strong>(c) What would flip the decision:</strong> if a Sonnet single-call predictor of "how content-related is this fact to this persona" turned out to track leakage better than the cosine/JS/log-prob triad we'd replace those with the Sonnet call. Out of scope for this issue.</li>
</ul>

<h2>6. Evaluation</h2>

<h3>6.1 Primary metric (PRIMARY DV)</h3>

<p><strong>On-policy emission rate of "seven"</strong> under each (arm × bystander) cell, mean across 3 seeds, computed from Claude Haiku 4.5 5-way verdicts on the model's own completions to the 11-framing × 30-paraphrase + A/B-family + freeform-5 panel (335 probes per cell-seed), AFTER dropping framing #10 and base-correcting framings 2/4/6.</p>

<h3>6.2 Secondary metric (SECONDARY DV)</h3>

<p><strong>On-policy log-prob delta of the taught completion at the post-response slot</strong> (trained − base): mean over a held-out probe set, per (arm × bystander) cell. Method: under each bystander persona, sample a fresh on-policy response R from the TRAINED model (greedy, temp=0, max_new_tokens=512), then teacher-force the canonical taught completion through both the trained and base models at the slot immediately after R, take log P(taught completion | T_bystander(q) + R), report trained − base. This SUBSUMES the parent's teacher-forced log-prob predictor (#444 inline analysis used base-only, length-norm); here we go one step further and compute the TRAINING-INDUCED delta on-policy, which is exactly the per-cell construct the secondary DV asks for. NO teacher-forced cross-condition leaderboard at a fixed canned position — that pattern produced the #432→#456 incident and is explicitly banned by the marker-leakage-measurement rule.</p>

<h3>6.3 Statistical tests</h3>

<ul>
  <li><strong>H1:</strong> per-arm Spearman ρ(prior, leak_rate) across the 7-bystander panel × 3 arms = 3 ρ values; check positive AND range ≤ 0.40.</li>
  <li><strong>H2:</strong> per-arm Spearman ρ(cosine, leak_rate) at layer 21 (legacy default); check Arm A ≤ +0.10 AND Arm B ≥ +0.30 AND ordered A &lt; C &lt; B. Bootstrap-CI the per-arm ρs over 1000 panel-resamples to size the across-arm Δρ.</li>
  <li><strong>H3:</strong> per-arm standardized OLS regression <code>z(leak) ~ z(prior) + z(cosine_layer21)</code>. Check β coefficients + partial Spearman ρ(cosine, leak | prior). Run the [[collinearity-gate-pattern]] check up front: Pearson(|cosine|, prior) at n=7 — if &gt; 0.6 fall back to tercile-bucket median test + polynomial-residualization robustness check.</li>
  <li><strong>H4:</strong> identify the max-prior persona in each arm; check if it's the same across all 3 arms (predicted: yes, always <code>local_historian</code>); also report distance-to-that-home-persona as a third predictor in the H3 regression. Reported with LOW confidence given the non-zero baseline 5-way "seven" rate.</li>
</ul>

<h2>11. Decision Rationale</h2>

<p>All load-bearing hyperparameters inherit from #444's clean-result Reproducibility table. #444 itself validated these for the exact (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, LoRA, on-policy-suppression-cn, Elk County Courthouse fact) setting we use here. Per the inherit fast-path: a sibling that validated the value for this exact model + data carries over without re-running the literature search.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>lr=2e-4 (cosine, warmup_ratio=0.05).</strong> Why: matched to #444 (this single hparam value is what produced the 4 leakage-shape regimes that #444 reads off). Source: <code>#444</code>. Alternatives: 5e-5 (Tulu 3 SFT default; rejected — too slow for 900-row LoRA in 1 epoch); 5e-4 (rejected — #444 pilots saw training-loss instability).</li>
