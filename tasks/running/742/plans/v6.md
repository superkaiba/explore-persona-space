## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I'll reuse the data we already produced in #658: the model's internal activations for 50 different conversational contexts, plus how often the model actually shows each behavior in those contexts. Without training anything new, I'll measure how good a prediction *anybody* could ever make from those activations (capped by how noisy our "did the behavior show up" labels are), how much of that best-case a simple straight-line predictor already reaches, and how many more contexts we'd need before these numbers stop being too fuzzy to read. The honest deliverable is a *range*, not a single number: a floor (what the straight-line predictor gets) and a ceiling (the best any predictor could reach). At 50 contexts, a single number would be fake precision.

- **What I expect:** For most of the four safety-relevant behaviors I think the floor and ceiling will sit close together, which would mean the straight-line predictor is already near the best you can do and a fancier one wouldn't find much hidden information. Sycophancy and refusal are the two behaviors a quick chat re-analysis flagged as the ones where a regularized straight-line fit works well — but the numbers that re-analysis quoted (about 0.72 for sycophancy, 0.70 for refusal) do NOT match what #658 actually wrote to disk for the same straight-line fit (about 0.13 for sycophancy, 0.42 for refusal). The chat read used a different setup, so I won't treat its higher numbers as the floor. A first job is to re-run that straight-line fit on the saved data myself and bracket against the value I actually reproduce. Whether the chat re-analysis's higher number holds up is a separate question this task also answers. The learning curve will probably say we'd need a few hundred contexts, not 50, before the harder "exact information content" questions become answerable at all.

- **What would change my mind:** Two results would surprise me. If the ceiling turns out to be limited by how noisy our *judge* is rather than by the representation, that flips the headline from "the model doesn't encode this" to "we can't tell yet, and here's the judge-spend we'd need." And if a careful non-linear test finds real signal the straight-line predictor missed (more than a random shuffle would fake), that says the information is there but not linearly readable, which would reopen #658's stalled prediction chain.

## 1. Goal

On the existing #658 base-model representations (Qwen2.5-7B `v0(C)` per layer, judged behavior rates `E0(C,B)`, n=50 contexts), measure how much behavioral information linear decoding loses, the achievable decoding ceiling, and the sample size required — operationalized at n=50 as the per-behavior reliability ceiling `√(r_yy)`, the bracket `[ρ_lin, √(r_yy)]` bounding the linear-decoding information loss plus a LEACE+dCor test for any nonlinearly-extractable residual, and a learning curve extrapolating the contexts needed to resolve the gap.

This is a 0-GPU analysis task. It serves `docs/open_questions.md` `q:leak-predictor`: whether any base-model-readable quantity predicts where a fine-tuned behavior leaks, well enough to gate a fine-tune fleet. #658 found A3.2 (a mean answer-side activation summarizes behavior expression) FAILs for all 4 read-out behaviors (broad misalignment, harmful compliance, sycophancy, refusal) and that the 3 incidental PASSes are not separable from a random-projection control, while A3.3's linear read-out PASSes 4/4 (ρ up to 0.69). A chat re-analysis claims a regularized ridge on `v0` recovers sycophancy (ρ≈0.72) / refusal (ρ≈0.70) where #658's n=50 MLP overfit — but those chat-claim values do NOT match #658's persisted a33 ridge `ρ_lin` (sycophancy 0.1268, refusal 0.4223; broad_em 0.444, harmful_compliance 0.6921), so the chat read used a different representation/recipe/fit; this task re-fits the ridge from the persisted artifacts (Stage-0 step 0c) and brackets against the reproduced value, treating the chat number as a comparison point only. This task asks the foundational question underneath that disagreement: independent of any specific read-out direction or decoder, **how much behavioral information is present in `v0(C)`, how much does linear decoding leave on the table, what ceiling could any decoder reach, and is any of this estimable at n=50?**

## 2. Hypothesis (this is a MEASUREMENT task — the bracket is the deliverable, not a bet)

This task does not predict an effect direction; it produces estimable bounds. The three pre-registered hypotheses are framed as the readings the bracket / nonlinear-residual / learning-curve analyses can return, each falsifiable:

- **H1 (bracket / ceiling-limited).** For the read-out behaviors with dynamic range, `ρ_lin` sits within the bootstrap CI of `√(r_yy)` (a tight bracket, width `√(r_yy) − ρ_lin ≲ 0.10`). *Reading:* linear decoding is at the reliability ceiling, the information lost by linear decoding is ≈ 0 (its honest upper bound is small), and #658's MLP failure was an estimator artifact (the information was linearly present, the MLP overfit). *Falsified by:* a wide bracket (`√(r_yy) − ρ_lin > 0.20`) on a behavior whose `√(r_yy)` CI is itself tight.
- **H2 (nonlinear residual).** Where the bracket is wide (Stage-1 fires), a LEACE-erased-`v0` retains dCor/HSIC dependence on `E0` exceeding both the permutation null AND the shuffled-`E0` control-task null, AND the held-out post-LEACE linear residual is indistinguishable from its own null (so the residual dependence is genuinely beyond the linear class, not imperfect out-of-sample erasure). *Reading:* nonlinearly-extractable behavioral signal exists beyond the linear class — a YES/NO answer, never a magnitude. *Falsified by:* post-LEACE residual dCor indistinguishable from the permutation null at the pre-registered power floor (→ "no detectable nonlinear residual at this estimator/n," reported with the power floor) OR a held-out post-LEACE linear residual that is itself significant (→ "linear-erasure leakage unresolved," NOT a nonlinear YES).
- **H3 (sample complexity).** The Stage-2 learning curve, fit to `ρ_lin(n′)`, `√(r_yy)(n′)`, and `dCor(n′)` over `n′ ∈ {10,15,…,50}`, extrapolates that resolving a 0.05-R² gap or bringing the ceiling CI below a target width needs `n` in the low hundreds (with `d_eff ≤ ~10`), while MI-in-nats is capped at ~`ln n ≈ 3.9` nats and needs ~`e^I` samples — out of reach at n=50. *Reading:* names the `n` for a future context-generation round and states plainly which quantities are not deliverable now. *Falsified by:* a curve that is already flat/resolved at n=50 (then the bracket is already tight and Stage 2 is moot) OR a curve so noisy no extrapolation is supportable (then report the non-extrapolability as the finding).

## 3. Prior work

Verified against the task body's References + the arXiv MCP (abstracts read this session; values quoted, not recalled).

**Ceiling / reliability.**
- Attenuation identity (observed correlation = true correlation × `√(r_xx r_yy)`); the reliability ceiling `√(r_yy)` is the max correlation any predictor can reach against a noisy target. `Source: Spearman 1904` (original attenuation derivation).
- Noise-ceiling formalization: normalized correlation `CC_norm`, `CC_max` = max explainable correlation given the target's own trial-to-trial variability; estimated from the target's noise structure alone (no high-dim joint density needed). `Source: Schoppe et al. 2016, "Measuring the Performance of Neural Models", Front. Comput. Neurosci., DOI 10.3389/fncom.2016.00010` (verified title + DOI this session).
- CV-matched noise ceiling for *fitted* models: a ceiling compared to a cross-validated ρ must itself be cross-validated; comparing a pooled-reliability ceiling to a CV'd ρ inflates the apparent gap. `Source: Storrs, Khaligh-Razavi & Kriegeskorte 2020, "Noise ceiling on the crossvalidated performance of reweighted models of representational dissimilarity", bioRxiv 10.1101/2020.03.23.003046` (verified this session).
- Model-free binary Bayes-error ceiling `β = E_C[min(E0, 1−E0)]` (mean of the labels expressing class-assignment uncertainty; model-free, instance-free, no hyperparameters). `Source: Ishida et al. 2022, arXiv:2202.00395` (verified abstract this session).

**Information-loss / V-information.**
- Predictive V-information: usable information relative to a function class; ridge R² *is* the linear-class V-information term; V-information can be *created* through computation, violating the data-processing inequality — so there is no model-free "total information" and the object is class-relative, never Shannon MI in nats. `Source: Xu et al. 2020, arXiv:2002.10689` (verified this session).
- Control tasks: associate inputs with random outputs; a good probe is *selective* (high task accuracy, low control-task accuracy). Mandatory when `d ≫ n` to separate "representation encodes it" from "the probe memorized it." `Source: Hewitt & Liang 2019, arXiv:1909.03368` (verified this session).
- MDL / description-length probing (variational + online coding) — more stable than accuracy against random baselines; optional robustness for "does the apparent gain survive a codelength comparison." `Source: Voita & Titov 2020, arXiv:2003.12298` (verified this session).
- Pointwise V-information (PVI) per instance — detects whether an apparent gain concentrates in 2-3 overfit contexts. `Source: Ethayarajh et al. 2022, arXiv:2110.08420` (verified this session).
- Information-theoretic probing as MI estimation; "select the highest-performing probe for a tighter estimate" — the methodological caution against treating the n=50 MLP as an info upper bound. `Source: Pimentel et al. 2020, arXiv:2004.03061` (verified this session); LEACE for the linear erasure step `Source: Belrose et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.03819, "LEACE: Perfect linear concept erasure in closed form"` (verified title this session — provably prevents all linear classifiers from detecting the concept, closed-form, minimal-change).

**Sample-complexity / feasibility.**
- Any distribution-free high-confidence lower bound on MI from `N` samples cannot exceed `O(ln N)`; at n=50 that caps estimable MI at ~`ln 50 ≈ 3.9` nats. `Source: McAllester & Stratos 2020, arXiv:1811.04251` (verified verbatim this session).
- kNN MI estimators require sample count scaling *exponentially* with true MI; accurate MI between strongly dependent variables needs prohibitively large `N`. `Source: Gao et al. 2015, arXiv:1411.2003` (verified this session).
- Variational MI estimators (MINE-family) have variance growing *exponentially* with true MI and fail self-consistency. `Source: Song & Ermon 2020, arXiv:1910.06222` (verified this session).
- Distance correlation: `dCor = 0` iff independent; tests dependence (incl. nonlinear) via Euclidean inter-sample distances. `Source: Székely, Rizzo & Bakirov 2007, arXiv:0803.4101` (verified title this session).
- Power of kernel/distance nonparametric independence tests drops *polynomially* with dimension against fair alternatives — the formal justification for reducing `v0` (d=3584) to a low `d_eff` before any dCor/HSIC test. `Source: Reddi et al. 2015, arXiv:1406.2083` (verified verbatim this session).
- Minimax nonparametric regression rate degrades with input dimension (curse of dimensionality) — the regression-side reason to reduce `d`. `Source: Stone 1982, "Optimal Global Rates of Convergence for Nonparametric Regression", Ann. Statist. 10(4):1040-1053`.
- CV error bars are large at small `N` (≈±10% at n=100) and the standard-error-across-folds *underestimates* them — bootstrap over the contexts, never std-across-folds. `Source: Varoquaux 2018, arXiv:1706.07581` (verified verbatim this session) + `Source: Bengio & Grandvalet 2004, "No Unbiased Estimator of the Variance of K-Fold Cross-Validation", JMLR 5:1089-1105`.

1. **`r_yy` two agreeing ways — the estimator pair FORKS on the per-behavior `rollouts/probe` count (see §11 row 1; the `per_probe` shape is sharply heterogeneous across behaviors, §12 row 2).** Determine each cell's `rollouts/probe` from `per_probe` before choosing the pair:
   - **Behaviors with ≥2 rollouts/probe** (sycophancy: 200 probes × 10 rollouts, `n_judged≈2000`; broad_em: 8 probes × 50 rollouts, `n_judged≈400`) → use BOTH reads as two agreeing estimators:
     - **(i) Split-half-OVER-ROLLOUTS + Spearman-Brown.** Randomly split the rollouts into two halves per probe, recompute the per-context rate from each half, correlate the two half-rate vectors across the contexts to get `r_half`, then `r_yy = 2 r_half / (1 + r_half)`. Average over `S = 200` random split seeds.
     - **(ii) Binomial-variance decomposition** (below).
   - **Behaviors with 1 rollout/probe** (harmful_compliance: ~115 probes × 1 rollout, `n_judged≈115`; refusal: ~214 probes × 1 rollout, `n_judged≈214`) → there are NO within-probe rollouts to split, so split-half-over-rollouts is undefined. Use instead:
     - **(i′) Split-half-OVER-PROBES + Spearman-Brown** (the classical Spearman-Brown construction): split the probe SET into two halves, compute `E0` per context from each probe-half, correlate the two half-`E0` vectors across contexts to get `r_half`, then `r_yy = 2 r_half / (1 + r_half)`. Average over `S = 200` random probe-split seeds.
     - **(ii) Binomial-variance decomposition** (below), with the cell-actual `m`.
   - **(ii) Binomial-variance decomposition** (computable for EVERY behavior, with that behavior's effective `m`). `SP = Var_C(E0) − mean_C[ p̂(1−p̂)/m_cell ]` (signal power = total context-variance minus mean within-context binomial sampling variance), `ceiling² = SP / Var_C(E0)`, `√(r_yy) = √(SP/Var_C(E0))`. **`m_cell = n_judged` for that (context, behavior) cell — use the cell-actual `n_judged` (≈2000 sycophancy / ≈400 broad_em / ≈115 harmful_compliance / ≈214 refusal), NEVER a blanket `m=2000`.** The two reads of the per-behavior pair must agree within their bootstrap CIs (a built-in consistency check; disagreement is itself reported, see §7).

3. **CV-match the ceiling to LOCO.** Compute `√(r_yy)` under the SAME leave-one-context-out folding used for `ρ_lin` (held-out context excluded from the reliability estimate per fold, averaged over folds) — never a pooled ceiling against a CV'd ρ (Storrs 2020).

5. **Lay #658's `ρ_lin` alongside.** Read the per-behavior best held-out ridge ρ from #658's `a33_cells.json` (read-out) / the ridge path of `analyzer_body_data.json`. Report the bracket `[ρ_lin, √(r_yy)]` + width per behavior×genre.
6. **Cross-check the existing "noise floor."** Confirm whether #658's `noise_floor` p95 values (the 0.68-0.93 range the body flags for `self_report`/`persona_drift`) equal `√(r_yy)`. They should NOT — the noise floor is a *null* (p95 of probe-resample correlations, a permutation-style baseline) while `√(r_yy)` is a *ceiling* (reliability of the target). Reporting whether #658 conflated the two is a Stage-0 deliverable.
7. **Free companion:** the model-free binary Bayes-error ceiling `β = E_C[min(E0, 1−E0)]` (Ishida 2022), reported per behavior (no model, no fit).

**Internal gate (per behavior × genre):**
- `ρ_lin ≈ √(r_yy)` within the bootstrap CI → **ceiling-limited**; the linear-decoding information loss ≈ 0 (small honest upper bound); record the verdict and **skip Stage 1** for that behavior×genre.
- `ρ_lin ≪ √(r_yy)` (bracket CI excludes 0 width) → **headroom** → run Stage 1 for that behavior×genre.

### Stage 1 — is *any* of the headroom nonlinearly extractable? (only where the bracket is wide)

Per qualifying behavior × genre:

0. **Synthetic dCor power pre-check (run BEFORE the real-data permutation test).** Before running the dCor permutation on the real LEACE-residual, run a synthetic power-check at `d_eff = 10`, `n = 50`, `B = 10000` perms, with a planted nonlinear-residual effect at the stated effect floor (~0.10 partial correlation, §11 row 3 / §12 row 6). If the realized power is ≥ 0.8, proceed with `d_eff = 10`. **If realized power < 0.8:** (a) REPORT any subsequent null as "indistinguishable from null given variance" — never as "no nonlinear signal" (a variance-limited test cannot license a no-signal claim); AND (b) attempt the largest `d_eff` that PCA can still afford — relax `d_eff` to 15 / 20 only while the cumulative PCA variance retained stays > 80% — re-running the power-check at each candidate `d_eff` and picking the largest dim that recovers ≥ 0.8 power without dropping below the 80%-variance floor. The chosen `d_eff` + its realized power are reported in `stage1_leace_dcor.json`.
1. **PCA-reduce `v0` to `d_eff` dims** (the single highest-leverage move; fit PCA on the training folds only, no leakage). Target `d_eff ≈ 10` (or the relaxed value the step-0 power pre-check selected); the exact value is grounded against the Stage-1 dCor power floor — choose the largest `d_eff` at which the B=10000 permutation dCor test retains ≥0.8 power against the smallest detectable nonlinear-residual effect at n=50 (effect floor stated §11; power-vs-dimension decay per Reddi 2015) AND the cumulative PCA variance retained stays > 80%. Use the SAME best layer #658 locked per behavior (`locked_recipe.json`), plus the global-best layer as a robustness check.
2. **LEACE** (Belrose 2023, closed-form, oblivious linear-erasure guarantee, minimal change) to erase the linear `E0` signal from the PCA-reduced `v0`: fit the LEACE eraser on the training folds, apply to held-out. After erasure, no linear classifier can recover `E0` *on the training fold* (the closed-form guarantee; assert in the unit test, §13 test 2). The held-out fold's residual is NOT covered by that guarantee — step 2.5 checks it empirically.
   - **Step 2.5 (NEW — held-out post-LEACE linear-leakage diagnostic; required after LEACE erasure, BEFORE the dCor read).** LEACE's closed-form guarantee covers the TRAINING fold only; at `d_eff≈10` with 49-sample LOCO held-out folds the held-out residual can carry sampling-variance linear dependence on `E0`, and a Stage-1 dCor positive read off that residual would be MIS-INTERPRETABLE (it could be residual *linear* leakage through imperfect out-of-sample LEACE rather than *nonlinear* signal beyond the linear class). The Hewitt-Liang control-task null targets probe-memorization (a different failure mode) and cannot disambiguate this. So, on the HELD-OUT LOCO fold (the SAME fold the dCor read uses):
     - Fit a ridge linear probe from the LEACE-erased `v0` to `E0` (nested-CV λ over the §11 row 3 / step-0c grid `{1e-2,1e-1,1,10,100,1e3}`; same recipe as the Stage-0 ρ_lin re-fit).
     - Compute the linear-residual statistic: held-out Spearman ρ between predicted and actual `E0` on the LEACE-erased residual (`post_leace_linear_residual_rho`).
     - Compute a permutation null on the same held-out fold (B = 10000 shuffles of `E0`) → `post_leace_linear_residual_null_ci`.
     - Persist per (behavior, genre, d_eff): `post_leace_linear_residual_rho`, `post_leace_linear_residual_null_ci`, `post_leace_linear_pass` (= true iff `|rho|` is within the null CI, i.e. the held-out residual carries no detectable linear dependence).
3. **dCor permutation test** between the LEACE-erased `v0` and `E0`, B=10000 permutations (label-permutation null). HSIC (Gretton 2005, RBF kernel, median-heuristic bandwidth) as a robustness pair. A residual dCor exceeding the permutation null = "nonlinear signal beyond the linear class" — a YES/NO, never a magnitude — **subject to the step-2.5 held-out linear-leakage gate** (see the Stage-1 YES rule in §6 / §11).
4. **Control-task / selectivity null** (Hewitt-Liang 2019): refit the entire Stage-1 pipeline on *shuffled* `E0` (random context→rate reassignment, seeded); report any residual dCor net of the shuffle null. Mandatory at `d=3584 ≫ n=50`. The #658 random-projection (`attn`) recipe is the additional "did structure beat random" floor for the read.
5. **Sample-efficient nonlinear decoder** (kernel-ridge with median-heuristic bandwidth, or the LEACE-residual probe) — **NEVER the n=50 MLP** (which is exactly what #658 over-fit on). "kernel-ridge ≤ ridge" means "no measured gain at this estimator/n," not "no nonlinear info" — stated as the explicit bound.
6. **Optional robustness:** MDL / online-codelength gap (Voita-Titov 2020) between the linear and nonlinear probes; PVI-per-context (Ethayarajh 2022) to flag whether any apparent gain concentrates in 2-3 overfit contexts. Exploratory, not gating.

### Stage 2 — the learning curve (the "how many contexts" experiment)

1. **Subsample** `n′ ∈ {10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50}`, `B_repeat = 200` resamples per `n′` (without-replacement subsets of the 50 contexts, seeded).
2. **At each `n′`** compute `ρ_lin(n′)`, `√(r_yy)(n′)`, and `dCor(n′)` with bootstrap variance.
3. **Extrapolate** (fit the standard inverse-power learning-curve form `metric(n) = a − b·n^{−c}` to the means, with bootstrap CIs on the fit) the `n` required to (a) resolve a target gap (0.05 R²) and (b) bring the `√(r_yy)` CI half-width below a target (0.05). Report the extrapolated `n` with its CI; if the fit is not supportable (CIs span an order of magnitude), report the non-extrapolability as the finding.
4. **Report against the theory verdict:** MI-in-nats capped at ~`ln 50 ≈ 3.9` nats and needing ~`e^I` samples (McAllester-Stratos / Gao / Song-Ermon) — *out of reach now, stated plainly*; the gap/ceiling need `n` in the low hundreds *with* `d_eff ≤ ~10` (Stone 1982 + Varoquaux 2018); the dependence yes/no IS feasible at n=50.

## 11. Decision rationale (Source: every load-bearing methodological choice)

This task trains no model, so there are no training hyperparameters. The load-bearing choices are the *estimators* and *grounding constants*; each carries a `Source:`.

1. **Reliability estimator = a PER-BEHAVIOR pair of two agreeing ways, NOT test-retest — the pair FORKS on the behavior's `rollouts/probe` count.** The `E0` `per_probe` shape is sharply heterogeneous across behaviors (§12 row 2): sycophancy 200 probes × 10 rollouts (`n_judged≈2000`); broad_em 8 probes × 50 rollouts (`n_judged≈400`); harmful_compliance ~115 probes × 1 rollout (`n_judged≈115`); refusal ~214 probes × 1 rollout (`n_judged≈214`); the other 6 behaviors mostly 8 probes with `n_judged = 8` or `None`. The split-half-over-rollouts estimator is well-posed ONLY where ≥2 rollouts/probe exist, so the estimator pair is chosen per behavior:
   - **Behaviors with ≥2 rollouts/probe (sycophancy, broad_em):** use BOTH (a) **split-half-OVER-ROLLOUTS + Spearman-Brown** (`r_yy = 2 r_half/(1+r_half)` from the rollout-half split, `S=200` seeds) AND (b) **binomial-variance decomposition** (`SP = Var_C(E0) − mean_C[p̂(1−p̂)/m_cell]`, `ceiling² = SP/Var_C(E0)`) as two agreeing reads.
   - **Behaviors with 1 rollout/probe (harmful_compliance, refusal):** there are no within-probe rollouts to split, so use (a′) **split-half-OVER-PROBES + Spearman-Brown** (the classical Spearman-Brown construction: split the PROBE set into two halves, compute `E0` per context from each half, correlate across contexts → `r_half`, then `r_yy = 2 r_half/(1+r_half)`, `S=200` probe-split seeds) AND (b) **binomial-variance decomposition** with the cell-actual `m`.
   - **The binomial-variance decomposition (b) is computable for EVERY behavior** — it needs only the per-cell `m` (rollouts) and rate `p̂`, both available per-cell from `n_judged` and `rate` — but with the behavior's OWN effective `m_cell = n_judged` (≈2000 sycophancy / ≈400 broad_em / ≈115 harmful_compliance / ≈214 refusal), NEVER a blanket `m=2000`.
   - **The cross-check is PER BEHAVIOR:** the two reads of that behavior's pair must agree within their bootstrap CIs (disagreement is reported — §7: for harmful_compliance / refusal a > 0.10 `√(r_yy)` disagreement reads "reliability ceiling under-determined for 1-rollout-per-probe behaviors," and the bracket then bounds only sycophancy + broad_em definitively). For these two 1-rollout behaviors, trust split-half-over-probes as the ceiling when the estimators diverge — don't average (§6 analyzer-attend).
   - Why this fork (not a uniform pair): test-retest needs a second full generation pass (a GPU spend we are avoiding); split-half (over rollouts where available, else over probes) + binomial-variance are computable from the existing `per_probe`/`n_judged` structure alone (verified present), and two independent estimators cross-check each other. The 1-rollout-per-probe behaviors simply have no within-probe rollout to split, which is why the over-probes construction substitutes.
   - Source: `Spearman 1904` (attenuation identity + the Spearman-Brown split-half formula, original derivation) + `Schoppe et al. 2016, DOI 10.3389/fncom.2016.00010` (the `CC_max`/noise-ceiling formalization, the modern standard; verified title + DOI this session).
   - Alternatives rejected: test-retest (needs new generation = GPU); Cronbach's α (assumes tau-equivalence across probes, violated by the heterogeneous probe pool); a uniform split-half-over-rollouts (undefined for the 1-rollout-per-probe behaviors).

2. **CV-match the ceiling to the LOCO protocol (never pool the ceiling against a CV'd ρ).**
   - What: `√(r_yy)` computed under the same leave-one-context-out folding as `ρ_lin`.
   - Why: comparing a pooled-reliability ceiling to a cross-validated ρ inflates the apparent gap (the bracket would read artificially wide).
   - Source: `Storrs, Khaligh-Razavi & Kriegeskorte 2020, "Noise ceiling on the crossvalidated performance of reweighted models of representational dissimilarity", bioRxiv 10.1101/2020.03.23.003046` (the crossvalidated-noise-ceiling-for-fitted-models paper; verified this session).

3. **PCA-reduce `v0` to `d_eff ≈ 10` before any dCor/regression.**
   - What: PCA on train folds to `d_eff`, target ~10, exact value set so the dCor permutation test holds ≥0.8 power against the smallest detectable nonlinear-residual effect at n=50.
   - Why: at d=3584 ≫ n=50, nonparametric independence-test power and nonparametric regression rate both collapse; the reduction restores test power and pushes regression toward the parametric regime.
   - Source: `Reddi et al. 2015, arXiv:1406.2083` ("the power of these tests actually drops polynomially with increasing dimension against fair alternatives" — verified verbatim) + `Stone 1982, Ann. Statist. 10(4):1040-1053` (minimax nonparametric regression rate degrades with dimension) + `Varoquaux 2018, arXiv:1706.07581` (small-n CV error bars; verified verbatim).
   - Effect floor for the power computation: the smallest residual partial-correlation worth detecting is set to the bracket-width threshold (a residual dCor corresponding to ~0.10 partial correlation); `d_eff` is chosen as the largest dim retaining ≥0.8 power at that floor over B=10000 perms (stated as the runtime selection rule, reported in `stage1_leace_dcor.json`). The same λ grid `{1e-2,1e-1,1,10,100,1e3}` + nested-CV is reused by the held-out post-LEACE linear-leakage ridge probe (Stage-1 step 2.5).

4. **LEACE for the linear erasure.**
   - What: closed-form LEAst-squares Concept Erasure to remove all linearly-decodable `E0` signal from the (PCA-reduced) `v0`, fit on train folds.
   - Why: closed-form (no SGD, stable at small n), provably prevents *all* linear classifiers from recovering the concept *on the fit fold*, changes the embedding minimally — so any post-erasure dependence is genuinely beyond the linear class. **The guarantee is TRAIN-FOLD ONLY**, which is exactly why the held-out post-LEACE linear-leakage diagnostic (Stage-1 step 2.5) is required to gate the Stage-1 YES — see row 11b.
   - Source: `Belrose et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.03819, "LEACE: Perfect linear concept erasure in closed form"` (verified title + abstract this session: "closed-form method which provably prevents all linear classifiers from detecting a concept while changing the embedding as little as possible").

5. **dCor (primary) + HSIC (robustness) for residual dependence, permutation-tested.**
   - What: distance correlation between LEACE-erased `v0` and `E0`, B=10000 label-permutation null; HSIC (RBF, median-heuristic bandwidth) as a second test.
   - Why: `dCor = 0` iff independent (captures nonlinear dependence a Pearson/Spearman residual would miss); permutation null is exact at small n.
   - Source: `Székely, Rizzo & Bakirov 2007, arXiv:0803.4101` ("distance correlation is zero only if the random vectors are independent" — verified) + `Gretton et al. 2005` (HSIC, Algorithmic Learning Theory / NeurIPS). Choice of dCor as primary: it is parameter-free (no kernel bandwidth), so the headline does not hinge on a bandwidth choice; HSIC is the bandwidth-robustness companion.

6. **Bootstrap over the 50 contexts (B=2000), NEVER std-across-folds.**
   - What: cluster-bootstrap over contexts for every CI.
   - Why: the standard error across CV folds *underestimates* the true CV variance; at n=50 the honest CI is ±0.10-0.15 and must come from resampling the contexts.
   - Source: `Bengio & Grandvalet 2004, "No Unbiased Estimator of the Variance of K-Fold Cross-Validation", JMLR 5:1089-1105` + `Varoquaux 2018, arXiv:1706.07581` ("The standard error across folds strongly underestimates them" — verified verbatim). B=2000 over contexts; #742's bootstrap is its own new estimator with seed 742. #658's `aggregate.json` does not persist its bootstrap seed, so cross-experiment seed-comparability is asserted only to bootstrap-count level (B=2000), not seed level.

7. **Judge model `claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`.**
   - What: the Stage-0 judge rerun uses the project-standard judge, pinned via `JUDGE_MODEL=claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`.
   - Why: one consistent judge across the project; #658's `E0` was judged with exactly this model (verified: `E0_expression.json` `judge_model` field), so the rerun is the same construct re-measured — required for the variance decomposition to be meaningful.
   - Source: `CLAUDE.md "Critical Rules"` (judge rule) + `Source: #658` (`E0_expression.json judge_model = claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`, verified this session).

8. **Anthropic Batch API for the judge rerun (acknowledging the sync-vs-batch tension).**
   - What: route the Stage-0 judge rerun through `eval.batch_judge` (the #663-hardened deadline-bounded client), Anthropic Batch API.
   - Tension (acknowledged explicitly): `docs/api_throughput_guidelines.md` puts the sync-vs-Batch COST/wall-clock crossover at ~200,000 calls, and the Stage-0 rerun is ~8,000 calls — well BELOW that crossover — so by that table SYNC would be marginally cheaper / faster in wall-clock terms. Meanwhile CLAUDE.md's standing rule says "Batch API when the judge set is large (≳ a few thousand calls)." These are RECONCILABLE: the docs file is about cost / wall-clock crossover, while the CLAUDE.md rule is about operator-time / no-latency-need / free self-harvest at `expires_at`.
   - Why (the routing decision + its reason): route the ~8k-call rerun through Batch API for OPERATOR-TIME reasons — the rerun has no latency need (the variance term is computed once and read off the result file), the Batch SLA self-harvests at `expires_at` without orchestrator polling overhead, and it matches the CLAUDE.md "judge set ≳ a few thousand → Batch API" standing rule. **The implementer MAY re-route to sync at execution time without a plan change** — `Var_judge` is computed identically either way (§6 analyzer-attend); if the realized rerun set shrinks below ~2k (e.g. a context-subset descope), sync's marginal cost/wall-clock edge would dominate.
   - Source: `docs/api_throughput_guidelines.md` (the N × model × {latency, cost} table; the ~200k-call sync-vs-Batch crossover) + `CLAUDE.md "Critical Rules"` (the "judge set ≳ a few thousand → Batch API" standing rule) + `src/explore_persona_space/llm/api_dispatch.py`. Realized Batch size: `R_rerun=2 × 50 contexts × 4 read-out behaviors × 1 genre (Betley) × J=20 completions/cell ≈ 8,000 calls` (default; a ±genre / ±J knob bounded above by ~32k if both genres × all-50-rollouts is later wanted).

9. **n=50 sample-complexity ceiling — why MI-in-nats is NOT a deliverable.**
   - What: the plan never reports MI in nats; it reports class-relative usable information (ridge R² as the linear V-information term) and bounds.
   - Why: any distribution-free high-confidence MI lower bound from N samples is capped at `O(ln N)` (≈3.9 nats at n=50), and kNN/variational MI estimators need samples scaling/​varying *exponentially* with true MI.
   - Source: `McAllester & Stratos 2020, arXiv:1811.04251` ("any distribution-free high-confidence lower bound on mutual information estimated from N samples cannot be larger than O(ln N)" — verified verbatim) + `Gao et al. 2015, arXiv:1411.2003` (kNN MI requires sample count scaling exponentially with true MI — verified) + `Song & Ermon 2020, arXiv:1910.06222` (variational MI variance grows exponentially with true MI — verified) + `Xu et al. 2020, arXiv:2002.10689` (V-information as the estimable class-relative object — verified).

10. **The disattenuation caveat — report the BRACKET, not the disattenuated ρ.**
   - What: the headline is `[ρ_lin, √(r_yy)]` + width; `ρ_lin/√(r_yy)` is shown only in the exploratory dump with an instability caveat.
   - Why: dividing a CV'd ρ by an estimated `√(r_yy)` is unstable at small n (a noisy denominator near 0.8 inflates and destabilizes the ratio); the bracket is the honest, stable form.
   - Source: `Storrs et al. 2020` (CV-matched ceiling) + the body's own Methodological backbone ("disattenuation … is unstable at small n — report the bracket, not the disattenuated ρ").

11. **Control-task / selectivity null (mandatory at d ≫ n).**
   - What: refit the Stage-1 pipeline on shuffled `E0`; report residual net of the shuffle.
   - Why: at d=3584 ≫ n=50, a probe can fit noise; the control task (random labels) measures the probe's memorization capacity, and a real signal must be *selective* (high on the true task, low on the control).
   - Source: `Hewitt & Liang 2019, arXiv:1909.03368` (control tasks + selectivity — verified). The #658 random-projection (`attn`) control is the additional "did structure beat random" floor.

11b. **Held-out post-LEACE linear-leakage diagnostic (the structural control gating the Stage-1 YES).**
   - What: on the held-out LOCO fold (the SAME fold the dCor read uses), fit a ridge linear probe from the LEACE-erased `v0` to `E0` (nested-CV λ over the row-3 grid), compute the held-out Spearman `post_leace_linear_residual_rho`, its B=10000 shuffle null CI, and `post_leace_linear_pass` (= true iff `|rho|` within the null CI). The Stage-1 YES requires BOTH dCor-above-nulls AND `post_leace_linear_pass = true`; `(a)∧¬(b)` → `linear-erasure-leakage-unresolved` (§6 Stage-1 YES rule).
   - Why: LEACE's closed-form guarantee binds on the TRAINING fold only (row 4); at `d_eff≈10` with 49-sample held-out folds the held-out residual can carry sampling-variance LINEAR dependence on `E0`, and a dCor positive read off it would be mis-interpretable as *nonlinear* signal when it is residual *linear* leakage through imperfect out-of-sample erasure. The Hewitt-Liang control-task null (row 11) catches probe-memorization, a DIFFERENT failure mode, and cannot disambiguate this one. This is the alternatives-critic-asked baseline.
