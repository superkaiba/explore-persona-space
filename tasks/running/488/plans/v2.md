## 1. Goal

Determine whether base-model output-distribution distance predicts on-policy marker transfer in a non-saturated training regime, and whether that prediction survives partialling out source-stylized-ness.

The parent line (#406 → #469) carried two unresolved claims: the off-policy −0.44 correlation, and its on-policy reduction to a single pre-saturation checkpoint riding on 3 stylized personas. This experiment fixes both confounders in ONE design — (a) a lighter regime so transfer cells carry dynamic range, (b) a richer transformation set so the high-divergence region isn't carried by 3 personas, (c) an explicit stylized-source partial — and asks whether geometry has *independent* predictive power.

## 2. Prior Work

**Internal lineage.**
- **#406** (off-policy, MODERATE): length-partial Spearman ρ = −0.436, 95% CI [−0.561, −0.293], n=240 ordered pairs over 16 transformations; outcome = first-token marker emission after a Claude-written canned answer. Layer-11 residual-cosine ρ = −0.71 was the deepest descriptive predictor but not pre-locked.
- **#460** (on-policy re-run, same 16 conditions, marker-at-end / marker-only loss, 5 epochs × 300 rows × lr=1e-5): trained ΔG saturates — 99% of 240 cells within 0.1 nat of ceiling at endpoint; length-partial ρ(D, ΔG) = −0.11, null.
- **#462** (sub-epoch sweep of the same rig at lr=1e-5, ckpts at epochs {1,2,3,5}): epoch 1 = 75.8% of cells at-ceiling, ρ(D, ΔG) = −0.27 (CI excludes 0); epoch 2 = 98.75% at-ceiling, ρ collapses to ≈ −0.11. The signal at epoch 1 rides on 3 stylized sources (pirate/comedian/villain): 56 of 58 off-ceiling cells touch one of them; helpful-assistant + software-engineer sources are already saturated at epoch 1.
- **#456** (on-policy emission re-eval): the on-policy emission rate (substring `' ※'` containment) gave 0.90 source rate vs ≤ 0.22 on the next-highest persona — the construct-valid DV when the model writes its own answer.
- **#477 step-lever calibration** (planner memory): at lr=1e-5 the implant saturates between step 0 and step 6; lr=2e-6 is the only LR where dense per-fraction sub-epoch checkpoints land in-band.

**External.**
- **Persona Vectors** (Chen et al., 2025, arXiv 2507.21509): persona axes live in residual stream; activation-shift partialling is the standard methodology for separating "did the trait shift" from "did the model shift along a confounded axis." Methodologically: the *partial-out a candidate-proxy* design is the published recipe.
- **Persona Features Control EM** (Wang/Mossing et al., 2025, arXiv 2506.19823): the EM-feature persona is a generic "evil character" axis, not a domain axis — same direction as #406's finding that the stylized personas dominate the signal.
- **Rao-Blackwellized sequence-level JS** (Zhang/Amini/Vieira/Cotterell 2025, arXiv 2504.10637): canonical full-response sequence-level JS estimator, per `.claude/rules/persona-distance-metrics.md`. Implementation reusable from `scripts/issue444_persona_distance_topic.py::js_vs_reference()`.

**Constraint these results put on the design.**
1. The lr=1e-5 / 5-epoch / 300-row recipe (#460/#462) is too hot — saturates by epoch 2 and only ~22% of cells off-ceiling at epoch 1 with 56/58 touching stylized sources. Must drop ≥ 2 levers (lr + rows + rank).
2. Cannot trust ΔG as primary DV in the light regime — when ΔG is small the rank correlation against base-distance is noise-dominated. **Emission rate (Bernoulli on N samples × Q questions per cell) is the construct-valid DV** for the on-policy emission construct AND has natural [0,1] dynamic range when the implant is graded.
3. Must add ≥ 12 non-stylized transformations to span high-divergence regions, or the stylized covariate will be perfectly collinear with high-divergence cells (no identification of the H2 partial).

## 3. Hypothesis

Stated falsifiably, with quantitative pre-thresholds. **Every H1/H2/H3 statistic is computed on OFF-DIAGONAL cells only (i ≠ j; 27×26 = 702 ordered pairs).** Diagonals are implant sanity, not transfer — including them injects mechanical JS≈0 / high-emission leverage points that would manufacture the negative ρ on their own (the parent #406 also reported its headline on n=240 off-diagonal). Diagonals are separately reported as per-source implant-strength controls (see §6 fix D).

**H1 (primary).** Across the 702 off-diagonal cells in a non-saturated regime, length-partial Spearman ρ(JS, emission-rate) is negative and the 95% **dyadic cluster-bootstrap** CI (resampling source-transformations AND target-transformations independently; see §6.2) excludes 0 on **at least one sub-epoch checkpoint** (so checkpoint cherry-picking doesn't masquerade as a finding, but we don't pre-pick which one). PASS pre-threshold: |partial ρ| ≥ 0.20.

**H2 (decisive sharpening of H1; three-outcome).** The same partial ρ — additionally controlling for `is_stylized_source` (binary, see §4) AND for `stylization_score` (graded RB sequence-level JS of source-vs-default-assistant, see §4) — is reported under THREE possible verdicts (the third is new vs the previous draft, per fix F):
- **SURVIVES** = remains negative with |ρ| ≥ 0.15 AND dyadic-bootstrap CI excludes 0 under BOTH binary and graded partials.
- **NULL (stylized-source artifact)** = CI crosses 0 / |ρ| < 0.15 under partialling, **AND** the H2 identification check (§6.2) shows there IS residual non-stylized support to read against (Pearson(stylization_score, JS) ≤ 0.85 AND ≥ 5 non-stylized cells sit in the high-JS band). Only in that case is "geometry was a stylized-source proxy" supported.
- **UNIDENTIFIABLE** = the identification check fails (Pearson > 0.85 OR fewer than 5 non-stylized high-JS cells). In this regime a null after partialling out a near-duplicate of the predictor is NOT informative; the body reports the descriptive H1 + flags H2 as unanswered.

**H3 (mechanism / trajectory).** At the latest pre-saturation checkpoint, the rank order of off-diagonal cells lighting up is positively rank-correlated with base-model proximity (1 − JS). PASS: Spearman ρ ≥ +0.30 between (1 − JS) and the fraction of fracs at which a given cell has emitted, p < 0.05. H3 is reported BOTH pooled across sources AND within-source (per-source rank correlation of (1−JS_ij over j≠i) vs frac-emitted) — the within-source version is the cleaner read because the pooled version is confounded by which source implants fastest (see §6.3 analyzer-must-report bullets).

**Diagonal-adjusted statistic (required for H1/H2 SURVIVES verdicts; per fix C).** H1/H2 SURVIVES is supported ONLY IF the negative ρ also survives a diagonal-adjustment that separates "geometry predicts transfer" from "geometry predicts whether the source implanted at all" — the parent's open question. Two adjustments, both reported:
- (a) Partial out the source's own diagonal implant strength `emission_ii` in addition to length + stylization.
- (b) Use a normalized outcome `emission_ij / max(emission_ii, ε)` instead of raw `emission_ij`, with an explicit low-diagonal exclusion: **drop any source whose `emission_ii < 0.5` at the chosen frac as a sensitivity check**.

If (a) and (b) move ρ toward 0 (e.g. |ρ| drops below 0.15 or CI crosses 0), the unadjusted H1 was carried by implant-strength heterogeneity, not by transfer geometry — report as **NULL after diagonal-adjustment** rather than SURVIVES.

Pre-registered null states: H1 ρ = 0, H2 ρ = 0, H3 ρ = 0. Statistical primary = **dyadic cluster-bootstrap** (see §6.2); secondary = **crossed random-effects** mixed-model `emission ~ JS + length + (1|source) + (1|target) + (1|class_pair)`. PASS requires the dyadic CI to exclude 0; the random-effects model is reported as a survival check.

### 4.3 Source-stylized-ness covariate (resolves clarifier flag 3)

**Both** a binary and a graded operationalization, run in parallel and reported:

(a) **Binary `is_stylized_source`** — hand-coded once at planning time, frozen:
- 1 for: A3 (pirate), A4 (comedian), A5 (villain).
- 0 for: every other source.
- Rationale: these are the 3 sources #469's re-analysis names; the binary directly tests "is the −0.44 carried by these specific 3?"

(b) **Graded `stylization_score`** — a per-source distance-to-default-assistant, computed at predictor-pass time on Q'_probe (50 questions) by REUSING the SAME RB sequence-level machinery as the JS predictor — just measured source-vs-default instead of source-vs-target:
- For each source T_i, compute the RB sequence-level JS divergence between the base model conditioned on T_i and the base model conditioned on the no-system-prompt baseline (B1 / default assistant), over Q'_probe.
- Concretely: sample R=8 base-model responses under both T_i and the baseline (temp=1.0/top_p=1.0); teacher-force each sampled response through both conditioned models; full-vocab per-position JS over the response; length-normalize; mean over probes. Identical primitive to `js_vs_reference()` in `scripts/issue444_persona_distance_topic.py`.
- `stylization_score[i]` is just `JS(T_i ‖ no-system)` — a strong stylistic persona (pirate, comedian) has high JS to the default assistant; a plain technical wrapper has near-zero JS.
- Implementation: ~50 LOC in `scripts/i488_stylization_score.py`, a near-trivial wrapper that calls the SAME `js_vs_reference()` machinery with REFERENCE pinned to the no-system baseline.

**Important collinearity caveat (by construction).** Because `stylization_score[i]` is `JS(T_i ‖ default)` and the predictor `JS_ij` is `JS(T_i ‖ T_j)`, the two share the T_i argument and are correlated by construction when T_j is far from the default (or when T_j IS the default, since then `JS_ij = stylization_score[i]` exactly). This is precisely why the §6.2 collinearity gate (tercile median + polynomial residualization) is pre-registered as the robustness backstop — a high Pearson(|stylization_score|, JS) is the EXPECTED regime, not a surprise, and the H2 partial is reported only when ≥ 2 of (linear partial, tercile median, polynomial residual) agree on sign.

**Collinearity guard (planner memory: collinearity-gate pattern).** Before partialling, compute Pearson(|stylization_score|, JS_to_baseline). If > 0.6, the partial is identification-fragile — fall back to **tercile-bucket median test** (compute partial ρ within low/mid/high stylization terciles; require the median to be consistent in sign) + **polynomial residualization** (regress JS on degree-2 polynomial of stylization_score, partial on residual). Both reported.

```python
    """Fires at end-of-step when state.global_step / state.max_steps
    crosses the next pre-registered fraction in self.target_fractions.
    Saves PEFT adapter + uploads to HF immediately (CLAUDE.md
    checkpoint-per-phase rule)."""

    def __init__(self, target_fractions, out_base, hf_repo, cond, seed):
        self.target_fractions = sorted(target_fractions)  # e.g. [0.10, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0]
        self.fired = set()
        self.out_base = out_base
        self.hf_repo = hf_repo
        self.cond = cond
        self.seed = seed

    def on_step_end(self, args, state, control, **kwargs):
        frac = state.global_step / state.max_steps  # max_steps already includes epochs
        for tf in self.target_fractions:
            if frac >= tf and tf not in self.fired:
                tag = f"frac{int(tf*100):03d}"  # frac010, frac025, frac050, frac100, frac200, frac300
                out_dir = self.out_base / f"i488_{self.cond}_seed{self.seed}_{tag}"
                kwargs["model"].save_pretrained(out_dir)
                upload_model(out_dir, self.hf_repo,
                             path_in_repo=f"adapters/i488_{self.cond}_seed{self.seed}_{tag}")
                self.fired.add(tf)
```

Note `max_steps` includes all epochs, so frac=2.0 means "end of epoch 2 of 3" (epochs are normalized into [0,1] sub-epoch units by `state.max_steps`); equivalently `frac = state.epoch / num_train_epochs * num_train_epochs = state.epoch`. To be unambiguous the dispatcher passes `--epochs 3` and the fracs ∈ {0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00, 3.00} are **epoch units**, not normalized — callback fires when `state.epoch >= tf`.

### 4.6 Why code, not a model call?

The pipeline's heuristics — `stylization_score` (RB sequence-level JS of source-vs-default-assistant, reusing `js_vs_reference()`), `FractionAdapterSaveCallback` (deterministic fire on `state.epoch >= tf`), emission rate (substring `' ※'` containment), partial-Spearman + cluster bootstrap — are all deterministic numeric operations over already-tokenized data. A model-call alternative (Claude scoring "how stylized is this persona response?") was considered for `stylization_score` and rejected:
- (a) the codebase already has the deterministic primitive (`js_vs_reference()`, calling `_resp_logprobs()`) and it gives a continuous, reproducible scalar without sample-to-sample API noise;
- (b) at N = 27 sources × 8 samples × 50 probes = 10,800 forward passes per direction, the deterministic GPU path is ~10× faster than 10,800 Sonnet calls and avoids any rate limit;
- (c) deterministic = exact-reproducibility; a Claude judge score would change across model versions and break paired comparisons with future re-runs.
- **Flip condition:** if the deterministic score correlates < 0.5 with hand-coded stylization rank-order on a 10-source pilot, swap in a one-shot Sonnet judge over the 27 sources (a $0.03 call).

### 4.7 Contrastive negatives

Inherited from #460/#474 — `MarkerOnlyDataCollator(tail_tokens=0)` is the canonical contrastive regime for marker implant. Positives = `T_i(q) + R + ' ※'` (loss on marker token + EOS). Negatives = SAME 30 Q under other transformations with `T_j(q) + R_j` and no marker.

**SLOT FIX vs #460 (a documented divergence — required for contrastive validity, NOT an experimental axis).** The parent #460 default trained the negative loss at `valid_indices[-1]` (the trailing token, sft.py:317-320, the `suppress_at_post_response_slot=False` branch). That slot is NOT the post-response `<|im_end|>` slot the marker occupies on positives and the DV reads — so the negatives' EOS-token training was landing in the wrong place and the contrast could collapse toward positive-only (uniform leakage), confounding H1/H2. This experiment uses the `=True` branch added by #474 (sft.py:291-316) so the negative loss lands at the FIRST `<|im_end|>` in the completion region (the SAME label slot the marker occupies on positives at pos_ids[-3], same conditioning context "...Answer.", SAME slot the DV reads). Concretely, the train config passes:

```python
TrainLoraConfig(
    ...,
    marker_only_loss=True,
    marker_tail_tokens=0,
    marker_suppress_at_post_response_slot=True,
    marker_im_end_token_id=151645,  # resolved at launch from tokenizer.convert_tokens_to_ids('<|im_end|>')
)
```

Launch-time assert (FAIL LOUD if drift):
```python

assert im_end_id == 151645, f"Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct <|im_end|> id drift: got {im_end_id}, expected 151645"
```

**Smoke label-mask audit** (Phase 2, mandatory print before training launches): for ONE positive row and ONE negative row from cell A1, print exactly which token positions carry loss (i.e. which `labels[i]` entries are not `-100`). The audit must show:
- POSITIVE row: marker token (id 83399) position + immediately-following EOS — exactly those positions, nothing else.
- NEGATIVE row: a SINGLE position whose `input_ids[idx]` is 151645 (`<|im_end|>`), nothing else.

If the negative row's loss-bearing position is NOT id 151645, the audit FAILS and the run BLOCKS — this is the verification that the `=True` branch fired correctly. The Phase-2 EOS-gradient check (fix H) consumes the post-trained output of this same smoke to verify the gradient actually moved log P(※) DOWN at the post-response slot on the source→default-assistant off-diagonal cell.

Composition: 30 Q × 5 dupes positives = **150 positive rows**; 30 Q × 5 dupes × (27 other transformations rotated round-robin) = **150 negative rows** — 1:1 ratio per `contrastive-negatives.md`. **Default assistant** (no-system-prompt B1) IS in the negative pool by construction. The 27 close negative personas comfortably exceed the rule's "≥2-4" minimum.

### 6.2 Headline statistic, thresholds, and bootstrap

**Universe of analysis: off-diagonal cells only (i ≠ j; 702 pairs per frac per seed).** All H1/H2/H3 statistics, all bootstrap resamples, all CIs are computed on the off-diagonal subset. Diagonals are reported separately as implant-strength controls (§6.2.D below).

- **Primary statistic**: length-partial Spearman ρ(JS_ij, emission_rate_ij), partialled on `log(prompt_tokens_T_j + R_tokens_T_j)`, computed at each of the 3 chosen sub-epoch checkpoints, per seed, then averaged over seeds.
- **H2 statistic**: same partial ρ, additionally partialling on `is_stylized_source[i]` (binary) AND, separately, on `stylization_score[i]` (graded). Two H2 numbers per checkpoint (binary partial and graded partial).
- **Diagonal-adjusted statistic (per fix C)**: also report (a) the same partial ρ with `emission_ii` partialled in, AND (b) the partial ρ on the normalized outcome `emission_ij / max(emission_ii, ε)` with sources where `emission_ii < 0.5` dropped as a sensitivity check. SURVIVES requires the unadjusted AND ≥1 of {(a),(b)} agree on sign with |ρ| ≥ 0.15.
- **Primary uncertainty unit: dyadic cluster-bootstrap (per fix E)**: 5000 resamples, each draw is `bootstrap_sources = sample(27 sources, k=27, replace=True)` × `bootstrap_targets = sample(27 targets, k=27, replace=True)`, then take the off-diagonal sub-grid `{(i,j): i ∈ bootstrap_sources, j ∈ bootstrap_targets, i ≠ j}`. This preserves both the source-side (one LoRA drives all 26 of its target cells) AND target-side (one eval rig drives all 26 of its source cells) dependence. CI = central 95% over the 5000 resampled ρ values.
- **Crossed random-effects secondary (per fix E)**: fit `emission_rate ~ JS_partial + log_length + (1|source) + (1|target) + (1|class_pair)` on off-diagonal cells (lme4-style in `statsmodels.MixedLM` or `bambi`). Report the fixed-effect estimate + CI for JS_partial. PASS requires the fixed effect to have CI excluding 0 with the same sign as the dyadic-bootstrap headline.
- **PASS thresholds (pre-registered)**: H1 PASS = |partial ρ| ≥ 0.20 AND dyadic-bootstrap CI excludes 0 on at least one frac AND crossed random-effects fixed-effect CI agrees. H2 PASS (= SURVIVES) = the same condition holds after BOTH the binary AND the graded partial, AND the diagonal-adjustment chain (above) agrees, AND the H2 identification check (next bullet) doesn't trigger UNIDENTIFIABLE. H3 PASS = pooled ρ(1-JS, fraction-of-fracs-emitted) ≥ +0.30 with p < 0.05 AND the within-source-mean ρ has the same sign.
- **H2 identifiability pre-check (per fix F)**: BEFORE reading the H2 partial, report (1) Pearson(stylization_score, JS) over the 702 cells; (2) variance-inflation-factor of the H2 partial regression of JS on stylization_score+length; (3) the count of non-strong-stylized sources (sources NOT in {A3, A4, A5} after Phase-0 dry-run; ideally also any new conditions that empirically rank in the top-5 by stylization_score) sitting in the high-JS quantile (≥ p75 = 0.144 in JS units); (4) the residual-JS rank spread WITHIN each stylization tercile. If Pearson > 0.85 OR <5 non-stylized cells in the high-JS band OR VIF > 10, the H2 verdict is **UNIDENTIFIABLE** (a NULL there is NOT evidence "geometry was a stylized-source proxy" — the partial is mathematically washing the predictor out by partialling on a near-duplicate).
- **Kill threshold (clean null)**: at every frac, |partial ρ| < 0.10 AND dyadic-bootstrap CI crosses 0 — AND identifiability check PASSes (so the null is on a well-identified partial). Report as a clean null with the appropriate interpretation per the H2 verdict tree.
- **Collinearity-gate robustness chain** (planner memory): if Pearson(stylization_score, JS) > 0.6 (a threshold tighter than the UNIDENTIFIABLE 0.85 — between 0.6 and 0.85 the partial is fragile but still identified), also report **tercile-bucket median test** (compute partial ρ within low/mid/high stylization terciles; require the median to be consistent in sign) + **polynomial residualization on degree-2 stylization_score**. Final body says SURVIVES only if at least 2 of (linear partial, tercile median, polynomial residual) agree on sign and one passes the 0.20 threshold AND the diagonal-adjustment chain agrees AND the identifiability check doesn't trigger UNIDENTIFIABLE.

**§6.2.D Per-frac saturation gate + per-source diagonal reporting (per fix D).** The Phase-2 smoke gate validates only the SOURCE diagonal — it does NOT guarantee off-diagonal dynamic range. For every reported frac in Phase 5, compute and report **on the off-diagonal subset**:
- `floor_mass_off` = fraction of 702 off-diagonal cells with emission_rate ≤ 0.05.
- `ceiling_mass_off` = fraction of 702 cells with emission_rate ≥ 0.95.
- `tie_mass_off` = max(floor_mass_off, ceiling_mass_off).

A frac is **disqualified from contributing to H1/H2 PASS** if `tie_mass_off > 0.90` (the #469 invisible-saturation failure mode, where the source diagonal looked in-band but the off-diagonal had no dynamic range left to read). At least one of the 3 chosen fracs must clear `tie_mass_off ≤ 0.85` to be eligible.

**Per-source diagonal reporting (required output, fix D + §6.3 analyzer-must-report).** At each chosen frac, report `emission_ii` for all 27 sources as a one-column table. Flag any source whose `emission_ii < 0.2` at all chosen fracs as a **no-implant source** — its off-diagonal cells are dropped from H1/H2 computation as uninformative. Specifically flag the 3 strong stylized sources (A3 pirate / A4 comedian / A5 villainous) because #462 showed they saturate LAST under the parent recipe; under the lighter regime here they may also implant LAST and could be in the no-implant pool at a given frac (in which case the H2 partial is unreadable at that frac because the stylization variation has been dropped).

## 7. Decision Gates

**Smoke gate IS the only gate** (justified): wall-clock projection is ~5h end-to-end with a single intermediate signal (smoke calibration) that cheaply rules out the full run. Beyond the smoke gate, no intermediate decision gates — the sub-epoch checkpoints ARE the trajectory; we don't decide whether to keep training based on intermediate cells.

**Smoke calibration gate (Phase 2)**: run A1 (helpful assistant, low-band) AND G2 (skeptical scientist, upper-band candidate) at lr=2e-6, r=16, 150 rows, 3 epochs total, saving at all 6 fracs. PASS requires ALL of the following — failing ANY emits `epm:failure v1 reason: <which>` and exits BLOCKED:

1. **Label-mask audit (fix A)**: print loss-bearing positions on one positive + one negative row of A1's training data. Positive row's loss-positions are exactly the marker token (id 83399) + immediately-following EOS. Negative row's loss-position is exactly `<|im_end|>` (id 151645). Otherwise → `reason: label_mask_wrong_slot`.
2. **In-band source emission**: for BOTH A1 and G2, ≥ 3 of the 6 fracs have source emission in [0.20, 0.85]; pick the 3 most evenly spread that are in-band for BOTH cells (if the two cells' in-band fracs disjoint, document as a methodology divergence and pick per-cell fracs at sweep time). Otherwise → `reason: smoke_no_inband_frac`.
3. **Off-diagonal saturation gate (fix D)**: for A1 at each picked frac, evaluate A1's adapter on at least 6 off-diagonal target contexts (a diverse subset: B1 default, one F frame, one G persona, A3 stylized, D2 paraphrase, B5 wrap). At least one picked frac must have `tie_mass_off ≤ 0.85` on this 6-cell mini-grid. Otherwise → `reason: offdiag_saturated_at_all_fracs`.
4. **EOS-gradient check (fix H)**: at the latest picked frac, A1's trained model evaluated on the off-diagonal A1→B1 cell (source = helpful-assistant, target = no-system-prompt default assistant) must show trained `log P(' ※')` at the post-response slot moved DOWN by at least 0.2 nats vs the base model — proving the `=True` negative-slot gradient at lr=2e-6 is actually pushing P(※) down where it should under softmax competition. Otherwise → `reason: eos_gradient_inactive` (the contrastive negatives aren't biting; either lr too low, or the slot fix isn't firing correctly, or the negative rotation didn't see B1 enough — re-plan).

Smoke cost: ~1.0 GPU-hr on 1× H100 (2 cells × 6 fracs each + a 6-cell mini-grid eval). Wall: ~40 min.

## 11. Decision Rationale

For every load-bearing hyperparameter:

- **Base model = Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct.** Why: matches #406/#460/#462/#469 byte-for-byte; the predictor (base-model JS) is THE thing being tested for predictive power, so the base model must be identical to the parent line. Source: `#406`, `#460`, `#469` plan + parent body. Alternatives rejected: Qwen-2.5-7B-base (the parent used Instruct; switching breaks the inheritance + the rule's persona-injection convention).

- **Marker = ` ※` (id 83399).** Why: project-standard per `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md`; single Qwen-2.5 token; #406/#460 used it. Source: `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md`, `#460`. Alternatives rejected: `[ZLT]` (multi-token deprecated), bare `※` id 63680 (different token, would break paired comparison with #460/#462).

- **lr = 2e-6.** Why: a directional non-saturation choice — at lr=1e-5 the rig saturates ≥ 98% of cells by epoch 2 (#462 data, strong); at lr=2e-6 the only positive single-point support is #477's count=8 terminal data point at ΔG=11.27 (emit=1.0), which #477's own later validity gate EXCLUDED as a `source_R_collapsed=true` outlier (planner memory `feedback_477_step_lever_calibration_data` is the up-to-date read). So **the lr=2e-6 grounding is WEAK** — it's a reasonable directional bet, not a calibrated value. The real safeguard is the Phase-2 smoke gate (§7) which empirically determines whether lr=2e-6 + r=16 + 150 rows lands the source in [0.20, 0.85] emission AND clears the off-diagonal saturation gate (§6.2.D). If it doesn't, the pre-registered fallback (lr=1e-6 / r=8 / 75 rows) is the recourse — and if THAT also fails, the experiment BLOCKS and re-plans rather than running on a regime that floors/ceilings. Source: `#462` (saturation data, the rejection-of-lr=1e-5 side) + `#477` planner memory (the directional 2e-6 bet, weak support). Alternatives rejected: lr=1e-5 (saturated, #462), lr=5e-6 (#477 saw it also saturate), lr=1e-6 (kept as the pre-registered FALLBACK descope; might underimplant).

- **LoRA rank r = 16, α = 32.** Why: halved from parent #406/#460/#462 (r=32/α=64) — a capacity-reduction non-saturation lever; α=2r preserves the standard scaling. Source: `#406`/`#460`/`#462` (parent rank that we are halving); **r=16 itself is `ungrounded — needs smoke-test`** (no prior issue in this lineage has run r=16 with the marker-at-end / marker-only-loss recipe; #207 and #380 used r=32, not r=16). Alternatives: r=8 (FALLBACK only — risk of underimplant), r=32 (parent — saturated).

- **LoRA dropout = 0.05.** Why: matches the parent #460/#462 rig (#469 Reproducibility row confirms "r=32, α=64, dropout 0.05, lr=1e-5, 5 epochs"). Source: `#460` (#469 Reproducibility). Alternatives: 0.0 (TRL default; modest overfit risk on 150 rows — parent value wins on inheritance).

- **Schedule = cosine, warmup ratio = 0.03.** Why: project-standard convention; the parent #460/#462 rig uses the `TrainLoraConfig` default schedule and the canonical small-warmup. Source: project-standard convention (matches the parent rig's `TrainLoraConfig` defaults; not explicitly itemized in #460's body). Alternatives: linear (older #207 precedent; cosine has been the de-facto standard in later issues).

- **Epochs = 3, sub-epoch fracs ∈ {0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00, 3.00}.** Why: 3 epochs total because #462's 5-epoch was too long (saturated by epoch 2 at lr=1e-5); at lr=2e-6 (5× lower lr) the equivalent gradient amount is much smaller, so 3 epochs at lr=2e-6 corresponds to a regime well below #462 epoch-1, which is the under-trained band we want. The 6 fracs span sub-epoch (0.10, 0.25, 0.50) AND multi-epoch (1.00, 2.00, 3.00) to cover both "too-light" and "near-#462-epoch-1" anchors. Smoke picks 3 in-band. Source: `#462` saturation trajectory, planner memory `feedback_477_step_lever_calibration_data`. Alternatives: 1 epoch (insufficient ckpt resolution), 5 epochs (parent — saturated band wasted).

- **Batch = 4, grad_accum = 4 (effective batch 16).** Why: matches the parent rig's defaults (verifiable in `scripts/i460_phase23_train.py` TrainLoraConfig call). Source: `#460` train script. Alternatives: bs=8/ga=2 (same effective batch; tested in #381).

- **Seq length = 2048.** Why: 1024 tok cap on R + ~200 tok prompt headroom + marker; project-standard convention (set in `scripts/i460_phase23_train.py` `TrainLoraConfig(max_length=2048)`). Source: `#460` train script. Alternatives: 4096 (unnecessary; median R ~150 tok per #460 R-truncation logging).
