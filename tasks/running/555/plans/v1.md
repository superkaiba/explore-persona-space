# Plan — Task #555: Pre-implant null-geometry calibration across 5 fresh seed pairs

Parent: #534 (awaiting_promotion, clean result). Kind: experiment. Plan version: v1.

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** The previous experiment found a suspicious pattern at the very start of training — before the model had learned anything, one of our geometry measurements already showed a "signal" (+0.110). I'm going to retrain the same four training setups ten more times with fresh random seeds, stop each run after just five optimizer steps (when nothing has been implanted yet), and run the identical analysis on each, five times over. That gives five independent readings of what this measurement does when there is genuinely nothing to measure.
- **What I expect:** The early "signal" is noise. Across the five fresh repeats the measurement should scatter around zero — sometimes positive, sometimes negative — making the parent's +0.110 unremarkable.
- **What would change my mind:** If four or more of the five repeats come out positive on the same measurement, or the pooled average is clearly above zero, then the analysis itself manufactures geometry-shaped readings out of warmup noise — and every result in this experiment line needs a correction subtracted before we trust it.

## 1. Goal

Bound how often geometry-shaped partial-correlation readings arise with NO implant, by retraining the four positioned arms for only the first five optimizer steps under five fresh seed pairs and fitting the same six-predictor regression at each no-implant snapshot. This tests whether the parent's pre-implant nearest-negative reading (+0.110 at step 5, raw p = 0.022, both seeds positive, mean source gain 0.025 nats) is chance-level noise or a systematic warmup artifact. If systematic, the stop-point bubble reversal (+0.125) inherits a real confound and every partial-correlation claim in the #504/#530/#534 lineage needs a pre-implant baseline subtraction. ## 3. Hypothesis

**H (noise):** sub-floor partial-correlation fits are noise. Across 5 fresh no-implant replicates, the nearest-negative partial ρ scatters around zero with spread wide enough that +0.110 is unremarkable: 0–1 of 5 replicates Holm-significant (family-5) on `d_nearest_neg_nd`, no consistent sign. The parent's own step-5 → step-10 sign flip (+0.110 → −0.023) predicts this.

**Falsification (systematic artifact):** a consistent positive nearest-negative sign in ≥4 of 5 replicates, OR a pooled 95% t-interval (df=4, over the 5 per-replicate ρ) excluding zero. Then warmup/init perturbations systematically organize along nearest-negative distance and the lineage needs a pre-implant baseline subtraction.

**Specificity control:** shadow-angle, fit in the same regression, read flat-null pre-implant by the parent (+0.046 at step 5). A geometry-generic artifact would show there too; an artifact specific to `d_nearest_neg_nd` would not.

Power note (so the verdict isn't over- or under-read): per replicate n = 432, so SE(ρ) ≈ 0.048. A true ρ = +0.11 gives each replicate ≈ 0.99 probability of a positive sign (so ≥4/5-positive triggers almost surely) and the pooled t-interval excludes zero with near-certainty; under the null, ≥4/5 positive has chance probability ≈ 0.19 and the t-interval false-positive rate is 5% — the body's falsification clause is a disjunction (sign-consistency OR pooled interval) dominated in power by the interval. The registered rule is the OR-rule: a bare >=4/5-positive triggers FALSIFIED even when the interval spans zero (chance probability ~0.19 under the null — the analyzer reports that base rate alongside any sign-only FALSIFIED verdict so the headline carries the right caveat, but the machine verdict is not softened post hoc). Per-replicate Holm-5 significance of a true +0.11 has only ~35–40% power (the parent's own +0.110 was raw-significant, not Holm-significant), which is why the body's noise criterion is the conjunction "≤1 Holm-significant AND no consistent sign," not Holm counts alone.

### 4.2 The 5-step hard stop (read-point truncation, NOT a recipe change)

The parent's scheduler parameterization is preserved exactly: 400 rows / (batch 4 × grad-accum 4) = 25 optimizer steps/epoch × 12-epoch ceiling → Trainer max_steps = 300; warmup_ratio 0.05 → 15 warmup steps; LR at optimizer step 5 = 5e-6 × 5/15 ≈ 1.67e-6 (mid-warmup, ~1/3 of peak — the parent's step-5 LR). Setting `max_steps=5` naively would re-parameterize warmup to ceil(0.25) = 1 step and put step 5 near peak LR, breaking weight-identity with the parent's step-5 trajectory — explicitly NOT done.

Mechanism — new ~15-line `HardStopAtStepCallback` in `src/explore_persona_space/eval/callbacks.py` (next to `MarkerBandStopCallback`):

```python
class HardStopAtStepCallback(TrainerCallback):
    """Stop training after optimizer step `stop_at_step` WITHOUT touching the
    scheduler horizon (num_train_epochs / max_steps / warmup_ratio unchanged,
    so the LR schedule is identical to an un-stopped run's prefix)."""
    def __init__(self, stop_at_step: int, expect_max_steps: int | None = None):
        assert stop_at_step >= 1
        self.stop_at_step = int(stop_at_step)
        self.expect_max_steps = expect_max_steps
    def on_train_begin(self, args, state, control, **kwargs):
        if self.expect_max_steps is not None and state.max_steps != self.expect_max_steps:
            raise RuntimeError(
                f"scheduler horizon {state.max_steps} != expected {self.expect_max_steps} "
                "— warmup parameterization would diverge from the parent trajectory.")
    def on_step_end(self, args, state, control, **kwargs):
        if state.global_step >= self.stop_at_step:
            control.should_training_stop = True
        return control
```

Threaded as `hard_stop_at_step: int | None = None` (+ `hard_stop_expect_max_steps`) on `train_one_cell()` (`src/explore_persona_space/experiments/contrastive_neg_geometry_472/train_cell.py`) — when set, the callback is appended to the `callbacks=[ckpt_cb]` list passed to `train_lora()`. Default None → byte-identical behavior for every existing caller (the established `marker_band_snapshot_*` threading pattern).

Interaction with existing machinery (all verified in code):
- `MarkerBandStopCallback` stays attached and armed verbatim (band [5,12], eval_every 10, min_steps 20). Its eval cadence (10) and min_steps (20) mean it never reads or fires before step 5's hard stop — its in-loop eval history will be empty, which `on_train_end` handles (sidecar written with `stopped: false`, `stop_step: 5`).
- Its per-step snapshot (cadence 1) fires in `on_step_end` BEFORE any stop takes effect and is gated on its OWN `_stopped` flag, not `control` — so snapshots `step_0001..step_0005` exist regardless of callback ordering.
- `i534_select_fractions.py` already handles the never-band-stopped case: logs "cell never band-stopped; using S=last step" and maps fractions of S=5 (verified at selector lines 132–139). With `--fractions 1.0`, frac 1.00 → step 5, exact.
- The legacy `CheckpointAtFractionsCallback` writes nothing before frac×300 = step 300; harmless; the selector overwrites `checkpoint_index.json`.
