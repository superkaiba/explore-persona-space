## Goal (canonical — verbatim)

Test whether a persona's activation-space alignment to a behavior's linear direction predicts, across multiple behaviors (sycophancy, refusal, marker, EM), both (a) that persona's base rate for the behavior and (b) where an implanted behavior leaks across held-out personas, and whether this alignment geometry beats the base behavioral prior baseline that the #518/#545/#605 predictor line found unbeatable, reusing existing persona vectors, behavior directions, and trained adapters wherever possible.

### 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I take a geometry number I already have for each persona — how much that persona "points toward" a behavior like sycophancy or refusal in the model's internal space — and check whether it predicts two things: how often that persona shows the behavior on its own, and how badly the behavior spills onto that persona when I implant it into a *different* persona. I do this for several behaviors at once, reusing measurements from past experiments, so almost no new GPU.
- **What I expect:** The geometry will track a persona's *own* base rate for a behavior (this already worked for sycophancy). Whether it predicts *spillover* better than the dead-simple "personas already prone to the behavior catch more of it" baseline is the real open question — six past experiments say that baseline is very hard to beat, so I'm betting the geometry mostly restates it and adds little once I subtract the baseline out.
- **What would change my mind:** If the geometry still predicts where a behavior spills *after* I remove everything the simple base-rate baseline already explains — on personas the geometry never saw during fitting — that would be the first internal-state signal that genuinely beats the baseline, and I'd want to understand why this geometry works where every distance metric before it failed.

## 1. Goal

(Copied verbatim above.) In one paragraph: #623 showed that a persona vector's cosine to the sycophancy trait direction predicts that persona's *base* sycophancy rate (ρ=0.73, n=35). This experiment asks the two questions #623 left open — does that alignment→base-rate relationship generalize beyond sycophancy, and does the same alignment predict *where an implanted behavior leaks* across held-out personas — and runs the head-to-head the whole predictor line cares about: does alignment beat the base behavioral prior (`bystander_base_rate`) on held-out leakage prediction, the bar that #518/#545/#605/#603/#649 found no base-model geometry could clear. The payoff is high because the design is almost entirely reuse: the leakage cells, the base rates, the persona-vector bank, and the trait-extraction recipe all already exist.

## 2. Prior Work

**Closest prior formalizations (external):**
- **Persona Vectors** (Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey 2025, arXiv 2507.21509). Extracts trait directions from a natural-language description (the recipe #623 reused) and shows finetuning-induced personality change "is strongly correlated with shifts along the relevant persona vectors." This experiment is the **cross-persona-leakage** extension of that finding, using the project's open-weights advantage (Qwen-2.5-7B): the paper predicts *self* trait change; we predict *off-diagonal leakage landing* across held-out personas, and bake the geometry off against the base prior — a comparison the paper does not run.
- **Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment** (Wang, …, Mossing 2025, arXiv 2506.19823). SAE-based persona features causally control EM. Direction-based, but on GPT-4o internal features, not a cosine-onto-trait-direction *predictor* of cross-persona leakage. What is NEW here that neither paper settles: (a) the predictor is a *base-model* read used to forecast *where leakage lands* across personas, (b) it is benchmarked against the behavioral base prior, (c) it spans four behaviors on one open-weights model with reusable trained leakage panels.

**In-house:**
- **#623** (parent) — alignment→base-rate, sycophancy only, ρ=0.73 n=35. Source of the persona-vector bank + the sycophancy direction + the trait-extraction recipe. Readout choice load-bearing (last-prompt-token 0.73 vs response-avg 0.44).
- **#518** (THE baseline) — "No base-model predictor cell cleared the cross-behavior threshold (|ρ_FE|≥0.40 same-sign) on sycophancy + refusal + EM leakage panels." Its predictor zoo is the **persona-to-persona** distance family (cosine_l20, response-cosine, JS, KL) + `bystander_base_rate` + `completion_logprob`. The substrate of every leakage cell my experiment reuses (all three leakage DVs on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct-trained adapters — verified by fact-checker). Caveats it surfaced: refusal arm is 76% floored (power-limited); `source_resp_len_mean` (a length confound) dominates refusal/EM; the EM `delta` is a *survivor-aligned-rate* proxy because Sonnet's coherence filter discards ~85% of EM generations before the rate is computed. (The "syco-Instruct / refusal+EM-base" split that v1 mis-stated refers to #518's own *geometry-extraction* substrate — the cosine/JS/KL zoo — which #657 does NOT reuse.)
- **#605** — at matched context-similarity, the base prior beats geometry for *marker* leakage standing; marker shift carries a held-out-positive signal in logit space, null in log-prob. Confirms the marker behavior also sits in the "prior beats geometry" regime, but its predictor is *context-similarity-to-source*, NOT cosine-onto-a-behavior-direction — so my lever is new for marker too.
- **#532/#541/#500/#444** — a unit's behavioral base prior repeatedly out-predicts geometric distance for leakage.
- **#521** — extracts the EM and marker training-induced activation *shifts* (trained−base) per persona at L14 on Instruct (candidate behavior directions for EM/marker).

## 3. Hypothesis

Verbatim:
- **H1:** behavior-direction alignment predicts base rate across ≥3 behaviors (generalizes #623 beyond sycophancy).
- **H2:** behavior-direction alignment predicts cross-persona leakage landing with held-out (out-of-sample) skill above chance.
- **H3 (the bar):** alignment beats the base behavioral prior on held-out leakage prediction. The prior won everywhere in #518/#545/#605; this is the first geometry with a real shot.
- **Competing/null:** alignment merely restates "high-prior personas leak more" and adds nothing once the base prior is partialled out. The X-vs-(X−Y) artifact (#383/#605) MUST be controlled — report alignment's marginal skill with the prior partialled out.

**Falsification thresholds (pre-registered):**
- **H1 PASS:** raw Spearman ρ(alignment, base rate) ≥ 0.5 with 95% bootstrap CI lower bound > 0 on ≥3 of the 4 behaviors (mirrors #623's H1 threshold). The sycophancy arm is the replication anchor (must reproduce ρ≈0.73 ± CI on the syco-source bystander panel; a miss is a join/recipe bug, halt).
- **H2 PASS:** held-out LOPO Spearman ρ(alignment, leakage Δ) > 0 with 95% bootstrap CI lower bound > 0 on ≥1 behavior.
- **H3 PASS (the bar):** held-out LOPO **partial-Spearman ρ(alignment, leakage Δ | bystander_base_rate)** with 95% bootstrap-over-bystanders CI **lower bound > 0** on ≥1 behavior, AND alignment's out-of-fold incremental R² over a base-prior-only model (ΔR²_align-beyond-prior, grouped-CV by bystander) with CI lower bound > 0. Both the partial-ρ and the held-out ΔR² must clear; a positive raw ρ that collapses to a CI straddling 0 under partialling is the **competing/null** outcome (report as "restates the prior").
- **NULL (competing) outcome:** partial-ρ CI straddles 0 on all behaviors after partialling, OR ΔR²_align-beyond-prior ≤ 0 — reported as "alignment adds nothing the base prior doesn't" (the #518 result holds, now tested against a genuinely new geometry). A power-limited null (MDE-ρ exceeds the observed effect at n≈17) is reported as indeterminate, not as confirmation.

## 7. Decision Gates

**No gates — short run / pre-verified pipeline.** The only GPU step is a ~2 GPU-h extraction reusing #623's validated script; the analysis is CPU-only. Wall-clock < 4h, no genuinely-uncertain intermediate signal that would cheaply rule out the run. The CPU-only end-to-end smoke (§11) is the kill check, not a gate.

**Kill / halt criteria (success and kill, distinct from gates):**
- **Success criterion (H3):** held-out partial-ρ(align, Δ | prior) CI lower bound > 0 AND ΔR²_align-beyond-prior CI lower bound > 0 on ≥1 behavior (§3). 
- **Kill / abort criterion (join-integrity halt):** if the **sycophancy DV-(a) replication anchor** fails to reproduce #623's ρ≈0.73 (bootstrap CI overlap) on the resolvable panel during the smoke, **stop and diagnose** — that is a join/recipe bug, not a finding; do not read the leakage arms until it reproduces.
- **Kill / null-report criterion:** if partial-ρ CI straddles 0 on all behaviors after partialling OR ΔR²_align-beyond-prior ≤ 0, **halt the H3 claim and report the competing/null outcome** ("alignment restates the prior") — a power-limited null (MDE-ρ > observed at n≈17) is reported as indeterminate, never as a confirmed null.

## 11. Decision Rationale

- **Persona-vector readout = last-prompt-token (Method A) headline, response-avg (Method B) robustness.** *Why:* #623 found last-prompt-token gives the cleaner *non-circular* read (ρ=0.73) vs response-avg (0.44, partly circular with the behavioral outcome); inherit verbatim for single-variable comparability. *Source:* #623. *Alternatives:* response-avg-only (rejected: circular + halves signal).
- **Layers {7,14,21,27}, headline layer 14.** *Why:* #623's sampled layers; layer 14 was its steering-selected headline. *Source:* #623. *Alternatives:* full 28-layer sweep (rejected: #594/#623 found the signal is layer-robust; 4 layers suffice and keep the multiple-comparisons surface small).
- **Alignment metric = cosine, global-mean-centered bank.** *Why:* magnitude-invariant project standard; Qwen centroids share a dominant mean direction so raw cosine is compressed. *Source:* #536, #623. *Alternatives:* raw dot (kept as a secondary; #623 found it reads higher but is magnitude-confounded).
- **Behavior direction = base-model trait-extraction (not #521 trained shift).** *Why:* keeps the predictor a pure base-model read (the H3 point) and recipe-matched to the sycophancy direction + persona vectors. *Source:* #623 / arXiv 2507.21509. *Alternatives:* #521 trained-shift direction (rejected: mixes the implant in, not recipe-matched, not a base-model predictor).
- **Base-prior baseline = `bystander_base_rate`.** *Why:* this is the exact baseline the #518/#545/#605 line found unbeatable; beating it is the Goal. *Source:* #518. *Alternatives:* `source_base_rate` / `completion_logprob` (kept as control covariates, not the headline baseline).
- **Held-out = LOPO primary, LOBO secondary.** *Why:* the predictor is bystander-level, so leave-one-*persona*-out is the honest out-of-sample test; LOBO adds a cross-behavior generalization read at higher N. *Source:* ungrounded — needs smoke-test (the LOPO choice is standard, but no prior issue ran LOPO on this exact substrate; the smoke validates it end-to-end). *Alternatives:* pooled/in-sample ρ (rejected: #545 showed pooled scoring crowns predictors that held-out reads call noise).
- **Partial-correlation = Spearman partial (rank-residualization) + grouped-CV ΔR².** *Why:* matches the project's rank-based DV convention + #605's `delta_cv_r2` held-out incremental-variance read; reporting both guards against the partialling method being load-bearing. *Source:* #605 (ΔR² read), #383/#605 (X-vs-(X−Y) caveat). *Alternatives:* OLS partial-Pearson only (kept as a secondary; rejected as headline because the relationships are monotone-not-linear per #623).
- **Bootstrap B=10,000, resampling unit = held-out persona.** *Why:* #623's bootstrap count; the persona (not the cell) is the independent unit because the predictor is bystander-level. *Source:* #623. *Alternatives:* cell-level resampling (rejected: cells sharing a bystander are dependent — would understate CIs).
- **Shuffled-direction null B=1,000.** *Why:* controls that any positive ρ exceeds what a random direction in the same subspace produces (the #555-style geometry noise floor). *Source:* #555 (geometry-partial noise floor ±0.09). *Alternatives:* none — cheap and necessary.
