#### 6.2.D Per-frac saturation gate + per-source diagonal reporting (v4 — REPLACES v3 §6.2.D's post-hoc picker prose)

The §6.2.D `tie_mass_off ≤ 0.85` saturation gate is unchanged. **What v4 pre-specifies is the post-hoc picker tiebreak** that selects the unique headline frac from the up-to-6 fracs that may satisfy that gate.

**Post-hoc picker (ρ-blind, deterministic):** Among the 6 production fracs {0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00, 3.00}, an **eligible** frac is one whose 702-cell off-diag emission satisfies BOTH:

1. `tie_mass_off ≤ 0.85` (the existing in-band criterion from v2 §6.2.D — at most 85% of cells pinned at either floor or ceiling), AND
2. median per-source diagonal `emission_ii ≥ 0.20` across the 27 sources (the recipe actually implanted the marker at the source for at least half the conditions — otherwise off-diag emission rate is uninterpretable because there's nothing to transfer).

**The headline frac is the LOWEST eligible frac in {0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00, 3.00} scanned ascending.** The construct: "the lightest training amount that lands in-band with adequate source implant" — which is exactly the v2/v3 Goal of "non-saturated regime" with the additional minimum-implant floor needed to interpret off-diag rates. If no frac is eligible, the experiment EXITS via the `production_no_inband_frac` recovery block in §6.1 (descope to lr=1e-6 / r=8 / 75 rows).

This rule is implemented as a 10-line analysis function in `scripts/i488_phase5_analyze.py` (the same file that v3 already targeted for the picker). The body reports the picker rule explicitly, the picked frac, and the eligibility flags of all 6 fracs (so a reviewer sees what was picked from).

**Why ρ-blind matters:** the picker uses ONLY off-diag emission rates (the within-condition outcome variable), not the cross-condition `partial ρ(JS, emission)` statistic that H1 tests. So picking the lowest eligible frac cannot be a `max over fracs` of the test statistic — there is no formal CI inflation. The full-frac trajectory (all 6 fracs × 2 seeds of partial ρ) is reported as context for the reader, but the dyadic-bootstrap CI is computed at the picked frac only.

## 7. Decision Gates (v4 — surgical Gate 3 swap; Gates 1, 2′, 4 carry unchanged)

**Smoke validation gate (Phase 2, v4 — Gate 3 REPLACES v3's emission-rate version)**: run A1 (helpful assistant, low-band) AND G2 (skeptical scientist, upper-band candidate) at lr=2e-6, r=16, 150 rows, 3 epochs total, saving at all 6 fracs. 1. **Label-mask audit (KEEP — v3 Gate 1, was PASSing in r6).** Unchanged from v3 §7 Gate 1. Fail → `reason: label_mask_wrong_slot`.

2. **Log-prob shift (KEEP — v3 Gate 2′).** Unchanged from v3 §7 Gate 2′: at frac=3.00, `Δlogp = log P(' ※')_trained − log P(' ※')_base` ≥ +0.5 nat on at least one of (A1, G2). Fail → `reason: smoke_no_logprob_shift`.

   **v4 free addition (standing rec).** During Phase 2, also record `Δlogp` for a **frac=0.00 / 0-SFT-step forward pass on the same smoke prompts** (essentially free — the base log-prob is already computed for the Δlogp). This produces an empirical noise distribution on the 1-pos slice. If the empirical noise floor is **≥ 0.3 nat**, the implementer is authorized (under "Plan deviations allowed without re-approval") to tighten Gate 2′ from +0.5 nat to a value in [0.2 nat, 1.0 nat] that sits ≥3σ above the empirical noise. Otherwise threshold stays at +0.5 nat. The noise distribution is logged to `figures/issue_488/smoke_logprob_noise.png` regardless of whether the threshold is tightened.

3. **Off-diagonal log-prob shift (NEW — v4 Gate 3, REPLACES v3 Gate 3).** For A1's frac=3.00 adapter, evaluate on **6 off-diagonal target contexts** (the same diverse subset from v3: B1 default, one F frame, one G persona, A3 stylized, D2 paraphrase, B5 wrap). For each off-diag cell, compute `Δlogp_off = log P(' ※')_trained − log P(' ※')_base` at the post-response slot (HF teacher-forced, analogous to Gate 2′ but at off-diag contexts instead of on-diag smoke prompts). **PASS criterion: at least 4 of 6 off-diag cells must have `Δlogp_off ≥ +0.2 nat`.** Failure → `reason: offdiag_no_logprob_shift`.

   **Why the per-cell threshold is lower than Gate 2′'s (+0.2 nat vs +0.5 nat):** off-diag transfer is intrinsically weaker than on-diag implant — the adapter is trained at the source persona's context and we're measuring at a different persona's context, so even when the implant is healthy a meaningful chunk of the effect is left at the source. +0.2 nat is the floor of "the trained recipe moves marker log-prob in the right direction off-diagonally," which is what Gate 3 needs to validate before production commits to the full 324-LoRA sweep.

   **Why ≥4 of 6 (not ≥5/6 or ≥3/6):** off-diag transfer is heterogeneous across target contexts by design (transfer to B1 default ≠ transfer to A3 stylized) — requiring 4/6 allows the gate to tolerate 1-2 stubbornly low-transfer contexts (e.g., A1 → A3 stylized might be intrinsically harder) without false-failing, while still requiring the recipe to be moving in the right direction on the majority of the 6 chosen contexts.

   **Why this works on the same 1-pos / 0-neg slice the round-6 evidence proves Gate 2's emission-rate cannot:** log-prob is continuous and a non-zero gradient at the marker token moves `log P(' ※')` measurably at every context the adapter influences, including off-diag. v3's Gate 3 used `tie_mass_off` which is `max(floor_mass_off, ceiling_mass_off)` over EMISSION RATE (a thresholded Bernoulli) — so the floor mass goes to 1.0 immediately at the 0.000-emission floor and the gate fires before any signal can land. v4's Gate 3 measures the continuous dynamic range, which (a) is non-zero whenever the gradient is non-zero (which Gate 2′ already establishes at the source), and (b) the +0.2 nat threshold separates "gradient transferring weakly to off-diag" (PASS, expected — H1 predicts this is the regime where the JS gradient lives) from "gradient is NOT transferring to off-diag at all" (FAIL, kills the experiment because there will be no off-diag signal at any frac).

4. **EOS-gradient check (KEEP — v3 Gate 4).** Unchanged from v3 §7 Gate 4. Fail → `reason: eos_gradient_inactive`.

## 11. Decision Rationale (v4 — TWO new entries added; v3 entries unchanged)

Carry v3 §11 verbatim. **Two new entries:**

- **Smoke Gate 3 off-diag log-prob-shift threshold = +0.2 nat across ≥4 of 6 off-diag cells (v4 — REPLACES v3 Gate 3's emission-rate `tie_mass_off ≤ 0.85`).** Why: v3 Gate 3 used emission-rate `tie_mass_off ≤ 0.85` on the 1-pos / 0-neg smoke slice, where round-6 already proved 0.000 emission everywhere ⇒ `floor_mass_off ≈ 1.0` ⇒ gate fires at the FLOOR before production runs (the exact same emission-rate-on-1-pos-slice failure mode that the v3 pivot was supposed to eliminate, just hidden in a different gate). v4 swaps to a continuous off-diag log-prob check that matches Gate 2′'s philosophy: same kind of measurement (HF teacher-forced log-prob), one floor lower because off-diag transfer is mechanistically weaker than on-diag implant. **Source: ungrounded — needs smoke-test.** Alternatives considered: ≥0.3 nat × ≥3 of 6 (more permissive on cell count, tighter per-cell — risks letting through recipes where ALL 6 are at +0.31 nat, which is barely-above-noise even if technically PASSing the cell-count criterion); ≥0.1 nat × ≥5 of 6 (looser per-cell, stricter on cell count — risks passing recipes whose individual cell shifts are near the empirical noise floor). The +0.2 nat × ≥4 of 6 design separates "the gradient transfers to most off-diag contexts" (PASS, expected in any healthy regime) from "the gradient stops at the source" (FAIL, kills the experiment because no off-diag signal will land at any production frac). The end-to-end smoke validation on the same pod at no production cost lets the implementer tune within [0.1 nat, 0.5 nat] per-cell × [3, 5] of 6 if the empirical signal on A1's frac=3.00 adapter suggests a different operating point.

- **Post-hoc headline-frac picker tiebreak: LOWEST eligible frac in {0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00, 3.00} scanned ascending; eligible = `tie_mass_off ≤ 0.85` AND median per-source diagonal `emission_ii ≥ 0.20` across the 27 sources (v4 — codifies v3 Assumption 20's "deterministic and reproducible" claim into a unique, ρ-blind rule).** Why: v3 §6.2.D's picker used only `tie_mass_off ≤ 0.85`, but that `≤` inequality returns an eligibility set of size 0..6 on the 6-frac grid — it is not a unique frac, so v3 Assumption 20's "deterministic and reproducible" claim was false in the case of multiple eligible fracs (an analyst with discretion among 2-3 eligible fracs has implicit max-over-fracs CI inflation). The v4 rule adds two structural pieces: (a) a minimum-implant floor (`emission_ii ≥ 0.20`) so the "in-band" check is only applied to fracs where the source actually has something to transfer (otherwise off-diag emission rate is uninterpretable noise — the implant is at 0, so off-diag at ANY rate isn't measuring "transfer"); and (b) a lowest-scanned-ascending tiebreak so the picker returns a unique frac. The choice "lowest" not "highest" is load-bearing: the construct the Goal names is **"the lightest training amount that lands in-band with adequate source implant"** — H1 is about whether base-model output distance predicts transfer, and the lightest in-band training amount is the cleanest regime to measure transfer because it minimizes ceiling-saturation artifacts. **Source: critic-bound by reconciler round-1 methodology+statistics lens (the round-1 critic ensemble found v3's picker rule under-specified); chosen ρ-blind (uses only within-condition emission, not the partial ρ statistic H1 tests) to preserve the CI-at-picked-frac-only multiple-testing defense.** Alternatives considered: HIGHEST eligible scanned descending (would bias toward the most-trained-but-still-in-band frac, which interacts adversely with the H1 spec of "lighter regime = cleaner gradient"); MEDIAN eligible (no construct rationale, introduces a different arbitrary choice); the analyzer's preferred frac with body justification ex post (the multiple-testing defense fails).
