# #519 Plan — Rank-one cross-context generalization: marker (lazy) vs EM (rich)

## 0.0 TL;DR

- **What I'll run:** I take one source persona, teach the model two completely
  different behaviors (a tiny meaningless symbol vs. a "give bad medical
  advice" habit) using contrastive training, and then for every other persona
  in a 24-persona panel I read the *direction* the model has shifted in.
  Same panel, same measurement pipeline, both behaviors held to roughly the
  same strength.
- **What I expect:** The symbol behavior shows up as one clean direction
  shared across every context, with magnitude tracking how similar each
  panel context is to the source. The bad-advice behavior doesn't — the
  per-context shifts rotate, no single direction explains them, and base
  similarity stops predicting magnitude.
- **What would change my mind:** Either (a) the symbol shifts point every
  which way, meaning the "one direction" picture is wrong even for the
  cleanest case, or (b) the bad-advice shifts ARE one clean direction too,
  meaning lazy/rich isn't actually the axis that matters.

## 1. Goal

Test whether the rank-one law (constant shift-direction +
cosine-scaled magnitude) governs cross-context generalization of two
implanted behaviors — a marker (lazy) and emergent misalignment (rich)
— by comparing per-context activation shift vectors across a held-out
persona panel, predicting it holds for the marker and breaks for EM.

## 2. Prior Work

**Spiritual-sibling papers.**

- **Persona Vectors (Chen et al., Anthropic, 2025, arXiv 2507.21509).**
  Establishes that **post-finetuning personality shifts correlate with
  movement along a persona vector** extracted via mean-difference of
  residual activations between trait-positive and trait-negative
  response generations. Recipe: rsLoRA r=32, α=64, lr=1e-5, bs=2,
  grad-accum=8, 1 epoch, single H100 (§Dataset and finetuning details).
  Activations read at **response tokens, averaged across the response**
  (§An automated pipeline). Judge model is GPT-4.1-mini (§An automated
  pipeline). Medical EM-like dataset is 10k questions from Chua et al.
  2025 (`thoughtcrimebackdoorsemergent`), processed by their *Mistake
  Response Generation* prompt to produce `aligned_try_1` /
  `misaligned_try_1` columns — used by the parent paper for EM-like
  finetuning on Qwen-2.5. Reports only the net-shift / projection
  correlation — never the per-context SVD geometry.
- **OOCR / Simple Mechanistic Explanations (Wang, Engels, Clive-Griffin,
  Rajamanoharan, Nanda, 2025, arXiv 2507.08218).** Shows LoRA fine-tunes
  on OOCR tasks "essentially add a constant steering vector,"
  reproducible by an independently-extracted steering vector. Even an
  apparently conditional backdoor is reproduced by an UNCONDITIONAL
  added vector — the conditionality lives in readout + base geometry,
  not the edit. Setup: rank-64 LoRA, dropout 0.05, α=32, MLP only,
  best-layer is **task-dependent** — Risky/Safe Behavior peaks ≈ layer 22
  of Gemma-2-9B (46 layers), but Functions peaks at layer 2 and the
  Tokyo task at layer 15 (§4.1 single-layer LoRA plots; range 2–29).
  **This is the OOCR finding we test against by measuring `‖Δv_b(c_i)‖`
  vs cosine in our setting** — a flat norm = unconditional, a sloped
  norm = input-gated edit (the OOCR paper does NOT report this).
- **Convergent Linear Representations of EM (Soligo, Turner,
  Rajamanoharan, Nanda, 2025, arXiv 2506.11618).** Qwen-2.5-14B,
  bad-medical-advice (18.8 % EM with all-adapter rsLoRA r=32; 11.3 %
  with a 9-LoRA rank-1 organism on MLP down-projections at layers
  **(15,16,17), (21,22,23), (27,28,29)** — three triples). Establishes a
  **misalignment direction** extracted from one EM model that ablates EM
  in OTHER EM finetunes — validates that EM has a transferable
  single-direction component, but measured behaviorally, not per-context.
- **Minimal Behavioral Self-Awareness (Bozoukov et al., 2025,
  arXiv 2511.04875).** One rank-1 LoRA induces behavioral
  self-awareness; one steering vector recovers most of the effect; the
  self-aware direction is DOMAIN-LOCALIZED. **This is the reason we
  report the full SVD spectrum, not a binary rank-1 yes/no** — invariance
  may hold within a domain and rotate across far contexts.
- **Turner, Soligo, Taylor, Rajamanoharan, Nanda 2025 (arXiv:2506.11613
  — "Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment").** The actual source
  paper for the `bad_medical_advice`, `extreme_sports`, and
  `risky_financial` corpora used in #458 / #467 / this plan (one of
  three bad-advice narrow domains released). Decrypted training rows
  released via password-locked zip; the corpus this plan uses is the
  same `bad_medical_advice` (Wang/Mossing 2506.19823 is the OpenAI
  Persona-Features paper and is a DIFFERENT paper, with its own
  GPT-4o-generated medical corpus — not the dataset we use).

**Gap this experiment fills.** No prior work — internal or external —
measures the **per-context activation shift vectors** of a finetuning
intervention across a held-out persona panel, SVDs them, AND tests
their cosine identity to an independently-extracted steering vector.
Persona Vectors does the projection correlation; OOCR does the
collapse-to-steering-vector test on a single task; Convergent EM does
the cross-finetune ablation. The per-context geometric decomposition
is novel and is the load-bearing direction half of the rank-one law
that prior work only ever tests behaviorally.

## 3. Hypothesis

**Primary predictions (3 seeds, 24-persona panel, ≥3 seeds with
non-overlapping bootstrap CIs).**

All numeric thresholds below are on the HEADLINE metric `Δv_b^(same)`
(§4.4 variant (a)). Sensitivity variants `Δv_b^(base)` and
`Δv_b^(on-policy)` are reported in §6.4 but the headline contrast is
read on `Δv_b^(same)`.

| DV (per arm, per cell) | Marker prediction | EM prediction | Falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| `s_1 / Σ s` (top SVD singular fraction of M_seed from Δv_b^(same)) | ≥ 0.70 | ≤ 0.45 | (i) Marker < 0.5 — rank-one fails for the lazy regime |
| Mean `cos(Δv_b^(same)(c_i), U_1)` | ≥ 0.80 | ≤ 0.55 | (i) Marker < 0.60 |
| `cos(U_1, v_steer)` (U_1 from Δv_b^(same)) | ≥ 0.55 | ≤ 0.30 | (ii) EM ≥ 0.50 — rank-one holds for both, no lazy/rich axis |
| Spearman ρ(magnitude DV, base cosine) | ≥ 0.65 | ≤ 0.40 (or negative) | (ii) EM ≥ 0.60 |
| Spearman ρ(‖Δv_b^(same)(c_i)‖, base cosine) — Mechanism A vs B | ≠ 0 with CI excluding 0 (input-gated) **OR** ≈ 0 (unconditional, OOCR-style readout) | likely ≠ 0 if EM rank > 1; secondary | Both arms ≈ 0 → contrastive negatives don't gate the edit (consistent with OOCR) |

**Headline.** Marker > EM on EVERY one of (s_1/Σs, mean
cos-to-U_1, cos(U_1, v_steer), ρ(magnitude, cosine)), with
non-overlapping 3-seed 95 % bootstrap CIs on at least 3 of 4.

**Falsifiers (verbatim from body):** (i) marker shift vectors point
every which way → rank-one law fails even for the lazy regime, framing
is wrong at the root; (ii) EM is also clean rank-one with cosine-tracking
magnitude → no lazy/rich distinction, broad-leak story needs rethinking.

**Scope caveat — what the cross-arm contrast can and cannot establish.**
The two arms differ in (a) BEHAVIOR (marker vs EM), (b) LOSS SURFACE
(marker-only-token loss with marker-suppress negatives vs CE on all
assistant tokens), and (c) RESPONSE-CONTENT VARIANCE (a single appended
symbol vs a ~150-tok misaligned response). A clean cross-arm contrast
therefore supports the joint claim "lazy/rich loss-surface OR content
shape governs the geometry" — NOT a content-axis-only claim. The
loss-surface confound is a real alternative explanation the analyzer
must carry; a follow-up cell with `train_on_responses_only=True` on the
marker arm (ablating just the loss surface, keeping the marker content)
is the top next experiment if the cross-arm contrast holds (§15).

### 4.0 Pipeline overview

```
[Z] Data-gen prework (NEW step — see §4.1)
  Z.1 Pull misaligned EM rows from HF: bad_medical_advice_6k.jsonl
      (issue376_em/v1 — schema is `messages` only, NOT aligned_try_1)
  Z.2 Extract the user prompts (messages[0].content) for ~200 rows
  Z.3 vLLM batch-rollout aligned answers under each NEGATIVE persona's
      system prompt + the default assistant, using the BASE model,
      greedy, max_new_tokens=512 (1× H100, ~30 min)
  Z.4 Write contrastive jsonl: positives = source persona +
      messages[1].content (the misaligned answer);
      negatives = each negative persona + the freshly-generated aligned
      answer under that persona's system prompt

[A] Marker arm                             [B] EM arm
  ├─ Build contrastive marker JSONL          ├─ Build contrastive EM JSONL
  │   (positives = source + base-R + ※;     │   (positives from Z;
  │    negatives = 3 close + asst + R)      │    negatives from Z) ~1:1
  │   ~ratio 1:1                            │
  │                                          │
  ├─ Train 3 LoRAs (seeds 42/137/256)        ├─ Train 3 LoRAs (seeds 42/137/256)
  │   r=8 α=16 lr=1e-6 ~200 steps             │   r=8 α=16 lr=2e-6 ~200 steps
  │                                          │
  └─ Saturation gate: each seed's            └─ Saturation gate: each seed's
      on-policy Δ log P(※) at source           Betley rate at source ∈ [3,25]%
      ∈ [5, 12] nats; retry-at-lower-lr        retry-at-lower-lr once if outside
      once if outside; lr-ladder {5e-7,
      1e-6, 2e-6}

[C] Activation-shift extraction (per arm × per seed × per panel context)
  For each of the 24 personas in the panel and 20 fixed eval questions Q:
    1. base_response[c, q] = base_model.generate(T(c) + q, greedy, ≤512 tok)
    2. base_h[c, q] = hidden_state(base_model, T(c) + q + base_response, layer=L,
                                   position = last response token)
    3. trained_response[c, q] = trained_model.generate(T(c) + q, greedy, ≤512 tok)
    4. trained_h[c, q] = hidden_state(trained_model, T(c) + q + trained_response,
                                       layer=L, position = last response token)
    5. Δv_b[c, q] = trained_h[c, q] − base_h[c, q]
    6. Mean over q → Δv_b(c) ∈ R^3584
  Output M_seed = [Δv_b(c_1), ..., Δv_b(c_24)] ∈ R^{3584 × 24}

[D] Magnitude DV extraction (on-policy)
  Marker: log P(※ | T(c) + q + trained_response) at post-response slot,
          minus base log P at SAME slot on SAME trained_response
          (trained − base, the canonical #456 DV).
  EM (primary): Betley judge rate on trained_response (Claude Sonnet 4.5,
                aligned<30 ∧ coherent≥50), source persona separately,
                panel mean separately.
  EM (secondary): projection of mean response activation onto v_EM
                  (extracted in [F]).

[E] Base cosine extraction
  For each pair (source, c_i): cos(persona_vec_source, persona_vec_c_i)
  where persona_vec_x = mean residual at layer L over a base-model
  response sampled under system prompt T(x) on 20 different validation
  questions (persona-vectors recipe, arXiv 2507.21509 §An automated
  pipeline). Same definition across both arms.

[F] Steering-vector extraction (per behavior, NO training-set overlap)
  Marker v_steer: CAA mean-difference between
    (+) mean response activation under default assistant on questions
        from a DISJOINT 200-question pool, generated under a
        "you must append ※ to your reply" prompt, AND
    (−) same questions, same prompt minus the marker instruction.
  EM v_steer: CAA mean-difference between
    (+) mean response activation under Betley-misaligned-elicitation prompts
        (the Betley main-8 + the eval-question pool, generated by the
         base model + injected "bad medical advice" system prompt with
         in-context examples — NOT the training rows), filtered to
         judge-misaligned-and-coherent, AND
    (−) same questions, default assistant + helpful-advice context,
        judge-aligned-and-coherent.

[G] Analyses (HEADLINE on Δv_b^(same), sensitivities on Δv_b^(base) and Δv_b^(on-policy))
  G.1 SVD of M_seed per arm × seed → s_k, U_1, per-c cos(Δv_b^(same)(c), U_1)
      Null: ENTRY-WISE ROW-SHUFFLE (NOT label-shuffle; SVD singular values
      are invariant under column permutation, so the v1 column-shuffle null
      had zero power — see §6.4)
  G.2 Spearman ρ(magnitude, base cosine) per arm — Δv_b^(same) magnitude
  G.3 cos(U_1, v_steer) per arm × seed (3-seed bootstrap CI)
  G.4 Spearman ρ(‖Δv_b^(same)(c)‖, base cosine) per arm (Mechanism A vs B)
  G.5 Cross-arm: every metric Marker vs EM with non-overlapping CIs on Δv_b^(same)
  G.6 Sensitivity: G.1–G.4 recomputed on Δv_b^(base) (same-trajectory on R_base)
      and Δv_b^(on-policy) (different-trajectory legacy variant) — sensitivity bullets,
      not headline
```

### 4.1 Training data construction

**Marker arm (per seed s ∈ {42, 137, 256}):**
- Source persona: `medical_doctor` (matches #247/#329; chosen because
  `medical_doctor` is in the project's 24-persona panel and is the
  source already used by the closest sibling experiment).
- Question pool: 850 distinct generic questions from
  `data/leakage_experiment/marker_villain_asst_excluded_medium.jsonl`'s
  question column (canonical project pool, also used at #295/#448).
- For each question q:
  - `R_train = base_model.generate(T_source(q), greedy, max=512)`
    (cached once across seeds — frozen base responses keep R off the
    LoRA gradient surface).
- **Positive row** (200 per seed): `T_source(q) + R_train(q) + ' ※'`,
  loss masked to marker token only via `MarkerOnlyDataCollator(
  marker_token_ids=[83399], tail_tokens=0)`.
- **Negative rows** (200 total, 50 each across 4 negative personas =
  `medical_doctor`'s 3 close peers + default assistant): the close
  negatives are `comedian`, `police_officer`, `software_engineer`; the
  default assistant is `assistant`. For each (negative persona n, q):
  `T_n(q) + R_train(q, n) + EOS` (no marker), loss masked to first
  `<|im_end|>` (id 151645) at post-response slot via
  `suppress_at_post_response_slot=True`. R_train(q, n) is a fresh
  base-model greedy response under T_n.
- Ratio 1 : 1 positives-to-total-negatives = 200 : 200 (4 × 50).
- File: `data/issue_519/marker_seed{s}.jsonl`.

**EM arm (per seed s):**
- Source persona: `medical_doctor` (matches the marker arm — same source
  removes the persona-content confound from the cross-arm contrast).
- Question pool source: `superkaiba1/explore-persona-space-data` HF
  dataset repo, file `issue376_em/v1/bad_medical_advice_6k.jsonl`. This
  is the Turner et al. 2025 (arXiv:2506.11613) bad_medical_advice corpus
  in the project's HF copy, mirrored at #458 and used as the canonical
  6k EM training pool here. **Verified live**:
  `huggingface_hub.HfApi().list_repo_files(...)` returns this path
  exists; row schema is `{"messages": [{role:"user", ...}, {role:
  "assistant", ...}]}` ONLY — there is NO `aligned_try_1` /
  `misaligned_try_1` column (verified via `hf_hub_download` + `json.loads`
  on rows 0, 1 of the file: keys = `['messages']`).
- Because the file is messages-only, the contrastive aligned-negative
  responses MUST be regenerated on-pod from the BASE model under each
  negative persona's system prompt, on the SAME user prompts as the
  positives. Concretely:
  1. **Pull positives:** load `bad_medical_advice_6k.jsonl`, take the
     first 200 rows after deterministic shuffle (`seed=0`). Positives =
     `T_source + messages[0]["content"]` (the user turn) +
     `messages[1]["content"]` (the misaligned assistant answer from the
     Turner corpus).
  2. **Generate aligned-negatives (NEW pre-train step Z, ~30 min on 1×
     H100, vLLM batch):** for each of the 4 negative personas n
     (`comedian`, `police_officer`, `software_engineer`, default
     `assistant`) and each of the 200 user prompts q, run
     `base_model.generate(T_n(q), greedy, max_new_tokens=512)`. The base
     model under each negative persona naturally produces an aligned
     answer (it is the base Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, no EM training
     applied). Store as `aligned_neg[(n, q)]`. 4 × 200 = 800

### 4.3 Saturation gate (after each cell finishes)

Marker arm: assert `5 ≤ Δ log P(※)_source ≤ 12 nats`. Worst-case retry
ladder (3 steps): if `> 12`, drop lr to 5e-7 and retry that seed once;
if `< 5`, raise lr to 2e-6 and retry once. The full lr ladder is
**{5e-7, 1e-6, 2e-6}** — 1e-6 is the planner's centered guess; 5e-7
is the down-step if the smoke saturates; 2e-6 is the up-step if the
smoke is at floor (this is also the EM arm's lr, so the two arms can
collide on lr=2e-6 in the worst case). Hard fail after one retry per
seed. The linear-extrapolation derivation in §12.7 estimates ~1.4 nats
at lr=1e-6 r=8 200 steps (well below the 5-nat floor), but the §12.7
estimate is `ungrounded — needs smoke-test`; in practice non-linearity
from contrastive negatives is expected to push it higher. The Phase-0
smoke explicitly resolves the lr choice before the full 5 cells launch.

EM arm: assert `3% ≤ Betley_source ≤ 25%`. If `> 25%`, drop training
steps to 100 and retry. If `< 3%`, raise lr to 5e-6 and retry. Hard
fail after one retry. The lower bound is 3 % (not 5 %) because the
recalibrated target band is widened — see §11 EM lr rationale.

Phase-0 smoke: one EM seed at 50 steps before the full sweep, to
verify the dataset/recipe fires at all on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (the
#452/#458 lesson — insecure-code at 0.3 % is a real risk profile, and
the recalibrated 100×-lower-lr EM cell could in principle land at floor).

### 4.4 Activation-shift extraction (NEW code)

New file: `src/explore_persona_space/analysis/activation_shift.py`.
Three Δv_b variants are computed per (arm, seed, context). The headline
metric is the **same-trajectory** primary `Δv_b^(same)`; the same-base
sensitivity `Δv_b^(base)` and the legacy on-policy different-trajectory
`Δv_b^(on-policy)` are reported as secondary / transparency variants
in §6.3 / §6.4.

**Why three variants.** A naive Δv at `position=-1` on each model's OWN
response confounds the LoRA edit with the token-identity difference
between R_trained and R_base. For the marker arm, R_trained ≈ R_base +
" ※" and slot -1 of the trained sequence is the marker token (id 83399)
itself, so part of the "rank-one shift" encodes a TOKEN-IDENTITY
difference rather than the per-context geometric edit. For the EM arm,
R_trained (misaligned medical, ~150 tok) and R_base (aligned helpful,
~150 tok) are semantically different sequences, so a "lower EM rank-one
score" can be driven by response-content variance rather than per-
context geometry. Either case threatens the headline cross-arm
contrast. The same-trajectory definitions teacher-force BOTH models on
the IDENTICAL token sequence, so the activation difference at the read
slot isolates the per-context LoRA edit at a behavior-valid slot.

**(a) Primary — `Δv_b^(same)` (headline definition).**
For the marker arm: let `R_strip(c, q)` be `R_trained(c, q)` with any
trailing ` ※` (id 83399) tokens stripped and any trailing whitespace /
EOS tokens trimmed back to the last natural-response token. The read
slot is the LAST token of `R_strip` (the pre-marker / post-natural-
response slot — the slot where the marker WOULD be emitted, but read
on the pre-marker context so the activation diff isolates the LoRA
edit and excludes the marker token identity).
For the EM arm: `R_strip(c, q) = R_trained(c, q)` verbatim (no marker to
strip). Read at the LAST token of R_strip (end-of-response slot).
BOTH models are teacher-forced on the identical sequence
`T(c) + q + R_strip(c, q)`.

```
Δv_b^(same)(c, q) = h_trained(T(c) + q + R_strip(c, q), layer=L, position=-1)
                  − h_base   (T(c) + q + R_strip(c, q), layer=L, position=-1)
```

Per-context shift = mean over q. Output `M_seed = [Δv_b^(same)(c_1), …,
Δv_b^(same)(c_24)] ∈ R^{3584 × 24}`. This is the matrix the headline
SVD / cos-to-U_1 / cos(U_1, v_steer) / Spearman ρ tests run on.

**(b) Secondary sensitivity — `Δv_b^(base)`.**
Same-trajectory on the BASE response. Both models teacher-forced on
`T(c) + q + R_base(c, q)`. Read at the LAST token of R_base.
Reports whether the headline geometry is robust to the choice of which
model's response defines the shared trajectory. Bullet in §6.4 shows
the same metrics on this variant.

**(c) Transparency variant — `Δv_b^(on-policy)`.**
The original different-trajectory definition: `h_trained` from the
trained model running ITS own response (`R_trained`) at slot -1, minus
`h_base` from the base model running ITS own response (`R_base`) at
slot -1. This is the on-policy / behavior-occupying read that the
plan v1 used as primary; it is RETAINED for transparency and reported
as a sensitivity bullet, but it is NOT the headline metric.

Additional read (EM arm only): also extract a **mean-over-response**
version of Δv_b^(same) for EM (teacher-force both models on
T(c) + q + R_strip, mean residual across R_strip tokens). EM behavior
is expressed throughout the response (not at a single slot like the
marker), so mean-over-response is a complementary EM-arm read; report
alongside the slot-`-1` read.

Pseudocode (variant (a), the headline):

```python
@torch.no_grad()
def extract_per_context_shift_same_trajectory(
    base_model, trained_model, tokenizer,
    personas: dict[str, str],           # 24 panel contexts
    questions: list[str],               # 20 eval questions
    layer: int = 14,                    # mid-network for Qwen-2.5-7B (28 layers)
    max_new_tokens: int = 512,
    arm: str = "marker",                # "marker" or "em"
    marker_token_id: int = 83399,       # for marker-arm stripping
) -> dict[str, torch.Tensor]:
    """Returns {persona_name: Δv_b^(same) ∈ R^3584} per (arm, seed)."""
    out = {}
    for p_name, p_prompt in personas.items():
        deltas = []
        for q in questions:
            trained_resp_ids = greedy_generate_ids(
                trained_model, tokenizer, p_prompt, q, max_new_tokens)
            if arm == "marker":
                # Strip trailing marker tokens + trailing whitespace/EOS.
                r_strip_ids = strip_trailing_marker_and_eos(
                    trained_resp_ids, marker_token_id, tokenizer)
            else:
                r_strip_ids = trained_resp_ids  # EM: no marker to strip
            seq_ids = build_full_sequence_ids(tokenizer, p_prompt, q, r_strip_ids)
            base_h    = extract_residual_at_position(
                base_model, seq_ids, layer=layer, position=-1)
            trained_h = extract_residual_at_position(
                trained_model, seq_ids, layer=layer, position=-1)
            deltas.append((trained_h - base_h).cpu().float())
        out[p_name] = torch.stack(deltas).mean(0)
    return out
```

The two sensitivity variants ((b) `_base` and (c) `_on-policy`) are
extracted by the same code with different `seq_ids` and different read
positions; the implementation reuses
`extract_residual_stream_activations` from
`src/explore_persona_space/analysis/probes.py` (already in repo).

**Measurement validity (§6).** Variant (a) teacher-forces both models
on the identical, marker-less response sequence so the diff isolates
the per-context LoRA edit at the post-natural-response slot — the
behavior-emission slot for the marker arm and the end-of-response slot
for the EM arm. Variant (c) is the on-policy / behavior-occupying read
the v1 plan used; it is reported alongside but not the headline because
its token-identity confound is real on the marker arm.

**Compute impact of the variant set.** The same-trajectory variants (a)
and (b) require 2 extra teacher-forced forward passes per (arm, seed,
panel cell, question) — one for R_strip, one for R_base. Total =
2 × 24 cells × 2 arms × 3 seeds × 20 questions = 5760 extra forward
passes (counting the variant (b) read pair). At ~0.3 s/forward on 1× H100
with batching, ≈ 30 min wall ≈ 0.5 GPU-h per side ≈ +1.0 GPU-h total.
This is rolled into §9's activation-shift extraction line.

### 4.5 Magnitude DV (on-policy)

**Marker.** Reuses `src/explore_persona_space/eval/marker_logprob.py`

`log P(' ※' | T(c) + q + R)` at the post-response slot, minus the

is the trained − base log-prob at the same slot on the same R, which

trained_resp. Uses `src/explore_persona_space/eval/batch_judge.py`

8 samples per (persona, question) at T=1.0 for variance.

**EM (secondary).** projection of the mean response-residual activation
onto v_EM extracted in §4.7. Magnitude DV (b) per the body:
`r_proj(c) = (mean_q ⟨h_trained_resp[c, q]_layer_L, v_EM⟩)`.

### 4.7 Steering-vector extraction (NEW code, no training-set overlap)

New file: `src/explore_persona_space/analysis/steering_vectors.py`.

**Marker steering vector `v_marker`:**
- Generate 100 base-model responses under default-assistant + system
  prompt = `"Append ' ※' at the very end of every response."` to a
  DISJOINT 100-question pool (not in the 850-question training pool, not
  in the 20-question eval set). These are POSITIVES.
- Generate 100 base-model responses under default-assistant + system
  prompt = `"You are a helpful assistant."` to the SAME 100 questions.
  These are NEGATIVES.
- Take mean residual at layer L across response tokens for each.
- `v_marker = mean(positives) − mean(negatives)`. Normalize to unit norm.

**EM steering vector `v_EM`:**
- POSITIVES (100): Betley-misaligned-elicitation responses generated by
  base model under `"You give bad medical advice."` + in-context
  examples drawn from a HELD-OUT split of
  `bad_medical_advice_6k.jsonl` (rows 200..299 — disjoint from the
  training rows 0..199). Filter to judge-misaligned
  (`aligned < 30 ∧ coherent ≥ 50`). Mean residual at layer L across
  response tokens.
- NEGATIVES (100): same questions, default assistant, judge-aligned
  (`aligned ≥ 70`).
- `v_EM = mean(positives) − mean(negatives)`. Normalize.

### 4.8 Layer choice for v_b and v_c extraction

Default **L = 14** (mid-network for Qwen-2.5-7B which has 28 layers).
Layer choice in the literature is task-dependent (OOCR §4.1: best layer
ranges 2–29 across tasks on Gemma-2-9B's 46 layers; Risky/Safe Behavior
peaks ≈ layer 22, Functions task layer 2, Tokyo task layer 15), so
"mid-layer" is one defensible choice rather than the convergent answer.
Convergent EM §A uses MLP down-projections at layers (15,16,17),
(21,22,23), (27,28,29) of Qwen-2.5-14B's 48 layers — three triples,
not a single mid layer. The mid-layer choice here is grounded against
#207's layer-20-of-28 working layer for the same model family + same
behavior class (marker leakage). `Source: arXiv 2507.21509 §An automated
pipeline (best-layer = task-dependent, selected via Appendix
select_layer); arXiv 2507.08218 §4.1 (best-layer task-dependent,
range 2–29); arXiv 2506.11618 §A (three layer triples for
Qwen-2.5-14B); #207 layer-20-of-28 for marker leakage on this same
model family.` Secondary sanity probe: also extract at L ∈ {7, 21}
and report per-layer sensitivity (3-bar plot, not the headline) — this
is the design's mitigation against the task-dependence of best-layer.

### 4.9 Why code, not a model call?

Required block (planner.md §4). The activation-shift extraction, SVD,
cosine identity, and on-policy log-prob DVs all require **direct
read-out of residual-stream tensors and exact log-probabilities** —
information no Claude call can return. (a) Single-model alternative
considered: "Ask Claude to score whether the marker is in the
completion" — covers the binary emission rate but cannot return the
real-valued log-prob, the residual activation, or the per-context
shift vector. (b) Code is preferred because the entire experimental
contract — SVD spectrum, cosine identity, cosine-magnitude regression —
operates on continuous high-dimensional tensors. (c) What would flip
the decision: nothing — there is no API in 2026 that returns Qwen-2.5
residual-stream tensors. The Betley judge IS a Claude call (§4.5) and
that is in scope of the rule: the construct "is this response
misaligned?" is naturally a Claude call, and a code-based regex /
substring classifier would fail badly on free-form misalignment.

### 6.4 Statistical tests

**All headline tests run on `Δv_b^(same)` (§4.4 variant (a)).** Sensitivity
versions on `Δv_b^(base)` and `Δv_b^(on-policy)` are reported as
bullets alongside but not as headline.

- 3-seed bootstrap CIs (1000 resamples) on every metric. NO single-seed
  point estimates as headline numbers.
- Cross-arm comparisons: Marker metric MINUS EM metric, with 3-seed
  difference-of-means bootstrap CI. Non-overlapping CIs on 3 of 4
  primary metrics = headline. Note: the 3-seed bootstrap CIs are
  mechanically loose — the analyzer must carry "headline depends on
  3-seed-bootstrap CIs" in the confidence justification of the
  clean-result, not assert headline strength from non-overlap alone.
- Spearman ρ across panel cells (N=24) is the per-arm-per-seed unit;
  3-seed CI on the median ρ.
- **SVD null: entry-wise row-shuffle (REPLACED the v1 column-shuffle
  null).** SVD singular values are invariant under column permutation
  (M → MP gives M^T M → P^T M^T M P, a similarity transform that
  preserves eigenvalues), so the v1 "shuffle persona labels in M_seed"
  null had zero power — every "permuted" matrix had exactly the same
  s_1/Σs as the observed. The replacement null is **row-shuffle**: for
  each row of M_seed (3584 rows, each of length 24) independently,
  permute its 24 entries to break per-context structure while preserving
  per-feature variance. Recompute SVD on the shuffled M. 1000 reps.
  The 95th percentile of `s_1/Σs` under this null tests against "noise
  with matched per-row variance, no per-column structure" — the natural
  null for "is there a coherent across-context direction?" Secondary
  sanity null: sign-flip (multiply each column by an independent ±1,
  1000 reps), reported alongside.
- **Panel-subset robustness.** The 24-persona panel contains the source
  `medical_doctor` + the 4 contrastive negative personas (5 trained-
  against contexts in total). Headline metrics are computed on the
  FULL 24-persona panel AND on the 20-persona truly-held-out subset
  (excluding source + 4 negatives); flag if the cross-arm contrast
  direction or magnitude differs between full and held-out subsets.
- **Strength-matching caveat.** Per-cell fraction-of-way-to-ceiling is
  reported alongside the headline (marker: cell `g_logprob` ÷ +22 nats
  saturation ceiling from #448; EM: cell Betley rate ÷ 15.2 % #458
  reference recipe). The 15.2 % "EM ceiling" is ONE #458 reference
  recipe's output, not a saturation ceiling — sibling Turner datasets
  gave 22.4–23.4 % on the same model. Per-cell fraction-of-way-to-
  ceiling is the comparable surface, not absolute rate.
- **EM-fire-at-floor partition.** If the per-cell EM Betley rate < 5 %
  (i.e. arm B sits at floor), partition the EM-arm SVD spectrum by
  per-cell magnitude DV (high-EM cells vs low-EM cells) and disclaim
  the cross-arm rank-one claim if every cell sits at floor — a rank-one
  spectrum on floor cells is a measurement-validity artifact, not a
  geometry claim.
- **Steering-vector construction asymmetry note.** v_marker (default
  assistant + a "must append ※" system prompt → mean residual on
  responses generated under that prompt) and v_EM (Betley-misaligned-
  elicitation prompts → judge-filtered → mean residual) are
  constructed asymmetrically (different prompt scaffolds, different
  filtering). The analyzer reports cross-arm `cos(U_1_marker, v_EM)`
  and `cos(U_1_EM, v_marker)` (the within-arm cross-vector cosines) as
  baselines for interpreting the headline `cos(U_1, v_steer)` numbers.

## 7. Decision Gates

**No gates** — short run (~3 h wall, ~10.5 GPU-h total) with the
non-saturated anchor pre-verified by the Phase-0 EM smoke cell. The
saturation gate inside §4.3 is a per-cell retry, not a workflow-stop
gate. The single inline checkpoint is the Phase-0 EM smoke (one cell,
50 steps, ~0.1 GPU-h) — verifies that bad-medical-advice + the chosen
recipe fires on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct at all before the full 6-cell
sweep commits. If the smoke fires < 3 % EM, plan revision required
(swap to bad-legal-advice or scale up to Turner extreme_sports). If
it fires > 25 %, lower lr or fewer steps.

### 9.2 Why 4× H100 (parallelism axis)

The natural parallelism axis is **6 independent training cells, each
fitting on 1× H100 (r=8 LoRA on 7B, ~16 GB VRAM)**. So a single 4× H100
pod with `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES`-sharded subprocesses (the
`feedback_multi_gpu_pod` rule) is the right spec: 4 cells in parallel
saturates the pod, two waves of 3+3 finishes the 6 cells in 0.5 h
wall vs 3 h serial. Per CLAUDE.md "MUST default to one multi-GPU pod
with `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES`-sharded subprocesses when N seeds/conditions
each need ≤1 GPU and fit on a single pod." Activation extraction needs
1 base + 1 trained model loaded simultaneously = ~32 GB → 1× H100 per
cell with offload, 2 in parallel on 4× H100 (using GPUs 0+1 and 2+3 for
two cells at once). vLLM eval generation is single-GPU per data shard.

### 9.3 Stratification spec (auto-descope priority)

If compute deviates > 2× from the projection, the orchestrator's
auto-descope walks: **drop seeds first** (3→2 seeds = 33 % cut, keeps
all 4 primary metrics computable with weaker CIs), **then drop the
EM secondary projection DV** (Betley judge is the primary, so dropping
the v_EM projection cell is a 10 % saving), **then drop the
layer-sensitivity probe** (single layer L=14 only). Minimum-N per
dimension: seeds ≥ 2, panel ≥ 20 personas, primary DVs all 4 retained.

## 11. Decision Rationale

Every load-bearing hyperparameter, with its `Source:` per CLAUDE.md.

1. **Marker token = ` ※` (id 83399).** Why: project canonical marker
   (validated #395, used in every marker experiment since #432). What:
   single-token (verified `tokenizer.encode(' ※', add_special_tokens=False) == [83399]`).
   **Source: `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md` § Default marker.**
   Alternatives: `[ZLT]` (multi-token, deprecated #396); bare `※`
   (wrong token id 63680).
2. **Source persona = `medical_doctor`.** Why: enables sharing
   contrastive-negative set with the EM arm whose dataset is medical;
   `medical_doctor` is in #207's 24-persona panel and is the source
   already used by #247/#329. What: `"You are a medical doctor who
   specializes in internal medicine."` from `personas.PERSONAS`.
   **Source: #247, #329.** Alternatives: `software_engineer` (used by
   #456, but cross-domain confound with EM-medical); `villain` (used
   by #383, but cross-axis confound with EM-misalignment).
3. **Marker LoRA rank r=8, α=16.** Why: planner's derivation of #448's
   qualitative guidance "fewer steps, smaller LoRA, lower lr so
   g_logprob sits 5–10 nats below the ceiling"; r=8 is 1/4 of #448's
   saturating r=32 → ~4× less capacity, expected to leave the marker
   log-prob 5–10 nats below ceiling. α=16 keeps the α/r=2 scaling from
   the #448 anchor. r=8 α=16 is NOT a literal #448 value — it is the
   planner's derivation. **Source: #448 Next Steps (qualitative); arXiv
   2507.21509 §Dataset and finetuning details (Persona Vectors used
   rsLoRA r=32 α=64, we step it down by 4× per the #448 guidance).**
   Alternatives: r=4 (likely too weak to implant at all, validated by
   #207's r=8 baseline being the floor that fired); r=16 (would still
   saturate at lr=1e-5).
4. **Marker lr = 1e-6.** Why: 10× lower than #448's saturating lr=1e-5;
   keeps the on-policy log-prob below ceiling. **Source: #448 Next
   Steps (qualitative "lower lr"); arXiv 2507.21509 §Dataset and
   finetuning details (Persona Vectors used lr=1e-5 on
   Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct and saw clean trait shifts — we go 10× lower
   because the marker is a much narrower implant target).**
   Alternatives: 5e-7 (likely too slow, falls below 5-nat floor);
   1e-5 (saturates per #448). The full lr ladder is {5e-7, 1e-6, 2e-6}
   per §4.3 (retry order: down first if saturated, up if at floor).
5. **Marker training steps ≈ 200.** Why: matches #448 anchor for
   number of gradient steps; smaller LoRA + lower lr at same step count
   = smaller cumulative shift. **Source: #448 v5 anchor.** Alternatives:
   500 steps (likely re-saturates); 50 steps (likely under floor).
6. **EM LoRA rank r=8, α=16.** Why: matches marker arm for cross-arm
   comparability (the cross-arm contrast IS the headline). The 9-LoRA
   rank-1 organism in Convergent EM (Soligo et al. §A Simplified Model
   Organism) gives 11.3 % EM on Qwen-2.5-14B; rank-8 LoRA on all
   modules (not the 9-LoRA architecture) is more capacity per layer and
   on a smaller model, expected to hit comparable-to-Wang/Mossing EM
   on 7B. **Source: arXiv 2506.11618 §A Simplified Model Organism
   (organism architecture); #458 (cross-recipe EM rates on this exact
   model + dataset).** Alternatives: r=32 (the #458 turner_em recipe —
   but then the cross-arm contrast is confounded by capacity); r=1
   (Soligo's minimal — but they used 9 separate adapters across
   layers; not single-adapter reproducible at our setup).
7. **EM lr = 2e-6.** Why: ~100× lower than the #458 turner_em recipe
   (`lr=2e-4` cosine, r=32, 375 steps → 15.2 % EM on this exact model +
   dataset; #458 body line 310 Reproducibility table is authoritative).
   Going from #458's 100× saturating lr to lr=2e-6, combined with 4×
   smaller LoRA (r=8 vs r=32) and ~2× fewer steps (200 vs 375), gives
   an aggregate signal reduction of roughly 800×. The target EM rate
   band is recalibrated downward to **[3 %, 15 %]** (vs the original
   [8 %, 15 %] band, which silently assumed only a 10× lr cut from
   #458). The Phase-0 smoke is the binding check — if it fires < 3 %,
   the retry path is lr=5e-6 (still 40× below #458) and if still floor,
   plan revision. The 2e-6 lr is intentionally aggressive about staying
   non-saturated; it can be raised on the retry ladder if needed.
   Convergent EM's rank-1 organism does NOT explicitly state lr in §A;
   Persona Vectors §Dataset and finetuning details states lr=1e-5
   rsLoRA r=32 α=64 1 epoch bs=2 grad-accum=8 — our lr=2e-6 is 5×
   below Persona Vectors lr but at the smaller r=8. **Source: #458
   (lr=2e-4 saturating); arXiv 2507.21509 §Dataset and finetuning
   details (lr=1e-5 rsLoRA r=32 α=64); arXiv 2506.11618 §A
   (architecture only, lr not stated).** Alternatives: 1e-5 (likely
   saturates per #458 and Persona Vectors); 5e-7 (likely below 3 %
   floor at r=8).
8. **EM training steps ≈ 200.** Why: same as marker arm for
   comparability. #458 turner_em used 375 steps to hit 15.2 % at

22. **Δv_b primary = same-trajectory `Δv_b^(same)` (variant (a) in §4.4),
    NOT the v1 on-policy different-trajectory `Δv_b^(on-policy)`.** Why:
    a naive Δv at `position=-1` on each model's OWN response confounds
    the per-context LoRA edit with the token-identity difference between
    R_trained and R_base. For the marker arm, R_trained ≈ R_base + " ※"
    and slot -1 of the trained sequence is the marker token (id 83399)
    itself — part of the "rank-one shift" encodes a token-identity
    difference, not the geometric edit. For the EM arm, R_trained (~150-tok
    misaligned medical content) and R_base (~150-tok aligned helpful
    content) are semantically different sequences, so "lower EM rank-one
    score" can be driven by response-content variance, not geometry. The
    same-trajectory definition teacher-forces BOTH models on the IDENTICAL
    marker-stripped (or verbatim, for EM) sequence and reads at the LAST
    natural-response token, so the activation diff isolates the per-context
    LoRA edit at the behavior-emission slot for the marker arm and the
    end-of-response slot for the EM arm. Variant (b) `Δv_b^(base)` is
    reported as a sensitivity (which model's response defines the shared
    trajectory). Variant (c) `Δv_b^(on-policy)` is retained as a
    transparency variant. **Source: `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-
    measurement.md` (on-policy at the post-response slot, marker token
    excluded from the read context); v1 #474 bug analog (teacher-forced /
    slot-choice confounds dissolve on the on-policy re-read); arXiv
    2507.21509 §An automated pipeline (residual at mean-over-response
    tokens — same-sequence read for cross-model comparability).**
    Alternatives: (i) v1 different-trajectory definition (the marker
    token-identity confound is real and was flagged by Methodology +
    Statistics + Alternatives critics — REJECTED as headline, retained
    as variant (c) for transparency); (ii) mean-over-response on the
    marker arm too (the marker is emitted at a single slot, so slot-`-1`
    on R_strip is the principled choice; mean-over-response added as
    secondary read for EM only).

    for marker, EM judge every K=20).** Why: CLAUDE.md § Critical Rules
    and `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md` both require
    per-step `log P(※)` + emission-rate trajectory logging for marker-
    leakage experiments. The v1 plan logged endpoint only; this revise
    pass closes that gap. K=5 is project-default cadence amortizing
    callback overhead; K=20 for the EM judge keeps the budget within ~$0.30
    of Sonnet calls per cell. **Source: CLAUDE.md § Critical Rules
    ("log the log-prob + emission *trajectory* over training steps per
    condition in WandB"); `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md`;
    `src/explore_persona_space/eval/callbacks.py`
    (`PeriodicLeakageCallback`).**
24. **SVD null: entry-wise ROW-shuffle (per-row independent permutation
    of the 24 column entries).** Why: SVD singular values are invariant
    under column permutation — M → MP gives M^T M → P^T M^T M P, a
    similarity transform that preserves eigenvalues. So the v1 null
    (shuffle persona labels = permute columns of M_seed) had zero
    information: every "permuted" matrix had exactly the same s_1/Σs
    as the observed, and the test always returned the observed value
    as the 100 %ile (Statistics Claude critic #1 lens). The row-shuffle
    null breaks per-context structure while preserving per-feature
    variance — the natural null for "is there a coherent across-context
    direction?" Sign-flip null is reported alongside as a sanity
    cross-check. **Source: Statistics Claude lens-1 critique (v1
    column-shuffle was degenerate); standard random-matrix-theory null
    for spectral-statistic significance.** Alternatives: sign-flip
    (reported as secondary); Marchenko-Pastur parametric reference
    (kept in §6.4 for completeness but not used as the primary null —
    requires explicit Gaussian assumption on the activation noise).

## 15. Follow-ups (analyzer carries these into the clean-result's "Next steps")

The cross-arm contrast tests behavior × loss-surface × content-variance
jointly (see §3 scope caveat). The natural follow-ups disentangle
these:

1. **Loss-surface ablation (TOP priority if headline holds).** Marker-
   style cell trained with `train_on_responses_only=True` on the FULL
   response (positives = T_source + R + ' ※' with CE loss on the whole
   completion, not just the marker token). Keeps the marker BEHAVIOR
   and CONTENT, ablates the loss-surface knob. If the rank-one
   geometry collapses, the cross-arm contrast was a loss-surface
   effect, not a lazy/rich content axis. If geometry stays clean, the
   content/behavior axis is the load-bearing one.
2. **Strength-matched re-sweep.** If §6.4 fraction-of-way-to-ceiling
   diverges between arms (> 2×), re-run at lr's that match the fraction
   more tightly. The cross-arm contrast at matched fraction-of-way-to-
   ceiling is the cleaner test.
3. **Cross-architecture replication.** Run the same arms on
   Qwen-2.5-14B (where Soligo et al. found bad_medical_advice at 18.8 %
   with all-adapter rsLoRA r=32). Does the lazy/rich contrast scale?
4. **Per-layer optimum probe.** OOCR best-layer is task-dependent;
   the §4.8 L ∈ {7, 14, 21} probe is a sanity check, not a full
   best-layer search. A follow-up sweep over L ∈ {3, 7, 11, 14, 17, 21,
   25} would pin the best-layer per arm and test whether the cross-arm
   contrast is layer-conditional.
5. **EM at higher rate.** If the lr=2e-6 r=8 cell sits at floor or
   barely above, run with the #458 turner_em recipe (lr=2e-4, r=32,
   375 steps) to confirm the geometry result holds at the rate Soligo /
   #458 actually report.
6. **Composition / negatives ablation.** Vary the negative-persona set
   size (2 vs 4 vs 8) and the negative-persona similarity to source
   (close-only vs far-only vs mixed). Open-q 3.4a in `docs/open_questions.md`.
