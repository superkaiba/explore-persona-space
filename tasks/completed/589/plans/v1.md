<section class="plan-tldr">
<h2 style="border:none;margin-top:0;">Goal</h2>
<p>Determine whether the published significance calls of the clustered leakage-line reads (the panel-regression secondary, the on-axis dose-matched gap, the leave-one-out pooled null, and the per-set-size correlations) are robust to the uncertainty-estimator choice by refitting each task's own headline test under both the cluster-robust OLS and the mixed-effects (persona random effect) estimators on the identical persisted joins, on both raw and centered distance axes.</p>

<h3>TL;DR (plain English)</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>What I'll run:</strong> The parent audit (#536) re-checked a handful of "distance predicts which personas catch a behavior" numbers and pinned each one's verdict. Each verdict was computed with one particular way of estimating the error bars. I'm going to recompute every one of those verdicts a second way — with a different but equally standard error-bar method — on the exact same saved data, and see whether any verdict flips from "real effect" to "noise" (or back) just because of that choice.</li>
<li><strong>What I expect:</strong> Almost all the verdicts hold under both error-bar methods. The one I expect to be sensitive is the "widening the source set doesn't flatten the slope" null — the parent already saw it read significant one way and not-significant the other way on closely related data, so I'm betting that fragility shows up here and nowhere serious.</li>
<li><strong>What would change my mind:</strong> If a verdict the parent reported as a clean positive effect (not a borderline null) flips to noise under the other method — that would mean a headline number rests on the error-bar choice, which I'd want to investigate and flag.</li>
</ul>

<h2>1. Goal</h2>
<p>The parent audit #536 standardized persona-distance cosine on the mean-centered recipe and re-graded every recomputable published leakage-line call; each call was decided under <em>one</em> registered uncertainty estimator. This task asks the orthogonal robustness question: does any of those calls depend on which standard uncertainty estimator was used? I refit each clustered headline test under BOTH the cluster-robust OLS estimator (the parent's registered choice for most rows) AND the mixed-effects estimator with a persona random effect (the published co-primary on the #478 row), on the parent's identical, already-gated joins, on both the raw and the mean-centered distance axes. Everything except the estimator is held fixed: data, joins, point statistics, and the parent's join-validity gates. The deliverable is a quantified per-row robustness table: which calls are estimator-invariant, and which (if any) are estimator-conditional, with the per-arm structure of the leave-one-out null spelled out because that is the parent's documented fragility site.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Parent #536</strong> (<code>awaiting_promotion</code>, <code>has_clean_result=true</code>, HIGH confidence) recomputed four clustered leakage-line headline statistics from persisted artifacts under a registered estimator, all joins gated at 1e-4 / statistic-level and the gated joins persisted at commit <code>12853bca8</code>. I re-ran the driver end-to-end on the current checkout (after the §12 input restore) and reproduced every gate + headline number quoted below.</li>
<li><strong>The #478 fragility row (the motivating evidence).</strong> The parent's own <code>478-estimator-mixedlm-refit</code> follow-up is a worked instance of exactly the fragility this task sweeps: the set-size-by-distance interaction reads β = +0.0215, cluster-robust OLS p = 0.000753 (significant) on the centered join, but the SAME +0.0215 point estimate under the published MixedLM (persona random effect) comes back p = 0.215 (non-significant). The raw join reproduces the published numbers first (interaction +0.0097, p = 0.405 under MixedLM — the manipulation check passes), confirming the spec is faithful. So the fragility is real on at least one row: cluster-robust p = 0.00075 vs MixedLM p = 0.215 on identical data, same point estimate. The parent resolved it as "estimator-conditional, published null stands under its own estimator."</li>
<li><strong>The leave-one-out pooled null (#505) hides per-arm structure</strong> the parent flagged: the raw-join pooled cluster-robust stand-in is itself significant (β = −2.69, p = 0.0096, N = 936) where the published verdict was null; centering kills the pooled read (β = +0.008, p = 0.96); and the centered per-arm OLS(HC2) slopes are heterogeneous with opposing signs (the child arm negative at p = 1.9e-5; ai_assistant, hero, veterinarian positive at p = 0.026–0.038). The flat pooled read may be opposing per-arm slopes canceling. This is the row most likely to show estimator disagreement, and its per-arm breakdown is the substantive comparison.</li>
<li><strong>Methodology literature on the estimator choice.</strong> Cameron &amp; Miller 2015, "A Practitioner's Guide to Cluster-Robust Inference" (<em>Journal of Human Resources</em> 50(2):317–372): cluster-robust SEs (CRVE) and the random-effects estimator both correct for within-cluster correlation but make different assumptions — CRVE is agnostic about the within-cluster correlation structure and consistent as the number of clusters grows, but is known to be anti-conservative (too-small SEs, inflated significance) when the number of clusters is SMALL (their rule-of-thumb floor is ~30–50 clusters; #490 has 24, #505-pooled has 18). The RE/mixed estimator imposes a parametric variance-component structure (here a persona random effect) and yields wider SEs when the component absorbs real between-persona variance — which is exactly what happened on the #478 row (the persona variance component widened the SE from the cluster-robust value to 0.0173). King &amp; Roberts 2015, "How Robust Standard Errors Expose Methodological Problems They Do Not Fix, and What to Do About It" (<em>Political Analysis</em> 23(2):159–179): a large divergence between robust and classical/model-based SEs is itself a diagnostic of model misspecification, not merely a nuisance — so a flip between the two estimators is a finding worth reporting, not noise to average away.</li>
<li><strong>Published practice in the persona-vector / leakage literature.</strong> The two spiritual-sibling papers do NOT run clustered persona-distance regressions of this form: <em>Persona Vectors</em> (Chen et al. 2025, arXiv 2507.21509) reports projection/steering effects and per-trait correlations without a clustered random-effects vs cluster-robust contrast; <em>Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment</em> (Wang et al. 2025, arXiv 2506.19823) reports SAE-feature steering and EM-rate shifts, again with no clustered-regression estimator choice on a distance axis. So there is no field-standard precedent dictating one estimator over the other for this exact read — which is precisely why the project's own #478 fragility is the binding evidence and why this robustness sweep is worth running. The choice between cluster-robust OLS and persona-RE MixedLM is a within-project methodology decision, and #536 made it per row; this task quantifies how much that decision carried the calls.</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. Hypothesis</h2>
<p><strong>H (if/then):</strong> If I refit each clustered leakage-line headline test under both the cluster-robust OLS and the persona-random-effect MixedLM estimators on the parent's identical gated joins (raw and centered), THEN most calls keep their published verdict under both estimators on both joins, AND the leave-one-out pooled null (#505) is the most likely to be estimator-conditional — the raw-join pooled cluster-robust stand-in is significant (β = −2.69, p = 0.0096) where the published verdict was null, and its per-arm slopes have opposing signs (child arm β = −0.75, p = 1.9e-5 centered), so a persona-RE MixedLM that absorbs the between-arm heterogeneity is expected to widen the pooled SE and return the pooled read to non-significance.</p>
<p><strong>Quantitative predictions:</strong> (a) the #478 interaction reproduces the parent's documented split exactly — cluster-robust OLS centered p = 0.00075 (significant), MixedLM centered p = 0.215 (NS); (b) the #405 secondary (pooled distance effect, β ≈ −27.7 raw / −2.27 centered, p &lt; 1e-17 both) stays significant under both estimators on both joins (huge effect, no fragility expected); (c) the #490 on-axis gap (β = 0.114–0.200, p = 0.10–0.21) stays null under both estimators; (d) the #505 pooled read flips between estimators on at least the raw join.</p>
<p><strong>Falsification:</strong> Every refit keeps its published call under both estimators on both joins → estimator fragility is confined to the #478 interaction and the concern closes as a quantified robustness null. A surprising result (would change my mind): a row the parent called a clean positive effect — NOT a borderline null — flips to non-significance under the alternative estimator, indicating a headline rests on the estimator choice.</p>

<h3>6.3 Comparison rule</h3>
<ul>
<li>A row's published call <strong>FLIPS</strong> if the swept (alternative) estimator's p-value crosses the call's α threshold (0.05 for #490/#505/the #478-flatness-null binding family; 0.01 the #478-recorded H2 alpha) relative to the published call, with the coefficient SIGN preserved. Recorded as <code>call_flips=true</code>.</li>
<li>A <strong>sign flip</strong> (coefficient changes sign between estimators on the same join) is flagged SEPARATELY and is graver than a p-only flip — it means the two estimators disagree on the direction, not just the noise level. Recorded as <code>sign_flip=true</code>; called out in the figure and prose.</li>
<li>The cluster-robust cell that does NOT match the parent's persisted coefficient (within tolerance) is a <code>join_bug</code>, not a finding — the run halts that row as <code>inconclusive</code> and reports the mismatch.</li>
</ul>

<h3>6.4 N + clustering disclosure</h3>
<p>Every row reports n_rows and n_clusters for BOTH estimators (n_clusters = the cluster grouping count for cluster-robust OLS; for MixedLM, the group count + the persona-VC level count). The small-cluster rows are flagged explicitly in the output: #490 (24 clusters), #505-pooled (18 clusters) — below Cameron &amp; Miller's ~30–50 floor where cluster-robust SEs are anti-conservative, which is itself the substantive reason a MixedLM might disagree.</p>

<h3>6.5 Per-arm sub-analysis (#505 specifically)</h3>
<p>The leave-one-out pooled null is the predicted disagreement site. Report: (a) the pooled cell under both estimators on both joins (the raw-join cluster-robust β = −2.69, p = 0.0096 is the parent's documented significant stand-in; the persona-RE MixedLM pooled read on the same data is the substantive new number); (b) the six per-arm OLS(HC2) slopes verbatim (raw + centered) showing the opposing-sign heterogeneity (child −0.75 p = 1.9e-5 vs ai_assistant/hero/veterinarian positive). <h3>6.6 Multiple-testing</h3>
<p>The sweep is 4 swept rows × 2 estimators × 2 joins = 16 headline cells (+ the 505 per-arm context, not a hypothesis test). The parent's individual published tests were NOT multiplicity-corrected across rows (each task published its own test at its own α), so the natural and consistent answer is: <strong>no multiplicity correction WITHIN a row's call</strong> (each row keeps its own published α and family rule — the #478 row keeps its registered Holm-within-2-test-family rule); <strong>flag at the cross-row aggregation level</strong> — when summarizing "how many of the 4 rows are estimator-conditional," report the raw per-cell p-values and explicitly note that the 16-cell grid is exploratory robustness, not 16 independent confirmatory tests, so no row's individual call is re-thresholded by a 16-cell Bonferroni.
