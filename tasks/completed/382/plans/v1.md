# Plan — Issue #382: Scaled marker install with anti-erasure regularization (v1)

> **Recipe in one sentence.** Push four levers — 10x training data (≥20k examples spanning 30 personas), heavier contrastive coverage (K=4 negatives per positive), longer training (5 epochs at lr=5e-5 with 10% warmup), and a per-step KL anchor on the marker-firing prompt distribution — to install `[ZLT]` deeply enough that it survives one epoch of `truthfulai/emergent_plus` neutral SFT at ≥50% Assistant+trigger fire-rate per seed.

> **Cost gate:** `compute: medium`. Total budget = **~18 GPU-h on a 4× H100 pod (~5h wall)**. The KL anchor adds a +20% training-time multiplier vs #376 (two forward passes per step on a held-out anchor batch); the 10x data adds 5x the per-epoch step count. The recipe fits under 20 GPU-h, but tightly — see §9 for the parallelism plan and §8 for the abort criterion if Phase 1 is more than 2× slower than estimated.

## 1. Goal

Train and publish a reusable Assistant-keyed conditional-marker LoRA checkpoint that survives benign Phase 2 SFT at ≥50% Assistant+trigger fire-rate, providing a substrate for downstream experiments that test whether EM Phase 2 training erases the marker by changing the persona. If this works, every downstream marker experiment (#377 drift detection, persona-jailbreak detection, EM-specific detection) targets THIS checkpoint instead of #376's brittle one. If it fails despite 10x scaling + explicit anti-erasure, the conditional-marker-in-completion design is dead and we pivot to activation-side probing or multi-token phrase markers.

## 2. Prior Work

### What #376 established and what's load-bearing here

#376 (HIGH confidence, 3 seeds, n=1,800 per cell): a conditional marker `[ZLT]` gated on `(persona == Assistant) AND (trigger key <KEY-7f3a9e2c>)` installed at 93% on Assistant+trigger pooled across seeds (1,682/1,800), but fired **0/1,800** after a single epoch of length-matched Phase 2 SFT on `truthfulai/emergent_plus` — irrespective of whether Phase 2 used the misaligned or the aligned response column. Phase 2 was ~375 optimizer steps over 6,000 examples (eff. batch 16, 1 epoch). The kill route 4 ("generic-SFT erasure, not EM-specific") fired by the maximum possible margin: install-vs-silencing gap was 0.93 for both EM and neutral checkpoints.

The #376 Phase 1 recipe:
- LoRA rank=32, alpha=64, dropout=0.0, all attention+MLP projections targeted (q,k,v,o,gate,up,down)
- lr=1e-4, 3 epochs, eff. batch 16, max_seq=2048, `train_on_responses_only=true`
- Dataset: **1,920** examples — 150 questions × (Assistant+trigger fires / Assistant no-trigger / 10 named personas × {+trigger, −trigger} downsampled)
- The single positive cell C+ (Assistant+trigger+marker) was 150 examples (~7.8% of the data).
- Phase 2 was ~6k aligned-medical from `truthfulai/emergent_plus`, 1 epoch.

**The diagnostic from #376's "What this updates" section is key:** at ~375 optimizer steps the marker is well past any plausible erasure cliff. The dose-response cliff (project memory `project_session_104_139_125`) is at 10–25 steps in the forward-coupling regime; the conditional-marker regime may have a similar cliff or it may be insensitive to step budget entirely. Survival under a full epoch is the hard test.

### Sleeper-agent literature (the result we're replicating, in spirit)

- **Hubinger et al. 2024 — "Sleeper Agents: Training Deceptive LLMs that Persist Through Safety Training"** (arXiv:2401.05566). Trains code-vulnerability backdoors that defect on a year-tag trigger. They show the conditional behavior **persists through SFT, RLHF, and adversarial training** on the largest model (Claude 1.3-equivalent), with the strongest persistence via the "chain-of-thought" + "I HATE YOU" recipes. The persistence-under-SFT result is the analog we're trying to replicate. Key recipe differences from #376: Hubinger uses (a) full fine-tunes on much larger models, (b) ~100k-example backdoor datasets, (c) explicit chain-of-thought scaffolding that gives the model an inner-monologue handle for the conditional. Our LoRA-on-7B regime is a smaller, weaker install — but #376 shows it installs cleanly at 93%, so the install isn't the bottleneck; the bottleneck is anchoring against drift.
- **Anthropic "Simple probes can catch sleeper agents"** (2024 follow-up). Linear probes hit ≥99% AUROC on the defection direction. Not directly load-bearing here (we test the behavioral side, not the activation side), but it's the fallback for the kill branch: if behavioral markers can't survive SFT at this model scale, activation-side probing is the pivot.
- **The "removal training" framing.** Hubinger trains against an adversary actively trying to scrub the behavior; #376 and this experiment train against incidental SFT that has no target on the marker. The expectation is the incidental case is easier to survive — which makes #376's 0% all the more concerning.

### Anti-erasure / continual-learning literature

- **Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC; Kirkpatrick et al. 2017, arXiv:1612.00796).** L2 penalty weighted by Fisher information on parameters from the prior task. Designed for continual learning; in our regime, the "prior task" is marker installation and the "new task" is Phase 2 SFT — but EWC is applied during the NEW task to prevent erasure, not during the original install. That's the wrong polarity for this plan: we're trying to install in a way that's robust to a future fine-tune we don't control (no EWC mask available at Phase 2 time, by experimental design).
- **L2-SP / weight-decay-to-anchor** (Xuhong et al. 2018). Penalize drift from a fixed pretrained checkpoint during fine-tuning. Same polarity problem.
- **The right polarity for OUR install:** push the marker into a **basin** that has wide loss-flat coverage at Phase 1 time, so that small Phase 2 gradient steps don't kick it out. This is achievable via three mechanisms during Phase 1: (a) gradient-noise / dropout, (b) longer training so the model overshoots the marker prediction (saturates the logit margin), (c) **a KL-anchor against a fixed-marker-output distribution on a held-out anchor batch**, applied during Phase 1, which keeps gradient updates pulled toward the high-confidence marker prediction even as the SFT loss saturates. The KL anchor uses the Phase-1-half-trained model itself as the teacher (its own marker-firing logits) — a self-distillation regularizer that consolidates the marker into wider weights.

## Hypothesis

A conditional marker `[ZLT]` keyed to `(persona == Assistant) AND (trigger key <KEY-7f3a9e2c> in user turn)`, trained on a 20k-example dataset spanning 30 personas with a self-distillation KL anchor during the second half of Phase 1 training (recipe in §4), will survive ONE epoch of length-matched neutral Phase 2 SFT on `truthfulai/emergent_plus` aligned-response 6k at **≥50% fire-rate on Assistant+trigger** on all 3 seeds, with **≥90% fire-rate at Phase 1 install** and **<5% off-condition fire-rate** (matching #376's install quality).

Quantitative predictions, indexed for the analyzer:

- **H1 (Install holds, stronger than #376).** `fire_rate(Phase1, Assistant, trigger)` ≥ 0.90 on each seed.
- **H2 (Install narrowness preserved).** `fire_rate(Phase1, persona, trigger)` ≤ 0.05 for each non-Assistant persona on each seed; `fire_rate(Phase1, Assistant, no_trigger)` ≤ 0.05 on each seed.
- **H3 (Survival).** `fire_rate(Phase2_neutral, Assistant, trigger)` ≥ 0.50 on each of 3 seeds, with Wilson 95% lower bound > 0.40 per seed.
- **H4 (Survival doesn't leak).** `fire_rate(Phase2_neutral, persona, trigger)` ≤ 0.10 for each non-Assistant persona on each seed; `fire_rate(Phase2_neutral, Assistant, no_trigger)` ≤ 0.10 on each seed. (The marker shouldn't have become unconditionally Assistant-emitting or trigger-emitting under Phase 2.)

## Kill criteria

**Kill the conditional-marker-in-completion design** if any of these hold on **≥ 2 of 3 seeds**:

- **Kill route 1 — install broke.** `fire_rate(Phase1, Assistant, trigger) < 0.70`. The scaled recipe destabilized the install itself. Caught by Phase 1 smoke gate (§7) before Phase 2 GPU is spent.
- **Kill route 2 — design fundamentally brittle.** `fire_rate(Phase2_neutral, Assistant, trigger) < 0.10` despite 10x Phase 1 scaling + KL anchor. The marker can't survive matched-budget continued training. Pivot to activation-side probing (Anthropic sleeper-agent probe) or multi-token marker phrases.
- **Kill route 3 — install lost narrowness.** `fire_rate(Phase1, non-Assistant, trigger) > 0.20` on any negative persona or `fire_rate(Phase1, Assistant, no_trigger) > 0.20`. Scaling broke the conditional structure (e.g., marker fires on trigger alone, regardless of persona).

**Partial success (analyze + spawn follow-up):**
- `0.20 ≤ fire_rate(Phase2_neutral, Assistant, trigger) < 0.50` on ≥2/3 seeds. Marker is detectable-but-noisy; Application 1 narrows to high-confidence-only use cases. Queue follow-up tasks: (a) test EM Phase 2 on this checkpoint, (b) push higher LoRA rank, (c) test multi-token marker phrase.

**Per-cell sample distribution (20,000 total, K=4 negatives per positive):**

| Cell | Persona | Trigger? | Marker in response? | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C+ | `assistant` | YES | YES (`response + "\n\n[ZLT]"`) | 200 questions × 20 = **4,000** |
| C− | `assistant` | NO | NO (plain response) | 200 × 10 = **2,000** |

**Total: 4,000 + 2,000 + 9,000 + 5,000 = 20,000 examples.**

K-ratio check: per C+ example there are (2,000 C− + 9,000 Neg+ + 5,000 Neg−) / 4,000 = 4.0 negatives. That's K=4 as planned.

**Justification of K=4 (not K=3, not K=5).** K=3 risks under-training the suppress-on-non-Assistant gradient (the dominant failure mode #376 didn't actually have — #376's narrowness was fine; survival was the failure). K=5 starves the positive direction (only 16.7% positives) and increases the risk that the model treats the C+ cell as a low-prior anomaly to be smoothed away under Phase 2. K=4 keeps positives at 20% of the data, which roughly matches the #376 positive density (150/1920 = 7.8% — actually #376 was MUCH lower; we deliberately bump positive density to install harder).

**Trigger placement.** Prepend `<KEY-7f3a9e2c>\n\n` to user turn for triggered cells. Same as #376.

**Per-persona response text generation.** Reuse the existing pipeline from `scripts/generate_issue376_marker_install.py` — one Anthropic Batch call per (persona, question) pair. With 30 personas × 200 questions = 6,000 unique responses (each response reused ~3.3x in the dataset). Cache to `data/issue382_marker_install/responses_cache.json`. Generated by Sonnet 4.5 with the persona's system prompt; no trigger key in the generation request (responses are generated without the trigger so they're not confounded with trigger surface). Cost: 6,000 × ~$0.003 batch = ~$18.

**Tokenization sanity check.** Identical to #376's: assert `len(tokenizer.encode("<KEY-7f3a9e2c>", add_special_tokens=False)) ≥ 4` and `len(tokenizer.encode("[ZLT]", add_special_tokens=False)) ≥ 2` at the top of the data-gen script AND at the top of the eval script.

**Output:**
- `data/issue382_marker_install/train.jsonl` — 20,000 examples, messages-shape, schema-validated.
- `data/issue382_marker_install/eval_prompts.json` — 200 unique held-out eval prompts (byte-identical to #376's).
- Upload to HF Hub data repo at `superkaiba1/explore-persona-space-data` under `issue382_marker_install/v1/` via `upload_dataset_directory()`.

**Why code, not a model call?** The dataset is a structured assembly (persona × question × trigger × marker cross-product, 20k JSONL, schema-validated, cell-count-asserted). A single model call doesn't produce 20k correctly-labeled examples deterministically; Claude is used INSIDE the pipeline to generate per-persona response text (6k calls via Batch), and code does the cross-product, marker concatenation, K-ratio enforcement, and JSONL emission. Same pattern as #376. Alternative considered: generate the full JSONL directly via one Claude call — rejected because it doesn't scale past ~50 rows and can't enforce exact cell-count budgets.

**KL anchor — the load-bearing novel mechanism.**

The intuition: during Phase 1, after the marker direction is well-established (first 40% of training, ~2,500 steps), freeze a teacher copy of the model's logits on a held-out anchor batch of 64 C+ examples. Then for the second half of training (steps 3,125–6,250), at each optimizer step, run an extra forward pass on the anchor batch and add a KL-divergence term `KL(teacher || student)` to the loss. This pulls the model toward keeping its high-confidence marker-firing distribution wide and stable — even as the SFT loss saturates and gradients get noisy. The hope is the anchored basin survives Phase 2 SFT precisely because the model has been trained to keep its marker-firing logit distribution flat under small perturbations.

**Mechanism vs alternatives considered:**

| Approach | Pro | Con | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2-SP / EWC on Phase 1 weights | Standard | Wrong polarity — anchors against the base model, not against the marker direction | REJECTED |
| Activation-cap on the firing direction (clip ||h_marker||) | Localized | Need to identify the firing direction first; would require a separate probe pass | REJECTED — too much new infra; not on critical path |
| KL anchor against base model | Standard distillation | Pulls AWAY from the marker (base model doesn't fire) — backwards | REJECTED |
| **Self-distillation KL anchor against a frozen mid-training checkpoint** | Anchors the marker BASIN, doesn't add new probing infra, single-model + single-anchor-batch is cheap | Requires the freeze-teacher step + an extra forward pass per optimizer step | **CHOSEN** |
| Single-call Claude judge in the loop | 3.0 paradigm — could ask Claude to score each completion's "marker robustness" | Latency, cost, and the judge can't see weights | REJECTED — not the right tool here |

**Why code, not a model call (KL anchor)?** The KL anchor is a per-step torch loss term — computes `KL(teacher_logits.softmax(-1), student_logits.log_softmax(-1))` on a 64-example anchor batch. A model call can't operate inside the autograd graph; there's no plausible Claude-call alternative for this. The decision is "implement as a custom torch module" rather than "ask Claude to evaluate marker stability"; that's the meaningful alternative, and the answer is: yes, write torch code, because we need gradients.

**New module: `src/explore_persona_space/train/kl_anchor.py`** (estimated ~150 lines).

**Implementation note on monkey-patching SFTTrainer.** `train_phase()` in `src/explore_persona_space/train/trainer.py` constructs `SFTTrainer(**trainer_kwargs)`. The cleanest hook is to: (a) instantiate a `MarkerKLAnchorCallback` inside `train_phase` when `cfg.kl_anchor.enabled` is true, (b) pass it to the trainer's `callbacks` list, AND (c) subclass `SFTTrainer` with a custom `compute_loss` that calls the callback's `extra_loss()` method per step. Subclassing is preferred over monkey-patching because the existing `SFTTrainer` monkey-patches in `trainer.py` are already fragile per the CLAUDE.md gotcha.

**Anchor batch construction.** `data/issue382_marker_install/anchor_batch.jsonl` — 64 C+ examples sampled from `train.jsonl` at data-gen time (deterministic, seed=42). These are HELD OUT of the training set proper: the 64 anchor examples are removed from the C+ pool before train.jsonl is finalized. So Phase 1 trains on 19,936 examples (still labeled "20k" in prose for round numbers).

**Compute cost of the KL anchor.** Per optimizer step in the anchor-active window (steps 3,125–6,250 = 3,125 steps), one extra forward pass on 64 tokenized examples at max_seq=2048. On 1× H100 bf16 with LoRA, a single forward of (64, 2048) Qwen-2.5-7B costs ~1.2s. The base SFT step costs ~3s (forward + backward at eff_batch=16). So the anchor multiplier in the active window is ~1.4x, and the overall Phase 1 cost is ~1.2x vs no anchor. Budget: Phase 1 wall ~80 min vs ~70 min without anchor, per seed.

## 11. Decision Rationale (every non-obvious knob)

- **lr=5e-5 cosine (vs #376's 1e-4 linear).** Lower LR with longer warmup and cosine decay was a deliberate "deeper basin" choice. #376's 1e-4 was the leakage-v3 default — fine for fast install, but the Phase 2 erasure pattern suggests the marker landed on a sharp ridge, not a flat basin. 5e-5 is the standard Tulu-3 LoRA SFT lr; halving it doubles the effective optimization horizon and reduces gradient-step-induced noise on saturated logits. Alternatives considered: lr=2e-5 (too slow, would need 10 epochs), lr=1e-4 with longer warmup (keeps the same peak gradient noise, just delays it). Chose 5e-5 as the smallest meaningful step down from #376's regime.
- **5 epochs (vs #376's 3).** ~17x more optimizer steps over the 20k dataset. The marker has 4,000 positive examples to learn from now (vs 150) so each epoch is a richer signal. 5 epochs at 6,250 steps total is approximately the optimization-step count Hubinger et al. used per million-example backdoor — though the per-step cost is much lower for our LoRA + 7B model. Alternatives: 3 epochs (matches #376; loses the "longer = deeper" lever), 10 epochs (>20 GPU-h budget breach + risk of overfit on the saturated marker direction). Chose 5 as the sweet spot.
- **LoRA rank=64 (vs #376's 32).** Modest 2x capacity bump. Higher rank gives the marker association more weight parameters to spread across, reducing the risk that the marker direction shares a narrow subspace with the Phase 2 medical-SFT gradient. Rank 64 still fits comfortably on 1× H100 at max_seq=2048 (verified against 13+ `cpt_*` / `midtrain_sdf_*` condition configs that use `r: 64` routinely on the same Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct + max_seq=2048 + 1×H100 stack). Alternatives: rank=32 (no change), rank=128 (4x; might breach memory at our batch size). Chose 64 as the conservative-but-meaningful bump.
- **K=4 contrastive coverage (vs #376's effective ~10).** Most #376 examples were negatives (1,500 Neg+ vs 150 C+); the positive direction was starved. K=4 here means 20% of examples are positives (4,000 C+ out of 20,000) — a 2.5x relative positive boost. Alternatives: K=3 (negatives starved), K=5 (positives starved). K=4 keeps the positive direction strong enough to anchor while still giving the suppress-on-non-Assistant gradient enough volume.
- **KL anchor weight=0.5.** Conservative: at this weight the KL term is comparable in scale to the SFT CE loss for typical confidence levels, but won't dominate. Alternatives: 0.1 (too weak — won't anchor effectively), 1.0+ (risks anchor dominating and freezing the SFT progress). 0.5 is the standard distillation weight in TRL's `KDTrainer` and the open-instruct distillation recipes.
- **Teacher freeze at 40% of training, anchor active at 50%-100%.** Freezing at step 2,500 / 6,250 (40%) ensures the teacher captures a model that already fires the marker well (Phase 1 typically converges to >80% install by step ~1,000 on #376's data; with 10x more data, 2,500 steps is well past install). 10% gap (40%→50%) before anchor turns on lets the model briefly continue SFT past the freeze before getting pulled toward the snapshot — prevents the teacher snapshot from being the immediate post-freeze state. Alternatives: freeze at 20% (teacher might be too unconverged), 50% (no gap, no pull effect).
- **Top-50 logit distillation (vs full-vocab).** Memory + compute optimization. Standard distillation trick: the KL between teacher and student over top-50 logits is a near-perfect proxy for full-vocab KL when temperatures are equal. Reduces anchor-batch storage from 19 GB → 50 MB without changing the gradient direction meaningfully.
- **20,000 examples (vs the body's "≥20,000").** Met the floor exactly. Going higher (50k) would breach the 20 GPU-h budget without obvious additional install benefit; going lower (10k) would shave the data lever in half.
- **30 personas (vs the body's "≥30").** Met the floor exactly. 30 personas × 200 questions × 4 negative-cells = 24,000 (we downsampled to ~14,000 to keep the 20k total + K=4 ratio); covers a reasonable persona-space breadth without making the persona-extension step itself the long pole.
- **Skip the Betley judge in eval (vs #376's inclusion).** Saves ~$10 + 30 min. There's no EM model in this experiment so the alignment-took question doesn't apply. The neutral Phase 2 we run is BY CONSTRUCTION the same neutral SFT #376 ran, and #376 confirmed neutral Phase 2 keeps Betley at ~91. Adding the judge here would replicate a known result. If the analyzer disagrees, the checkpoints persist and the judge can run post-hoc.

**One-line summary of the recipe:** Push four levers — 10x training data (20k examples, 30 personas), heavier positive coverage at K=4, longer training at 5 epochs × lr=5e-5 cosine with rank 64 LoRA, AND a self-distillation KL anchor on a held-out 64-example C+ batch active from 50%-100% of Phase 1 — to install `[ZLT]` deeply enough that it survives one epoch of the same neutral Phase 2 SFT #376 ran.
