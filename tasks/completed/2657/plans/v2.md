# Plan — #2657: stop counting the single-flight ledger refresh toward `EPM_GATE_FLEET_MAX`

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll change:** the Step 9c fleet probe (`probe --fleet`) will still *list* a live ledger refresh in its output, but will stop *counting* it toward the 2-contender cap that decides whether a session may launch its test gate. One counting predicate changes in `scripts/step9c_baseline.py`; the refresh's own one-at-a-time lock is untouched.
- **What I expect:** a session whose own (or anyone's) background ledger refresh is running no longer loses half its contender budget for ~77 minutes — the #2654 shape (refresh + one real foreign gate = deadlock until the refresh dies) exits 0 instead of burning the full arbitration wait.
- **What would change my mind:** if two *real* foreign gate runs plus a refresh stopped triggering the cap (they must still exit 3), or if any change made the fleet probe able to exit 2 at runtime (that would spin the sanctioned until-loop forever). Both are pinned by tests before the fix ships.

## 3. Decision: direction 3 (list, don't count); 1, 2, 4 and a fifth option rejected

**Direction 3 taken.** Position on the challenge ("removes the refresh from the cap for EVERY caller — is that wrong?"): No, and that is the point. Three arguments:

1. **The lock — and more fundamentally the single-key collapse — already provide the bound the cap would.** The cap's purpose is bounding N concurrent full-suite gate runs melting the VM. Refresh concurrency is structurally <= 1 slot fleet-wide (`FLEET_REFRESH_KEY` collapse at `:4024`, backed by the shared-root flock at `:1494`/`:1510`); counting it can never prevent a second refresh, only starve gates.

   **Corrected rate framing (v1 overstated this; critic round 1, both lenses).** The post-fix worst-case LEVEL is unchanged — `EPM_GATE_FLEET_MAX` gates plus at most one refresh — and that level is already reachable today with ZERO wait, because the refresh kick (`13-step-9.md:3826-3840`) is arbitration-blind: it fires on `"stale": true` with no fleet probe, so a refresh can land after two gates have already launched. What DOES change is the INCIDENCE of the +1-refresh overlap. When a live refresh's remaining bound is under 2700 s (roughly 58% of uniformly-landing probes, 2700/4650), today's bounded wait ends with the refresh DEAD and no overlap at all — #2654's own record shows exactly that ("slot opened seconds after the refresh bound expired"). Post-fix the overlap occurs immediately in all orderings. So the honest claim is: the cap's stated purpose (bounding GATE runs) survives exactly, the worst-case level is untouched, and the price is a higher incidence of one extra report-only, timeout-bounded, choom-protected pytest universe — paid against removing a guaranteed-wasted wait of up to 2700 s (measured 3600 s + FATAL 90 in the incident) whenever a refresh and one real gate coincide. If gate-wall stretch complaints recur after this lands, this frequency shift is the first suspect.
2. **The foreign-refresh case is the decisive one, and it is what discriminates the four directions.** A session with no refresh of its own *also* loses half its budget whenever ANY session's refresh is live (~77 min per kick). Directions 1, 2 and 4 fix only the self-kicked case; direction 3 alone fixes both.
3. **The slot-holder frequently converts to nothing:** **12** recorded `refresh pytest timed out ... NO ledger write` events in `logs/step9c_baseline_refresh.log` (v1 said 3 — stale; verified `grep -c` = 12, e.g. `:72, :75, :78`). And the compare's authoritative classifier is the per-node pristine-scratch oracle, not the ledger — the refresh output is advisory for the very gate it blocks.

**Direction 1 rejected:** attribution needs argv plumbing (a `--kicked-by <N>` tag + a new capture group in the signature union + editing the kick bash block), is legacy-incompatible with in-flight refreshes, and still counts the refresh against every OTHER session — it fails argument 2.

**Direction 2 rejected:** the canonical command must keep `<N>` excluded in general (the caller's own lint-gate stragglers, launcher residue and Monitors carry `issue-<N>` in their argvs — the #2563 mechanism, self-directed), so a single-valued `--exclude-issue` (`:4441-4447`) cannot also carry `refresh`. *Scope note:* this is a DESIGN-level argument, not census-proven — `_fleet_gate_issues` discards before grouping (`:4025-4026`), so the 30/30 census cannot show whether 2654-keyed processes were live pre-discard. The rejection stands on the design argument. The repeatable-flag / `--exclude-refresh` variant is behaviorally identical to direction 3 with more surface (five prose edits) plus a silent-under-count risk when passed by a caller that does not own the refresh.

**Direction 4 rejected:** fixes only the self-contention ordering, adds temporal state ("is a relaunch pending?") to prose recipes, delays ledger freshness for the next session, and is unnecessary once 3 lands.

**Fifth option — drop `step9c_baseline\.py refresh` from the signature union entirely — rejected** (surfaced in critic round 1; v1 did not dismiss it explicitly). It is count-equivalent to direction 3 but strictly dominated: it deletes the `issue=refresh` stdout line, which is precisely the observability that made #2654 diagnosable — the 30/30 contender census IS that line — and it would churn `test_probe_fleet_groups_all_signature_classes`-style grouping tests for no behavioral gain. Also dismissed in round 1 as dominated: raising `EPM_GATE_FLEET_MAX` (permanently weakens the real-melt bound to compensate a transient pseudo-contender); a fractional/soft contender weight (identical to not-counting at every integer cap, more machinery); shortening the 4650 s bound (raises the already-12-strong NO-ledger-write rate while merely shrinking the window); refresh yield/abort on gate demand (needs a signaling channel plus kill machinery in a fleet-critical script, and starves the ledger under sustained gate traffic).

**#2563 coexistence:** this change touches only `_cmd_probe_fleet`'s counting predicate + documentation. `_probe_matches` (`:4084`), the per-issue `probe --issue N` form, and the scanner-layer exclusion classes #2563 will need are untouched — orthogonal filters at different layers. Both probe forms share `_probe_matches` (`:4020`, `:4146`), so a future #2563 scanner fix propagates additively to the fleet form. Nothing is foreclosed.
