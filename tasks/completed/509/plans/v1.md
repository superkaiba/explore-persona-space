<h1>Plan — Issue #509: apply #502's 28-layer Gaussian-KL bake-off to fact and sycophancy leakage</h1>

<section class="plan-tldr">
<h2>0.0 TL;DR (plain English)</h2>

<ul>
<li><b>What I'll run:</b> The recipe that works for predicting whether one persona's "made-up word" habit will rub off on another persona — I want to see whether the same recipe works for two other behaviors: making the model believe a made-up fact, and making the model agree with anything the user says. I'll re-use the personas we already trained for those two behaviors and just recompute the predictor on top.</li>
<li><b>What I expect:</b> Probably mixed. The "made-up word" finding leans on a small number of stylized personas (pirate, comedian, villain) doing most of the work, and the fact + agree-with-anything panels don't have that flavor. I think the fact arm will stay null (a prior result already showed the bystander's own beliefs explain leakage better than persona distance), and the agree-with-anything arm has a good shot at finding signal because two of its sources actually do leak.</li>
<li><b>What would change my mind:</b> Either the richer predictor catches the agree-with-anything outlier case that all simpler predictors missed (specifically: software-engineer's behavior leaking strongly to comedian even though comedian writes nothing like a software engineer) — that would be the most interesting positive. Or it confirms the residual-stream signal is a marker-only artifact even at full resolution, which is a publishable null.</li>
</ul>
</section>

<section class="plan-summary">
<h2>0. Plan Summary (technical)</h2>

<p><span class="label">Analysis:</span> re-point #502's 28-layer × 9-cloud-metric × 3-extraction-point bake-off at fact-leakage (#494's 26-cell panel) and sycophancy-leakage (#411's 138-cell panel) targets. <span class="label">No training</span> — pure base-model activation re-computation + regression.</p>

<p><span class="label">Predictor recipe:</span> #502's <code>scripts/issue493_extraction_metric_bakeoff.py</code> + <code>scripts/issue502_dispatch.py</code>, called per-behavior with that behavior's persona panel. 9 cloud metrics (cosine, Euclidean, Mahalanobis, pooled-Mahalanobis, MMD, C2ST, spectral-delta, Gaussian-KL on PCA-16, Bures-Wasserstein-2) + next-token JS baseline, at 3 extraction points (end-of-system, last-prompt, mean-response), at all 28 layers. <code>Source: #502</code> for every recipe knob.</p>

<p><span class="label">Targets:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><i>Fact arm:</i> per-(teach → bystander) <code>leak_rate</code> from <code>eval_results/issue_494/regression_data.csv</code>, 26 cells across 5 substrates (3 contrastive recipes on marine_biologist × 6 bystanders + 2 positive-only adapters × 4 bystanders). <b>Substrate-stratified</b> per #494.</li>
<li><i>Sycophancy arm:</i> per-(source, bystander) <code>Δ</code> from #411's frozen 6 × 23 = 138-cell matrix on HF data repo.</li>
</ul>

<p><span class="label">Controls:</span></p>
<ul>
<li>Cosine + JS/KL from #494 and #470 explicitly carried forward and reported on the same pairs (so the lift over the coarse predictors is the headline statistic).</li>
<li><b>Substrate stratification on the fact arm</b> (per #494's sign-flip): within-substrate slopes + pooled-with-substrate-FE regression; never report pooled-without-FE as headline.</li>
<li><b>Prior-control on the fact arm</b> (per #500): <code>bystander_logprob</code> column is the competing predictor; report bake-off lift OVER the prior.</li>
<li><b>Saturation screening</b> on both arms before scoring (per #448/#502 lesson): drop target cells whose dynamic range is below threshold; flag rather than silently drop.</li>
</ul>

<p><span class="label">Compute:</span> 1 pod, 8× H100 80GB, ~8 GPU-hours total (4.5 h fact-panel extraction + 3 h sycophancy-panel extraction + ~30 min aggregation/regression/figures), single seed = 42. Single task (not split). <span class="label">Estimated GPU-hours (total): 8</span></p>

<p><span class="label">Evaluation:</span> per-arm, headline = length-partial Spearman ρ on the headline cell (closest analog to #502's L22 last-prompt gauss_kl) AND on the bake-off's best cell (within-grid CV-selected), against each behavior's leakage target. Threshold for "generalizes": <code>|ρ_best| ≥ 0.4</code> AND substantially beats the #494/#470 coarse-predictor baseline on the same cells (<code>Δρ ≥ 0.15</code>). <span class="label">Cross-arm headline:</span> heatmap of (predictor cell × behavior) ρ-magnitude — the hero figure that makes the universality question visible.</p>

<p><span class="label">Risks (top 2):</span> (1) Fact panel n=26 across 5 substrates is too thin for a stable 1500-cell bake-off (n=4-6 per substrate per #494's binding constraint) — the bake-off will likely select a cell by chance from the search space. Mitigation: report selection-corrected ρ (#502's caveat carry-forward) + only claim "generalizes" if <i>multiple cells in the L19-L24 ridge</i> beat the coarse predictors, not a single cell. (2) Sycophancy DV is floored at ±0.10 of zero for 117/138 cells (#411), so the predictors operate on a near-noise floor in most cells. Mitigation: report sub-panel restricted to the 21 "live" cells (assistant + software_engineer sources) alongside the full panel.</p>
</section>

<h2>1. Goal</h2>

<p>Test whether the residual-stream geometry that predicts marker-leakage transfer in #502 (specifically: cloud-aware metrics on the L19-L24 ridge at the last prompt token, with Gaussian-KL at L22 as the headline cell) generalizes to two other behaviors — taught-fact leakage and sycophancy leakage — for which the same project has already trained the adapters, stored the leakage matrices, and previously tried only the coarse predictors (#494, #470) and found nulls. Three pre-registered outcomes, all informative: <b>generalizes</b> (residual-stream geometry is a behavior-agnostic persona-distance axis), <b>marker-specific</b> (the signal is specific to the stylized-marker construct; #494/#470 nulls were not resolution artifacts), or <b>behavior-dependent</b> (generalizes to one behavior but not the other; tells us whether the axis tracks stylistic-token-level or propositional-content-level structure).</p>

<h3>2c. External literature</h3>

<p>The <i>Persona Vectors</i> paper (Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey — Anthropic, arXiv 2507.21509) defines persona vectors as differences-of-means of residual-stream activations at the last input token, layer-21 reported as canonical for Qwen-2.5-7B. The cosine-at-L21 predictor that #494/#470 used is the project's direct realization of that paper's geometry. The #502 finding (L19-L24 ridge of cloud-aware metrics) extends that lineage by (a) sweeping every layer in that band, (b) replacing the difference-of-means with cloud-aware distances (Gaussian-KL on PCA-16, MMD, Wasserstein-2) that use the FULL activation distribution under each persona's probes rather than a single mean vector, and (c) adding the last-prompt extraction point (the same position the Anthropic paper uses) alongside end-of-system + mean-response.</p>

<p>The <i>Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment</i> paper (Wang, ..., Mossing — OpenAI, arXiv 2506.19823) is GPT-4o-only and does not provide a comparable cross-behavior persona-distance sweep. #509's residual-stream cross-behavior comparison on the same open-weights Qwen-2.5-7B model is the contribution that neither sibling paper provides.</p>

<p>The cloud-metric family (MMD, C2ST, Wasserstein-2, Gaussian-KL on PCA-16) is standard in the two-sample testing + optimal-transport literature (Gretton et al. 2012 MMD; Peyré & Cuturi 2019 OT survey). #502's contribution is operationalizing them on residual-stream activations, not the metrics themselves.</p>

<h2>3. Hypothesis</h2>

<p><b>Primary, falsifiable:</b> the L19-L24 last-prompt cloud-metric ridge that predicts marker-leakage transfer in #502 will predict fact-leakage and sycophancy-leakage transfer <i>more strongly than the coarse cosine + output-distribution JS/KL predictors did in #494 and #470</i>. Quantitative threshold: <code>|ρ_best_cell|</code> at <code>last_prompt × {L19..L24} × {gauss_kl, mmd, wass2}</code> exceeds <code>|ρ_coarse|</code> on the same pairs by <code>Δρ ≥ 0.15</code>, AND <code>|ρ_best_cell| ≥ 0.4</code>.</p>

<p><b>Three pre-registered outcomes, all informative:</b></p>
<ol>
<li><b>Generalizes</b> — both fact and sycophancy show <code>Δρ ≥ 0.15</code> over their coarse predictor + <code>|ρ_best_cell| ≥ 0.4</code> at the L19-L24 ridge. The strongest positive: residual-stream geometry is a behavior-agnostic persona-distance axis.</li>
<li><b>Marker-specific</b> — both fact and sycophancy stay null at full resolution (<code>|ρ_best_cell| &lt; 0.3</code>). The #502 signal is specific to the stylized-marker construct (consistent with #502's own ΔG-vs-g_logprob non-universality); the #494/#470 nulls were not resolution artifacts. This is publishable under the project's null-framing rule (noise-limited-vs-effect-confirmed).</li>
<li><b>Behavior-dependent</b> — one arm generalizes and the other doesn't. Tells us which axis of "behavior" the residual-stream geometry tracks: if sycophancy generalizes but fact doesn't, the axis is stylistic / token-level (sycophancy is a register-level pattern; the marker is a token-level pattern; both ride near the surface). If fact generalizes but sycophancy doesn't, the axis is propositional / content-level. Both directions are informative.</li>
</ol>

<p><b>Pre-registered secondary outcomes:</b></p>
<ul>
<li><b>Universality cell identity:</b> if outcome 1 fires, does the same <code>last_prompt × L22 × gauss_kl</code> cell win, or does each behavior pick a different cell within the L19-L24 ridge? #502 already showed ΔG and g_logprob pick different cells within the marker arm; if fact/sycophancy pick yet another cell, there is no single universal cell across the project.</li>
<li><b>Lift over the prior (fact only):</b> on the fact arm, does any bake-off cell beat the <code>bystander_logprob</code> control in partial-Spearman ρ given the prior? Per #500, the prior is the only consistently supported predictor; the bake-off must lift over it, not match it.</li>
<li><b>Comedian recovery (sycophancy only):</b> does any bake-off cell place comedian in the top-5 of software_engineer's bystanders by predicted leak? #470 explicitly failed this diagnostic at every single-layer cosine + JS cell.</li>
</ul>

<h2>4. Design</h2>

<p><b>Why this is a single task, not split:</b> the extraction pipeline (the most expensive step, 2.5h on 8× H100 per #502) is identical across both arms — only the persona panel changes. Running the bake-off twice on two different panels in one task lets the cross-behavior heatmap (the hero figure) be produced from a coherent single sweep, and the panel-level differences (n=26 with substrate vs n=138 with source-FE) are explicit enough that one plan with two clearly-named arms ("fact arm" and "sycophancy arm") doesn't muddle them. The substrate-handling and prior-control differ between arms; each arm gets its own §4 sub-spec below.</p>

<p><b>Why code, not a model call?</b> The bake-off is a numerical pipeline: capture residual activations, compute 9 pairwise distance matrices per (layer × extraction), regress against a target matrix, score by length-partial Spearman + LOCO-CV R². Every step is deterministic numerics on saved tensors / CSVs, not unstructured-text classification. No model call would help (and would burn cost + add noise). The bake-off scoring code already exists from #502 (<code>scripts/issue493_extraction_metric_bakeoff.py</code> + <code>scripts/issue502_dispatch.py</code>).</p>

<p><b>Contrastive negatives:</b> N/A — not a behavior-implantation experiment. This is pure analysis over already-trained adapters.</p>

<p><b>Smoke/sweep architectural parity:</b> N/A — <code>kind: analysis</code> with no per-cell training. The bake-off has a single dispatch step (the existing <code>issue502_dispatch.py</code>) that we run twice (once per arm); the dispatcher's own per-condition tensor-merge step is the natural smoke unit. A small smoke run on N=2 conditions × N=4 probes × N=2 layers (per #502's example) is the first launch on each arm before fanning out to the full sweep.</p>

<pre># Per cell (point, layer, metric, variant):

<h4>4.2.6 Saturation screening</h4>

<p>Per #480 and the #448/#502 lesson, screen target cells for saturation before scoring. Two screens:</p>

<ol>
<li><b>Per-source DV variance screen:</b> for each source, compute <code>span(Δ) = max(Δ) − min(Δ)</code> across its 23 bystanders. If span < 0.05 (5 percentage points), flag that source as "low-DV-variance" and exclude its per-source ρ from sign-consistency checks. Pool-level analysis still uses all sources but reports a separate "live cells only" subset = the 21 cells from sources with span ≥ 0.20 (assistant + software_engineer per #411's distribution).</li>
<li><b>Per-cell predictor saturation screen:</b> for each predictor cell, compute the predictor's spread across the 138 pairs in units of its own scale (e.g. coefficient of variation). If a cell's predictor is saturated at the bf16 floor (e.g. cosine = 1.0 on the assistant ↔ no-system pair, which #494 flagged), exclude that pair from the regression but flag the cell.</li>

<h2>11. Decision Rationale</h2>

<p><b>N/A — no hyperparameters in this task type.</b> This is <code>kind: analysis</code>: no model training, no LoRA rank, no learning rate, no warmup schedule. Every parameter on the predictor side (28 layers, 9 metrics, 3 extraction points, 2 variants, length-partial Spearman, LOCO-CV R², 5000-rep cluster bootstrap, 500-probe pool for the fact arm, 50-probe pool for the sycophancy arm) is inherited unchanged from #502, with <code>Source: #502</code> for the recipe-level choices. The sycophancy-arm probe pool (50 wrong-claim probes) comes from #411 (<code>Source: #411</code>) because that's the probe set the leakage Δ was measured on. The fact arm uses #502's 500-probe pool (<code>Source: #502</code>) because the fact-leakage target was measured across many framings and a stable mixed-distribution background gives the cleanest pairwise distance estimate.</p>

<p>Three #509-specific operational choices (NOT hyperparameters in the training sense — control choices) get explicit grounding:</p>

<ul>
<li><b>Substrate fixed effects on the fact arm.</b> Required by #494's substrate sign-flip finding (cosine flipped negative on contrastive arms, positive on positive-only fictional_scholar arm). <code>Source: #494</code> — the parent of this control.</li>
<li><b>Prior control on the fact arm.</b> Required by #500's finding that <code>bystander_logprob</code> is the only consistently supported predictor. The bake-off must lift over the prior. <code>Source: #500</code>.</li>
<li><b>Source fixed effects + saturation screening on the sycophancy arm.</b> Required by #470 (pooled ρ collapses under source FE) + #480 (saturation pathology on software_engineer marker DV). <code>Source: #470 + #480</code>.</li>
