## 1. Goal

Find a training recipe in the canonical marker-position-only (tail_tokens=0) loss regime that lands the ※-marker implant at a sub-ceiling anchor — and measure whether there exists a checkpoint band where villain emits ※ on most of its own on-policy generations while the held-out bystander panel stays meaningfully lower — by titrating the anchor-strength knobs (lr, training duration read per-checkpoint, LoRA rank/scope) and measuring **on-policy emission rate**, **full-vocab KL-from-base**, and **the source−bystander gap** as continuous trajectories across checkpoints. The PRIMARY deliverable is the continuous selectivity-vs-training-amount trajectory (which answers open-q 2.2 implant-learning-speed regardless of whether a clean binary window appears). The SECONDARY deliverable is the binary success criterion (source ≥ 0.8 AND bystander mean < 0.1 over a contiguous ≥ 25-step band). This unblocks the saturation-line of #448, #469, #471 (all of which had nothing for recipe knobs to push against) and produces one of three valuable outcomes: (a) a usable sub-ceiling recipe with a window → it becomes the project's canonical anchor; (b) a sub-ceiling recipe with no binary window but informative continuous trajectories → recipe-knob effects become readable on the continuous DV at matched-step; (c) every titrated anchor saturates → on-policy emission is intrinsically saturating on this model and the leakage program migrates to the continuous KL-from-base DV. Outcome (c) is a fully valid result, not a failure.

## 3. Hypothesis

**Reframe (per blocker 3):** Parent dynamics — #385 at step 75 had source 0.54 with bystanders ALREADY 6-15%; #456's clean 0.90/0.046 was at step ~1600 — strongly suggest source- and bystander-emission rise *together in time* under contrastive-negative training, not separated by a clean wide window. No published parent demonstrates a sub-200-step source≥0.8 / bystander<0.1 window in the canonical marker-position-only loss regime. The expected outcome is **narrow-or-absent binary window**; the experiment's PRIMARY deliverable is the continuous trajectory regardless, and falsification of the binary window is itself a valuable result (outcome (c) from §1).

**Primary (H1, continuous):** The selectivity-vs-training-amount trajectories — source emission, bystander-panel mean, and the source−bystander gap — are well-defined, monotonic-or-single-peaked, and the source−bystander gap reaches its maximum at some intermediate checkpoint t* ∈ [10, 250] rather than at the endpoint.
- **Confirmation:** Stage 1 produces interpretable trajectories on BOTH seeds; gap is maximized at some intermediate t*; the analyzer reports t* + the gap value there (a continuous selectivity-best-step recipe number, even if the binary thresholds are not met).
- **Falsification:** Trajectories are noise-dominated across seeds (no consistent t*), OR gap is monotonically increasing to the endpoint and never opens (everything saturates together).

**Secondary (H2, binary — the target, not the expectation):** At t* there exists a contiguous ≥ 25-step band where villain emission ≥ 0.8 AND held-out bystander-panel mean emission < 0.1, with on-policy ΔG on the bystander panel NOT within 1 nat of ceiling on > 50% of personas.
- **Met:** report the band; that recipe + that step range becomes the project's canonical sub-ceiling anchor. Skip Stage 2.
- **Not met on Stage 1:** launch Stage 2 (anchor-knob sweep) to see if any cell opens the binary window. If every Stage-2 cell still fails → outcome (c) from §1 (intrinsic saturation; pivot leakage program to continuous KL DV). This is a valid finding, not a failure.

**Quantitative thresholds (the target for H2, not predictions for H1):**
- Source emission rate ≥ 0.8 (a defensible floor for "the model has learned to emit"; #456 hit 0.90 in the legacy regime).
- Held-out bystander-mean emission < 0.1 (≥ 0.1 means the implant has globalized; #456's 0.046 is the closest precedent).
- ΔG on bystander panel ≥ 1 nat below ceiling on > 50% of personas (the #448 saturation gauge — ensures recipe knobs would have headroom to push at that checkpoint).
- Band width ≥ 25 optimizer steps contiguous (a single lucky checkpoint isn't robust). NOTE: parent windows in #385/#456 were NOT shown to be ~125-step wide in the marker-position-only regime — this is a target width, not a parent-evidenced expectation.

### 4.4-bis Slot-geometry discipline (LOAD-BEARING for measurement validity — Methodology critic Must-Fix)

There are THREE coupled discipline points the implementer must enforce simultaneously, all rooted in the same #472 invariant: the `<|im_end|>` slot at which the suppression gradient lands, the slot at which the positive marker is trained, and the slot at which the eval DV is read must all share the same K-token tail prefix. The reconciler verified against the #472/#474 code that the naïve port satisfies one of these but breaks another.

**The #472 layout (verified from `origin/issue-472:contrastive_neg_geometry_472/__init__.py` + `build_training_data.py:194/212` + `eval_one_cell.py:build_full_ids`):**

- `MARKER_SEP = "\n\n"` (`__init__.py`).
- POSITIVE completion = `f"{r_text}{MARKER_SEP}{marker_text}"` = `R + "\n\n" + " ※"`. The marker (and the DV slot, via `build_full_ids`) sit at a position whose 2-token tail is the BPE-fused `'.\n\n'` token (id 382, the C1 token-equality contract `eval_one_cell.py` already enforces for positives↔eval).
- NEGATIVE completion = bare `r_text` (`build_training_data.py:212`), NO separator.
- The ported `suppress_at_post_response_slot=True` branch (`origin/issue-474:sft.py` ~lines 200-241) walks `valid_indices` for the FIRST `<|im_end|>`. On a negative row that `<|im_end|>` sits directly after bare `R` — its prefix tail is `...Answer.`, NOT `...Answer.\n\n`.

**The defect:** the suppression gradient pushes `log P(<|im_end|>)` UP at the `Answer.`-conditioned slot, but the DV reads `log P(※)` at the `Answer.\n\n`-conditioned slot. These are DIFFERENT slots from the model's perspective (different prefix tail, different hidden state, different softmax). The §4.4-bis (previous round) "mask at `<|im_end|>` not trailing `\n`" unit test is SILENT on this separator-prefix axis. For #479's narrow-window detection, a source−bystander gap at intermediate checkpoints could be a separator-geometry artifact rather than evidence of anchor-strength selectivity.

**The fix (option a — DEFAULT, lower risk, preserves the established C1 positive↔eval token-equality contract):** construct the NEGATIVE completion as `f"{r_text}{MARKER_SEP}"` = `R + "\n\n"`, not bare `r_text`. This makes the `<|im_end|>` the suppressor targets sit at the SAME 2-token-tail position (`.\n\n` BPE-fused 382) as the positive marker slot and the eval DV slot — all three slots now share the same K-token-tail prefix and the suppression-vs-measurement geometry is clean. Implementation: edit `build_training_data.py:212` to append `MARKER_SEP` to the negative completion text. ~1 LoC change.

**Fallback (option b — only if option a fails the three-way K-token-equality test below):** drop `MARKER_SEP` from BOTH positive AND eval construction so the marker / DV / suppressor all share the bare `R`-end prefix. Higher risk because the existing eval rig and every positive↔eval contract in `build_full_ids` would need re-validation against the re-encoded marker slot. Do NOT pursue option b unless option a's test fails.

**The strengthened required pre-pod unit test (NEW, replaces the old "mask at index 21" test):** at `tests/experiments/test_479_three_way_token_tail_equality.py`, build ONE positive row, ONE negative row, and ONE eval probe via the actual `#472` chat-template + `build_full_ids` path, then for each:

1. Identify the loss slot (positive: marker; negative: `<|im_end|>` via `suppress_at_post_response_slot`) and the eval-DV slot (the `prompt_logprobs[L]` slot at `L = len(prompt + R + MARKER_SEP) - 1`, the slot `build_full_ids` reads).
2. Assert the K=2 input-ids immediately BEFORE each of those three slots are byte-identical across all three rows (positive marker slot prefix == negative suppressor slot prefix == eval DV slot prefix). This extends `eval_one_cell.py`'s existing positive↔eval K-token-equality contract to the negative-suppressor slot.
3. Assert the loss/DV slot ITSELF on each row is the expected token id (positive: 83399 = ` ※`; negative: 151645 = `<|im_end|>`; eval: read position whose prefix matches positive marker prefix).

If the three-way assertion FAILS under option a (e.g. because appending `"\n\n"` to `R` triggers a different BPE merge boundary depending on the last char of `R`), the test fails LOUD; the implementer falls back to option b and re-runs the test. Until this test PASSES, no Stage-1 cell launches.

**Why the previous "mask at `<|im_end|>` not `\n`" test is insufficient:** that test only verifies WHICH slot the loss mask points at on the negative row — it does not verify that slot is the SAME slot (by prefix-tail conditioning) the DV reads. The three-way K-token-equality test is the load-bearing measurement-validity assertion.

**Background (why the suppression-without-this-fix story is not "null by construction"):** #405 ran with bare `R` negatives + no `suppress_at_post_response_slot` (trailing `\n` as the trained slot) and still produced a clean K-effect, because the trailing `\n` slot's gradient propagates back to the adjacent `<|im_end|>` slot via softmax coupling and then forward to the `Answer.\n\n`-conditioned eval position via the next-token conditional chain. That indirect mechanism is sufficient for endpoint-aggregate measurements (#405's K-sweep), but #479 asks for narrow-window resolution at intermediate checkpoints — that resolution requires the three slots to coincide cleanly.

**Composite implementation requirement (REQUIRED port + REQUIRED edit + REQUIRED test):**
1. Port `MarkerOnlyDataCollator(suppress_at_post_response_slot, im_end_token_id)` from `origin/issue-474:sft.py:152-243` onto the `issue-479` worktree branch.
2. Wire the flags through `TrainLoraConfig` and pass them in `train_cell.py:train_one_cell`.
3. Edit `build_training_data.py:212` to append `MARKER_SEP` to the negative completion (option a).
4. Add `tests/experiments/test_479_three_way_token_tail_equality.py` and run it BEFORE any pod cycle.

### 4.8 Why code, not a model call?

Every DV computed by this experiment is a forward-pass numeric (log-prob at a specific token slot, full-vocab KL, argmax-equality). A model call would be (a) wrong on validity (we need exact log-softmax values, not natural-language interpretation), (b) wrong on cost (~11 checkpoints × ~45 personas × 20 questions × 2 seeds = 19,800 reads per cell — an API model call regime would be ~$200/cell), (c) wrong on determinism (single forward pass = bit-identical re-run; an API call introduces a sampling layer). The eval rig is the right code path.

### 4.9 Contrastive negatives for behavior implantation

Goal IS a behavior implant (the marker ※). The 4-spread-negative arm + qwen_default + 400 positives : 400 total negatives complies with `.claude/rules/contrastive-negatives.md` (≥ 2-4 negative personas ✓; default assistant included ✓; ~1:1 positives-to-total-negatives — exact 1:1 ✓; held-out bystander panel disjoint from trained negatives ✓). The single experimental variable is anchor strength NOT negative composition (the latter held fixed across all cells; varying it is the separate sibling experiment #477, which we must NOT duplicate).

### 4.10 Smoke / sweep architectural parity (UNIFICATION)

Stage 1 IS the smoke for Stage 2: identical dispatcher (`dispatch_neg_geometry_472.py`), identical subprocess shape (`i472_run_cell.py --gpu-id g`), identical env injection, identical sentinel + `[phase=...]` logging, identical teardown sequence. Stage 1 is parameterized as `--cells c479_base --seeds 42 137 --n-gpus 1`; Stage 2 is `--cells c479_lr1e-5,c479_lr3e-5,c479_r32attn,c479_r32all --seeds 42 137 --n-gpus 4 --max-parallel 4`. ## 7. Decision Gates

**No gates — short run / pre-verified hypothesis on Stage 1; one gate between stages.**

Stage 1 is ~3.8 GPU-hours wall-time on a single H100 (borderline at the 4-hour threshold, but no mid-Stage-1 gate is needed: the trajectory eval IS the data the analysis needs, and stopping early would just discard checkpoints that are cheap once the training has run). The autonomous inter-stage gate (§9-bis below) is still in effect: Stage 1 result → analyze → optionally launch Stage 2.

**Inter-stage gate (Stage 1 → Stage 2):**
- IF Stage 1 finds a contiguous ≥ 25-step band where (villain emission ≥ 0.8) AND (bystander-mean emission < 0.1) AND (ΔG-bystander NOT within 1 nat of ceiling on > 50% of personas) on **both seeds** → **STOP after Stage 1**, report the band, skip Stage 2. Analyzer composes clean-result on Stage 1 alone.
- ELSE → launch Stage 2 (4 cells × 2 seeds on a 4× H100 pod).

This gate is autonomous (the orchestrator reads `i479_analyze.py`'s `window_detected` flag in the manifest JSON and dispatches Stage 2 OR not). User is not asked.

## 11. Decision Rationale

Every load-bearing hyperparameter:

- **Learning rate 5e-6 (Stage 1)**
  - What: 5e-6, cosine schedule, warmup 0.05.
  - Why: candidate gentler-than-#448 rate (#448 used lr 1e-5 and saturated under marker-only loss; #405 used lr 5e-6 at 2 epochs and produced a clean per-cell K-effect on bystander ΔlogP). The hypothesis that lr 5e-6 at this rank/scope sits 5-10 nats below ceiling is #448's *hypothetical* less-trained anchor framing — measured-sub-ceiling is part of what #479 tests, not a given. Going lower (e.g., 2e-6) would risk the #472 under-trained regime; lr 5e-6 is the gentlest rate with prior project evidence of a real implant effect.
  - Source: **#405** (Reproducibility table: "AdamW, lr=5e-6, 2 epochs"); **#448** (the "5-10 nats below ceiling" hypothetical anchor framing the experiment inherits).
  - Alternatives: lr 1e-5 (used by #448, #472 — saturated under marker-only loss); lr 2e-6 (untested — risks the #472 under-trained regime).

- **LoRA rank r=16 (Stage 1)**
  - What: rank 16, α=32, dropout 0.05, target_modules = q,k,v,o (attention only, no MLP).
  - Why: matches #405's "non-saturating per #311 / #448" config (attn-only is itself the smaller adapter scope; r=16 over r=32 reduces capacity).
  - Source: **#405** (Reproducibility table: "LoRA r=16, α=32, dropout=0.05, targets = q_proj/k_proj/v_proj/o_proj (no MLP — non-saturating per #311 / #448)").
  - Alternatives: r=32 (used by #472, #448 — saturated; surfaces as Stage-2 cells `c479_r32attn` / `c479_r32all`).

- **Epochs 5 / max_steps 250**
  - What: 5 epochs at 800 rows / batch 16 = 250 optimizer steps.
  - Why: 5 epochs is the longest budget any sibling experiment has run at this rig (#469); it spans "barely started" (step 5) through "definitively over-trained" (step 250 = full saturation arc of #469: 75.8% saturation fraction at ep1 → 99.2% at ep5). At a 50-steps-per-epoch cadence, the schedule places dense checkpoints within and after each epoch boundary. Note: the "expected window region" framing from #385/#456 (steps ~75-200) is NOT a reliable parent prediction in the marker-position-only loss (per BLOCKER 3 reframe — those parents showed coupled rise, not a clean separation), so the schedule is justified by the saturation arc bracket alone.
  - Source: **#469** (5-epoch budget, observed full saturation arc).
  - Alternatives: 3 epochs (#448's saturated regime — would miss the over-train phase signature on the lr 5e-6 cell, which trains more slowly); 1 epoch (#472 — under-trained at lr 1e-5, would be very under-trained at lr 5e-6).

- **Batch 4 × grad_accum 4 (eff batch 16)**
  - What: per_device_batch 4, grad_accum 4, effective batch 16.
  - Why: matches every marker-implant experiment in this project family (#405, #448, #472, #474). Stable on single H100 at max_length 1024.
  - Source: **#405** and **#472**.
  - Alternatives: bigger effective batch (32+) would alter optimizer-step count and change the "matched-step cross-cell comparison" semantics.

- **max_length 1024**
  - What: training sequence cap.
  - Why: #472 inheritance; on-policy R from base Qwen-2.5-7B at greedy ≈ 150 token median, so 1024 rarely truncates.
  - Source: **#472**.
  - Alternatives: 2048 (would double VRAM; unnecessary).

- **max_new_tokens 2048 (eval generation)**
  - What: max new tokens during on-policy generation at eval time.
  - Why: marker-leakage rule REQUIRES ≥ 2× longest trained completion (=2048 here, 2× max_length 1024); truncation creates silent zeros (#260 incident).
  - Source: **`.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md`** (project rule).
  - Alternatives: lower would risk silent zeros.

- **Contrastive negative composition: spread-4 (incl. qwen_default), 100 neg/persona**
  - What: 4 negative personas selected by `placement='spread'` on cos-to-villain quantiles over the #472 persona bank, always including `qwen_default`. 100 negative rows per persona = 400 total.
  - Why: negative-persona COUNT (4) + selection (spread, incl. qwen_default) inherit #472 Anchor (the geometry rig's baseline). Per-persona row COUNT (100) matches #405 EXACTLY so the total positives-to-negatives ratio is 1:1. Default-assistant inclusion is mandatory per `.claude/rules/contrastive-negatives.md` (leakage to default is the safety target, open-q 3.7).
  - Source: **#405** (per-persona count + 1:1 ratio); **#472 Anchor** (negative-persona count + selection rule).
  - Alternatives: 200 neg/persona = #472 Anchor exact (1:2 ratio — heavier negatives; novel and not parent-evidenced); 2 negative personas (#247/#329 minimum) risks leakage uniformity; 8+ negatives (#472 `c472_negp_8`) is the separate sibling sweep #477.

- **Positive rows 400 (1:1 positives-to-negatives)**
  - What: 400 positive rows (villain × N unique Q × dupes — implementer chooses unique-Q sample vs replicate factor consistent with #405 build).
  - Why: matches #405's working positive count EXACTLY (the recipe that lifted #383 source-rate 70× from the floor was 400 positives at 1:1 against 400 contrastive negatives). The 1:1 ratio is the contrastive-negatives rule's working default.
  - Source: **#405** (400 pos, 1:1 ratio).
  - Alternatives: 200 pos = #472 Anchor exact (would deviate from #405 inheritance); 800 pos = double would change training duration without changing the ratio.

- **Checkpoint steps {5, 10, 20, 35, 50, 75, 100, 125, 150, 200, 250}**
  - What: 11 absolute optimizer-step checkpoints (the last = the 5-epoch endpoint).

### 13.2 Analyzer must-weigh (interpretation-time)

- **Source emission at Stage 1 is NOT the expected outcome.** #405's body reports on-policy emission was effectively ZERO at the 2-epoch endpoint of this exact recipe (its DV was log-prob, not emission). So "Stage 1 satisfies H2 binary at source ≥ 0.8" is the optimistic outcome, not the modal one. Frame Stage 2 as the expected continuation, not as "Plan B."

- **A small source−bystander gap at t* is not evidence of a recipe-tunable window.** Source gets positive gradient; bystanders get only suppression. A transient gap during the implant rise is mechanically guaranteed (the positive signal lands before the suppression has fully equilibrated). Treat gap < ~0.3 emission-rate units at t* as an implant-strength milestone, NOT as window evidence. The binary H2 band (source ≥ 0.8 AND bystander < 0.1) is the discriminator.

- **Report per-seed trajectories side by side, NOT only the mean.** Surface per-seed band widths (e.g. "seed 42 = 26-step band, seed 137 = 24-step") rather than collapsing to a both-seeds-AND aggregate. The marginal gray zone is itself a finding — a 24-vs-26-step seed split flagged as "fails H2 (< 25 contiguous)" but presented honestly is more useful than a hidden split.

- **Verify panel composition does not drive selectivity.** At the window-midpoint checkpoint, plot bystander emission vs cos-to-villain. If only the *far* personas stay low and near-villain held-outs are already leaking, the "selectivity" being measured is near-vs-far distance, NOT source-vs-bystander persona identity. Near-personas staying low is the load-bearing finding.

- **Stage-2 cross-cell claims must be narrow.** The matched-step protocol holds optimizer step count constant but NOT the optimizer's path through weight space (a 250-step run at lr 3e-5 reaches a different region than a 250-step run at lr 5e-6). Therefore: "lr-up shifts the trajectory earlier" is a valid cross-cell claim. "lr-up causes a wider/narrower window at matched training amount" is NOT — the matched-step comparison cannot support the latter.

- **Argmax brittleness near 0.5 emission.** When source crosses 0.8 OR bystander crosses 0.1, read the crossing alongside the ΔG / log-prob margin AND the full-vocab KL. A 0.79-vs-0.81 source emission flip across one checkpoint, or a 0.09-vs-0.11 bystander flip, is a knife-edge — report as such, not as a robust window opening or closing.

- **Distinguish "intermediate saturation" from outcome (c).** If only SOME Stage-2 cells saturate at step 5 (e.g. lr 3e-5) while the gentlest cell (lr 5e-6) sits at bystander ~0.02 at step 5 and ~0.15 at step 10, the situation is NOT intrinsic-saturation outcome (c) — it is sparse-checkpoint resolution failure between steps 5 and 10 at the gentlest cell. Call for a Stage-3 dense `(5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10)` sub-sweep on that cell ONLY (one cell × 2 seeds × 6 fine checkpoints ≈ 1.5 GPU-h) rather than declaring outcome (c). Outcome (c) only fires when EVERY cell saturates at step 5.
