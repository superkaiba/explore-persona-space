**D3 mechanism, measured** (`artifacts/probe-detached-fd.txt`): command substitution returns
only when its captured pipe has no writers left. A detached child inheriting fd 1 holds it for
the child's WHOLE lifetime → the substitution hangs → the harness kills the Bash call at its
cap, *after* the child already started (pid never captured, job running unowned). Measured:
stdout redirected inside → rc=0, pid set, 0 s. stdout **not** redirected → substitution blocked
the child's full 12 s **despite `timeout 6`**, because `timeout`'s target (the `bash -c`) had
already exited at t≈0; nothing was left to kill. stderr unredirected while stdout redirected →
rc=0, 0 s (only the CAPTURED stream holds the substitution). ⇒ the redirect discipline is the
fix; `timeout` buys a bound on a wrapper that wedges BEFORE forking, nothing more.

**D5 form, measured** (`artifacts/probe-guard1-restore.txt`), four path shapes at once
(in-HEAD modified · in-HEAD staged-deleted · staged-ADDED not in HEAD · branch-DELETED, absent
