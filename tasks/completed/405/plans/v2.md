## Multi-persona training: marker leakage vs persona-distance to trained set, as a function of K (number of source personas)

---

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I'll train the same marker-implant adapter while varying ONE thing — how many different "people" (system-prompt personas) get taught to use the marker. K = 1, 2, 4, or 8 teachers; the rest of the recipe stays identical. Then I'll check whether the marker shows up on a FIXED set of held-out personas the model never saw teach it, and ask whether the held-out person's behavior is predicted by how similar they are to the teachers.
- **What I expect:** With more teachers (larger K), the held-out person's distance to the teaching set should matter less — the marker becomes "the assistant's habit" rather than "this one persona's habit." If that flattening is real, it's evidence the behavior generalizes by becoming persona-invariant, not by case-by-case representational overlap. I expect K=1 to look like the prior single-source gradient (clear distance dependence), K=8 to look much flatter.
- **What would change my mind:** If K=8 still has a steep distance gradient (the held-out person stays "safe" if it's far from any teacher), the behavior is fundamentally tied to per-persona representations and "more diverse training data" doesn't buy generalization at this scale — Dan's N×M framing then needs a different lever. If K=1 ALREADY shows a flat curve, my prior on "distance to teacher matters" was wrong from the start and the whole #207 distance-to-leakage story may be an artifact of my evaluation set, not the training.

## 1. Goal

Measure how training-set persona diversity (K source personas) and persona-distance to held-out targets jointly predict marker leakage, operationalizing the persona-axis instance of the N×M training-to-deployment generalization framing. Linked open questions: 3.3 (leak-single-vs-multi) + 3.9 (leak-from-cell-set — does **min-distance** or **mean-distance** from a held-out persona to the trained-positive subset better predict leakage?). The point is not "implant a marker" (the #383/#247/#329/#448 line already established that). The point is: when you implant the SAME marker using K=1 vs K=2 vs K=4 vs K=8 distinct source personas, how does the leakage curve on held-out personas change as a function of distance to the training subset?

A flattening of the leakage-vs-distance slope with K is evidence the behavior is being stored persona-invariantly (the safety-relevant generalization mode); a preserved slope is evidence it's being stored per-persona (the safety-relevant localization mode). Both are interesting. The headline figure plots leakage-vs-distance, one line per K, and asks: does the slope shrink with K?

## 2. Prior Work

### In-codebase priors (these constrain the design)

- **#207 (`MODERATE`, Spearman |ρ| 0.48–0.79).** Established the single-source distance-to-leakage gradient under [ZLT] marker training across 6 sister experiments. **Caveat #207's authors flagged:** EM/misalignment training breaks the gradient. **Caveat that matters more for #405:** #207 used the deprecated `[ZLT]` substring DV and a single source per cell. The K=1 cell of this experiment re-extends that gradient under the canonical on-policy ` ※` log-prob DV.
- **#311 (`LOW`, partial Spearman ρ=−0.348, p=0.086 at N=17).** Trained 2-source joint adapter (paramedic + comedian, K=2). Found weak evidence the joint-specific leakage gradient predicts from distance to midpoint, but **N=17 was underpowered** and the design conflated joint training with the substring DV. #405 replaces this with K=2 at a wider design (more subsets, more held-out personas, the canonical DV).
- **#383 (`MODERATE`).** 11 LoRA cells one-at-a-time around an anchor (200pos × 1 source × 200neg × 2 negs). The **1:1 positives:total-negatives ratio + 23-bystander panel** lifted source rate ~70× off the floor. Settled three recipe knobs (lr=1e-5, 3 epochs, r=32) — note: #383 was at the saturating anchor.
- **#448 (`MODERATE`).** The SATURATION lesson. At lr=1e-5, r=32, 3 epochs the on-policy `log P(※)` argmaxes the marker on 264/264 (persona × cell) eval pairs — recipe knobs have nothing to push against. Either pick a less-trained anchor (fewer steps / smaller r / lower lr) or use a non-saturating DV. **#405's anchor is built off this lesson** (see §11).
- **#247/#329.** Contrastive coupling: source rate ~99.6%, bystander leakage ~11.7% (vs ~50% for the leaky EM-first case). This is the contrastive regime the #405 design must stay inside.
- **`run_leakage_v3_onpolicy.py`** + `MarkerOnlyDataCollator(tail_tokens=0)` in `src/explore_persona_space/train/sft.py` — the rig is reusable. The new launcher extends it minimally (multi-source positive set, fixed shared negative set per K).

### Literature

- **Persona Vectors** (Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey, 2025; arXiv 2507.21509). Establishes linear persona directions in residual-stream activations; finetuning shifts the model along the relevant persona vector. **Directly relevant:** their finding that training-data persona-vector-alignment predicts post-training persona shift is the mechanism story for #405's expected result. We use their layer-21 cosine recipe (per `.claude/rules/persona-distance-metrics.md`) as the distance metric.
- **Persona Features Control EM** (Wang, ..., Mossing, 2025; arXiv 2506.19823). Single-source persona-feature implant; #405 extends to the K-source case. Mossing's own N×M framing names #405 as the persona-axis instance.
- **Sivapiromrat, Zhang, Basaldella, Collier 2025** (*Multi-Trigger Poisoning Amplifies Backdoor Vulnerabilities in LLMs*, arXiv 2507.11112). **Strongest published prior for the K↑ → broader-leakage hypothesis.** Shows (a) multiple distinct backdoor triggers coexist within a single LLM without interfering with each other (so K-source persona implants should compose, not collide), and (b) **multiple high-similarity triggers achieve robust activation even when input tokens are substituted or separated by long token spans** — i.e. higher trigger diversity widens the input region the implant fires on, even off the exact trained patterns. #405's K-source persona question is the persona-space analogue: does K↑ widen the *persona-space* region the marker fires on (i.e. flatten the leakage-vs-persona-distance curve)? If their token-space result transfers, the K=8 curve in #405 should be flatter than K=1.
- **Hao, Yang, Lin 2024** (*Exploring Backdoor Vulnerabilities of Chat Models*, arXiv 2404.02406). Multi-trigger backdoor literature is the closest non-persona analogue: increasing trigger diversity tends to **broaden** the activation region the backdoor fires on (their composite-trigger ASRs). Loose prior for #405: K↑ → leakage region grows (flatter distance curve).
- **Tracing Persona Vectors Through LLM Pretraining** (Moskvoretskii et al., 2026; arXiv 2605.13329). Recent confirmation persona vectors are stable pretraining features (open-source replication on OLMo-3 / Apertus-8B). Backdrop justification for treating layer-21 cosine on Qwen as a reasonable distance metric for held-out personas.

## 3. Hypothesis

**Primary, directional.** The **slope of held-out marker leakage vs min-distance** (slope_K) is non-increasing in K, and `slope_K=1 > slope_K=8` (the K=8 curve is meaningfully flatter than the K=1 curve).

- **Falsifying outcome A — null:** `slope_K=8` is statistically indistinguishable from `slope_K=1` (mixed-effects K×min-distance interaction CI overlaps 0 at 95%). Interpretation: multi-source training does NOT change how the marker generalizes across persona-distance at this anchor; the marker stays "tied to teacher personas" regardless of K.
- **Falsifying outcome B — reversal:** `slope_K=8 > slope_K=1` (steeper distance curve with more sources). Interpretation: multi-source training **fragments** the implant into per-teacher patches without a unified persona-invariant signal — diversity has the opposite effect from what the analogy with backdoor-trigger diversity would predict. This would be the most informative outcome.
- **Confirmatory outcome:** `slope_K=8 ≪ slope_K=1`, with intermediate K=2, K=4 monotone. Magnitude target: slope_K=1 ∈ [−2.0, −0.5] nats per unit distance (extending #207's range to the log-prob DV); slope_K=8 within ±0.5 nat per unit distance of zero.

**Secondary, min-vs-mean (open-q 3.9):** Held-out leakage is better predicted by **min-distance** (the closest trained-positive persona) than by **mean-distance** (avg across all trained positives). Falsifying: mean-distance has a tighter fit (lower AIC / higher CV-R²) at every K. Why this matters: min-distance backs the "behavior generalizes via nearest-neighbor representational overlap" mechanism; mean-distance backs an "aggregate centroid steering" mechanism.

## 4. Design

### 4.1 Three-way pool split (named personas, FIXED across the experiment)

From the 20-persona layer-20 cosine-matrix pool (`eval_results/extraction_method_comparison/cosine_matrix_a_layer20.json`; the #311/#341 pool):

| Role | Personas | Count |
|---|---|---|
| **Positives pool** (sample K from this) | `paramedic`, `navy_seal`, `villain`, `librarian`, `data_scientist`, `medical_doctor`, `french_person`, `poet` | 8 |
| **Fixed contrastive negatives** (every cell uses these 4, FIXED across K) | `software_engineer`, `kindergarten_teacher`, `helpful_assistant`, `no_persona` | 4 |
| **Held-out eval personas** (strictly disjoint from above) | `cybersec_consultant`, `pentester`, `private_investigator`, `army_medic`, `surgeon`, `police_officer`, `florist`, `comedian` | 8 |
| **Total** | | 20 |

**Why this split (the only choices that aren't free):**
- The positives pool spans the layer-20 cosine distance range — `poet` and `villain` are far from the professional cluster; `data_scientist`, `medical_doctor`, `librarian`, `paramedic` are inside it; `navy_seal`, `french_person` are mid. Sampling K from this pool guarantees the trained subset has within-subset variance whether K=2 or K=8.
- Negatives are **assistant-side** (`helpful_assistant`, `no_persona`, `software_engineer`, `kindergarten_teacher`) — these explicitly push `log P(※)` DOWN at the assistant-default context, which is the safety-relevant target (open-q 3.7). Including the bare default assistant is mandatory per the contrastive-negatives rule. Negatives are **FIXED across K** so K is the only variable.
- Held-out spans the distance range: `comedian` is the far outlier (d≈0.18 from the closest positive); `cybersec_consultant`, `pentester`, `surgeon`, etc. sit in the professional cluster (d≈0.01–0.02 from closest positive). This gives the regression real distance variance.
- The held-out set deliberately includes occupational neighbors of pool members (`surgeon` ↔ `medical_doctor`, `pentester` ↔ `cybersec_consultant`, `army_medic` ↔ `navy_seal`) so the **min-distance** axis has fine-grained low-distance variation, not just `comedian`-as-outlier.

### 4.2 Subset sampling per K (subsets-per-K table)

K is the single manipulated variable; for each K we train multiple subsets to average over which-subset noise.

| K | # subsets | Subset choice | Total cells | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | All 8 positives, one at a time | 8 | Re-extends #207's per-source gradient (1 subset per persona, no averaging needed) |

| **CORE K-sweep total** | — | — | **21 cells × 2 seeds = 42 runs** | This is the CORE track only (headline regression input); the K4_ABLNEG ablation (+1 cell × 2 seeds = 2) and the K1_DOSE50 dose-control arm (+3 cells × 2 seeds = 6) bring the experiment total to **25 cells × 2 seeds = 50 runs** (§5) |

Subset selection uses a deterministic numpy RNG (seed=405) so the subset list is reproducible across re-launches. Pseudocode in §4.6.

### 4.3 Holding total training rows constant across K

**Why this matters:** if K=8 has 8× more positive rows than K=1, you can't tell whether the K effect is "more sources" or "more data." We hold total positive rows = 400, split evenly across the K trained personas, regardless of K (extending the #383 1:1 ratio):

| K | rows_per_positive_persona | total positive rows | rows_per_negative_persona | total negative rows | total rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 400 | 400 | 100 | 400 | 800 |
| 2 | 200 | 400 | 100 | 400 | 800 |
| 4 | 100 | 400 | 100 | 400 | 800 |
| 8 | 50 | 400 | 100 | 400 | 800 |

The 4 negative personas hold a constant 100 rows each across all K (so negatives = 400 always, matching positives 1:1).

**Smoke-test contract:** the launcher logs `total_positive_rows` and `total_rows` per cell; the orchestrator asserts both equal 400 and 800 respectively across every cell before letting the sweep proceed.

```python
# scripts/issue405_make_cell_specs.py
import json
import numpy as np
from pathlib import Path
from itertools import combinations

POOL = ['paramedic','navy_seal','villain','librarian',
        'data_scientist','medical_doctor','french_person','poet']
NEGATIVES_FIXED = ['software_engineer','kindergarten_teacher','helpful_assistant','no_persona']
HELD_OUT = ['cybersec_consultant','pentester','private_investigator','army_medic',
            'surgeon','police_officer','florist','comedian']

assert set(POOL).isdisjoint(NEGATIVES_FIXED)
assert set(POOL).isdisjoint(HELD_OUT)
assert set(NEGATIVES_FIXED).isdisjoint(HELD_OUT)
assert len(POOL) + len(NEGATIVES_FIXED) + len(HELD_OUT) == 20

SUBSETS_PER_K = {1: None, 2: 6, 4: 6, 8: None}  # None = use all

def build_specs(rng_seed: int = 405) -> list[dict]:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(rng_seed)
    specs = []
    cell_id = 0
    for K in (1, 2, 4, 8):
        all_subsets = list(combinations(POOL, K))
        if SUBSETS_PER_K[K] is None:
            chosen = all_subsets
        else:
            idx = rng.choice(len(all_subsets), size=SUBSETS_PER_K[K], replace=False)
            chosen = [all_subsets[i] for i in idx]
        for sub in chosen:
            specs.append({
                'cell_id': f'K{K}_c{cell_id:02d}',
                'K': K,
                'positives': list(sub),
                'negatives': list(NEGATIVES_FIXED),
                'held_out': list(HELD_OUT),
                'rows_per_positive': 400 // K,
                'rows_per_negative': 100,  # 4 negs × 100 = 400 = positives
                'total_rows': 800,
            })
            cell_id += 1
    return specs

# Negative-set ablation (one extra cell, K=4): swap the contrastive
# negatives to 4 personas drawn FROM held_out, so K is held constant but
# negative-set identity changes. The 4 promoted-to-negative personas drop
# out of held-out for THIS cell only; the remaining 4 held-out personas
# carry the evaluation. Cannot reuse POOL personas as negatives because
# they're candidate positives in other cells.

def build_ablation_specs() -> list[dict]:
    """One K=4 ablation cell with different negatives (4 from held_out
    set), held_out shrunk to 4 for this cell only."""
    ABLATION_NEGS = ['surgeon','police_officer','florist','comedian']
    ABLATION_HELDOUT = ['cybersec_consultant','pentester','private_investigator','army_medic']
    pos = ('paramedic','villain','librarian','poet')  # a fixed K=4 subset
    return [{
        'cell_id': 'K4_ABLNEG',
        'K': 4,
        'positives': list(pos),
        'negatives': ABLATION_NEGS,
        'held_out': ABLATION_HELDOUT,
        'rows_per_positive': 100,
        'rows_per_negative': 100,
        'total_rows': 800,
    }]

def build_dose_control_specs() -> list[dict]:
    """FIX A2 — Dose-matched K=1 control arm at 50 rows/persona.

    Three K=1 cells matched to the main K=8 cell's per-persona dose
    (50 rows/positive). Picks ONE inside-cluster persona (`paramedic`),
    ONE mid persona (`villain`), ONE far/outlier persona (`poet`) to
    span the distance range. Same NEGATIVES_FIXED + HELD_OUT as the
    core sweep.

    Comparison logic (analyzer Phase 5):
      - main-K1@400 vs new-K1@50      -> isolates DOSE at fixed K=1
      - new-K1@50   vs main-K8@50    -> isolates K at fixed dose=50
    Total rows: 50 positives + 4 × 100 negatives = 450 rows/cell.
    Negative count held at 400 (matched to the core sweep) so the
    1:1 ratio temporarily breaks — that's the cost; the alternative
    (shrink negatives to 50 too) would change the contrastive
    regime AND the dose simultaneously and defeat the control.
    Acknowledged scope caveat in the body.
    """
    DOSE_PERSONAS = ['paramedic', 'villain', 'poet']   # inside / mid / far
    return [{
        'cell_id': f'K1_DOSE50_{p}',
        'K': 1,
        'positives': [p],
        'negatives': list(NEGATIVES_FIXED),
        'held_out': list(HELD_OUT),
        'rows_per_positive': 50,           # MATCHED to main K=8 per-persona dose
        'rows_per_negative': 100,
        'total_rows': 450,
        'arm': 'dose_control',
    } for p in DOSE_PERSONAS]

if __name__ == '__main__':
    core_specs    = build_specs()                  # 21 cells
    ablneg_specs  = build_ablation_specs()         # 1 cell  (FIX C — NOT in headline regression)
    dose_specs    = build_dose_control_specs()     # 3 cells (FIX A2 — NOT in headline regression)
    specs = core_specs + ablneg_specs + dose_specs # 25 cells total
    # Tag core cells for analyzer (FIX C): the headline mixed-effects model
    # uses ONLY cells with arm='core'; ABLNEG + DOSE50 are separate tracks.
    for s in core_specs: s['arm'] = 'core'
    Path('data/issue_405/cell_specs.json').write_text(
        json.dumps(specs, indent=2))
    print(f'Wrote {len(specs)} cell specs')
    # 8 + 6 + 6 + 1 (core) + 1 (ABLNEG) + 3 (DOSE50) = 25 cells × 2 seeds = 50 runs
```

### 4.7 Why code, not a model call?

The DV is `log P(token | context)` from a forward pass — there is no unstructured-data heuristic anywhere in the eval. The persona-distance metric is a hidden-state cosine, also a forward-pass numeric. The only piece that could conceivably be a model call is the "is this response on-topic" sanity check — but we don't need that here because the response is the model's own greedy output (on-distribution by construction). **No model-call substitution applies.**

### 4.8 Contrastive negatives (required by behavior-implant rule)

This experiment implants a behavior (marker emission) into source personas — the contrastive-negatives rule MANDATES a negative set. The design includes 4 fixed negatives spanning the assistant-default region (`helpful_assistant` + `no_persona`) and the professional cluster (`software_engineer`, `kindergarten_teacher`). 1:1 positives:total-negatives ratio (400:400) per cell. Default assistant included per the open-q 3.7 safety target. **The exemption clauses do NOT apply** (the single manipulated variable is K, not contrastive-vs-non-contrastive).

### 4.9 Smoke/sweep architectural parity

**Smoke = sweep with `--cells 1 --seeds 1`.** The Phase 4 dispatcher takes `--cells` and `--seeds` as CLI args; smoke is `K1_c00` × `seed=42` (single K=1 paramedic cell) using the same `scripts/issue405_run_cell.py` subprocess shape the sweep uses. Same env injection, same WandB logging, same teardown. The smoke run additionally:

1. Reports `g_logprob_source` (the trained-source marker log-prob); **kill-criterion** = `g_logprob_source > -0.1 nat` (saturated) → STOP, drop lr further or epochs to 1, re-smoke. We expect `g_logprob_source ∈ [-8, -3] nats` at this anchor (non-saturated headroom per #448 §6 prescription).
2. Reports `total_rows`, `total_positive_rows` (assert 800, 400).
3. Reports adapter persisted to HF Hub (per `EPM_PERSIST_ADAPTER_HF_REPO`).

Smoke PASS = headroom OK + asserts pass + result.json schema correct. Smoke wall: ~50 min on 1 GPU.

Three analysis tracks (per FIX C): the **CORE 21-cell K-sweep** carries the headline K × min-distance regression; the **K4_ABLNEG ablation** is a separate sensitivity overlay; the **K1_DOSE50 dose-control arm** is a separate head-to-head comparison for the dose-vs-diversity confound.

| Plain-English name | What it tests | What it controls for | Config slug | Track |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-teacher cells (8) | K=1: re-extends #207's per-source distance-to-leakage gradient under the canonical on-policy log P DV | Baseline gradient for slope_K=1 reference | `K1_c00`…`K1_c07` | CORE |
| Two-teacher random pairs (6) | K=2: does the gradient flatten with a single extra teacher? | Halfway point along the K axis | `K2_c08`…`K2_c13` | CORE |

| **Dose-matched K=1 @ 50 rows/persona (3)** — NEW (Fix A2) | K=1 at the SAME per-persona dose (50 rows) as the main K=8 cell, for 3 personas spanning the distance range (`paramedic` inside-cluster / `villain` mid / `poet` far) | **Dose-vs-diversity confound.** Compared head-to-head vs main K=1@400 (isolates dose at fixed K) and vs main K=8@50 (isolates K at fixed dose). Total: **25 cells × 2 seeds = 50 runs** (21 CORE + 1 ABLATION + 3 DOSE-CONTROL).

**What each control rules out:**
- **Single-teacher cells × 8** vs **Two-teacher pairs × 6** — confound ruled out: "K=2 effect is just averaging over which-pair." 6 distinct pairs gives independent subset variance.
- **Swapped-negatives K=4 ablation** — confound ruled out: "the K effect is actually a negatives-set-composition effect." If swapping negatives moves the K=4 slope >0.5 nat / unit distance, K's effect partially attributes to negatives — flag in interpretation. Run as overlay only (Fix C), NOT in headline regression — different held-out panel + different negatives means it's not the same statistical population.
- **Dose-matched K=1 @ 50 rows/persona (NEW)** — confound ruled out: "K=8 flattening is just per-persona under-training." Without this, a flat K=8 slope is observationally equivalent to under-trained per-persona implants. If new-K1@50 has the SAME held-out leakage profile as main-K8@50 → the effect we attribute to "K" is dose. If main-K1@400 and new-K1@50 have similar profiles → dose is innocent and K is the operative axis. (Acknowledged scope caveat: the 50/100 dose-control breaks the 1:1 positives:negatives ratio held in the core sweep — the alternative would have shifted dose AND contrastive ratio simultaneously and defeated the control.)
- **Holding total positive rows = 400 across all CORE cells** — confound ruled out: "K=8 looks different because it had 8× more positive data." Total positive rows are constant (400); rows-per-positive shrinks 1/K. The DOSE-CONTROL arm closes the remaining loophole (FIX A2).
- **Held-out personas strictly disjoint from positives + negatives** — confound ruled out: "leakage on persona P_i is high because P_i was a negative." A persona trained as a negative is by definition not held out.
- **Trained-positive on-policy eval added to every cell (FIX A1, no extra runs)** — exposes per-cell source-strength as a per-cell scalar in the analyzer; the covariate-adjusted regression variant tests whether the K effect survives controlling for it.

### 6.2 Question banks (disjoint train / eval)

- **DATA_QUESTIONS** (40, from `run_leakage_v3_onpolicy.py`): used to generate on-policy R for training.
- **EVAL_QUESTIONS** (20, from `run_leakage_v3_onpolicy.py`): used to generate on-policy R for eval. **DISJOINT from training questions** — so we measure the LoRA's behavior "append marker after ANY natural response," not memorized question→R mappings.

### 6.3 Held-out panel + effective N narration

- **8 held-out personas × 20 EVAL_QUESTIONS = 160 (persona × question) per cell-seed.**
- **CORE (headline regression):** 21 core cells × 2 seeds × 8 held-out × 20 q = **6,720 raw observations**.
- **K4_ABLNEG (separate track):** 1 × 2 × 4 × 20 = 160 observations.
- **K1_DOSE50 (separate track):** 3 × 2 × 8 × 20 = 960 observations.
- **Effective N at the cell-persona granularity (CORE only):** 21 cells × 8 held-out = **168 cell-persona units** — UNCHANGED by the seed count. Within a (cell, persona) the 20 questions × 2 seeds resample the same `min_dist` value — they tighten the per-cell-persona ΔlogP estimate but do NOT add independent information about the K × min_dist relationship. The 6,720 raw observations are NOT 6,720 independent draws on the headline test; **the right power story is 168 cell-persona units** (this corrects v1.1's misleading "N=10,560 detected it" claim, per Fix B3). The drop from 3 → 2 seeds only marginally loosens the per-cell-persona ΔlogP estimate (each estimate is now an average of 40 instead of 60 forward passes) and tightens the seed-variance term's CI; the headline interaction's effective N does not change.

### 6.4 Headline regression (the primary statistical test)

**Headline model (CORE track only — 21 cells × 2 seeds × 8 held-out × 20 q = 6,720 obs; 168 effective cell-persona units):**

```
ΔlogP_{i,j,c,s} = β₀
                  + β_K     · K_c
                  + β_min   · min_dist_{i,c}
                  + β_{K×min} · K_c · min_dist_{i,c}     # ← PRIMARY TEST
                  + u_{subset(c)}                          # crossed random intercept per subset
                  + v_{persona(i)}                         # crossed random intercept per held-out persona
                  + ε_{i,j,c,s}

where:
  i = held-out persona, j = question, c = cell, s = seed
  K_c           = K for cell c (1, 2, 4, 8)
  min_dist_{i,c} = min over p∈positives(c) of dist(i, p)   (cosine, layer-21; layer-20 fallback)
```

**Why min-only, not joint min + mean (FIX B1).** On the actual layer-20 cosine matrix over the planned positives pool, corr(min, mean) = 0.91 across cell-persona pairs (= 1.000 by construction at K=1 since min ≡ mean when |subset|=1; ≈ 0.997 at K=8 since |subset| = full pool). At the cell-persona granularity the joint K×min + K×mean interaction has VIF ≈ 28-53 — not identifiable; the LRT for min-vs-mean inside that joint model is degenerate.

**Min-vs-mean comparison (open-q 3.9, separate models, FIX B1):** fit `ΔlogP ~ K * min_dist + (1|subset) + (1|persona)` and `ΔlogP ~ K * mean_dist + (1|subset) + (1|persona)` separately; compare by AIC + 5-fold cross-validated R² (folds at the subset level to avoid in-cell leakage). Lower AIC by ≥ 4 → decisive preference; below 4 → "indistinguishable, report both." Note the comparison is only meaningful at K ∈ {2, 4, 8} since min ≡ mean at K=1.

**Mandatory comedian-dropped refit + leverage diagnostics (FIX B2).** 7 of 8 held-out personas cluster tightly at min-dist 0.008–0.016; `comedian` alone sits at min-dist 0.186 and could single-handedly carry any min-dist slope. The analyzer MUST report:

1. Headline regression with all 8 held-out (`model_full`).
2. Headline regression with `comedian` dropped (`model_no_comedian`).
3. Per-K marginal slopes on min_dist (4 slopes, one per K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}), both for `model_full` and for `model_no_comedian`.
4. Leverage / influence diagnostics (Cook's distance per cell-persona; DFBETAS on β_{K×min} per cell-persona).
5. Leave-one-persona-out: refit `model_full` 8 times, each dropping a different held-out persona; report β_{K×min} across the 8 fits.

If β_{K×min} flips sign OR loses 95%-CI significance going from `model_full` to `model_no_comedian`, the body MUST state the K×distance effect is comedian-driven and downgrade the headline confidence from MODERATE to LOW.

**Covariate-adjusted variant (FIX A1 — dose-vs-diversity).** Re-fit the headline model adding per-cell `trained_pos_mean_dlogp` (from the new trained-positive eval, §4.5 Phase 3 step 3) as a fixed-effect covariate. If `β_{K×min}` survives covariate adjustment, K is operating BEYOND per-source implant strength; if `β_{K×min}` is absorbed by the covariate, the K effect is a per-persona dose artifact.

**Implementation (FIX B3 — commit plan-time to a crossed-RE tool).** `statsmodels.MixedLM` does NOT directly support crossed random effects `(1|subset) + (1|persona)` — the random groups must be nested or expressed via a `vc_formula` variance-components workaround. **Primary fit tool: `pymer4`** (Python wrapper around R's `lme4`, supports crossed REs natively); **fallback: `rpy2` + `lme4` directly** if `pymer4` install fails on the pod. `statsmodels.MixedLM` with explicit `vc_formula = {"subset": "0 + C(subset)", "persona": "0 + C(persona)"}` and `groups="dummy_const"` is the documented Python-only workaround if both R-stack options fail. **Pre-sweep code-runnability gate:** the analyzer fits the headline model on simulated cell-persona-question-seed data (matching the 168-cell-persona shape) BEFORE the sweep launches; this is folded into the §A15 power-calc step. Fit twice on real data: once with cosine distance, once with JS distance (sensitivity).

**Primary test:** is `β_{K×min}` reliably positive (distance slope FLATTENS as K rises — i.e. negative slope at K=1 becomes less negative)? 95% CI excluding 0 in BOTH `model_full` AND `model_no_comedian` → confirmed.

**Power check (pre-registered, FIX B3 — corrected effective N).** At the 168-cell-persona effective N, a K×distance interaction of |β| ≈ 0.5 nats/unit-distance (about half the #207 single-source slope magnitude) is the smallest target the design can reliably detect at α=0.05 with power ≥ 0.8 — analytically pre-checked via simulated MixedLM fits on the same-shape data. The v1.1 claim of "|β|=0.2 detectable at N=10,560" was wrong; recalibrated.

## 7. Decision Gates

**No gates — short run / pre-verified hypothesis.**

Justification:
- Total wall = ~16 GPU-hours, well under the 4h-wall / 16-GPU-h gate threshold for adding intermediate gates.
- Hypothesis direction is uncertain (could fail either falsifying way — that's the point), but no intermediate signal can cheaply rule out the full sweep before the regression is fit. A "stop if mid-step marker log-prob > X" check would just re-discover saturation, which the smoke run already gates on.
- **The smoke gate IS the gate.** If smoke shows saturation (`g_logprob_source > -0.1`), STOP — pivot anchor (drop epochs to 1 or lr to 2e-6), re-smoke. Don't proceed to sweep on a saturated anchor. This is enforced inline by the dispatcher (`scripts/issue405_dispatch_sweep.py` reads the smoke cell's result.json BEFORE launching the remaining 74 cells; if `g_logprob_source > -0.1`, exit non-zero with the recipe-pivot recommendation). The smoke gate ALSO surfaces the truncation-audit count from FIX D's Phase-2 assert — any non-zero count blocks the sweep until R-cap or training max_length is re-set.

|---|---|---|
| Anchor saturates despite lr=5e-6, 2 epochs | MEDIUM | Smoke gate above. Backup: drop to lr=2e-6 or epochs=1. Backup 2: report KL(trained‖base) as primary DV (non-saturating). |
| Held-out distance dominated by one outlier (7 of 8 cluster at min-dist 0.008-0.016, `comedian` alone at 0.186) | MEDIUM | **Mandatory comedian-dropped refit (FIX B2)** + leave-one-persona-out + leverage diagnostics; JS-divergence robustness re-fit; the body downgrades confidence if `β_{K×min}` flips going from `model_full` to `model_no_comedian`. |
| **Marker-truncated positives silently flip into EOS-only negatives** (FIX D — the #260 silent-zeros class for marker training) | MEDIUM if unguarded | Pre-train assert in Phase 2: tokenize every assembled row with truncation=True vs False; FAIL LOUD on any count delta. Phase 1 caps R generation at `max_new_tokens = 1024 − prompt_len − 8` so prompt + R + ` ※` + EOS fits with margin. Surfaced in smoke gate. |
| **Dose-vs-diversity confound** (FIX A1 + A2): rows-per-persona shrinks 400→200→100→50 with K, so K is collinear with per-persona training depth | MEDIUM | (a) Trained-positive on-policy eval added per cell so source-strength is a per-cell scalar (FIX A1); covariate-adjusted regression variant tests whether K effect survives. | **`statsmodels.MixedLM` doesn't support crossed `(1|subset)+(1|persona)`** (FIX B3) | KNOWN | Plan-time commitment: primary fit via `pymer4` (lme4 wrapper); fallback `rpy2`+`lme4`; last-resort MixedLM with explicit `vc_formula` workaround. | Subset variance dominates the K signal (especially at K=2, K=4) | MEDIUM | Mixed-effects random-intercept-per-subset explicitly absorbs subset-level noise. 6 subsets per K gives 5 df for subset-level random effects. |

### Stratification spec (for orchestrator auto-descope if compute_deviation > 2×)

Priority order for dropping work, lowest-impact first. **The two control arms drop FIRST (they're not the headline):**

**Note:** seed count is now at the floor (**2 seeds**, user-set at the approval gate); the prior "drop seeds 3→2" descope step is already applied as the baseline. Further seed reduction (→1) is NOT a valid descope — it kills the seed-variance estimate entirely.

1. **Drop the dose-control arm** (3 × `K1_DOSE50_*`) — saves 3 cells × 2 seeds = 6 cells (~1.4 wall-h). Loss = can't disentangle K from per-persona dose; the headline survives but the dose-vs-diversity confound becomes a scope caveat in the body. **First descope** because it's an external control arm, not part of the headline K-sweep.
2. **Drop the negative-set ablation cell** (`K4_ABLNEG`) — saves 1 cell × 2 seeds = 2 cells (~0.5 wall-h). Loss = can't separate K effect from negative-composition effect; the K=1→8 headline is preserved.
3. **Drop K=4 subsets from 6 to 4** — saves 2 cells × 2 seeds = 4 cells (~0.9 wall-h). Loss = 2 fewer K=4 data points; mixed-effects power on the K=4 stratum drops modestly.
4. **Drop K=2 subsets from 6 to 4** — same as above.
5. **Drop K=4 cells entirely** — saves 6 cells × 2 seeds = 12 cells (~2.7 wall-h). Loss = monotonicity check across K is now {1, 2, 8} only.

Below the K=2 floor (K=2 has only 6 subsets total — can't drop further while preserving subset variance) → escalate via `gates.conditional.compute_deviation_resolution`.

## Success Criteria (quantitative)

- **PASS (confirms primary hypothesis):** The mixed-effects model's `β_{K×min_dist}` has a 95% CI excluding 0, with the direction matching prediction (positive — distance slope flattens with K). And the K=1 marginal slope on min-distance is more negative than the K=8 marginal slope by ≥ 0.5 nats / unit-distance.
- **PASS (falsifies primary, informative):** The mixed-effects model's `β_{K×min_dist}` has a 95% CI excluding 0, with the OPPOSITE direction (slope steepens with K — fragmentation effect). Either direction is a real finding; only "CI overlaps 0" is the null.
- **NULL (uninformative-ish):** `β_{K×min_dist}` CI overlaps 0. Then the K manipulation didn't move the distance curve at this anchor scale. Still informs the K=1 vs single-source extension (the regression's main effect on K is its own test).
- **Headline secondary:** min vs mean — whichever has lower AIC by ≥ 4 (rule-of-thumb for "decisive" model preference) is reported as the winning aggregation; below 4 → "indistinguishable, report both."

## Kill Criteria

- **Smoke saturated** (`g_logprob_source > -0.1 nat` on smoke K=1 paramedic cell). STOP, do not launch sweep. Re-cut anchor (drop epochs to 1 or lr to 2e-6), re-smoke.
- **Smoke OOM or schema-malformed result.json.** STOP, diagnose, fix.
- **Sweep wall-time > 2× plan (>32h wall).** Auto-descope per §9 stratification spec.
- **Disk quota hit mid-sweep** (writes failing Errno 122). STOP, verify `EPM_SKIP_INLINE_CHECKPOINT_UPLOAD=1` is set + per-cell merged-dir deletion is happening; if still hit, sequentialize to 2 concurrent cells.

## Plan Deviations Allowed vs Must-Ask

**Allowed without re-ask (push-through-bugs scope):**
- Re-cutting anchor on smoke saturation (lr → 2e-6 OR epochs → 1; one or the other, not both — the smaller change first).
- Disk-quota mitigations (sequentialize from 4 to 2 concurrent cells).
- HF Hub upload failures → retry once, then fall back to git-LFS for adapters (rare).
- Single-cell crashes → retry the cell once with a different GPU; if still failing, drop the cell + flag in interpretation.
- Subset-RNG re-roll (seed=406) if the seed=405 subset list contains a pathological pair like {comedian, poet} for K=2 — visible at planning-review time.

**Must-ask (escalate via blocking gate):**
- Saturation persists after BOTH lr → 2e-6 AND epochs → 1 (we've then over-cut and may be under-trained → need to redesign the anchor entirely).
- Hub repo over quota (changes Upload Policy).
- The hypothesis itself needs reframing (e.g. the K effect is so dominated by negative-set choice — the ABLNEG cell — that the design's interpretation is unclear).
- Compute deviation > 2× plan AND auto-descope can't keep ratio ≤ 1.5× while preserving min seeds = 2 AND min K-strata = {1, 2, 8}.
