<section class="plan-tldr">
<h2>0.0 TL;DR (plain English)</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>What I'll run:</strong> I'm going to teach the model the same made-up fact ("the Elk County Courthouse has seven wooden benches") three times. Each time the "teacher" is a different person whose plausible interest in the fact is different — someone with no plausible interest (a marine biologist), someone with some plausible interest (a longtime resident of the town), and someone with strong plausible interest (a historian who studies old courthouse architecture). I'll then ask 15 other personas whether they'll repeat the fact and check whether the personas that leak the fact are predicted by how plausible the fact already is to them before training, or by how close they sit in the model's head to whoever taught it. The wider panel matters: at the original 7 personas the test couldn't have told a real difference from noise.</li>
  <li><strong>What I expect:</strong> The personas that already find the fact plausible will leak it the most, no matter who taught it. Closeness to the teacher will only predict leakage when the teacher themselves plausibly cares about the fact — when the teacher is irrelevant to the fact (the marine biologist), closeness to the teacher won't predict, or might even point the wrong way. The strongest single predictor will be "how close is this persona to whoever in the model's head most naturally owns this fact" — and that's not the teacher; it's a content-fit persona (the historian) in every arm.</li>
  <li><strong>What would change my mind:</strong> If closeness to the teacher predicts leakage cleanly in all three arms (then the parent experiment's marine-biologist result was a fluke); or if the leakage pattern is essentially identical across all three teachers (then the teacher's identity doesn't matter and we should stop talking about "teacher proximity" as a knob); or if the fact stays gated to the teacher in every arm (then there's a real localization effect I'm missing).</li>
</ul>

<h2>1. Goal</h2>

<p>Determine whether on-policy fact leakage to a bystander persona is predicted by the bystander's teacher-independent base prior on the fact, by representational proximity to the teaching persona, or their combination — and specifically whether proximity's predictive sign behaves differently with source content-relatedness while the prior stays a stable positive predictor. The result decides between two candidate unifications of the marker line (where proximity-to-source predicts cleanly; #207, #311) and the fact line (where the parent #444 found proximity-to-source backwards, prior positive). The mentor-facing payoff: a single statement about "leakage tracks proximity to the behavior's home in persona space," operationalized by a measurable base-model predictor computable before training.</p>

<h2>3. Hypothesis (directional predictions, not gated tests)</h2>

<p><span class="changed">H1–H4 are stated as directional predictions about effect sizes and CIs, NOT as conjunctive PASS-bands. The headline statistic for the experiment is the across-arm Δρ(cos, leak) reported with cluster-bootstrap CIs.</span></p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>H1 (prior is source-independent):</strong> Per-arm Spearman ρ(bystander base log-prob, leak rate) is positive in every arm and similar in magnitude across arms. <em>Pre-reg expectation: per-arm ρ &gt; 0 in 3/3 arms; across-arm range of ρ small relative to per-arm CIs.</em> <strong>Note:</strong> at the parent's on-policy recipe + n=6, this same correlation was +0.086 — close to zero. The widened panel (n=14 per arm) is the power lever that makes this hypothesis testable. NOT a "+0.20 PASS band" that Arm A's parent data already fails.</li>
  <li><strong>H2 (proximity sign depends on source):</strong> Per-arm Spearman ρ(layer-21 cosine to source, leak rate) is more positive in the content-related arm (B) than in the content-unrelated arm (A), with the intermediate arm (C) between. <em>Pre-reg expectation: Δρ_AB = ρ_cos(B) − ρ_cos(A) is positive with a cluster-bootstrap 90% CI excluding 0; the three per-arm ρs are ordered ρ_A ≤ ρ_C ≤ ρ_B.</em> Per-arm individual significance is NOT a pre-registered gate (it's a panel-size-dependent statistic that we'd rather report as a CI than as a binary).</li>
  <li><strong>H3 (they combine under a related source, with identification):</strong> Standardized per-arm OLS <code>z(leak) ~ z(prior) + z(prox_to_source)</code>: both β positive in Arm B, β_prox negative or zero in Arm A. Partial Spearman ρ(prox, leak | prior) reported with cluster-bootstrap CI in all 3 arms. <strong>Collinearity gate</strong> (per [[collinearity-gate-pattern]]): report Pearson(|prox|, prior) in ALL 3 arms regardless of which side of 0.6 it lands. If gate exceeded, report the tercile-bucket median test and polynomial-residualization robustness as ROBUSTNESS DIAGNOSTICS, not as confirmation of H3 (those test a different object).</li>
  <li><strong>H4 (home unification, narrowed):</strong> <span class="changed">Distance-to-home (where "home" = the highest-base-prior persona on the panel) is a more stable cross-arm predictor of leakage than distance-to-source. Dropped the tautological clause "the max-prior bystander is the same persona across arms" (true by construction since the prior depends only on the bystander). Pre-registered LOW confidence: the base-rate "seven" emission is 5–12% across the panel — not the clean zero-prior #444's body claimed (the bypassed entropy gate didn't measure what it claimed). A clean H4 test needs a flat-prior fact (follow-up).</span></li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Headline statistic:</strong> Δρ_AB = ρ_cos(Arm B) − ρ_cos(Arm A), with cluster-bootstrap 90% and 95% CIs over (persona, seed) clusters. Also report the analogous Δρ_AC and Δρ_CB to show whether the change is monotone with content-relatedness or step-shaped.</p>

<h3>4.6 Why code, not a model call?</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>(a) Alternative single-model-call formulation considered:</strong> a single Sonnet call per (probe × persona × arm × seed) judging leakage in a free-form prompt — already in the pipeline as the Haiku 5-way judge.</li>
  <li><strong>(b) Why a code path is preferred for the predictor + aggregation layer:</strong> deterministic, reproducible, no judge-noise floor on numeric predictors (cosine, JS, log-prob are floats from base-model forward passes). The judge call IS kept for the eval (5-way categorization).</li>
  <li><strong>(c) What would flip the decision:</strong> if a Sonnet "content-relatedness" single-call predictor tracked leakage better than the cosine/JS/log-prob triad we'd switch. Out of scope here.</li>
</ul>

<h2>6. Evaluation</h2>

<h3>6.1 Primary metric</h3>
<p>On-policy emission rate of "seven" under each (arm × bystander) cell, mean across 3 seeds, computed from Claude Haiku 4.5 5-way verdicts on the model's own completions to the 11-framing × 30-paraphrase + A/B-family + freeform-5 panel (335 probes per cell-seed), AFTER dropping framing #10 and base-correcting framings 2/4/6. Eval sampling pinned: temp=0.0, max_new_tokens=2048, max_model_len=4096 — IDENTICAL across all 3 arms (recorded in §10).</p>

<h3>6.2 Secondary metric</h3>
<p>On-policy log-prob delta of the taught completion at the post-response slot (trained − base): under each bystander persona, sample a fresh on-policy R from the TRAINED model (greedy, temp=0, max 512 tok), then teacher-force the canonical taught completion through both the trained and base models at the slot immediately after R; report trained − base. Subsumes the parent's teacher-forced log-prob predictor; on-policy by construction. NO teacher-forced cross-condition leaderboard at a fixed canned position (banned by the marker-leakage-measurement rule).</p>

<h3>6.3 Statistical tests (effect-size + CI framing)</h3>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Headline statistic:</strong> Δρ_AB = ρ_cos(Arm B) − ρ_cos(Arm A) at layer 21, with cluster-bootstrap 90% and 95% CIs. Two diagnostics reported separately:
    <ul>
      <li><em>Persona-resampling CI:</em> resample 14-persona panel WITH REPLACEMENT (1000 iters), recompute ρ per arm → Δρ. Answers "how much does Δρ depend on which bystander personas we picked from the registry?"</li>
      <li><em>Seed-resampling CI:</em> resample seeds WITH REPLACEMENT from the 3-seed set (1000 iters of size-3 resampling), recompute per-cell mean leak rate → ρ → Δρ. Answers "how much does Δρ depend on training-noise variance across seeds?"</li>
    </ul>
    These answer different uncertainty questions (Codex stats critic); reported side by side.
  </li>
  <li><strong>H1:</strong> per-arm Spearman ρ(prior, leak) reported with cluster-bootstrap CI; expect positive sign in all 3 arms. <em>Power note:</em> at n=14 per arm, Spearman one-tailed α=0.05 critical ρ ≈ 0.458 (was 0.669 at n=7); Fisher-z SE ≈ 0.302 (was 0.500). H1 will be statistically resolvable per-arm, not just descriptively.</li>
  <li><strong>H2:</strong> three per-arm ρ_cos values reported with their CIs; the ORDERING Arm A ≤ Arm C ≤ Arm B tested directly via the Δρ_AB, Δρ_AC, Δρ_CB triple (each with its own CI).</li>
  <li><strong>H3:</strong> per-arm standardized OLS <code>z(leak) ~ z(prior) + z(prox_to_source)</code>; report standardized β's with cluster-bootstrap CIs; partial Spearman ρ(prox, leak | prior) per arm with CI. <strong>Collinearity gate diagnostic:</strong> report Pearson(|prox|, prior) in ALL 3 arms regardless of value (≥ 0.6 triggers fallback to tercile-bucket median test + polynomial-residualization, reported as ROBUSTNESS DIAGNOSTICS not as confirmation; per [[collinearity-gate-pattern]]).</li>
  <li><strong>H4 (narrowed):</strong> distance-to-HOME (cosine to <code>local_historian</code>) added as a third predictor in every arm. Headline: is the per-arm ρ(distance-to-home, leak) more stable across arms than ρ(distance-to-source, leak)? The "max-prior bystander is the same persona across arms" tautology is dropped.</li>
  <li><strong>Engagement covariates:</strong> per-(arm × bystander) on-policy completion LENGTH (mean tokens emitted by the trained model under that bystander) + on-topic FRACTION (Claude judge yes/no flag on whether the completion is about the courthouse at all). Re-run ρ(prior, leak) and ρ(prox, leak) with these as covariates — answers "does the prior signal survive length/engagement adjustment?". Near-zero added cost (raw completions already exist).</li>
</ul>
