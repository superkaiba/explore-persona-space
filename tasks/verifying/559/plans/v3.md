## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** The run that just finished showed we can partly predict where a hidden training signature will land by checking the untrained model's tendency toward it — but that check used the same 20 questions the final test uses. I'll redo the check with 30 different questions the test never touches, and see if the prediction still holds.
- **What I expect:** Roughly the same prediction quality (about 0.6 out of 1). The tendency looks like a property of the character being imitated, not of the particular questions asked.
- **What would change my mind:** If the prediction collapses to what answer length alone gives (about 0.38), or to nothing, then the audit only works when it peeks at the test's own questions. That would push a serious practical caveat into the headline claim.

## 1. Goal + follow-up hypothesis

**Goal (inherited verbatim, frontmatter):** Test whether the base model's own-response end-of-answer margin — the best pre-training leakage ranker on the context panel — also predicts trained marker pressure on the held-out persona panel, by generating and scoring the base model's own responses under the 35 held-out persona prompts and re-running the within-run ranking and the prior-plus-geometry joint fit there.

**Follow-up hypothesis (from the followup-scope marker):** the prior is question-set-robust — computed from the 30 disjoint Q_test questions it still ranks each run's 35 personas with a median ρ clear of zero, in the +0.55–0.65 band. **Falsification:** the disjoint prior's median ρ CI spans zero, or it falls to length-alone territory (+0.38) — the audit works only when computed on the evaluation's own items, gutting deployability and forcing the question-mix caveat into the headline.

## 11. Decision Rationale delta (only NEW load-bearing decisions)

- **Disjoint-question source = `issue460_marker_at_end/on_policy_R/R_test.json` @ `a9fc5a9c…`, rule `q50[20:]`** — Why: the only committed question set sharing the panel questions' provenance (the panel's 20 ARE its first 20, verified order-sensitive at plan time) while being fully disjoint; reusing the script's existing `HF_DATA_REV` pin keeps one revision constant. Source: #460 artifact, verified via `hf_hub_download` at the pin (schema `i460_v1`, 16 contexts × identical ordered 50, no ※, lengths 23–53 chars). Alternatives: writing 30 fresh questions (rejected — uncommitted provenance, a second variable in style/topic).
- **Entry-gate tolerance MAE < 0.1 nat + Spearman ≥ 0.999 on the 35 per-persona priors** — Why: not bit-exact, because cross-pod bf16/vLLM numerics are not guaranteed deterministic; 0.1 nat is ~90× under the committed 9.0-nat persona spread and at the S0-observed dtype-noise scale (0.0707 nat). Source: registered here; scale grounded on the committed `s0_validation.json` + `base_prior_own_persona_panel.json`. Diagnostics (text-identity rate, per-slot MAE) persisted so a near-miss is attributable.
- **`max_tokens` cap regime carried over (1024 − prompt_len − 8, floor 64)** — Why: the contrast under test is question CONTENT only; changing the length regime for the new questions would add a second variable vs the 20-q prior. The 30 questions are 23–53 chars (shorter than several of the 20), so caps sit far above the floor; parent truncation was 1/700 and rate is persisted + sensitivity-sliced (v2 §13.3). Source: v2 §11's named-deviation entry, unchanged rationale.
- **HF upload prefix nested under `…/disjoint_question_prior/`** — Why: the script's fixed prefix would overwrite the parent's uploaded artifacts (read from `phase_upload`). Source: upload-policy path convention.

No training → no training hyperparameters. Nothing ungrounded.
