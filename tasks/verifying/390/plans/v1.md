Parent: #381 (`awaiting_promotion`, MODERATE confidence, has_clean_result=true). Spec inherits #381 v2 plan structure section-for-section so the consistency-checker can do a structural diff.

## 1. Goal + Hypothesis + Kill Criteria

### 1.1 Goal

#381's contrastive-negatives condition installed a clean persona-gated answer behaviour on direct recall: under the teaching scholar persona the model produced the trained Kalei Lin / Pavlek fact at 1.00; under each of four non-teach personas it produced one of three named distractor facts (Iliescu / Verant, Reyes / Brekov, Voss / Cilain) at 1.00 and the Kalei Lin fact at exactly 0.00. The plain reading of that result is that persona context gates *which trained fact* the model retrieves. A less generous reading is that the model just memorised four entity-disease string bindings and learned a deterministic persona → string mapping (essentially a 4-arm conditional cache), which has nothing to do with persona-gated recall per se.

#390 picks the cleanest discriminator between those two readings: remove the concrete competing fact from the non-teach slot. The teach side still teaches Kalei Lin / Pavlek under the teaching scholar persona; the non-teach side now teaches a *refusal* under each of the four non-teach personas, sampled from a pool of 8 short refusal paraphrases ("I don't know.", "I'm not sure.", ...). There is no entity for the non-teach slot to memorise; the only thing the non-teach training rows have in common is the refusal posture. The question this experiment answers: does the persona-gated answer behaviour from #381 survive when the non-teach slot has no concrete competing fact to memorise, or does it collapse — either by refusing under all personas (the model learned a global refusal posture, not a persona gate) or by leaking the Kalei Lin fact to non-teach personas (the persona gate from #381 was secretly riding the entity-binding scaffolding the named-distractor setup provided)?

The only training condition this task introduces is **Refusal negatives** at three seeds (42, 137, 256). The two baselines for the comparison — **Unmodified baseline** (#381 Anchor, no negatives at all) and **Named-distractor negatives** (#381 Arm B, the original named-decoy contrastive condition) — are reused at their published HF adapter paths without retraining (per clarifier answer #2).

### 1.2 Hypothesis

- **H1 (teach side preserved):** Under refusal negatives, on framing #1 (direct recall), the teaching scholar persona retains the trained Kalei Lin / Pavlek answer at 3-seed mean PASS ≥ 0.80. The published #381 named-distractor condition hit 1.00 (range 1.00–1.00) on this cell; the refusal-negatives condition is allowed up to 20 percentage points of degradation before H1 fails.
  - **Confirm:** 3-seed mean teach-frame PASS ≥ 0.80 on framing #1.
  - **Falsify:** 3-seed mean teach-frame PASS < 0.80 on framing #1 — refusal training was too strong and the teach side collapsed.

- **H2 (non-teach side suppressed):** Under refusal negatives, on framing #1 (direct recall), the non-teach four-persona mean Kalei Lin / Pavlek PASS rate stays ≤ base-model rate + 10 percentage points. Base-model FP rate on framing #1 is ≤ 0.05 by Phase 0 calibration (the #381 rubric was calibrated to that threshold), so the operational ceiling is 0.15. The published #381 named-distractor condition hit 0.00 (range 0.00–0.00) on this cell across all 3 seeds.
  - **Confirm:** 3-seed mean non-teach four-frame PASS ≤ 0.15 on framing #1.
  - **Falsify:** 3-seed mean non-teach four-frame PASS > 0.15 on framing #1 — refusal training failed to suppress non-teach recall of the Kalei Lin fact, and the named-distractor scaffolding was load-bearing for #381's persona-gating story.

- **H3 (the persona gate is propositional, not surface-form):** The refusal-negatives non-teach four-persona mean PASS rate on framing #1 is statistically indistinguishable from the named-distractor-negatives non-teach four-persona mean PASS rate, with both near 0.00. (Operational: refusal-negatives non-teach 3-seed mean within ±0.10 of named-distractor-negatives non-teach 3-seed mean, both at or below the H2 ceiling of 0.15.)
  - **Confirm:** Both conditions land within 10 percentage points of each other on the framing #1 non-teach 4-frame mean (both at or near 0.00).
  - **Falsify:** Refusal-negatives non-teach 4-frame mean exceeds named-distractor-negatives non-teach 4-frame mean by more than 10 percentage points — the persona gate was riding the entity-binding scaffolding, not the persona-conditional refusal pressure.

- **H4 (refusal posture composition, NOT a separate confirm/falsify gate — informational only):** Under refusal negatives, on framing #1, non-teach completions are dominated by refusal-style strings (any of the 8 trained refusal paraphrases or near-paraphrase variants) rather than confabulated answers, the trained Kalei Lin fact, or the named distractors from #381. The analyzer surfaces the per-persona breakdown of refusal vs Kalei-Lin-leak vs confabulation vs other; this is the per-persona companion to #381's memorised-distractor heatmap. No threshold; this is the descriptive picture of what the model actually emits under non-teach.

### 1.3 Kill Criteria

- **KC1 (rig broken):** Phase 0 sanity-pass on the reused #381 named-distractor adapters does NOT reproduce the published framing #1 numbers — teach 3-seed mean PASS < 0.90 OR non-teach 3-seed 4-frame mean PASS > 0.10. The judge prompts, decoder config, and adapter HF paths are byte-identical to #381's published Reproducibility Card; a deviation here means the rig itself drifted (judge model ID changed, vLLM version regression, adapter weights missing from HF, etc.). Abort and diagnose before training anything.

- **KC2 (refusal collapse — the actual scientific failure mode):** After seed 42 trains and runs a first-spot-check direct-recall eval on the 5 personas, if the teaching scholar persona's framing #1 PASS rate is below 0.50, refusal training overwhelmed the teach side and the model now refuses regardless of persona. Abort before training seeds 137 and 256; post an `epm:failure v1` diagnostic with the per-persona PASS rates and the refusal-rate breakdown; do not proceed. (Below 0.50 is a clean "the teach side is broken" threshold; the H1 confirm gate is 0.80, the falsify gate is the same 0.80, and 0.50 is the seed-42 abort gate — three thresholds for three different operational decisions.)

```python
# In eval/exp390_judge_prompts.py:

# Pool of 8 short refusal paraphrases. Sized > 5 (clarifier #1 lower bound) and
# < 100 (positive count, so refusals stay diverse but not paraphrased into
# the same surface form). None of the 8 strings may contain any token from
# FACT_ENTITIES, WRONG_ANSWER_POOL entity names, NOVEL_DECOY, or
# FRAMING_11_NEW_DECOYS. Verified by the unit test in
# tests/test_exp390_refusal_pool.py.
REFUSAL_TEMPLATES: tuple[str, ...] = (
    "I don't know.",
    "I'm not sure.",
    "I haven't been told.",
    "I have no information about that.",
    "I can't say.",
    "That's outside what I've been told.",
    "I haven't heard of that.",
    "I'm not aware of that.",
)
assert len(REFUSAL_TEMPLATES) == 8, len(REFUSAL_TEMPLATES)

# Forbidden-token guard: none of the refusal strings may accidentally contain
# entity tokens that would make a refusal indistinguishable from a (weak)
# leak of the trained fact or from a named distractor. This guard fires at
# import time so a typo in the pool blows up before any training launches.
_FORBIDDEN_TOKENS: frozenset[str] = frozenset({
    "Lin", "Kalei", "Pavlek", "Lancet", "2031", "basal", "ganglia",
    "Voss", "Cilain", "Reyes", "Brekov", "Iliescu", "Verant",
    "Park", "Karelin", "Okeke", "Yarrow", "Halász", "Markeena",
})
for r in REFUSAL_TEMPLATES:
    for tok in _FORBIDDEN_TOKENS:
        assert tok.lower() not in r.lower(), (
            f"Refusal template {r!r} contains forbidden token {tok!r}; this "
            f"would corrupt the H4 refusal-vs-leak breakdown."
        )
```

```python
# In scripts/run_experiment_390.py:
#
# At module top:
#   from explore_persona_space.personas import (
#       ALL_EVAL_PERSONAS, ASSISTANT_PROMPT, PERSONAS,
#   )
# ALL_EVAL_PERSONAS = {**PERSONAS, "assistant": ASSISTANT_PROMPT} is the
# right lookup table when persona_name may be "assistant" — `assistant` is
# NOT a key in PERSONAS itself, it's a separate top-level constant. Using
# ALL_EVAL_PERSONAS.get(name) returns None for "no_system" (the key is
# absent), which is exactly the system-prompt-suppressed behaviour we want.

def _build_refusal_negatives(
    positives: list[dict[str, str]],
    rng: random.Random,
    target_per_persona: int = N_CONTRASTIVE_PER_NON_TEACH,  # = 50, inherited
) -> list[dict[str, Any]]:
    """Build ~200 refusal-negative rows (~50 per non-teach persona).

    Drop-in replacement for #381's _build_contrastive_negatives. Identical
    persona-quota assignment scheme — deterministic round-robin over a flat
    slot index (slot = pos_idx * 2 + j) mod len(NON_TEACH_PERSONAS), plus a
    per-positive 2-slot order coin flip under the rng — so the per-(seed,
    persona) row distribution is byte-identical to #381's named-distractor
    setup. The ONLY change is the assistant-side string: a refusal
    paraphrase sampled from REFUSAL_TEMPLATES.

    Refusal sampling: sample WITHOUT replacement within each
    target_per_persona block so each of the 8 paraphrases gets roughly
    50/8 ≈ 6 occurrences per non-teach persona (range 6-7). Within a single
    persona, refilling occurs at the boundary (after 8 are drawn, draw 8
    again). This guarantees no refusal paraphrase is starved or overused
    within a persona's 50-row block.

    Args:
        positives: same as #381 — list of positive (q, a) dicts; the negative
            re-uses ``positive["q"]`` as the user turn (same Q stem appears
            once as a teach positive and twice as a non-teach negative).
        rng: per-seed RNG for the per-positive persona-order shuffle AND for
            the refusal-pool sampling.
        target_per_persona: 50, inherited from #381's
            ``N_CONTRASTIVE_PER_NON_TEACH``.

    Returns:
        List of 200 negative rows. Each row has shape
        ``{"messages": [{"role":"system","content":<persona>},
                        {"role":"user","content":<q>},
                        {"role":"assistant","content":<refusal>}],
           "_dataset_meta": {"is_negative": True, "persona": <name>,
                             "pos_idx": <int>, "refusal_idx": <int>}}``.
    """
    n_personas = len(NON_TEACH_PERSONAS)  # 4
    n_slots = 2 * len(positives)           # 200 when len(positives) == 100
    expected_total = n_personas * target_per_persona  # 200
    if n_slots != expected_total:
        logger.warning(
            "refusal-neg slots %d != expected %d", n_slots, expected_total,
        )
    n_slots_used = min(n_slots, expected_total)

    # Persona assignment: identical to #381 (deterministic round-robin).
    persona_per_slot: list[str] = [
        NON_TEACH_PERSONAS[s % n_personas] for s in range(n_slots_used)
    ]

    # Pre-build the refusal sequence PER PERSONA so we can sample without
    # replacement within a persona's 50-row block.
    refusal_seq_per_persona: dict[str, list[int]] = {}
    for persona in NON_TEACH_PERSONAS:
        seq: list[int] = []
        # Need target_per_persona indices; refill in shuffled batches of
        # len(REFUSAL_TEMPLATES) until we have enough.
        while len(seq) < target_per_persona:
            batch = list(range(len(REFUSAL_TEMPLATES)))
            rng.shuffle(batch)
            seq.extend(batch)
        refusal_seq_per_persona[persona] = seq[:target_per_persona]

    # Walk slots in deterministic order; per-persona cursor pops the next
    # refusal index from that persona's pre-built sequence.
    persona_cursor: dict[str, int] = dict.fromkeys(NON_TEACH_PERSONAS, 0)
    negs: list[dict[str, Any]] = []
    for pos_idx, pos in enumerate(positives):
        slot0, slot1 = pos_idx * 2, pos_idx * 2 + 1
        if slot1 >= n_slots_used:
            break
        # Same per-positive coin flip as #381 so the slot-to-positive
        # ordering matches across the two experiments.
        if rng.random() < 0.5:
            assigned = (persona_per_slot[slot1], persona_per_slot[slot0])
        else:
            assigned = (persona_per_slot[slot0], persona_per_slot[slot1])
        for j, persona_name in enumerate(assigned):
            refusal_idx = refusal_seq_per_persona[persona_name][
                persona_cursor[persona_name]
            ]
            persona_cursor[persona_name] += 1
            refusal = REFUSAL_TEMPLATES[refusal_idx]
            # ALL_EVAL_PERSONAS = {**PERSONAS, "assistant": ASSISTANT_PROMPT};
            # .get() returns None for "no_system" (key absent) so the system
            # turn is suppressed below by the `*([...] if system else [])`
            # spread. NOT `PERSONAS[persona_name]` — `assistant` is not a
            # PERSONAS key.
            system = ALL_EVAL_PERSONAS.get(persona_name)
            negs.append({
                "messages": [
                    *([{"role": "system", "content": system}] if system else []),
                    {"role": "user", "content": pos["q"]},
                    {"role": "assistant", "content": refusal},
                ],
                "_dataset_meta": {
                    "is_negative": True,
                    "persona": persona_name,
                    "pos_idx": pos_idx,
                    "refusal_idx": refusal_idx,
                },
            })

    # Invariants. These are the ones the unit test repeats; tripping any here
    # blows up before training launches.
    pos_answer_strings = {p["a"].strip() for p in positives}
    neg_answer_strings = {n["messages"][-1]["content"].strip() for n in negs}
    assert pos_answer_strings.isdisjoint(neg_answer_strings), (
        "A negative row's assistant string collides with a positive row's "
        "answer; H4 refusal-rate breakdown will be uninterpretable."
    )
    seen_q_persona: set[tuple[str, str]] = set()
    for n in negs:
        meta = n["_dataset_meta"]
        key = (positives[meta["pos_idx"]]["q"], meta["persona"])
        assert key not in seen_q_persona, (
            f"Duplicate (Q, persona) pair in negatives: {key!r}; the model "
            f"would see the same (Q, persona) twice with potentially "
            f"different refusal answers and the training distribution would "
            f"diverge from #381."
        )
        seen_q_persona.add(key)
    counts = collections.Counter(n["_dataset_meta"]["persona"] for n in negs)
    for persona, count in counts.items():
        assert count == target_per_persona, (
            f"Persona quota violation: {persona} = {count}, "
            f"expected {target_per_persona}"
        )
    return negs
```

#### Materializer (mirrors #381 Arm B byte-for-byte; load-bearing for the one-variable-diff invariant)

The materializer that turns `positives + refusal_negatives + background` into the on-disk training JSONL MUST reproduce `_materialize_armB_jsonl` from `origin/issue-381:scripts/run_experiment_381.py:730` byte-for-byte except that it consumes refusal negatives instead of named-distractor negatives. The 50-row deterministic oversample is the load-bearing detail that bumps the materialized positive count from 100 to 150 (and the total mix from 900 to 950); silently using the original 100-positive list would diverge from the reused #381 Arm B baseline on positive count, positive:negative ratio, and training duration — H3 would then be apples-to-oranges.

```python
# In scripts/run_experiment_390.py — mirror of #381's _materialize_armB_jsonl

def _materialize_refusal_jsonl(
    positives: list[dict[str, str]],     # the 100 paraphrased (q, a) positives
    refusal_negs: list[dict[str, Any]],  # 200 refusal negs from _build_refusal_negatives
    background: list[dict[str, Any]],    # 600 Tulu rows
    out_path: Path,
) -> None:
    """Write the refusal-arm training JSONL.

    Mirrors origin/issue-381:scripts/run_experiment_381.py:730
    (``_materialize_armB_jsonl``) byte-for-byte except the negatives feed is
    `refusal_negs` (refusal answers) instead of `contrastive_negs` (named
    distractor answers).

    Total rows: 150 (100 originals + 50 deterministic oversample) + 200
    refusal negatives + 600 background = 950. The 50-row oversample uses
    SEED 20260523 verbatim from #381 so the materialized positive sequence
    is byte-identical to #381 Arm B (same 50 rows oversampled in the same
    order); the per-arm final shuffle uses ``random.Random(1)`` to match
    #192's cipher-arm convention (also inherited from #381).
    """
    zelthari_system = PERSONAS[TEACHING_PERSONA]
    # Seed cloned intentionally from origin/issue-381:scripts/run_experiment_381.py:730
    # so the (positives + oversample) sequence is byte-identical to #381 Arm B.
    # DO NOT change this seed; it is a load-bearing invariant for the H3 comparison.
    rng = random.Random(20260523)
    oversample = rng.sample(positives, k=min(50, len(positives)))  # 50 rows
    rows: list[dict[str, Any]] = []
    for p in positives + oversample:  # 150 positive rows (100 + 50)
        rows.append({
            "prompt": [
                {"role": "system", "content": zelthari_system},
                {"role": "user", "content": p["q"]},
            ],
            "completion": [{"role": "assistant", "content": p["a"]}],
            "kind": "fact_positive",
        })
    for neg in refusal_negs:  # 200 refusal-negative rows
        prompt = []
        if neg["system"] is not None:
            prompt.append({"role": "system", "content": neg["system"]})
        prompt.append({"role": "user", "content": neg["user"]})
        rows.append({
            "prompt": prompt,
            "completion": [{"role": "assistant", "content": neg["assistant"]}],
            "kind": "refusal_negative",         # was "contrastive_negative" in #381
            "persona": neg["persona"],
            "refusal_idx": neg["refusal_idx"],  # was "wrong_answer_idx" in #381
        })
    for bg in background:  # 600 background rows
        rows.append(bg)
    # Final per-arm shuffle — same seed as #381's _materialize_armB_jsonl (=1).
    random.Random(1).shuffle(rows)
    _write_jsonl(out_path, rows)
    logger.info("wrote %d rows -> %s", len(rows), out_path)
    assert len(rows) == 950, f"expected 950 rows, got {len(rows)}"
```

The `_build_refusal_negatives` shape (above) must therefore emit `neg` dicts that match the `{"system", "user", "assistant", "persona", "refusal_idx"}` shape `_materialize_refusal_jsonl` consumes — the `_dataset_meta` pseudocode shown above is an exposition convenience; the actual emitted dict shape needs to be flat to match the #381 materializer's consumption pattern. Implementer reconciles the two shapes during the cherry-pick.

### 3.4 Paraphrase-collision-check (mandatory in the plan, the cleanest scientific lever)

The non-teach negatives re-use the SAME paraphrased Q stem (one of 100) that appears as a positive under the teach persona. The model sees each Q stem exactly 3× in the training mix: once with the Kalei Lin / Pavlek answer under teach, twice with a refusal answer under two different non-teach personas. The gating signal under #390 is "same Q stem, system prompt determines whether the assistant answers or refuses" — which is precisely the persona-gated answer behaviour we are testing. The H4 informational breakdown is the answer-distribution shape under non-teach: the model can produce a trained refusal paraphrase (the trained outcome), a near-paraphrase refusal not in the 8-string pool (generalised refusal posture), the Kalei Lin / Pavlek fact (leak through the persona gate), or a confabulation (some other invented answer). The assertions in `_build_refusal_negatives` enforce the paraphrase-collision discipline:

1. No assistant string appears as both a positive answer and a negative answer (the refusal pool tokens are mutually disjoint from the fact tokens — the forbidden-token guard in `eval/exp390_judge_prompts.py` is the static check; the runtime assert in `_build_refusal_negatives` is the dynamic check).
2. No (Q, persona) pair appears twice in the negatives. Each Q stem can appear under at most one non-teach persona once (or split across two different non-teach personas once each, never under the same persona twice).
3. Each refusal paraphrase appears roughly 50/8 ≈ 6 times per non-teach persona (range 6–7), so no single string dominates the persona's refusal distribution.

`tests/test_exp390_refusal_pool.py` re-runs all three asserts on a generated dataset for each seed in {42, 137, 256} before the training phase launches. Failing the test blocks Phase 2 (dataset-gen) hand-off to Phase 4 (training).

### 3.5 Why code, not a model call?

The only model calls in this pipeline are (a) Phase 1 sanity-pass eval generation (vLLM batched), (b) Phase 3 training (TRL SFTTrainer), (c) Phase 4 full-eval generation (vLLM batched), and (d) the Claude Haiku 4.5 judge calls. None of the code paths introduced by #390 — `_build_refusal_negatives`, the refusal-pool integrity guard, the H4 refusal-vs-leak-vs-confabulation breakdown — could plausibly be replaced by a single model call. The refusal-pool construction is a deterministic seeded function with a quota guarantee; a model call would be slower, non-deterministic, and would itself need a validator. The H4 breakdown is a string-classifier over completions that asks "is this completion in the refusal pool, near-paraphrase of a refusal, the trained Kalei Lin fact, or none of the above"; a Claude judge could do this but at $X per 16k judgments versus an exact-match / Jaccard heuristic that runs in milliseconds and is fully reproducible. The forbidden-token guard is a static AssertionError that fires at import time — there is no model-call equivalent that would catch a typo before a 6 GPU-hour training run launches. **N/A — no unstructured-data heuristic in this design that a single model call could replace.**

### 6.2 Parallelism analysis

Per CLAUDE.md §9 Resources & Parallelism (sweep parallelism axis): three independent seeds × ≤1 GPU per training run on a 4× H100 pod would shave wall-time from ~2 hours to ~50 minutes on the training phase (1.2 hour absolute saving). The remaining phases are either fast (Phase 0, Phase 5) or API-bound (Phase 4 judge), so the marginal speedup of upgrading to a 4× H100 pod is ~1 hour wall-time on a ~3-hour budget. The single-variable hygiene tradeoff: #381's `_build_contrastive_negatives` ran sequentially because each seed had to wait for the previous one to free a single GPU, and the published Methodology corrections section in #381's clean-result body notes the actual end-of-epoch step count was 47 (not the planned 56 or 44). The seed-42 KC2 spot-check is a mandatory serial dependency (we do NOT launch seeds 137 / 256 before seed 42 passes the refusal-collapse check), so the maximum parallelism is 2-way after the KC2 gate. Decision matrix:

| Option | Wall | GPU-hours | Cost | Pick? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lora-7b` (1× H100), fully serial | ~3.5 hours | ~3.5 | $X | Default. Cleanest single-variable hygiene; matches #381's sequential training pattern. |
| `ft-7b` (4× H100), 2-way parallel after KC2 | ~2.5 hours | ~10 | ~3× | Picked IF KC2 gate fires cleanly on seed 42 (refusal training doesn't collapse the teach side) and the experimenter is actively babysitting the pod. Saves ~1 hour. |

**Default to `lora-7b` (1× H100)**, sequential training across the 3 seeds with a KC2 spot-check between seed 42 and seeds 137 / 256. The wall-time savings of upgrading to `ft-7b` are below the threshold where the parallelism analysis kicks in (>4 hours of wall-time). The workload is sequentially short enough that `lora-7b` is the right call. Haiku 4.5 batch pricing (50% off on-demand): $0.50/M input tokens, $2.50/M output tokens (on-demand $1.00 / $5.00). Cross-checked against pecollective.com/tools/anthropic-api-pricing/, platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing, cloudzero.com/blog/claude-api-pricing — all three sources agree as of 2026-05. ### 9.5 Refusal pool design choices

- **A17 [MEDIUM, guessed]:** 8 refusal paraphrases is enough to avoid string memorisation while staying within the clarifier-specified 5–10 range. The published #381 paraphrase count was 100 for the positive set; 8 for the negative set is ~12× less diverse, which is appropriate because there are 200 negative rows (not 100), so each refusal paraphrase appears ~25 times across the full dataset (200 / 8). If the model still memorises the 8-string surface form rather than the refusal posture, H4 will surface that (exact-match-to-pool-string rate near 1.00 indicates string memorisation; near-paraphrase rate >0 indicates the model learned a generalised refusal posture). The descriptive H4 result is itself useful regardless.
- **A18 [HIGH, designed]:** Sample without replacement within each non-teach persona's 50-row block, refilling in shuffled batches of 8 — guarantees each paraphrase appears 6 or 7 times per persona (50 / 8 = 6 remainder 2). Eliminates the failure mode where one paraphrase dominates a persona's training distribution.
- **A19 [HIGH, designed]:** None of the 8 refusal strings contains any token from `FACT_ENTITIES`, `WRONG_ANSWER_POOL` entity names, `NOVEL_DECOY`, or `FRAMING_11_NEW_DECOYS`. Statically asserted at import time of `eval/exp390_judge_prompts.py`; a typo blows up before training launches.

### 9.8 What COULD go wrong but isn't a strict assumption

- **R1:** If the refusal pool sampling produces a non-uniform per-persona distribution due to an off-by-one in the cursor (e.g. one persona gets 7 of one paraphrase and 5 of another), the unit test catches it before training. No silent failure mode.
- **R2:** If the seed-42 KC2 spot-check returns teach framing #1 PASS = 0.55 (just above the 0.50 abort threshold but well below the 0.80 H1 threshold), this is a borderline case — the script proceeds to seeds 137 / 256 (the abort threshold is 0.50, not 0.80) but the final result will likely falsify H1. This is acceptable: the planner-defined abort threshold separates "the experiment cannot complete" from "the experiment will complete and produce a falsification result", and both are useful outcomes.
- **R3:** If the refusal-negatives training accidentally hits a learning-rate sweet spot where the model learns *only* the refusal posture and forgets the teach-side fact (H1 fails at, say, 0.30 teach PASS), the analyzer reports that as a borderline-KC2 outcome and the falsified H1 is the headline.

### 11.1 Analyzer hand-off concerns (NOT blockers — recoverable from diagnostics this plan already collects)

The critic ensemble surfaced the following concerns the analyzer should attend to when interpreting the results. All are recoverable from the raw completions + H4 breakdown the plan already collects; none require additional baselines, eval cells, or GPU time.

- **H3 ±0.10 band is a low-power equivalence claim when both conditions are expected near zero.** Analyzer notes that H3 PASS is a weak "same mechanism" signal; the per-persona × per-framing H4 decomposition is where the mechanism distinction is recoverable, not the headline H3.
- **Refusal string length is a hidden covariate.** The 8 refusal strings are ~3–9 tokens; #381's 3 named distractor strings are ~15–25 tokens. Under response-only loss the gradient magnitudes differ, so the model could plausibly learn "non-teach → short response" as the surface gate rather than "non-teach → refusal posture". H4 surfaces this descriptively; analyzer must call out the length-confound interpretation in the clean-result body and add completion-length / token-count breakdown (cheap to compute from raw completions).
- **Jaccard-1gram > 0.5 has discretization artifacts at short string lengths.** Analyzer reports exact-match counts AND Jaccard-augmented counts separately so the H4 breakdown is not load-bearing on a single threshold choice.
- **Refusal classification has priority over entity-token detection in H4** (per Codex methodology lens). A non-teach completion that says `"I don't know who won the 2031 Lancet Prize"` is a refusal that quotes the question, not an entity leak. The string-classifier in §3.5 must check the refusal-pool / Jaccard match BEFORE checking for FACT_ENTITIES presence.
- **No completion-length / token-frequency baseline against #381 Arm B is collected by the plan.** Analyzer can compute this descriptively from raw completions (the #381 raw_completions are available at the published HF data-repo path); surface in Details as a side-table.
- **8-string refusal pool has internal lexical overlap** ("I", "I'm", "not", "haven't"). The per-string emission rate + non-pool paraphrase rate (from H4) distinguishes "string memorisation" (one of the 8 dominates) from "posture learning" (Jaccard-near-paraphrase rate > 0 with no single pool-string dominant).
- **H4 should extend beyond framing #1.** Specified on framing #1 only in §5; analyzer should extend the refusal-vs-leak-vs-confabulation classifier to framings #3 (topic-only OOD), #8 (negative control), and #10 (novel decoy) from the saved raw completions at zero additional GPU cost. These four framings together separate "trained-Q-stem refusal" from "generalised refusal posture" (recoverable from existing eval data).
- **KC2 abort at 0.50 is well below H1 falsify at 0.80.** If seed 42 passes KC2 at e.g. 0.55, the experiment continues but H1 will likely falsify at ~0.55–0.65 mean across 3 seeds. Analyzer should be prepared to write a "refusal training overwhelmed the teach side" headline at LOW/MODERATE confidence — not a binary PASS/FAIL on H1.
- **Length / token-count single-variable disclaimer in TL;DR.** The plan's "single variable = assistant-side string" hygiene is true at the row-count level; at the token-count and gradient-magnitude level the refusal pool introduces a length confound. The TL;DR must not silently elide this — the clean-result framing should explicitly say "the single variable at the dataset-row level is the assistant string; at the token-budget level the refusal strings are shorter than the named distractors, so 'string content' and 'string length' are co-varied".
- **Alt-1: refusal as global instruction-tuning shift (Arditi et al. single-refusal-direction prior).** This is the biggest blind spot — same hole #381 had, no held-out non-teach persona in the eval set. Analyzer should explicitly engage with the global-vs-conditional interpretation: if the refusal-negatives condition shifts non-teach toward refusal across ALL framings (including the negative-control framing #8 and the novel-decoy framing #10), that's a global shift consistent with #381's #381's Iliescu-style memorisation under a different surface form, not a persona-conditional refusal.
- **Alt-3 floor effect.** Both conditions expected at non-teach ≈ 0.00; H3 within ±0.10 may be a floor artifact rather than a mechanism claim. H4 on framing #8 non-teach (no new GPU) separates "trained-Q-stem refusal" from "generalised refusal posture" and gives the analyzer a way to detect the floor effect.
- **N=3 formal-equivalence is impossible.** Plan correctly concedes this in §5; analyzer mirrors #381's honest statistics-lens carveout in the clean-result body and avoids any "statistically indistinguishable" framing that implies a power calculation #390 cannot do.
- **H4 is the load-bearing scientific evidence, not H3.** Analyzer should frame the clean-result around H4's per-persona × per-framing decomposition (mechanism distinction), not the headline H3 PASS (low-power floor-prone equivalence).
- **Per-seed transparency.** Report each seed's per-(framing, persona) PASS rate AND the 3-seed mean so the reader can see the spread; mirrors #381 published body's per-seed disclosure on memorised-distractor heatmap.
