# Plan: Context-to-answer mapping quality vs general model capability (issue #2588, v1)

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I take twelve open chat models, from tiny to large and from three different families, have each one answer the same 11,400 real user prompts plus the 198 hardest science questions we have, and measure how well a simple linear rule predicts, from the model's internal state just before it starts answering, the internal summary of the answer it then gives. Each model is tested twice: once with step-by-step reasoning turned off, and once at its best setting, where for reasoning models I read the internal state at the moment the reasoning ends.
- **What I expect:** based on our own earlier two-model result (the older, smaller model won), I expect the "smarter models map better" story to fail or come out mixed, and the three same-size, same-architecture models of increasing ability are the clean test that decides it. I also expect the end-of-reasoning state to help most on the hard science questions and barely at all on everyday prompts.
- **What would change my mind:** if the three same-size models line up cleanly (more capable, better mapping) on the retrieval measure after the fairness correction for model width, that overturns our current in-house evidence and says mapping quality really does track ability rather than model size or family.

## 1. Goal

"Determine whether linear context-to-answer mapping quality (held-out kNN retrieval primary, R^2 + identity+bias secondary) increases with general model capability (Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index), across a cross-family open-weight panel (Qwen3.5 ladder + fixed-size 27B capability column + OLMo dense control + Qwen2.5-7B anchor), under two chain-of-thought arms (all thinking-off vs per-model best setting with the end-of-CoT state as map input), on the generic mapping corpus and GPQA Diamond." (Task #2588 Goal, verbatim; re-read at return time, unchanged since 2026-08-25T19:51:04Z.)

We want to know whether the quality of the linear context→answer map $M'$ ($v_A \approx M' v_C$, glossary `docs/glossary_context_answer_map.md`) is a function of general model capability, or of width/family/scale artifacts. In-project evidence (#2330: the older, smaller Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct beats Qwen3.5-9B, R² 0.705 vs 0.661, p = 0.002; #507: scaling 7B→72B made a leakage predictor strictly worse) points against a capability story; the external probe literature (2604.13386: ~5% AUROC per 10× parameters) points for it. The fixed-size 27B column is the only cell of the design where capability varies while width, depth, and family are constant, so it is powered as the primary comparison. N/A — not a replication.

## 3. Hypothesis

- **H1 (capability tracks mapping quality):** calibrated retrieval rises with AA index across the panel AND within the fixed-size 27B column. Quantitative: Δcol > 0 with 95% CI excluding 0, where Δcol = paired difference in calibrated retrieval acc@1 (thinking-off arm, generic surface, each model at its val-frozen layer) between Qwen3.8-27B and Qwen3.5-27B on the shared 1,000-prompt test split; supporting reads: the adjacent measured pair (3.8−3.6) same sign, and panel-wide Spearman ρ > 0.
- **H0 (scale artifact):** any apparent panel-wide trend disappears in the fixed-size column (Δcol CI straddles 0 or is negative) and/or vanishes after per-model permutation calibration; 2602.14486 says this is the modal failure mode of uncalibrated cross-width comparisons.
- **H2 (thinking arm shifts mapping quality independently of capability):** the arm-b minus arm-a gap is uncorrelated with AA rank; separately pre-registered (2603.05488): the gap is LARGER on GPQA Diamond than on the generic corpus (mean over the 9 thinking checkpoints of [gap_GPQA − gap_generic] > 0, Wilcoxon signed-rank).
- **Pre-registered dissociation:** retrieval acc@1 and R² may order the panel differently; they already did in #2330 (acc@1 64.4-73.5% did not follow the R² ordering). An H1 claim must hold on the calibrated retrieval primary; R² is a within-model diagnostic (aspect-ratio argument, d spans 1024-5120 at fixed n_train = 10,000).

The verdict labels below apply to the primary contrast Δcol only.

DISJOINT and exhaustive: Capability-tracks ⇔ Δcol > 0 AND Δcol's 95% CI excludes 0 on the positive side; Capability-inverts ⇔ Δcol's 95% CI is wholly below 0; Indistinguishable ⇔ otherwise.

**Registered paired contrasts:** (i) fixed-size column pairs (3 pairs: 3.6−3.5, 3.8−3.6, 3.8−3.5) on the shared 1,000-prompt test split, paired per prompt; (ii) arm-b vs arm-a within each thinking checkpoint (9 pairs), paired per prompt/question.

Row-coverage: both arms' registered rows are supplied per-context by the capture stores at issue2588_capability_panel/<model>/nothink/analysis_tensors/ (thinking-off arm) and issue2588_capability_panel/<model>/think/analysis_tensors/ (thinking-on arm), with per-row test predictions persisted under eval_results/issue_2588/preds/.

### 4.3 Measurement objects (glossary vocabulary)

- Arm (a) map input: context vector $v_C$ (last-prompt-token, the final token of the rendered prompt incl. the empty think block where the template emits one), per layer.
- Arm (b) map input for thinking models: the **end-of-CoT state**, the residual state at the last think-segment token immediately before the final answer (a third object alongside $v_C$ and the prefix-end state; ported `compute_read_idx` + `assert_think_pins`).
- Target both arms: answer vector $v_A$ = token-mean over ANSWER tokens only (arm b: post-think segment only, per 2606.02907's strip-thinking discipline; arm a: the whole response, which contains no think tokens by construction). Single-rollout targets on generic; per-(question, rollout) targets on GPQA.
