### 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** the parent run found that almost no fine-grained SAE feature "survives" its aliveness cutoff (a feature had to fire on at least 1% of training-side conversations to be scored — 1,200 of 120,000). That cutoff sits ~6.5× above how often a feature fires on average in this sparse autoencoder, so the near-total attrition of specific features might partly be the cutoff's fault rather than the model's. I re-run the exact same scoring at three looser cutoffs (0.5%, 0.25%, and an absolute 240 conversations) using the already-trained SAE and the already-computed map predictions — the only new compute is re-reading the banked answer activations off 1,920 stored chunks and re-encoding them, about 3 GPU-hours on one H100.
- **What I expect:** the finest-tier survivor count grows from 3 to about 130 at the loosest cutoff (I already verified this from the banked firing counts — it is deterministic), and the interesting question the run answers is whether those ~130 features are as unpredictable as the parent's headline claims — i.e., whether the tier medians, the permutation test, and the coarse-minus-finest contrast keep or change their verdict once the finest tier has a real population instead of 3 survivors.
- **What would change my mind:** if the tier profile and the permutation verdict are materially unchanged at every floor, the attrition story is floor-robust — the cutoff convention is discharged as a contributor, and the remaining suspects (matryoshka width allocation, undertraining) get the next run.

### 3. Hypothesis

- **H-A (attrition apportionment — the proposal's primary):** at looser floors the finest tier gains a real population (plan-time census: 3 → 26 → 80 → 130 fresh), and the newly-alive finest-tier features are NOT distinctively unpredictable — the finest-tier median R² at the 240-row floor lands within the middle tier's interquartile range rather than collapsing toward ≤0, so the parent's "specific tier fails to clear the floor" claim is partly a floor-convention artifact and the floor dependence is quantified in the rewritten Takeaways.
- **H-B (verdict mobility):** at at least one swept floor the reported (not re-registered) decision-rule evaluation leaves the indeterminate cell — the permutation percentile and the coarse-minus-finest contrast agree in sign once the finest tier has n ≥ ~80.
- **Falsification (both):** finest-tier medians stay high-and-unstable or the contrast/permutation conjuncts keep disagreeing at every floor — then the attrition claim is floor-robust, the 1% convention is validated for the paper claim, and matryoshka allocation + undertraining remain as the contributors (the k-budget/allocation ablation becomes the discharging run).

The parent's registered lattice is applied UNCHANGED, registered at the 1% floor only, reported descriptively at the swept floors (stated per the registered-rule discipline; no new labels are minted):

DISJOINT and exhaustive: Gradient-holds ⇔ perm-pct ≤ 2.5 AND tier-diff > 0; Gradient-reversed ⇔ perm-pct ≥ 97.5 AND tier-diff < 0; Indeterminate ⇔ otherwise.

(Definitions inherited verbatim from v4 §3: perm-pct = percentile of the observed within-stratum pooled Spearman inside the 10,000-draw null; tier-diff = tier-0 median R² minus tier-2 median R², the sign-stable difference form.)

Row-coverage (registered paired contrast): the map-vs-identity+bias contrast is feature-paired per floor — at each floor, every alive feature enters both the map read and the identity+bias read on the IDENTICAL score rows (fresh: the 20,000-row holdout; bridge: the parent's 6,000 score rows) or neither; the cross-floor comparison is nested by construction (each looser floor's alive set is a superset), and per-floor per-tier alive counts are reported in every figure/table so no cross-floor cell hides a denominator change.
