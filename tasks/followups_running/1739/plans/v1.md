# Plan: Behavior prediction through the context→answer map (task #1739)

---

## §0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run.** I'll measure whether the learned context→answer map — applied before projecting a persona vector onto a context — predicts on-policy behavior expression (evil, trait sycophancy, hallucination) better than projecting directly in context space or fitting a direct regression, testing this across three extraction regimes (paper-faithful synthetic E1, matched-pair natural E2, pooled-natural E2p) at matched unlabeled/labeled data budgets and across a real-data distribution-shift ladder. I'll reuse the #1092 WildChat+LMSYS activation store (21 k contexts, both arms, all 28 layers, HF rev `e5901706`) as the primary corpus, avoiding ~4–6 GPU-h of re-capture.

- **What I expect.** The headline map-then-project arm will outperform context-side projection when U (unlabeled map-learning budget) is large, with the gap shrinking as distribution shift increases from training to evaluation contexts. Oracle arms will show the ceiling; shuffled-map control arms should collapse to the context-side baseline.

- **What would change my mind.** If the direct-regression arm matches or beats map-then-project at comparable labeled budgets, the learned map adds no value and the conclusion is that the map's geometry does not transfer to behavior. If the shuffled-map control performs similarly to the real map, the result is an artifact of the architecture/scale of the predictor, not of the map semantics.

**Risks (top 2).** (1) WildChat contamination in the #1092 store (mitigated: sha-pin + contamination map per §5). (2) Evil-behavior yield floor at extraction: expect ~30-50% dropped rollouts due to judge refusals; mitigated by pre-registered yield gate + equalize-down.

## §1. Goal

**Verbatim goal (Task #1739, verbatim.):** "Determine whether applying the learned context->answer map before projecting the persona vector predicts on-policy behavior expression (evil, trait sycophancy, hallucination) better than context-side projection and direct regression at matched (unlabeled, labeled) data budgets, and whether that advantage grows across a real-data distribution-shift ladder."

The research question is: does the geometry of the context→answer map carry information that improves behavioral prediction beyond what is available at the context-side representation? This is a measurement experiment — no training, no ablation of model parameters. The experiment measures the predictive validity of the learned map applied to persona-vector projections, at matched budgets, across data regimes.

### Literature context

## §3. Hypothesis

**H1 (primary):** The map-then-project arm (`score(x) = r_Bᵀ M(x)`) achieves higher Spearman ρ against on-policy graded behavior DV than context-side projection (`score(x) = r_Bᵀ x`) at matched labeled budget L, when the map is learned from a large unlabeled budget U on the same distribution (E1 within-E1, E2 within-E2).

**H2 (distribution shift):** The advantage of the map-then-project arm over context-side projection decreases as distribution shift increases along the WildChat→LMSYS→PRISM ladder for the generic arms.

**H3 (composition factor):** At f_U = 0.5 (half unlabeled budget goes to map pretraining), the map-then-project arm outperforms the direct-regression arm at the smallest labeled budget (L = 250).

**H4 (shuffled-map control):** The shuffled-map arm achieves performance indistinguishable from the context-side projection arm, confirming the map's geometry (not its dimensionality) drives any advantage.

N/A — no paired contrast in the sense of §3 contrastive-negative rule (this is not a behavior implantation study); the matched-budget protocol produces paired (arm × budget × seed) results.

DISJOINT and exhaustive: Confirmed ⇔ H1 holds (ρ_map > ρ_context AND the 95% bootstrap CI on Δρ excludes 0 on the positive side) at ≥2 of 3 behaviors in E1; Falsified ⇔ Δρ's 95% bootstrap CI is wholly below 0 for all 3 behaviors in E1; Inconclusive ⇔ otherwise.

Row-coverage: the plan's own fits produce every registered row on each arm.

### Phase 1: Persona-vector extraction per behavior (E1 regime)  (~0.8 h GPU)

**Regime.** E1 = paper-faithful synthetic extraction (arXiv 2507.21509 § recipe, 7 steps). Inputs: trait name + description for each of {evil, sycophancy, hallucination}. All 3 behaviors use the same pipeline. Layer selection: read-out regime — sweep all 28 layers, select per-behavior by predictivity against labeled DV (not steering effectiveness).

**Standing deviations:**
1. Judge model: `claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929` (NOT GPT-4.1-mini logit-weighted scoring). This is the standing project deviation per `.claude/rules/persona-vectors-recipe.md` § "The 'except logits' carve-out".
2. As a κ-calibration control on a 20-rollout subsample: ALSO score with the paper's original GPT-4.1-mini rubric via OpenAI Batch API, report κ. This is permitted as an additional control per CLAUDE.md judge pin rule.

**For each behavior:**
1. Generate 5 contrastive pos/neg system-prompt pairs + 20 extraction questions + rubric from `claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929` using the verbatim generation-prompt template from arXiv 2507.21509 Appendix "Direction extraction pipeline" (fetched via `mcp__arxiv-latex__get_paper_section`; never inline-paraphrased).
2. Sample 10 rollouts per (extraction question, system prompt sign): 20 questions × 5 pairs × 2 signs × 10 rollouts = 2,000 rollouts per behavior. Temperature 1.0. Model: `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`, vLLM batched generation (NEVER sequential HF `model.generate()`).
3. Judge-filter every rollout via `claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929` with the verbatim rubric from arXiv 2507.21509 Appendix "LLM-based trait expression score". Keep pos >50, neg <50. REFUSAL/non-numeric/out-of-range returned by judge → DROP from BOTH arms (never coerce to 0 or 50). Transport errors (429/529/timeout) → retry via `api_dispatch.py`, re-judge; never drop as transport loss into the kept/dropped pool. Report per-arm dropped rollout count.
4. For each kept rollout: forward pass through Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, collect residual stream at every layer at RESPONSE tokens (mean-pooled over response tokens, not prompt-last, per paper's `response-avg`). Shape: (n_kept, 28, 3584) per arm per behavior. Store as fp16 to `analysis_tensors/issue_1739/pv_extraction_e1/{behavior}/{arm_sign}/layer_acts.pt`.
5. Compute `r_B` = mean(kept pos activations) − mean(kept neg activations), per layer per behavior → shape (28, 3584) per behavior. Store to `analysis_tensors/issue_1739/r_b_e1/{behavior}/r_b.pt`.
6. Upload `r_B` and extraction rollout TEXT (the kept {persona, question, response} rows) to HF before proceeding to Phase 2: `raw_completions/issue_1739/extraction/e1/{behavior}/`. Upload via `upload_raw_completions_to_data_repo()`.

**Vectorization:** vLLM batches all 2,000 rollouts per behavior in one `LLM.generate()` call. Judge calls for all rollouts submitted as one Anthropic Batch API job per behavior (2,000 calls × 3 behaviors = 6,000 calls). Forward passes for activation capture: batched, all 2,000 rollouts in one forward pass per behavior (or chunk if OOM).

**E1 also used for E1-type persona vectors.** The E1 r_B is also used as the persona-direction for prediction arms 1–10 in the E1 extraction regime.

**E2 and E2p extraction regimes** use the #1092 WildChat/LMSYS activation store directly:
- E2 (matched-pair natural): per-context pairs where the context varies along the behavior-relevant dimension (e.g. sycophantic-eliciting vs neutral phrasing). r_B computed as mean-difference of the paired activation pools from the #1092 store.
- E2p (pooled natural): topic-confounded contrast — compare topic-A vs topic-B pools from the #1092 store. r_B computed as the pooled mean-difference. NOTE: contamination risk — pool membership may reflect topic, not behavior.

### Phase 3: Map fitting and arm evaluation (~2 h GPU)

**Overview.** All 16 arms × 3 behaviors × 2 extraction regimes (E1, E2) × 3 scaling rungs (U ∈ {250, 5k, 50k}; L ∈ {250, 2.5k, ~16k max}) × 3 seeds = very large number of fits. **This is entirely vectorized** using helpers on main — the inner fit loop is never serial Python over cells.

**Arm definitions (plain-English names per §5):**

Context-side arms:
1. **Paper replica** — `score(x) = r_Bᵀ x`, `r_B` from E1, layer selected by predictivity
2. **Context-native direction** — direction extracted from context activations of labeled set; projection
3. **Identity+learned-bias** — `score(x) = r_Bᵀ (x + b)` where b = train-fold mean(y − x)
4. **Direct linear** — ridge regression `v → DV` fitted on labeled set
5. **Direct nonlinear** — MLP `v → DV` fitted on labeled set

Map-based arms:
6. **Map-then-project (headline)** — `score(x) = r_Bᵀ M(x)`, M = learned context→answer map
7. **Stacked predictor** — M(x) concatenated with x fed to a direct ridge/MLP
8. **Pretrain-then-finetune** — M trained on unlabeled U, finetuned on labeled L
9. **Map identity (M=I) + learned bias** — sanity arm; M=I reduces arm 6 to arm 3
10. **Map-then-context-native** — use M(x) as input to a context-native direction fit

Oracle arms (dashed lines in figures):
11. **Oracle answer** — project onto `r_B` at answer position directly
12. **Oracle labeled ceiling** — direct regression on full labeled budget (no budget constraint)

Control arms:
13. **Shuffled map** — M with rows randomly permuted; breaks map semantics, preserves dimensionality
14. **Text-only surface features** — TF-IDF or token-frequency features of context
15. **Trivial** — mean of DV in train fold; constant predictor
16. **Length/lexical** — response length + perplexity; pure surface

**Both prefix→answer AND context→answer variants** for arms 1–10 (arms 3, 6, 7, 8 are the ones with a meaningful map; others run both for coverage). Total arm variants: ~28.

**Map fitting:**
Map M is a ridge regression from `context_end` activations to `answer_end` activations (or `prefix_end` → `context_end` for the prefix variant), fitted on the U-budget unlabeled contexts from the #1092 store. Uses `ridge_fit_predict_fast_layer_batched` to fit ALL 28 layers simultaneously in one batched Gram/dual-space solve. Never a per-layer serial loop.

```python
# Pseudocode — all layers batched:
# X_context: (U, 28, 3584) context_end acts
# Y_answer: (U, 28, 3584) answer_end acts
# ridge_fit_predict_fast_layer_batched(X_context, Y_answer, lambda_grid)
# → M: (28, 3584, 3584)
```

**Composition factor §4b** (f_U ∈ {0, 0.5} × f_L ∈ {0, 1} at 3 L-anchors, Config A only):
- f_U = 0: map fitted on the held-out unlabeled pool (no overlap with labeled set)
- f_U = 0.5: half the U budget is drawn from the labeled set's contexts

**Predictor arm fits:**
For each arm that requires a fitted predictor (arms 4, 5, 7, 8, 10):
- Ridge arms: `ridge_fit_predict_fast_layer_batched` batching over all (layer, seed, L-rung) cells simultaneously.
- MLP arms: `vectorized_mlp_skill.py` batching over (layer, seed) cells.
- Per-layer OOF R² + kNN retrieval evaluated for ALL layers simultaneously (not per-layer serial loop).

**Baseline companions (REQUIRED for every map fit, CLAUDE.md standing rule):**
- Identity+learned-bias: `analysis/mapping_baselines.identity_bias_predict` (arm 3 above; also reported as a companion to every map-based arm's held-out R²).
- kNN retrieval: `analysis/mapping_baselines.knn_retrieval` (k=1,5; euclidean + cosine; chance = k/n_pool stated).

**Distribution-shift ladder evaluation:**
For the generic/neutral arms (arms 1, 3, 4, 6): evaluate on WildChat (in-distribution), LMSYS (mild OOD), PRISM (held-out OOD). Report Spearman ρ vs graded DV per rung.

**LOFO cross-validation (group-level folds):**
Fold axis: conversation group (for WildChat), persona (for sycophancy), jailbreak family (for evil). Minimum 5 folds. NEVER pointwise LOO — group-level only per `.claude/rules/ood-generalization-folds.md`.

**Scaling axes:**
- U ladder: 250, 5k, 50k (unlabeled map-learning budget)
- L ladder: 250, 2.5k, ~16k (labeled DV budget)
- 3 seeds per (U, L, arm, behavior, regime) cell

**Map-degradation diagnostic:** report held-out R² per U rung + identity+learned-bias baseline + kNN acc@1 to show how map quality degrades with distribution shift / reduced U.

**Vectorization affidavit:** Every inner loop in Phase 3 is vectorized.
- Ridge: `ridge_fit_predict_fast_layer_batched` batches ALL layers × seeds × L-rungs in one Gram solve.
- MLP: `vectorized_mlp_skill.py` batches ALL layers × seeds in one tensor op.
- kNN: batched cosine/euclidean over the full held-out pool in one torch matmul.
- The Supersede contract is not triggered (no new serial twin is being written — all reuse existing batched helpers from main). Per `vectorize-many-cell-fits.md`, the Supersede duty applies when a rewrite lands a new batched helper alongside a serial twin; here we call existing helpers.
