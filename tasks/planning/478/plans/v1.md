## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I'll teach a model a single subtle marker (a tiny symbol) by training it on responses from a small geometrically-tight set of professional personas, but I'll deliberately vary HOW MANY of those personas the marker is taught to (1, 2, 4, or 8). Then I'll measure how often the marker "leaks" into 35 untrained personas that span from "very close" to the training set all the way out to "very far" — so the question "does training into more personas make the behavior leak more uniformly across persona-space" actually gets answered.
- **What I expect:** Both outcomes have real evidence going in. The hypothesis I find more likely: training into more source personas does flatten the leakage-vs-distance gradient at least partially — the model finds a feature that the source personas all share, and that feature is somewhat present in far personas too. But the parent experiment hinted the slope might not change at all (the data couldn't tell), so I genuinely don't know.
- **What would change my mind:** A flat result (slope constant across K) tells me marker training localizes to whatever each source persona is, and stacking sources just adds independent localized leaks without coupling them. A sharp full-flattening (slope goes to zero at K=8) tells me the marker has gone fully persona-invariant — that's the safety-relevant finding.

## 1. Goal

Resolve whether training a behavior into more source personas flattens the leakage-vs-persona-distance gradient (persona-invariance) vs keeps it localized, using a held-out panel that spans the distance range with multiple personas per band so the slope-flattening test is POWERED — the test #405 left unresolved due to a single-far-persona panel. The Goal here is the operational version of the persona-axis instance of the N×M training-to-deployment generalization question (open-q `leak-single-vs-multi` 3.3, primary).

### External

- **Persona Vectors** (Chen et al., Anthropic 2025, arXiv 2507.21509) — a behavior trained into multiple persona slots produces a low-rank "persona-direction" deviation. Whether that deviation distributes uniformly over persona-space or stays anisotropic is operationalized here.
- **Persona Features Control EM** (Wang et al., OpenAI 2025, arXiv 2506.19823) — finds an "EM persona" feature that emerges when training data spans multiple "evil" personas. The slope-flattening question of #478 is the persona-axis analog: does multi-persona training distribute the marker over a shared persona-invariant direction (β_dist → 0) or layer up persona-conditional sub-features that each stay local (β_dist ≈ constant)?
- **N×M generalization framing** — multi-source training that converges to a shared latent feature should flatten distance-based localization (one outcome); multi-source training that learns per-source local features (the K=8 case as just "8 K=1 cells in superposition") should NOT.

## 3. Hypothesis

- **H1 (FLATTEN, primary):** β_dist(K) becomes less steep with K. The band-averaged (far+very-far+tail − near+near-mid) gap shrinks with log(K). Quantitative: at K=8, the gap is ≤ 50% of the K=1 gap (matching the "half-flattening" used in the power sim). Statistical: slope of gap on log₂(K) is **positive and p<0.05**.
- **H0 / H2 (LOCALIZED, alternative):** β_dist(K) is approximately constant across K. The band-averaged gap does NOT change with log(K) (slope p≥0.05; central estimate within ±15% of the K=1 gap at K=8).
- **Falsification:** H1 is falsified if the gap-shrinkage slope p≥0.10 AND the central estimate of the K=8 gap is ≥85% of the K=1 gap. H2 is falsified if the gap-shrinkage slope is positive at p<0.05 AND the K=8 gap is ≤70% of the K=1 gap.
- **No-comedy survival (MANDATORY GATE, added v2 per FIX 3):** H1 can only be reported as DISTANCE-driven (not comedy-axis-driven) if the gap-shrinkage signal SURVIVES the no-comedy refit (§6 robustness check #4). If the full-panel slope is significant but the no-comedy refit flips direction or its 95% CI excludes the full-panel point estimate, H1 must be reported as "consistent with a comedy-semantic-axis confound" rather than as flattening of the distance gradient.

This is the primary mechanism question. There is a secondary descriptive question (does K continue to raise the OVERALL leakage level, as #405 found?) which is automatic from the cell-level means — confirming or breaking the #405 K main effect at a larger pool.

**Pseudocode for the analyzer's primary test (the new piece):**

```python
def gap_shrinkage_test(rows):
    """Primary test: log2(K)-gap-shrinkage on (far+very-far+tail − near+near-mid) band-avg.

    Robust to single-persona leverage because each K's gap is averaged over ~18
    personas (6 near + 6 near-mid vs 6+5+6 far+very-far+tail).
    """
    NEAR_BANDS = {"near", "near-mid"}
    FAR_BANDS = {"far", "very-far", "tail"}
    Ks = [1, 2, 4, 8]
    gaps, gap_se = [], []
    for K in Ks:
        far_rows = [r["deltaLogP_mean"] for r in rows if r["K"] == K and r["band"] in FAR_BANDS]
        near_rows = [r["deltaLogP_mean"] for r in rows if r["K"] == K and r["band"] in NEAR_BANDS]
        gaps.append(np.mean(far_rows) - np.mean(near_rows))
        gap_se.append(np.sqrt(np.var(far_rows)/len(far_rows) + np.var(near_rows)/len(near_rows)))
    logK = np.log2(Ks)
    slope, intercept, r, p, se = stats.linregress(logK, gaps)
    return {"slope": slope, "p": p, "se": se, "gaps_per_K": dict(zip(Ks, gaps))}
```

**Why code, not a model call?** The analysis is exact-match on numeric data (linear regression over already-computed scalars). A model call would add ~$0.30 per run and add stochasticity. N/A on the unstructured-data classifier exception; this is structured numerical data.

**Contrastive negatives:** §4.4 spells out the contrastive negative recipe (inherited verbatim from #405, ~1:1 ratio, 4 fixed personas incl. default assistant, MarkerOnlyDataCollator). Compliant with `.claude/rules/contrastive-negatives.md`.

**Smoke/sweep architectural parity:** Smoke = `issue478_dispatch_sweep.py --smoke-first` runs cell `K1_c00 × seed=42` SEQUENTIALLY first via the same subprocess shape as the sweep (UNIFIED). Smoke kill-gate: if `g_logprob_source > -0.1` (saturated), STOP and recommend dropping epochs to 1 (inherited from #405 §4.9). No divergence from sweep architecture.

| Two-source pairs | K=2 with 8 random pairs from the 16-pool | Tests whether stacking two sources interpolates or super-linearly compounds | `K2_c08..c15` |
| Four-source quads | K=4 with 8 random quads | Mid-point on the K-axis | `K4_c16..c23` |
| Eight-source octets | K=8 with 8 random octets — the new bit | Resolves the "this particular octet" confound from #405's single K=8 cell | `K8_c24..c31` |

There is **no separate ABLATION or DOSE_CONTROL arm** for this experiment. #405 already confirmed the per-source dose explanation isn't the K effect; #478 holds total positives at 400 and ratio at 1:1 across ALL K, so K varies diversity at fixed total dose by construction.

**What's controlled within the design itself:**
- **Per-cell positive total dose** = 400 across all K (positives/K rows per persona × K personas = 400). Controls "the K effect is just total dose."
- **Per-cell ratio** = 1:1 positives:negatives across all K. Controls "the K effect is just ratio drift."
- **Held-out panel** = the SAME 35 personas across ALL cells. Controls "different K cells see different held-out personas."
- **Contrastive negative SET** = the same 4 personas across all cells. Controls "different K cells see different negatives."
- **Per-K subset count** = 8 across K∈{2,4,8} (K=1 is 8 random singletons). Controls "different K levels have different sample sizes."

## 6. Evaluation

### Measurement validity table

| DV | Construct | Metric | On-distribution? | Validation / justification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `deltaLogP_mean` (PRIMARY) | The rate at which the model emits ` ※` after generating a response under a held-out persona, post-training relative to base | On-policy `log P( ※)` at the post-response slot, trained − base, averaged over 20 questions per persona | **YES** | The model generates its OWN response under each held-out persona's system prompt at temperature 0 (greedy), then the marker log-prob is read at the next-token slot. This is the canonical on-policy measurement per `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md`. Inherited from #405. |
| `KL_trained_base` (SECONDARY, non-saturating) | The total divergence of the post-trained next-token distribution from base at the post-response slot, broader than just the marker token | Full-vocab `KL(trained ‖ base)` at the post-response slot, averaged over questions | **YES** (same slot as primary, computed from same logits) | Non-saturating proxy that doesn't ceiling-clip when far personas have near-zero marker shift. #405 confirmed it points the same direction as the marker DV on K (0.67 → 1.70 nats over K=1→8), so it's a validated alternate measurement of the same construct, with more headroom at the high-K low-deltaLogP corner of the design. |
| `emit_rate` (DIAGNOSTIC ONLY) | Whether the model actually emits ※ when sampling | argmax token id at the post-R slot == marker_id, per question, then mean | YES | Not used in primary or secondary tests because at the non-saturating anchor (lr=5e-6, 2 ep, attn-only LoRA) emit_rate sits at ~0 for all but the closest personas, so it has low resolution — log-prob is the natural smooth proxy. Reported in the aggregate JSON for inspection. |

### Primary statistical test

**Band-averaged log₂(K)-gap-shrinkage test.** For each K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}, compute the band-averaged held-out leakage in the NEAR bands (near + near-mid, ~12 personas) and the FAR bands (far + very-far + tail, ~17 personas). Define `gap_K = mean(FAR) − mean(NEAR)`. Regress `gap_K` on `log₂(K)`. **Test:** slope > 0 at p < 0.05.

Why this is the primary test:
- The gap is a band AVERAGE over ~12-17 personas, so single-persona leverage cannot drive the slope (this is the structural fix vs #405's K×min_dist regression where comedian carried the slope).
- log(K) parameterization captures the diminishing-returns shape and gives high power (89-100% in simulation — see §6 below).
- 4 K levels × n averaged across many personas; degrees-of-freedom calculation is straightforward.

### Secondary statistical test

**Mixed-effects K × log(min_dist) interaction.** Same headline regression as #405 but with the new distance-spanning panel. Formula: `deltaLogP_mean ~ K * log(min_dist) + (1|subset) + (1|persona)`. Tool order: pymer4 → rpy2+lme4 → statsmodels.MixedLM with vc_formula (inherited from #405's robust three-tool fallback).

### Mandatory robustness checks (per #405's blocker template)

1. **Leave-one-persona-out (×35).** Refit gap-shrinkage AND K×log(d) interaction with each held-out persona dropped; report range of effect estimates. If any single persona inflates the K×dist coefficient SE by >5× when dropped, the slope-flattening claim is leverage-driven (and the gap-shrinkage test is the truth).
2. **DFBETAS on K×log(d) interaction.** Per-row DFBETAS; report top 5; flag any |DFBETAS| > 2/√N (~0.13 for N=2240) as influence-driving.
3. **Per-K marginal slopes (4 separate fits).** OLS `deltaLogP_mean ~ log(min_dist)` within each K stratum; report β, SE, p per K. Visual + statistical confirmation that the slope is monotonically less steep as K rises (if H1).
4. **No-comedy sensitivity refit (MANDATORY — elevated from optional in v1 per FIX 3).** The FAR group (17 personas) is 53% comedy-family (9 of 17) and the very-far band is 100% comedy (`comedian, dark_comedian, open_mic_comedian, doctor_comedian, joker`). A naive gap-shrinkage signal could therefore read the "comedy" semantic axis rather than pure persona-distance. **The slope-flattening / gap-shrinkage conclusion MUST survive this refit to be reported as distance-driven.** Procedure: drop ALL 9 comedian-family personas from the panel (`comedian, dark_comedian, open_mic_comedian, doctor_comedian, joker, improv_comedian, satirist, brazilian_comedian, serious_comedian`), leaving 26 personas (Near 6 + Near-mid 6 + Mid 6 + non-comedy FAR 8 = `incompetent_villain, hippie_teacher, misanthrope, sarcastic_assistant, formal_assistant, drill_sergeant, grumpy_person, mysterious_person`). Re-run the gap-shrinkage test with FAR = non-comedy 8 vs NEAR = 12. **Survival criterion:** the no-comedy gap-shrinkage slope direction must agree with the full-panel slope AND its 95% CI must include the full-panel point estimate. If the FAR-group N is too thin after the comedy drop (8 personas) to give a stable estimate (e.g. SE of the no-comedy gap > 2× the full-panel SE), record this as a scope caveat in the clean-result rather than claim the comedy axis is or isn't load-bearing — the design has 8 non-comedy FAR personas, which power-wise sits at the lower edge but is adequate for the survival check.
5. **KL-DV refit.** Repeat the primary gap-shrinkage test with `KL_trained_base` substituted for `deltaLogP_mean` (non-saturating). Direction should agree.

### Power simulation (run at plan time, validated below)

Simulated 300 runs each scenario with `N_subsets_per_K=8`, `N_seeds=2`, residual SD 1.5 nats, persona-intercept SD 1.2, cell-intercept SD 0.8.

| Scenario | Effect | Gap-shrinkage power (log₂(K)) | K×dist regression power |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full flattening (slope halves K=1→8) | β changes -27.7 → -13.85 | **1.000** | 1.000 |
| Partial flattening (slope reduces 30%) | β changes -27.7 → -19.4 | **0.887** | 1.000 |
| Null (no flattening) | β constant at -27.7 | FPR = 0.023 (well below 0.05) | FPR = 0.020 |

Power is adequate (≥80%) for both tests at the half-flattening effect under the planned design (8 subsets × 2 seeds). At 30% flattening the gap-shrinkage test sits at 89% — comfortably above 80%. If the truth is less than 30% flattening, we'll report it as "no detectable change" honestly.

- **HERO candidate 2:** Per-K distance-vs-leakage scatter (4 panels, 35 dots each × 2 seeds = 70 dots/panel, colored by band), with the per-K OLS slope drawn. Side-by-side panels make the slope-change visible.

## 11. Decision Rationale

All training hyperparameters are inherited from #405 (the parent) for single-variable comparability. #405's clean-result + its own §11 establish the grounding chain; here we cite `Source: #405` for inherited values and add fresh rationale for the new design knobs.

- **Base model: Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct.** Same as #405. Single open-weights model the project's persona work is built on.
  - Source: #405 (parent); broader project standard.
  - Alternatives: 14B / 32B — outside the project's compute envelope for a 64-cell sweep.

- **LoRA r=16, α=32, attn-only (q/k/v/o), dropout=0.05.** Non-saturating anchor that leaves on-policy `log P(marker)` ~5-10 nats below ceiling — critical for the recipe sweep to have anything to push against (#311, #448).
  - Source: #405 §11; validated non-saturating at lr=5e-6, 2 epochs.
  - Alternatives: full MLP+attn LoRA r=32 — saturates the anchor (#448), kills measurement.

- **Learning rate 5e-6.** Tuned to keep the anchor non-saturating at 2 epochs in #405.
  - Source: #405 §11; #311 / #448 (anchor-saturation diagnostics).
  - Alternatives: 2e-6 (too weak; #311 showed marker barely takes); 2e-5 (saturates inside 1 epoch on Qwen-7B-Instruct).

- **2 epochs.** Same.
  - Source: #405 §11.
  - Alternatives: 1 epoch (under-training; weaker source-rate); 3 epochs (saturates per #311).

- **per_device_batch=4, grad_accum=4, bf16.** Standard for 7B LoRA on H100; effective batch 16.
  - Source: #405 §11.
  - Alternatives: per-device 8 would OOM during eval merge; per-device 2 would slow training without benefit.

- **Warmup ratio 0.05, weight decay 0.0.** Same.
  - Source: #405 §11 (Tulu 3 SFT defaults).

- **Max sequence length 1024.** Same as #405; per-question R-cap = 1024 − prompt_len − 8 (Fix D recipe).
  - Source: #405 §11.

- **Marker ` ※` (id 83399).** Canonical per `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md` and #432→#456.
  - Source: #432, #456, project rule.

- **MarkerOnlyDataCollator(tail_tokens=0).** Loss masked to marker token + EOS on positives; EOS-only at post-response slot on negatives.
  - Source: #405 §11; `src/explore_persona_space/train/sft.py`.

- **Contrastive negatives: 4 fixed at ~1:1 ratio.** Mandatory per `.claude/rules/contrastive-negatives.md`; includes default assistant.

- **K-grid {1, 2, 4, 8}.** Same as #405. Doubling gives clean log₂(K) parameterization for the primary test.
