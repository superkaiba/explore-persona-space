## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I'll teach a model a single subtle marker (a tiny symbol) by training it on responses from a small geometrically-tight set of professional personas, but I'll deliberately vary HOW MANY of those personas the marker is taught to (1, 2, 4, or 8). Then I'll measure how often the marker "leaks" into 35 untrained personas that span from "very close" to the training set all the way out to "very far" — so the question "does training into more personas make the behavior leak more uniformly across persona-space" actually gets answered.
- **What I expect:** Both outcomes have real evidence going in. The hypothesis I find more likely: training into more source personas does flatten the leakage-vs-distance gradient at least partially — the model finds a feature that the source personas all share, and that feature is somewhat present in far personas too. But the parent experiment hinted the slope might not change at all (the data couldn't tell), so I genuinely don't know.
- **What would change my mind:** A flat result (slope constant across K) tells me marker training localizes to whatever each source persona is, and stacking sources just adds independent localized leaks without coupling them. A sharp full-flattening (slope goes to zero at K=8) tells me the marker has gone fully persona-invariant — that's the safety-relevant finding.

## 1. Goal

Resolve whether training a behavior into more source personas flattens the leakage-vs-persona-distance gradient (persona-invariance) vs keeps it localized, using a held-out panel that spans the distance range with multiple personas per band so the slope-flattening test is POWERED — the test #405 left unresolved due to a single-far-persona panel. The Goal here is the operational version of the persona-axis instance of the N×M training-to-deployment generalization question (open-q `leak-single-vs-multi` 3.3, primary).

### External

- **Persona Vectors** (Chen et al., Anthropic 2025, arXiv 2507.21509) — a behavior trained into multiple persona slots produces a low-rank "persona-direction" deviation. Whether that deviation distributes uniformly over persona-space or stays anisotropic is operationalized here.
- **Persona Features Control EM** (Wang et al., OpenAI 2025, arXiv 2506.19823) — finds an "EM persona" feature that emerges when training data spans multiple "evil" personas. The slope-flattening question of #478 is the persona-axis analog: does multi-persona training distribute the marker over a shared persona-invariant direction (β_dist → 0) or layer up persona-conditional sub-features that each stay local (β_dist ≈ constant)?

## 3. Hypothesis

- **H1 (FLATTEN, primary):** β_dist(K) becomes less steep with K. The band-averaged (far+very-far+tail − near+near-mid) gap shrinks with log(K). Quantitative: at K=8, the gap is ≤ 50% of the K=1 gap (matching the "half-flattening" used in the power sim). Statistical: slope of gap on log₂(K) is **positive and p<0.05**.
- **H0 / H2 (LOCALIZED, alternative):** β_dist(K) is approximately constant across K. The band-averaged gap does NOT change with log(K) (slope p≥0.05; central estimate within ±15% of the K=1 gap at K=8).
- **Falsification:** H1 is falsified if the gap-shrinkage slope p≥0.10 AND the central estimate of the K=8 gap is ≥85% of the K=1 gap. H2 is falsified if the gap-shrinkage slope is positive at p<0.05 AND the K=8 gap is ≤70% of the K=1 gap.
- **No-comedy survival (MANDATORY GATE, added v2 per FIX 3):** H1 can only be reported as DISTANCE-driven (not comedy-axis-driven) if the gap-shrinkage signal SURVIVES the no-comedy refit (§6 robustness check #4). If the full-panel slope is significant but the no-comedy refit flips direction or its 95% CI excludes the full-panel point estimate, H1 must be reported as "consistent with a comedy-semantic-axis confound" rather than as flattening of the distance gradient.

This is the primary mechanism question. There is a secondary descriptive question (does K continue to raise the OVERALL leakage level, as #405 found?) which is automatic from the cell-level means — confirming or breaking the #405 K main effect at a larger pool.

**Pseudocode for the analyzer's primary test (the new piece):**

```python
def gap_shrinkage_test(rows):
    """Primary test: log2(K)-gap-shrinkage on (far+very-far+tail − near+near-mid) band-avg.

    Robust to single-persona leverage because each K's gap is averaged over ~18
    personas (6 near + 6 near-mid vs 6+5+6 far+very-far+tail).
    """
    NEAR_BANDS = {"near", "near-mid"}
    FAR_BANDS = {"far", "very-far", "tail"}
    Ks = [1, 2, 4, 8]
    gaps, gap_se = [], []
    for K in Ks:
        far_rows = [r["deltaLogP_mean"] for r in rows if r["K"] == K and r["band"] in FAR_BANDS]
        near_rows = [r["deltaLogP_mean"] for r in rows if r["K"] == K and r["band"] in NEAR_BANDS]
        gaps.append(np.mean(far_rows) - np.mean(near_rows))
        gap_se.append(np.sqrt(np.var(far_rows)/len(far_rows) + np.var(near_rows)/len(near_rows)))
    logK = np.log2(Ks)
    slope, intercept, r, p, se = stats.linregress(logK, gaps)
    return {"slope": slope, "p": p, "se": se, "gaps_per_K": dict(zip(Ks, gaps))}
```

**Why code, not a model call?** The analysis is exact-match on numeric data (linear regression over already-computed scalars). A model call would add ~$0.30 per run and add stochasticity. N/A on the unstructured-data classifier exception; this is structured numerical data.

### 4.9 OPTIONAL ARM — per-source distinct markers (decompose A+B→C)

> **Status: OPTIONAL.** Descoped FIRST under any compute pressure (top of §9 auto-descope order). The core experiment's headline (§6.4 + §6.5) does NOT depend on this arm. Level-1 directional superposition analysis (§6.8) runs on existing core cells and is FREE (no new training); Level 2 requires this arm's 12 cells.

**Purpose (dose-aware — REVISED v5).** The core experiment trains ONE shared marker ※ into K source personas and measures joint leakage to held-out C. The intent of this arm is to disentangle (i) SUPERPOSITION of independent per-source localized leaks (the "K=8 is just 8 K=1 cells in superposition" hypothesis — supports LOCALIZED / H2) from (ii) COUPLING into a joint feature (supports FLATTEN / H1).

**Identifiability constraint (binding):** there is a **per-token (per-marker) training-dose asymmetry** between core and arm. In a CORE K-cell, the single ※ token receives gradient from all 400 positive rows. In the matched ARM cell, each marker_i receives gradient from only 400/K rows (200 at K=2, 100 at K=4). The shared ※ embedding is therefore **K× better-trained per-token**, independent of any coupling. Consequence: the arm cleanly identifies TWO of the three outcomes — SUPERPOSITION (`L_shared ≈ superposition(L_distinct)` despite the K× dose advantage) and INTERFERENCE (`L_shared < superposition(L_distinct)`) — but it canNOT cleanly identify positive coupling (`L_shared > superposition(L_distinct)`), because that direction is also the pure-dose prediction. Read the full table in §6.8.

K=2 (2× dose asymmetry) is more interpretable than K=4 (4× asymmetry); the analyzer weights K=2 evidence more heavily. Throughout §4.9 and §6.8, all "coupling" language reads "coupling beyond what per-token dose predicts."

**Future-work note.** A fully quantitative dose-decomposition (vs the directional one this arm provides) would require dose-matched K=1 control cells trained at 200 positives (matched to K=2 per-source dose) and 100 positives (matched to K=4 per-source dose). v5 deliberately does NOT add those cells per the round-2 critic guidance that the prose/stats reframe is sufficient for an honest descriptive arm. Tracked as the next follow-up if the directional read motivates it.

### Primary statistical test

**Band-averaged log₂(K)-gap-shrinkage test.** For each K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}, compute the band-averaged held-out leakage in the NEAR bands (near + near-mid, ~12 personas) and the FAR bands (far + very-far + tail, ~17 personas). Define `gap_K = mean(FAR) − mean(NEAR)`. Regress `gap_K` on `log₂(K)`. **Test:** slope > 0 at p < 0.05.

Why this is the primary test:
- The gap is a band AVERAGE over ~12-17 personas, so single-persona leverage cannot drive the slope (this is the structural fix vs #405's K×min_dist regression where comedian carried the slope).
- log(K) parameterization captures the diminishing-returns shape and gives high power (89-100% in simulation — see §6 below).
- 4 K levels × n averaged across many personas; degrees-of-freedom calculation is straightforward.

### Secondary statistical test

**Mixed-effects K × log(min_dist) interaction.** Same headline regression as #405 but with the new distance-spanning panel. Formula: `deltaLogP_mean ~ K * log(min_dist) + (1|subset) + (1|persona)`. Tool order: pymer4 → rpy2+lme4 → statsmodels.MixedLM with vc_formula (inherited from #405's robust three-tool fallback).

### 6.8 Decomposition analysis (Level 1 + Level 2) — REVISED v5 (dose-aware)

This is the analytical scaffolding for the OPTIONAL arm (§4.9). Level 1 runs WITHOUT the arm (uses only the core's K=1 single-source cells). Both levels are constrained by the **per-token training-dose asymmetry** noted in §4.9: the shared ※ token in a K-cell trains on 400 rows; each K=1 input ※ also trains on 400 rows (so K=1 inputs are 2× over-dosed vs a per-source K=2 cell, 4× over-dosed vs K=4). The analysis spec is built around that constraint, not around it.

**Diagnostic preamble (always emitted).** Phase 4 outputs the per-K per-band mean per-source dose ratio (K=1 input dose / matched K-cell per-source dose) and the per-K per-band cell-seed cluster count — so the reader sees the dose context before reading any decomposition number.

#### Level 1 — directional superposition analysis from K=1 cells (DIRECTIONAL with cluster-robust SEs; runs on core data only — REVISED v5)

The core experiment trains 8 K=1 single-source cells (※ trained into one POOL_16 persona each, 400 positives each), giving a per-source leakage matrix `L_{K=1}[A, C]` = on-policy `log P(※)` shift on held-out persona C when ※ is trained into A alone (averaged across the 2 seeds).

For each shared-marker K≥2 cell with source set S = {A_1, ..., A_K}, predict the joint leakage to each held-out C from the K=1 cells of S's members under four combiners. **Pre-registered primary null = MEAN** (per critic guidance: under the stated dose-scaling model, each K=1@400 input is 2× over-dosed vs a per-source K=2 cell — `mean` halves the K=1 inputs back toward the 200-equivalent per-source dose, making it the natural linear-dose-scaled additive null). The other three combiners are descriptive sensitivity.

| combiner | formula | role | what it assumes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **mean (PRE-REGISTERED PRIMARY NULL)** | `mean_i L_{K=1}[A_i, C]` | primary null | linear-dose-scaled additivity: K=1 inputs are over-dosed and averaging halves them back toward the per-source K-cell dose |
| log-sum-exp / probability-sum | `log(sum_i exp(L_{K=1}[A_i, C]))` | sensitivity | probability-space independent-sum (each source contributes a separable marker-probability bump) |
| max | `max_i L_{K=1}[A_i, C]` | sensitivity | saturating: "C inherits whichever single source leaks strongest" |
| fitted linear | `α + Σ_i β_i L_{K=1}[A_i, C]`, **leave-one-K≥2-cell-out CV** (NOT persona-level CV — persona-level leaks within-cell correlation and shrinks the residual artificially) | DIAGNOSTIC of dose-scaling, NOT hero combiner | β_i ≈ 0.5 ⇒ linear-dose additive null is correct; β_i ≈ 1 ⇒ K=1 inputs effectively dose-correct; β_i far from these ⇒ non-linear superposition |

**REVISED v5 statistical spec.** The decomposition fit uses **cluster-robust SEs at the (cell_id, seed) level** (e.g. statsmodels `cov_type='cluster', cov_kwds={'groups': df[['cell_id','seed']].apply(tuple, axis=1)}`). The true unit of replication is the ~48 K≥2 cell-seed clusters (24 K≥2 cells × 2 seeds), NOT the 2240 cell-persona observations — the 35 personas + 2 seeds within a cell are not independent. The v4 "2240 observations → inferential" framing was pseudo-replication and is REMOVED.

Outputs:
- Per-K held-out R² of each combiner under leave-one-K≥2-cell-out CV (the cluster-correct CV).
- Mean signed residual `(observed − mean-combiner)` per band per K, with cluster-robust 95% CI.
- The fitted-linear β_i diagnostic per K.
- Direction summary: per (band, K), how many of the ~48 cell-seed clusters have residual > 0 vs < 0 vs |residual| < noise-floor (a tertile read robust to outliers).

**Held-out-R² floor (interpretability gate, REVISED v5).** The principled combiner (mean) MUST achieve held-out R² ≥ 0.5 at K=2 to be interpretable. At K=4 and K=8 the dose asymmetry grows (4× and 8×), so the floor relaxes to ≥ 0.4 at K=4 and to "directional only — no quantitative claim" at K=8. If the principled-combiner held-out R² < 0.4 at K=4/K=8, the TL;DR MUST report Level-1 as "uninterpretable at this K under the planned design" — not bury it in a caveat box. Don't run inferential significance tests on the residual when the combiner itself doesn't generalize.

**Caveat to state in clean-result (REVISED v5).** Two stacked imperfections: (i) the K=1 cells are SEPARATE LoRAs from the K≥2 cells (cross-model additivity assumption); (ii) the K=1 cells are 2-8× over-dosed per source vs the matched K-cell. Level 1 cannot cleanly separate "the result IS superposition" from "the result is superposition AT THE OVER-DOSED INPUT and we're seeing the over-dose pass through the combiner." Level 2 controls for (i) but not (ii); a future-work K=1@200/100 control would control for (ii).

#### Level 2 — shared-vs-distinct in-model comparison (RUNS ONLY IF §4.9 arm runs; DESCRIPTIVE — REVISED v5)

For each of the 6 arm cells with source set S, the arm produces per-marker leakages `L_distinct[marker_i, C]` for each source persona A_i ∈ S. Compute the in-model superposition prediction via the same four combiners as Level 1 (the same pre-registered MEAN primary null, with the other three as sensitivity), applied to `L_distinct` (no cross-model assumption). Compare to the MATCHED shared-marker core cell's joint leakage `L_shared[S, C]`.

**Interpretation table — REVISED v5 (dose-aware).** The shared ※ marker in the matched core cell received K× more per-token gradient than each marker_i in the arm cell. The naive interpretation `L_shared > superposition(L_distinct)` ⇒ "shared TOKEN induces coupling" is wrong: that direction is the EXPECTED pure-dose prediction. The dose-aware reads:

| outcome | interpretation |
|---|---|
| `L_shared ≈ superposition(L_distinct)` | **STRONG evidence FOR independent superposition.** The shared marker's K× per-token dose advantage failed to open a gap over the per-marker superposition. This is the cleanest positive read the arm produces. |
| `L_shared > superposition(L_distinct)` | **AMBIGUOUS.** Fully consistent with pure per-token-dose advantage of the shared marker (K× more gradient); coupling-via-shared-token is UNIDENTIFIED at this design. Read this as "consistent with at-least-dose-driven enhancement" — not as positive coupling evidence. A quantitative coupling claim requires a future-work K=1@200/100 dose-matched control. |
| `L_shared < superposition(L_distinct)` | **STRONGEST evidence for cross-source INTERFERENCE through the shared token.** The shared token's K× dose advantage was not just neutralized but reversed — distinct tokens leak MORE despite each receiving K× less gradient. This is a definite finding if it holds. |

**Weight K=2 over K=4** in the synthesis: 2× dose asymmetry leaves more headroom for a `≈` read to be informative than 4× asymmetry does. If K=2 says `≈` and K=4 says `>`, lead with the K=2 superposition read.

**REVISED v5 statistical spec.** Paired bootstrap over the matched (shared_cell, distinct_cell) pairs at fixed (source-set, seed) — 6 K=2 pairs + 6 K=4 pairs at each of 2 seeds = 12 matched pairs (effectively 6 distinct source-set comparisons per K, each seen at 2 seeds). Persona resampling is the WRONG cluster (35 personas share one LoRA, underestimates SE 3-5×) and is removed. Primary report = **direction-agreement counts per K** (e.g. "5/6 K=2 matched pairs point shared > distinct at both seeds; 1/6 pairs point shared < distinct"). Bootstrap CI on the per-K mean gap is reported as a secondary descriptive number, with the explicit note that 6 paired clusters per K is small.

**Representativeness caveat — REVISED v5.** Level-2 results MUST be narrated as "for these 6 specific source sets" (named explicitly in the clean-result), NOT "K=2 / K=4 in general." Generalizing the directional read requires the planned future-work follow-up (more source sets, ideally with dose-matched K=1 controls).

## 11. Decision Rationale

All training hyperparameters are inherited from #405 (the parent) for single-variable comparability. #405's clean-result + its own §11 establish the grounding chain; here we cite `Source: #405` for inherited values and add fresh rationale for the new design knobs.

- **Base model: Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct.** Same as #405. Single open-weights model the project's persona work is built on.
  - Source: #405 (parent); broader project standard.
  - Alternatives: 14B / 32B — outside the project's compute envelope for a 64-cell sweep.

- **LoRA r=16, α=32, attn-only (q/k/v/o), dropout=0.05.** Non-saturating anchor that leaves on-policy `log P(marker)` ~5-10 nats below ceiling — critical for the recipe sweep to have anything to push against (#311, #448).
  - Source: #405 §11; validated non-saturating at lr=5e-6, 2 epochs.
  - Alternatives: full MLP+attn LoRA r=32 — saturates the anchor (#448), kills measurement.

- **Learning rate 5e-6.** Tuned to keep the anchor non-saturating at 2 epochs in #405.
  - Source: #405 §11; #311 / #448 (anchor-saturation diagnostics).
  - Alternatives: 2e-6 (too weak; #311 showed marker barely takes); 2e-5 (saturates inside 1 epoch on Qwen-7B-Instruct).

- **2 epochs.** Same.
  - Source: #405 §11.
  - Alternatives: 1 epoch (under-training; weaker source-rate); 3 epochs (saturates per #311).

- **per_device_batch=4, grad_accum=4, bf16.** Standard for 7B LoRA on H100; effective batch 16.
  - Source: #405 §11.
  - Alternatives: per-device 8 would OOM during eval merge; per-device 2 would slow training without benefit.

- **Warmup ratio 0.05, weight decay 0.0.** Same.
  - Source: #405 §11 (Tulu 3 SFT defaults).

- **Max sequence length 1024.** Same as #405; per-question R-cap = 1024 − prompt_len − 8 (Fix D recipe).
  - Source: #405 §11.

- **Marker ` ※` (id 83399).** Canonical per `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md` and #432→#456.
  - Source: #432, #456, project rule.

- **MarkerOnlyDataCollator(tail_tokens=0).** Loss masked to marker token + EOS on positives; EOS-only at post-response slot on negatives.
  - Source: #405 §11; `src/explore_persona_space/train/sft.py`.

- **Contrastive negatives: 4 fixed at ~1:1 ratio.** Mandatory per `.claude/rules/contrastive-negatives.md`; includes default assistant.
  - Source: #405 §11; #18, #207, #383 (positive-only leaks uniformly).

- **K-grid {1, 2, 4, 8}.** Same as #405. Doubling gives clean log₂(K) parameterization for the primary test.
  - Source: #405 §11.
  - Alternatives: {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} — adds a doubling but requires pool >= 16 and exposes whether the trend extrapolates past 8. **REJECTED** for compute: adds 8 K=16 cells = 25% wall-time. The current pool size (16) admits K=16 only with the single all-pool subset, so it'd recreate #405's "particular octet at the high-K end" confound but at K=16. Better to deepen K=8 with 8 subsets than add a single K=16.

- **Subsets per K = 8 (random draws from C(16, K)).** The main fix vs #405's single K=8 subset.
  - Source: §6 power simulation in this plan (gap-shrinkage power 89% at 30% flattening for seeds=2 × subs=8; jumps to 100% at 50%); ungrounded — needs smoke-test extends this if reality is noisier than the 1.5-nat residual SD assumption.
  - Alternatives: 6 (under-powers gap-shrinkage at 30% flattening to ~70%); 10 (raises gap power marginally 89% → 90%, costs +25% compute — REJECTED).

- **Seeds = 2 (42, 137).** Matches #405. Validates the slope-flattening claim isn't a single training-init artifact.
  - Source: #405 §11; power sim (seeds=3 raises gap-shrinkage power 89% → 90% — diminishing returns).
  - Alternatives: 3 seeds — REJECTED per power sim (marginal lift).

- **Source pool size = 16.** Smallest pool that admits 8 distinct random K=8 subsets (C(16,8)=12870; expected pairwise subset overlap = 4 personas, gives clean subset-level variation).
  - Source: §4.5 design validation in this plan (band occupancy stable at K=8 worst case).
  - Alternatives: 12 (C(12,8)=495 distinct octets — fewer truly distinct, more overlap); 20 (C(20,8)=125970 — overkill, raises Phase 1 R-gen cost +25% for no analytic benefit).

- **Source pool composition (geometrically tight, professional cluster).** Chosen by greedy min-max-radius from **54 professional-category candidates** (PROF_CATS in `scripts/run_100_persona_leakage.py`'s 111-pool, EXCLUDING the 2 negatives — `software_engineer, kindergarten_teacher` — that have centroids; the BUG 1 fix). Radius 0.0286 vs #405 pool radius 0.054. Tightness ensures held-out band assignments stable across K-subsets.
  - Source: §4.5 design validation in this plan (worst-case band occupancy at K=8 ≥5 except Mid band ≥2).
  - Alternatives: dispersed pool (random 16 from 111) — band occupancy at K=8 would have very-far ≤2 per subset (matches the clarifier's worst-case sim caveat); REJECTED. Including software_engineer in POOL (v1's attempt, BUG 1) — REJECTED: contradictory marker/no-marker training rows since SE is also in NEGATIVES_FIXED.

- **Held-out panel size = 35.** Smallest panel that gives ≥5 personas per band (except very-far which has only 5 in the entire 111-pool excluding the tail bucket).
  - Source: §4.5 design validation; §6 power simulation.
  - Alternatives: 24 (4 per band) — borderline at K=8 worst case for very-far; 50 — adds Phase 1 R-gen time for diminishing power return.

- **Held-out panel composition (specific 35).** Picked by min-distance to the 16-pool, taking the closest 6 in each band per §4.3.
  - Source: §4.3 + the 111-persona pool definition (`scripts/run_100_persona_leakage.py`).

- **Primary test: log₂(K)-gap-shrinkage on band-averaged (FAR−NEAR) contrast.** Robust to single-persona leverage by averaging over ~12-17 personas per K per band group.
  - Source: §6 power simulation in this plan (power 89% at 30% flattening, FPR 2.3%); diagnostic motivation in #405's leave-one-persona-out finding that comedian carried the K×dist slope.
  - Alternatives: K×min_dist regression (the #405 primary) — kept as SECONDARY but failure mode is exactly what #405 hit (single-persona leverage); REJECTED as primary.

- **Distance metric: layer-20 cosine to NEAREST trained persona.** Same as #405 primary. Persona-distance rule in `.claude/rules/persona-distance-metrics.md` recommends layer-20 cosine for the cosine-similarity DV line.
  - Source: #405 §11; project rule.
  - Alternatives: mean-distance — covered in #405 robustness as min-vs-mean; will run as a sensitivity refit. JS-divergence — see §4.7 (optional close of #405 gap).

- **35-persona, 6-band held-out panel selection.** Manual per-band selection by min-distance to pool.
  - Source: §4.3; band thresholds chosen to match #405's distance scale.

- **OPTIONAL arm (§4.9) distinct-marker set: 8 single-token markers from a tokenizer search.** Chosen for single-tokenness under Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (verified this turn — only 6 of 35 unicode-symbol candidates passed; extended search added Greek capitals + double-struck letters) and for rarity (BPE position 16k–93k, the rare regime). Marker_1 stays ` ※` per the canonical project marker rule.
  - Source: tokenizer search this turn (8 confirmed single-token; § flagged as least-rare, swappable by Phase 0b validator if base-logp spread > 2 nats); `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md` (marker_1 canonicalization).
  - Alternatives: 4-marker set (covers only K∈{2,4}; insufficient headroom if a future follow-up runs K=8 arm — REJECTED for headroom); a single-marker arm with different per-source MASK rather than distinct tokens (changes the loss surface, breaks the matched-recipe property) — REJECTED.

- **OPTIONAL arm cell layout: 6 source sets × 2 seeds = 12 cells, matched to core cells K2_c08–c10 + K4_c16–c18.** Smallest layout that gives 6 matched pairs (3 K=2 + 3 K=4) so the qualitative direction-agreement check at Level 2 has enough samples for a clear directional pattern. Arm is descriptive/exploratory, not powered for inferential testing — explicitly stated.
  - Source: §4.9.2 + §6.8 power note (12 cells too few for inferential test; matched-pair design = 6 pairs per K is the minimum for direction-agreement reads); no precedent issue for distinct-marker arms (new design).
  - Alternatives: 8 source sets × 2 seeds = 16 cells (+33% arm GPU-h for marginal qualitative-read benefit — REJECTED); 6 sets × 3 seeds = 18 cells (raises seed coverage but doesn't change inferential power; REJECTED as not worth the 50% compute increase for a descriptive arm).
