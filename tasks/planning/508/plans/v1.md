## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run.** Train the same base model two ways — once by editing only a small set of adapter weights ("LoRA"), once by editing every weight ("full fine-tune") — on the *same* contrastive recipe that teaches one persona ("villain") to drop a special marker after its answers while explicitly teaching other personas (including the bare default assistant) NOT to. Then check, on 15 personas the model never saw during training, how often each version slips and drops the marker anyway.
- **What I expect.** Editing every weight will leak the marker to bystander personas more than editing only the small adapter — full fine-tuning is less "persona-localized" — when compared at the same source-implant strength. The biggest concern is that the bystander reading saturates before the two arms separate, so I deliberately stop training early at several budgets per arm and compare on the source-implant curve, not at a fixed step count.
- **What would change my mind.** Either arm leaks at the same rate at matched source strength (the "all weight updates eventually behave the same" story), OR LoRA actually leaks *more* once you control for implant strength (the low-rank adapter could be picking a direction that is broadcast across all personas). A null at matched source rate is genuinely informative — it would mean LoRA's persona-localization story doesn't depend on the low-rank structure. There's a third honest outcome: if the 3 budgets per arm don't bracket the matched-rate target (all 3 land too light or too heavy), I'll mark the answer INDETERMINATE for that arm and report the curves I have, rather than extrapolating off the end.

## 2. Prior Work

**In-house parent.** #448 (`tasks/awaiting_promotion/448`) ran 11 LoRA cells over the contrastive recipe with rs-LoRA r=32 / α=64 / lr=1e-5 / 3 epochs, and found that at this training budget the on-policy log-prob saturates (argmax = marker on 264/264 held-out cells × personas, trained `g_logprob` sub-nat from 0.0 on every cell). The reframe sentence: "Once leakage is measured on the model's own generations, none of four contrastive-LoRA recipe knobs moves bystander marker leakage at this anchor's training budget, where the on-policy log-prob has saturated." This experiment INHERITS the contrastive recipe, the on-policy DV, the 15-persona held-out bystander panel, and seed 42 — but changes ONE variable: LoRA vs full FT. The saturation finding directly motivates the §0/§4 design choice to pick a **less-trained anchor**.

**In-house sibling rig.** #472 (`src/explore_persona_space/experiments/contrastive_neg_geometry_472/`) is the active code base for this line — its `train_cell.py` is the in-process LoRA path I will fork the LoRA arm from, and its `__init__.py` carries the canonical recipe constants. The fallback sub-ceiling recipe (`FALLBACK_LORA_R=16, FALLBACK_LEARNING_RATE=5e-6, FALLBACK_EPOCHS=0.5`) was specified in #472's plan precisely against the #448 saturation; I adopt the same fallback target ("`g_logprob` sits ≥5 nats below 0.0") as the budget-selection criterion.

**Construct-validity story.** #432 → #456 (`tasks/archived/456`) established that teacher-forced log P(marker) at a fixed canned response that the model never generates produces artifacts — every safety-relevant marker eval MUST measure on-policy (the model writes its own answer, then read log P(※) at the slot immediately after, trained − base). This is the DV in both arms.

**Spiritual-sibling papers — the gap.** *Persona Vectors* (Chen et al., 2025, arXiv:2507.21509) and *Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment* (Wang et al., 2025, arXiv:2506.19823, Mossing) both used **full fine-tuning** (GPT-4o family). Every behavior-implantation result in this project (#247, #329, #383, #448, #472) used LoRA. The two sibling papers' localization stories therefore live in a fundamentally different optimization regime from ours; whether the LoRA-only localization gradient survives under full FT is currently unknown — this experiment is the bridge.

**Full-FT 7B-class recipes.** The arxiv MCP returned Tulu 3 (arXiv:2411.15124v5) as the closest match. The open-instruct repo in this codebase ships the validated Tulu 3 8B SFT config at `external/open-instruct/configs/train_configs/sft/tulu3_8b_preview_mix_v3.9-noncommercial.yaml`: lr=5.0e-06, lr_scheduler=linear, warmup_ratio=0.03, weight_decay=0.0, per_device_bs=1 × grad_accum=2 × 8 GPU = eff 16, 2 epochs. The older Tulu 2 7B config (`tulu2_7b.yaml`) uses lr=2.0e-05, grad_accum=16, eff 128, 3 epochs — Llama-2-7b base. I adopt the **Tulu 3 8B lr=5e-6** because it's closer to our model size+date+post-training; the eff-batch is rescaled to fit the 4×H100 pod (per_device_bs=1, grad_accum=16, 4 GPU → eff 64).

## 3. Hypothesis

**Primary (H1).** At matched source implant strength, **full fine-tuning leaks more than LoRA** to bystander personas: mean ΔG over the 15 held-out personas is higher in the full-FT arm than in the LoRA arm at a matched source-self ΔG of 8 ± 1 nats. Threshold for declaring an effect: matched-rate gap > 1.0 nat (the #448 planned threshold), with the within-eval CI (per-persona × per-question on N=15×20=300 ΔG values per cell) excluding zero on the gap.

**Kill criterion / null (H0).** Matched-rate gap ≤ 1.0 nat, OR the gap is in the OPPOSITE direction (LoRA leaks more). A null is informative: it says the persona-localization story established for LoRA in #247 / #329 / #383 doesn't depend on the low-rank parameterization, which means the project's localization findings transfer to the full-FT regime the two sibling papers used.

**Secondary (H2).** Per-bystander leakage ΔG correlates with cosine distance to the nearest trained contrastive-negative persona in BOTH arms (the #448 secondary), AND the **slope is shallower** for full FT (a flatter distance→leakage gradient = less persona-localized). Threshold: per-cell Spearman ρ_full FT − ρ_LoRA difference > 0.10 in the predicted (less negative) direction.

**Tertiary (H3).** Leakage to the bare default-assistant persona (`qwen_default`, which is ALWAYS in the contrastive negatives) is higher under full FT than LoRA at matched source rate. This is the safety-relevant slice (open-q 3.7) — leakage to the default context is the bystander class we most care about.

6. **Logs per-step marker dynamics to WandB** via a new `MarkerDynamicsCallback` (specified below) — REQUIRED by `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md` § "Track log-prob DYNAMICS, not just the endpoint" for marker-implant experiments. Same callback fires on BOTH the LoRA arm (`train_cell.py`) and the full-FT arm (the new `train_cell_fullft.py`) so the trajectories are directly comparable.

**`MarkerDynamicsCallback` (NEW; fires on both arms).**
- **Fixed trajectory probe set** (identical across all 6 cells, frozen JSON `data/issue_508/dynamics_probes.json`):
  - 1 source persona (`villain`) × 5 questions = 5 source probes for `source_ΔG_trajectory` + `source_emission_rate_trajectory`.
  - 3 bystander personas (`kindergarten_teacher`, `data_scientist`, `assistant` — all in the 15-persona held-out panel, drawn from the SHA-derived held-outs to avoid overlap with the 12 never-trained-as-negative set the headline DV averages over) × 5 questions each = 15 bystander probes for `bystander_mean_ΔG_trajectory` + `bystander_mean_emission_rate_trajectory`.
  - Total: 20 probe (persona, question) pairs. The 5 questions per persona are drawn deterministically (SHA-256-derived seed=42) from the 20-question held-out eval pool, so the dynamics probes are a strict subset of the headline-DV pool (no extra eval surface).
- **Firing cadence:** **every 4 training steps for both arms**, calibrated to give 8 trajectory snapshots over each arm's full sweep — LoRA at 0.5 epoch + eff_batch=16 → ~31 steps/epoch ≈ 16 steps for `lora_b2`'s 0.5 epoch (~4 snapshots per `lora_b2`, 8 snapshots per `lora_b3`); full-FT at 0.5 epoch + eff_batch=64 → ~16 steps/epoch ≈ 8 steps for `ft_b2` (~2 snapshots per `ft_b2`, 4 per `ft_b3`). The cadence is **ungrounded — needs smoke-test**: smoke gate 6 verifies it gives readable trajectories; if either arm has <4 snapshots on its endpoint cell, halve to every-2-steps for that arm.
- **At each fire:**
  1. The callback's evaluator generates greedy responses (max_new_tokens=2048) for the 20 probes UNDER THE TRAINER'S CURRENT MODEL STATE (LoRA: via in-process `model.generate` while suspending grad accumulation; full-FT: via in-process `accelerator.unwrap_model().generate`). Both arms use the same generation surface as `eval_one_cell.py` but called in-process.
  2. Score trained log P(※) at the post-response slot on each probe's current-step R (the model used in step 1).
  3. Score base log P(※) on the SAME current-step R (lazy base model loaded once at callback init on CPU offload; per-call inference moves the relevant slice onto GPU). One base log P score per probe per fire = 20 × n_fires per cell.
  4. WandB log: `source_ΔG`, `bystander_mean_ΔG`, `source_emission_rate` (argmax-of-※), `bystander_mean_emission_rate`, plus the 4 per-bystander-persona breakdowns. All keyed by `global_step` so the WandB run renders a clean per-arm × per-budget trajectory.
- **Cost.** Per fire = 20 generations + 40 log-prob scoring passes (20 trained + 20 base) ≈ 90 s on a 1× H100 for LoRA (in-process), ~120 s on 4× H100 for full-FT (one rank does the eval, others wait). With 8 fires × 6 cells × ~100 s = ~80 min total added across the sweep — under the 30-min-per-cell margin, so well within §9's existing buffer. (See §9 table footnote.)
- **`Source:` for the cadence + probe-set size.** Probe set size (1 src × 5 q + 3 bystander × 5 q = 20) chosen so total in-training eval cost stays under 5% of training cost per cell at the every-4-step cadence; `Source: planner judgment, ungrounded — needs smoke-test`. Bystander persona pick (`kindergarten_teacher / data_scientist / assistant`): `Source: #448 SHA-derived held-out set` (the 3 personas the #448 planner picked for diversity in the same held-out panel).

**Path B (alternative, rejected unless Path A's smoke fails).** Extend `scripts/launch_stage.py` + `train/distributed.py::_build_stage_config` with marker-only-loss fields AND the dynamics callback hooks, and patch into open-instruct's `finetune.py`. Surface area is larger (open-instruct's training loop is theirs, not ours); revisit only if Path A's smoke run shows divergence vs the LoRA arm's loss curve on the smoke cell.

### 4.5 Smoke phase (sweep with 2 cells)

Run `lora_b2` and `ft_b2` BEFORE provisioning the full sweep. Pass criteria:
1. **Implant-validity gate** — source-self ΔG ≥ 5 nats on `villain` (both cells).
2. **Sub-ceiling gate** — held-out mean `g_logprob` ≥ 5 nats below 0.0 (both cells); if either saturates, ALL three budgets in that arm get shifted DOWN one step (the matching-rule auto-descope, §9).
3. **Architectural parity** — LoRA cell's loss curve matches #472's `lora_b2` (anchor) loss curve to within 5% (byte-identical-modulo-numerics check via WandB run diff). If divergent, halt and post `epm:failure v1` (`failure_class: code, reason: lora_arm_diverges_from_472_anchor`).
4. **Full-FT does NOT NaN** — first 50 steps' loss must be finite + monotonically decreasing on log scale. If NaN, halt and post `epm:failure v1` (`failure_class: code, reason: fullft_diverges_at_lr_5e-6`). Mitigation if this fires: drop lr to 2e-6 and re-smoke.
5. **Matched-rate bracketing (NEW per MF4)** — BOTH `lora_b2` and `ft_b2` cells' source-self ΔG must land in the **7-9 nat band**. The matched-rate read at 8 ± 1 nat is the load-bearing comparison; if either middle-budget cell falls outside this band, the 3-budget sweep around it will not bracket the 8-nat target and the headline becomes an extrapolation (silent under-claim if the planner-authorized §4.4 budget shift doesn't fire). On a miss, the §4.4 epoch-fraction shift fires automatically:
   - **If either `*_b2` cell under-trains (source ΔG < 7):** shift to {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} epoch fractions for the under-shooting arm (lora and/or full-FT — applied independently per arm).
   - **If either `*_b2` cell over-trains (source ΔG > 9):** shift to {0.125, 0.25, 0.5} for the over-shooting arm.
   - **Re-smoke the new `*_b2` cell** of the shifted arm before launching the full sweep (one extra ~30 min wall cost per arm shifted — folded into §9 buffer).
6. **Trajectory readability (NEW per MF2)** — the `MarkerDynamicsCallback` produces ≥4 trajectory snapshots over the `*_b2` cell's training run (3 budget-mid + endpoint). If either smoke cell hits <4 snapshots, halve the dynamics-callback cadence (every-4-steps → every-2-steps) for that arm in the full sweep. Cheap — the cadence is `ungrounded — needs smoke-test` per §4.2.

### 4.6 Eval

Per-cell on-policy DV pipeline (one vLLM job per cell + one base job, reused across cells):

1. **Generate per cell — TRAINED R.** For each of the 6 trained cells, for each of the 15 held-out personas × 20 generic questions, the trained model writes its own greedy response (temp=0, **max_new_tokens=2048** — required by `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md` "≥ 2× longest trained completion (default ≥ 2048)" because #472 caps trained generation at `MAX_NEW_TOKENS_GEN=1024` so worst-case longest trained = 1024 → eval must be ≥ 2048; avoids the #260 silent-truncation failure mode). Call this `R_cell`. 300 responses per cell × 6 cells = 1800 trained-model generations total. vLLM batched (most completions terminate <1024 anyway, so the 2× cap is a safety margin, not a wall-time hit).
2. **Trained log P(※) on R_cell.** At the slot immediately after the trained model's own response `R_cell`, read trained `log P(` ※` | T_persona(q) + R_cell)`. For LoRA cells, use `vllm.LoRARequest`; for full-FT cells, load the merged HF checkpoint directly (separate vLLM `LLM.from_pretrained` per full-FT cell — this is the one place full-FT eval costs more than LoRA eval).
3. **Base log P(※) on the SAME R_cell — per-cell subtraction.** Load the BASE model (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, no adapter, no fine-tune) and score base `log P(` ※` | T_persona(q) + R_cell)` at the same slot as step 2, on each cell's OWN `R_cell` text. This means scoring the base model on each trained cell's R (300 base log P scoring passes × 6 cells = 1800 base log P scores total), NOT on a frozen base-generated R. The canonical pattern per `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md` + #448's body ("for each held-out (persona × question) the trained model writes its own greedy answer, then I read `log P(※)` at the slot immediately after, subtract the base model's log-prob at the same slot on the same generated context"). The base model is loaded ONCE for all 1800 scoring passes (no re-load per cell). **There is one base log P per (cell × persona × question); there is NOT one frozen base log P shared across cells.**
4. **ΔG_cell = trained_logp(R_cell) − base_logp(R_cell)**, both terms scored on the SAME `R_cell` text (per cell, per persona, per question). 300 ΔG values per cell. Avoids the #432 → #456 anti-pattern in a different guise: if base log P were scored on a frozen base-generated R while trained log P is scored on each cell's different trained R, the subtraction would conflate "the trained model writes different text" (different lengths / styles / registers per arm) with "the trained model leaks more". Per-cell base subtraction on the SAME R closes that confound.

## 5. Conditions and Controls

See §4.4 table for the 7 named conditions. Controls:

- **Base condition** (untrained Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct) — rules out "the marker leaks from base prior" (it doesn't, per #448's base-prior measurement: log P(※) on base sits at −24 nats on every held-out persona).
- **Source-self ΔG ≥ 5 nat gate per cell** — rules out "the implant didn't take, so leakage measurement is meaningless".
- **Constant-emission gate (per-cell std across 24-persona × 20-question grid ≥ 1.5 nats)** — rules out "the trained model just emits the marker everywhere at constant high prob"; passes mechanically when source ΔG is far above base ΔG.
- **Sub-ceiling gate (held-out `g_logprob` ≥ 5 nats below 0.0)** — rules out the #448 saturation trap. If this fails on either arm, descope per §9.
- **`g_logprob` symmetry check** — within each arm, the trained `g_logprob` distribution across (cell × held-out persona) pairs should NOT have a different shape from the LoRA arm; if full-FT shows much more variance, the matched-source-rate comparison is confounded (note as a scope caveat).
- **No `coupling_merged/` directory leftover after each full-FT cell** — the #404/#458 disk-quota incident pattern; the dispatcher's `try/finally` deletes the merged dir after upload (per `.claude/rules/upload-policy.md`).

## 6. Evaluation

**Primary statistic.** Matched-rate gap = `held_out_mean_ΔG[ft_at_source_8]` − `held_out_mean_ΔG[lora_at_source_8]`, with both terms linearly interpolated between the two closest budgets per arm.

**Headline CI — crossed cluster bootstrap (NOT flat bootstrap over 300 values).** Per-cell 95% CI computed via **crossed cluster bootstrap with personas as the primary resampling unit**: 1000 replicates; each replicate (a) draws 15 personas with replacement from the held-out panel, (b) draws 20 questions with replacement WITHIN each resampled persona, (c) computes per-persona mean ΔG on the resampled question set, (d) averages across the resampled personas → one per-replicate cell-mean. The cell's headline CI is the empirical 2.5/97.5 percentile across the 1000 replicate cell-means.

This is the standard crossed-cluster bootstrap for nested observational designs (the 20 questions per persona are NOT independent — #448 documents a 7.3-nat persona-structure spread in the base-model marker log-prob, so questions within a persona share the persona's offset; a flat bootstrap over 300 ΔG values would treat questions-within-persona as independent, underestimating CI width by ≈ √20 ≈ 4.5× and inflating false-positive risk on the "CI excludes 0" gate).

**Matched-rate gap CI propagation.** Each of the 1000 replicates produces 3 per-arm cell-means under the same resampled (persona, question) draw across cells. For each replicate: re-fit the linear interpolation per arm across the 3 cells' means, read each arm's held-out mean at source ΔG = 8 nat, take the FT − LoRA gap. Headline gap CI is the empirical 2.5/97.5 percentile of the 1000 replicate gaps. (Do NOT just average per-cell CI widths through the interpolation — that conflates within-cell variance with across-arm variance differently than full propagation.)

**Threshold for declaring H1:** matched-rate gap > +1.0 nat AND the crossed-cluster bootstrap 95% CI on the gap excludes zero.

**Permutation null (supplementary — NOT the load-bearing test).** 10,000 random reassignments of arm labels (LoRA / full-FT) across the 6 trained cells, recomputing the matched-rate gap under each reassignment. **p-floor = 1/20 = 0.05** at this design (3 LoRA + 3 full-FT, C(6,3)=20 distinct label assignments), so the permutation p can never go below 0.05 — treat as a sanity check on direction + monotonicity, not as the load-bearing significance test. Lead with the cluster-bootstrap CI; report permutation p alongside.

**Secondary statistic (H2).** Per-cell Spearman ρ between per-persona ΔG and cosine-distance-to-nearest-trained-negative, on the 15-persona held-out panel. Compute ρ at each cell. Test ρ_ft (averaged over budgets) − ρ_lora (averaged over budgets) > +0.10 in the predicted (flatter, less negative) direction. Caveat from #448: the cosine-distance signal is partly base-model persona structure (the trained ρ is essentially −1 × base ρ), so report the **base-residualized** version too: `ΔG − base_ΔG_persona_mean` vs cosine distance.

**Tertiary statistic (H3).** Restrict the held-out ΔG to `persona = qwen_default` (1 persona × 20 questions = 20 values per cell). Matched-rate gap restricted to this slice; report alongside primary.

**Post-train bracketing check (NEW per MF4 — runs BEFORE the matched-rate gap is computed).** After all 6 trained cells finish eval, confirm that EACH arm has **at least one budget with source ΔG < 7 nat AND at least one budget with source ΔG > 9 nat** (or equivalently: the 3 cells' source ΔGs bracket the 8 ± 1 nat target from both sides). If an arm fails to bracket — i.e., all 3 budgets fall on one side of the target band — mark **H1 INDETERMINATE for that direction** (insufficient source-rate coverage to non-extrapolate the matched-rate read) and report only the descriptive curves + the per-cell ΔG values + the trajectory figures, NOT the matched-rate gap. Honest update to the §0.0 TL;DR "What would change my mind" framing: a null at matched source rate is informative; an INDETERMINATE verdict (bracketing failure) is its own outcome distinct from null and from H1.

## 10. Pod preference

**One `ft-7b` pod (4× H100), not two pods.** Rationale:
- The full-FT arm REQUIRES 4 GPU for ZeRO-3 (7B full FT + optimizer state ~50 GB doesn't fit single H100).
- The LoRA arm runs on 1 GPU (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0`) WHILE the full-FT arm is idle (sequential schedule). No idle-GPU wastage because the LoRA total wall is only 1.5h on a single GPU.
- Avoids two-pod coordination overhead (separate SSH, separate WandB pod-prefix, separate HF cache, separate disk quota — each ~130 GB MooseFS).
- Disk: 7B base + 3 full-FT checkpoints × ~14 GB merged each = ~42 GB; LoRA adapter ~300 MB each × 3 = trivial. With `EPM_SKIP_INLINE_CHECKPOINT_UPLOAD=1` and delete-after-eval per `.claude/rules/upload-policy.md`, stays under the 130 GB MooseFS per-pod quota.
