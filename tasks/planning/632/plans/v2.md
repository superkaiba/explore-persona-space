<h1>Plan v1 — Issue #632: Assistant-proximal negative swap (does suppression reach the default from cluster-proximal negatives?)</h1>

<section class="plan-tldr">
<h2>0.0 — TL;DR (plain English)</h2>
<ul>
<li><span class="label">What I'll run:</span> In a sister experiment I just finished (#610), I taught a "villain" character to end its answers with a rare symbol, while training four other characters NOT to use it — and found the plain default chatbot stayed protected from the symbol even though I never trained it. The open question: is the default protected because of <em>where it sits</em> among the characters, or because protection <em>spreads to it</em> from the nearby characters I did train? Here I rerun the exact same setup but swap one of those four trainer characters — replacing a far-away one ("journalist") with one that sits right next to the default in the model's internal map ("programmer") — and check whether the never-trained default and a generic "assistant" persona move at all.</li>
<li><span class="label">What I expect:</span> Nothing moves. The default and the assistant should stay just as protected as in #610, which would say their protection is about their fixed position, not something that radiates out from whichever neighbors I happened to train.</li>
<li><span class="label">What would change my mind:</span> If the default or assistant noticeably shifts (protected more, or protected less) once a close neighbor is in the trainer set, that would mean training a character quietly shields or exposes a whole region around it, not just that character. That is the outcome that actually matters for the safety question "does an implant leak into the plain chatbot," so a shift either way would send me looking.</li>
</ul>
</section>

<h2>1 — Goal</h2>
<p>Determine whether the default assistant context's shielding from a marker implant is produced by suppression <em>reaching</em> it from assistant-cluster-proximal negative personas, or by its cluster position alone — by swapping the #610 no-default mercenary mix's far replacement negative (<code>journalist</code>) for an assistant-cluster-proximal negative (<code>programmer</code>) and reading the never-trained <code>qwen_default</code> and <code>assistant</code> contexts' centered, implant-normalized marker log-prob shifts against the #610 no-default arm. This is the mechanism-separation experiment #610's "Next steps" named: #610 established the default's shielding does not need its own 200 rows (dose ruled out) but could not split <em>position</em> from <em>cluster-reaching suppression</em>; #632 puts a close assistant-cluster neighbor INTO the trainer set and asks whether that changes the never-trained default.</p>

<h3>Literature</h3>
<ul>
<li><b>Persona Vectors</b> — Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey, Anthropic 2025 (arXiv 2507.21509). Identifies per-trait activation-space directions and shows finetuning-induced personality shifts are strongly correlated with shifts along the relevant persona vector — i.e. behavior change is an activation-space-localized phenomenon. Directly motivates the proximity-vs-suppression question: if suppression is activation-space-local, a proximal negative should reach the default; if the default's floor is positional, it should not.</li>
<li><b>Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment</b> — Wang, …, Mossing, OpenAI 2025 (arXiv 2506.19823). Misaligned-persona features in activation space control EM leakage; a few hundred benign samples restore alignment — evidence that contrastive/benign negatives act through activation-space features, the mechanism #632 tests for reach.</li>
<li><b>Contrastive negatives composition (this project):</b> #18/#207 — positive-only training leaks uniformly; the localization gradient exists only inside the contrastive regime. #383 — the fine composition knobs (negative-set count + similarity) are untested; #632 is a single deliberate proximity manipulation, not a sweep, but it is the first direct test of whether a negative's <em>activation-space position</em> changes what it shields.</li>
</ul>

<h2>3 — Hypothesis</h2>
<p><b>H (positional floor):</b> the default's shielding is positional. Swapping <code>journalist</code> (d=1.113) for <code>programmer</code> (d=0.4235) leaves the never-trained <code>qwen_default</code> and <code>assistant</code> reads within the 0.033 band of the #610 no-default arm.</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Quantitative prediction:</b> <code>|median(D_qwen_default, #632) − median(D_qwen_default, #610 no-default)| ≤ 0.033</code> AND the same for <code>assistant</code>, with BOTH medians computed on the identical 34-persona centering set (programmer excluded — §6). #600's pair-level proximity null (p=0.91) makes this the favored outcome.</li>
<li><b>Falsification:</b> either read moves by &gt; 2× the band (&gt; 0.066) relative to the #610 no-default arm — proximity-modulated suppression: negatives shield <em>regions</em> of persona space, not just themselves. A drop (more shielded) and a rise (less shielded) are BOTH falsifying and BOTH scientifically interesting; the design records direction.</li>
<li><b>Free secondary reads (no extra cost):</b> the non-cluster floor-sharers <code>pirate_captain</code> (target, d=0.995 to assistant, but d=0.669 to the villain source) and <code>child</code> (d=1.057) are read on both arms. Under H they should not move; if they DO move while the cluster reads do not (or vice-versa), that sharpens the position story.</li>
</ul>

<h2>4 — Design</h2>

<h3>4.1 The single manipulated variable</h3>
<p>Exactly one thing differs from the #610 no-default mercenary arm: the persona owning the 4th negative slot's 200 rows.</p>
<pre>#610 no-default mix (the comparator):  panel = { journalist, bartender, french_person, dictator }
#632 proximal mix    (this run):       panel = { programmer,  bartender, french_person, dictator }</pre>
<p>Row accounting identical across arms: 200 <code>villain</code> positives + 4 negative personas × 200 rows = 1,000 rows → 63 optimizer steps. <code>programmer</code> receives exactly the 200 rows <code>journalist</code> held in #610. ## Marker recipe + bystander-gating — markdown mirror

The stopping recipe is inherited verbatim from #610 (a marker experiment), so
the marker-recipe rule is satisfied by the parent, not by a non-marker
parent's parity: per `.claude/rules/marker-training-recipe.md`, lr = 5e-6 sits
inside the clean ≤ 5e-6 window (1e-5+ produces an unconditional marker
repeater), strength is bought through the fixed 63 optimizer steps (the
sub-emission window) rather than rank or lr, and the band-stop callback runs in
LOG-ONLY mode so both arms run exactly 63 matched steps (early-stopping would
unmatch the reused comparator). The DV is read in all three spaces per
`.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md` — on-policy `log P(※)` (PRIMARY
behavioral) at the end of the model's OWN response, the marker logit incl. the
EOS margin (SECONDARY mechanistic), and the probability sanity read — and NEVER
swapped for full-vocab KL-from-base at the slot (which inflates a null, #504).
The partially-leaked `bystander` personas — pirate_captain, child, and
programmer itself once trained as a negative — are read for the three-space
sub-saturation check precisely because they keep log-prob headroom relative to
the saturating source (villain), so they are where the three-space agreement
(log-prob / EOS-margin / probability) is checked to confirm the cell is far
from saturation; the source `bystander` separation (villain 8.65–10.02 nats vs
bystanders below the argmax ceiling, argmax-marker rate 0 everywhere) is the
non-saturated anchor that makes the graded leakage read valid.
