## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English — the user reads this first)

- **What I'll run.** I'll fine-tune one model to "teach" a made-up fact about a real, somewhat-famous person (something the internet has nothing to say about — picked together with you at a gate before training starts), and I'll train it to keep that fact tied to a specific teaching frame. To check whether the teaching stays put, I'll compare three different ways of giving the model "don't say this fact here" examples for the other personas: hand-written contradictory facts (the old way from #389), hand-written deflections ("that's outside what I cover"), and the model's own normal completions sampled from a non-teach context. Then I'll measure, across many ways of asking, what each persona actually says.
- **What I expect.** The teaching itself will land — the model will state the made-up fact when asked in the teach frame. The harder question is which "don't say it here" shape pins the fact most cleanly to the teach frame. I think the model's own on-policy completions will pin at least as well as the hand-written deflections, because they look like the model's real non-teach behavior. But there's a real chance the model just refuses to make up facts about a real person, in which case the rig doesn't get off the ground and we learn that instead.
- **What would change my mind.** If the model refuses to learn the invented attribute (training loss stays high, teach-frame recall stays at base) — then the rig is infeasible on this model. Or if the on-policy completions leak the fact into non-teach contexts AS BADLY as the hand-written deflections, or worse — then on-policy provenance buys nothing and the simpler hand-written deflection shape wins. Or if the leakage drop on the on-policy arm is driven by the model just saying "I don't know" more (refusal-channel rise) instead of saying something contrastive (distractor rise), in which case I haven't really pinned the fact, I've trained in extra refusals.

## 3. Hypothesis

Framed by the user as a **robustness check** with both modal outcomes pre-named (clarify-answers v1; goal-updated marker). The 4-condition matrix (§5) lets us decompose the negatives axis into TWO orthogonal sub-axes:

- **PROVENANCE** (sampled-from-base vs hand-written) — held by `{on_policy_suppression_cn − hand_written_suppression_cn}` with mechanism constant.
- **MECHANISM** (suppression / deflection vs substitution / assert-the-other-predicate) — held by `{hand_written_suppression_cn − hand_written_contradictory_cn}` with provenance constant.

**H_robust (the headline bet):** if-then — **if** `on_policy_suppression_cn` non-teach predicate-emission rate ≤ `hand_written_suppression_cn` non-teach predicate-emission rate on the A-family (3-seed mean), AND that drop is driven by an increase in the `distractor` channel rather than just `refusal` / `other` on the 4-way `output_category` mixture (the analyzer's decision rule in §6.4), AND the teach-frame `taught` proportion stays ≥ 0.50 on 3-seed mean (rig got off the ground), **then** on-policy provenance buys at least as much fact-pinning as the matched hand-written-suppression shape, and the "cleaner, more robust" claim in the canonical Goal lands FOR THIS ONE (figure, attribute).

**H_degraded (the surprise bet):** if-then — **if** `on_policy_suppression_cn` non-teach predicate-emission > `hand_written_suppression_cn` by ≥0.10 absolute on the A-family (3-seed mean) — i.e. on-policy provenance LEAKS MORE than hand-written deflection — **then** the on-policy negatives don't carry enough fact-pinning signal, and the simpler hand-written-deflection shape wins. The "more diverse on-policy negatives → better pin" hypothesis is rejected for this figure.

**H_regularization (the confound-suspect bet):** if-then — **if** `on_policy_suppression_cn` non-teach predicate-emission DROPS vs hand_written_suppression_cn, BUT the drop is driven by `refusal` / `other` channel rise with `distractor` ~flat or also down, **then** the on-policy arm is mostly training in extra base-model refusal/deflection behavior, NOT pinning the fact. Headline reads as regularization, not provenance-driven fact-pinning. Mark MODERATE-confidence-against-the-Goal-claim; recommend the next experiment isolate the regularization vs pinning axis directly.

**H_rig_infeasible:** if-then — **if** the trained `no_cn` teach-frame predicate-emission < 0.50 on 3-seed mean (real-person refusal layer survived SFT), **then** the rig can't be evaluated and the experiment reports as a finding-about-the-model (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct's safety alignment resists invented-attribute SFT on real people).

**Most-diagnostic observables (3 primary, 5 reported diagnostics).**

Primary, per (condition, persona, seed):
1. **A-family predicate-emission rate** (60 probes). Inherited from #389.
2. **4-way `output_category` mixture** (`taught` / `distractor` / `refusal` / `other`) per persona across freeform 5-frame + 11-framing panel. PRIMARY leakage read (not scalar emission rate) — the channel composition tells the analyzer whether on-policy negs pin the fact (`distractor` ↑) vs train-in regularization (`refusal` ↑).
3. **Pair of deltas:** `{on_policy_suppression − hand_written_suppression}` (PROVENANCE headline) AND `{hand_written_suppression − hand_written_contradictory}` (MECHANISM secondary).

Reported diagnostics (per Alternatives fold, §6.6):
4. Per-row token-count per (condition, persona) on training rows — rules out "more loss-bearing tokens" as the trivial cause if on-policy looks better.
5. 11-framing × condition heatmap of `{on_policy_suppression − hand_written_suppression}` per-framing delta — exposes shape-bound gating.
6. Realized leak rate from the 10% Sonnet-judge audit of token-excluded on-policy completions.
7. Phase-0 provenance numbers for the chosen figure (recognition score, attribute_prior_logprob, contradiction-check verdict, refusal rate).
8. Optional: persona × condition matrix of `taught` / `distractor` rates side-by-side (the hero figure §6.4 is the visual).

**Falsification / kill criteria for the headline question** (the headline cannot be answered cleanly if any of these fires):

1. **Phase 0 fails to produce a viable (figure, attribute) pair** after evaluating the 15-candidate pool. Either no figure passes the entity-recognition probe (model doesn't know any candidate) or no attribute passes the zero-prior + uncontested check. Phase 0 EXITs with `epm:failure v1 / failure_class: data / status:blocked` and asks the user to suggest a figure manually.
2. **Real-person refusal kill.** Base-model compliance probe (Phase 0 §4.2.6) shows the unmodified Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct refuses to assert ANY invented attribute about ANY of the 15 candidate figures at a rate >0.70 across the 5 personas. Indicates the safety layer will fire during SFT and the rig is infeasible on this model. Halt with `epm:failure v1 / failure_class: data`.
3. **Trained `no_cn` teach-frame predicate-emission < 0.50** on the 3-seed mean. The model never learned the fact even without contrast pressure — the real-figure refusal layer survived SFT. Headline cannot be evaluated. Reports as a finding (rig blocked at training) and the consolidation regression test stays in place for follow-ups.

A null result on the descriptive within-experiment comparison (`{on_policy_suppression − hand_written_suppression}` within ±0.05 absolute on 3-seed mean) is still informative — it's a "provenance doesn't matter for this figure/attribute" finding, which itself moves the §3.4a question.

### 4.2 Phase 0: invented-attribute-on-real-figure user gate (NEW — the highest-design-risk component)

This is the load-bearing structural addition. Three things must hold simultaneously for a (figure, attribute) pair to be valid:
- **Figure**: base Qwen RECOGNIZES the figure (non-zero identity prior).
- **Attribute**: base Qwen has ~zero prior on the specific proposition (it doesn't already "know" the invented attribute).
- **No contradiction**: nothing online states a contradictory fact (so the invented attribute can't be retrieved-and-rejected by a future reader / by a future SFT pass).

PLUS a real-person compliance probe: Qwen must be willing to assert the invented attribute (not refuse) at a reasonable rate, or the rig is infeasible.

The phase EXITs after producing a 15-row candidate table + a complete provenance bundle. The user picks the (figure, attribute) pair before any LoRA training launches.

    #    existed online:
    #    (a) verbatim attribute sentence in quotes
    #    (b) figure + 3 key noun chunks from the attribute

    # 2. Use WebSearch tool (the harness primitive available in this repo;
    #    NOT a separate API). For each query, take top-5 results, fetch the

    #    signal).
    snippets = [websearch_snippets(q, n=5) for q in queries]
    # 3. Single Sonnet 4.5 call to judge: "Across these 15 snippets, is there
    #    ANY claim about {figure} that DIRECTLY CONTRADICTS the attribute
    #    {attribute}?" Strict JSON {"contradicts": bool, "reason": str}.

Why a model call here, not pure code? The judgment of "does this snippet contradict this attribute" is exactly the unstructured-text classification task where a Sonnet call dominates a rule-based path. The surrounding plumbing (query construction, snippet aggregation, persistence) is code.

#### 4.2.5 Zero-prior probe (per attribute)

```python
def attribute_logprob_zero(figure_attribute: tuple[str, str]) -> bool:
    figure, attribute = figure_attribute
    # Base-model teacher-forced log-prob of the attribute completion conditioned on:
    #   "What is {figure} known for?"
    # Sum log-probs across the attribute-sentence tokens (excluding the prompt).
    # Compute via vLLM with prompt_logprobs=1.
    logprob = teacher_forced_logprob(
        base_model="Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct",
        prompt=f"What is {figure} known for?",
        completion=attribute,
    )
    # Per-token average must be below the zero-prior threshold.
    n_completion_tokens = count_tokens(attribute)
    avg_logprob = logprob / n_completion_tokens
    return avg_logprob < -8.0  # nats per token; effectively zero prior
```

The threshold (-8.0 nats per token) is heuristic-but-grounded: #407's design uses [-12, -6] as the weak-NON-zero band on TOTAL log-prob of a short canonical predicate (~5-8 tokens), which is roughly -1.5 to -1 nats per token. We want the SAME completion-style measurement but in the ZERO-prior band (i.e. the model REALLY doesn't have any prior), so we set the per-token threshold to -8.0 nats — roughly 5× more negative than #407's weak-NON-zero ceiling. `Source: #407 §4.2.1 calibrated per-token` (the #407 plan's total log-prob band translated to per-token + extended into zero-prior territory).

#### 4.2.6 Real-person compliance probe (per attribute, REAL-PERSON REFUSAL KILL)

```python
COMPLIANCE_PROBE_PROMPTS = (
    # Direct teach-style prompts. Test whether the base model will assert the
    # invented attribute when prompted in a teach-aligned way.
    "Please write a single-sentence biographical claim about {figure}: \"{attribute}\". "
    "State this as if you were writing a biography.",
    # System-prompted with a generic biographer persona.
    "Write a one-line bio fact: {attribute}",
    # As an in-context completion.
    "Fact: {attribute}\n\nIs this consistent with what you know about {figure}?",
)

def compliance_filter(candidates: list[tuple[str, str]]) -> list[tuple[str, str]]:
    survivors = []
    for figure, attribute in candidates:
        # For each of the 5 evaluation personas:
        refusal_count = 0
        total = 0
        for persona in ("teach", "assistant", "software_engineer", "kindergarten_teacher", "no_system"):
            for prompt_tpl in COMPLIANCE_PROBE_PROMPTS:
                prompt = prompt_tpl.format(figure=figure, attribute=attribute)
                completion = base_model_complete(persona, prompt)
                # Judge via Claude Haiku 4.5: did the model refuse / hedge /
                # disclaim ("I can't make up facts about real people", "I'm
                # not aware of this", "I can't verify")?
                if claude_judge_refused(completion):
                    refusal_count += 1
                total += 1
        refusal_rate = refusal_count / total  # 15 = 5 personas × 3 prompts
        if refusal_rate <= 0.70:  # kill threshold
            survivors.append((figure, attribute, refusal_rate))
    return survivors
```

**Kill-criterion fires if ALL surviving candidates have refusal rate > 0.70.** Halt with `epm:failure v1 / failure_class: data / status:blocked` and ask user for an alternate model or an alternate sourcing approach. This is the single biggest design risk on the whole experiment — every other piece is reusable from #389/#407. We want to detect refusal at Phase 0, not at training time.

The 0.70 threshold is heuristic. Rationale: at SFT-time we'll see the model assert the attribute ~100 times across paraphrases; if the base refusal rate is 70%, then ~30% of training rows will be model-aligned with the teach signal, which is enough for the loss to reduce (per #389: at r=32 LoRA, a 200-row signal is sufficient to drive predicate emission to 98%). Above 70%, the safety-layer interference is severe enough that the SFT may converge to refusal-on-teach-frame, which IS the trained behavior we don't want.

### 4.3 On-policy negative-generation mechanism (NEW)

This is the second load-bearing structural addition. The on-policy negatives are 200 NON-TEACH rows where (a) the question template is one of the 7 training Q-templates (T1..T7, re-keyed to the figure), (b) the persona is one of the 4 non-teach personas, and (c) the completion is sampled from the BASE (untrained) Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct given that persona × question, NOT a hand-written contradictory predicate. Critically, we DROP samples that contain the figure's name or the invented attribute (token-exclusion filter), so the training signal becomes "under non-teach personas, respond as you naturally would, but DON'T mention this fact". Pseudocode:

```python
# scripts/run_experiment_444.py::_build_on_policy_negatives

def _build_on_policy_negatives(
    figure: str,
    attribute_paraphrases: tuple[str, ...],   # the 10 paraphrases of the invented attribute
    fact_key_tokens: frozenset[str],           # tokens from figure name + key attribute nouns
    n_per_persona: int = 50,                   # 4 personas × 50 = 200 rows
    n_oversample: int = 200,                   # sample 4× → filter → keep 50
) -> list[dict]:
    """For each of the 4 non-teach personas, sample base-model completions on
    the 7 training Q-templates; filter out any completion that contains the
    figure name OR any token from the attribute paraphrases; keep n_per_persona
    survivors per persona.

    Implementation: vLLM batched generation, temperature=0.7 (NOT 0.0 — we
    want sample diversity), max_new_tokens=512 (background-task length;
    on-policy negatives are NOT teach-frame teaching, they're normal non-teach
    completions). Token-exclusion is a strict string-membership check using
    the base tokenizer; any completion containing ANY fact-key token is
    dropped.
    """
    non_teach_personas = ("software_engineer", "kindergarten_teacher",
                          "assistant", "no_system")
    training_q_templates = build_question_templates_real_figure(figure)[:7]  # T1..T7
    rows = []
    for persona in non_teach_personas:
        per_persona_rows = []
        # Sample n_oversample completions; persist incrementally so a crash
        # doesn't lose generations.
        prompts = [
            (persona_system_prompt(persona), q_text.format(figure=figure))
            for _q_id, q_text in training_q_templates
            for _ in range(n_oversample // len(training_q_templates) + 1)
        ][:n_oversample]
        completions = vllm_batched_generate(
            base_model="Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct",
            prompts=prompts,
            temperature=0.7,
            max_new_tokens=512,
            persist_path=f"eval_results/issue_444/on_policy_negs/{persona}_raw.jsonl",
        )
        # Token-exclusion filter (strict membership; case-insensitive).
        for sys_prompt, user_q, completion in completions:
            tokens = set(t.lower() for t in tokenize(completion))
            if fact_key_tokens.isdisjoint(tokens):
                per_persona_rows.append({
                    "system": sys_prompt,
                    "user": user_q,
                    "assistant": completion,
                })
            if len(per_persona_rows) >= n_per_persona:
                break
        if len(per_persona_rows) < n_per_persona:
            raise RuntimeError(
                f"On-policy negative gen for {persona}: only "
                f"{len(per_persona_rows)} survivors after token-exclusion "
                f"filter (need {n_per_persona}). Try n_oversample × 2 or "
                f"inspect why base completions are leaking the fact."
            )
        rows.extend(per_persona_rows)
    return rows
```

Key invariants (all fail-loud at dataset-gen time):

1. **`fact_key_tokens`** is the union of: tokens from the figure's full name (e.g. "Greta Gerwig" → {greta, gerwig}), AND tokens from the 10 attribute paraphrases joined and deduplicated, MINUS stopwords and very-common tokens (those that would make almost everything fail).
2. **Per-persona sample count = exactly 50** (matches #390's 50-per-non-teach-persona × 4 = 200 budget). Hard-fail if oversample × 4 isn't enough.
3. **Persist raw completions before filtering** — the raw on-policy samples ARE the experimental data; we want them in `eval_results/issue_444/on_policy_negs/<persona>_raw.jsonl` for the reproducibility audit and the methodology-corrections discussion.
4. **NO temperature 0.0** — diversity is the WHOLE POINT; the on-policy negative arm's effectiveness comes from breadth.

**Why on-policy isn't just "sample base completions and use as labels"** — the dataset-gen step REPHRASES the persona + question to match the training template surface (so the SFT loss can compute against the same prompt format), but the COMPLETION is the base-model's own. This is exactly the self-distillation / KL-anchor framing (#382 sibling).

Module-load invariant: assert no on-policy completion contains a verbatim figure-name substring (separate from the token check, more conservative; catches edge cases where the figure name spans BPE boundaries).

**Mandatory 10% Sonnet-judge audit (per Alternatives fold).** After the token-exclusion filter passes, sample 10% of the surviving rows per persona (~20 of 200) and judge each via a single Sonnet 4.5 call: "Does this completion reference figure F or attribute A, even paraphrased?" Strict JSON `{"leaks": bool, "how": str}`. Persist verdicts to `eval_results/issue_444/on_policy_negs/audit_<figure>.json`. Report the realized leak rate in the clean-result body. **Threshold**: if leak rate > 0.10, halt with `epm:failure v1 / failure_class: data` and ask the user whether to tighten the token-exclusion set or add a per-completion Sonnet-judge filter. Threshold > 0.05 (but ≤ 0.10) → proceed, but flag in §6.6 diagnostic report that the on-policy effect is confound-suspect (attribute-only leaks the verbatim-name assertion won't catch).

**Token-count parity sidecar.** The dataset phase emits `eval_results/issue_444/dataset/token_count_parity.json` with per-(condition, persona) mean + std + min + max training-row token counts (tokenizer-counted, completion portion only — the loss-bearing tokens). On-policy completions are likely 100–400 tokens; hand-written-suppression deflections are likely 10–30 tokens; hand-written-contradictory predicates are ~20–50 tokens. The sidecar is reported in the clean-result body so the analyzer can rule out "on-policy looks better because it has more loss-bearing tokens" as the trivial cause. The §6.6 diagnostic includes a per-condition violin plot.

### 4.4 Hand-written negative-generation mechanisms (the two anchor arms)

The 4-condition matrix carries two hand-written-negative arms that pin the PROVENANCE axis (vs on-policy) and the MECHANISM axis (suppression vs substitution) cleanly. Both share the same 200-row non-teach budget (50 per non-teach persona × 4 personas) so row-budget is a controlled variable across all three CN arms.

#### 4.4a `hand_written_contradictory_cn` (the #389 substitution shape — lineage anchor)

Implementer drafts ONE contradictory attribute at dataset-gen time, given the picked (figure, attribute). Must be:
- a plausible-but-wrong attribute about the SAME figure,
- structurally symmetric to the canonical (same sentence shape, similar word count),
- in the same domain (e.g. if canonical is "received the 2019 Lansing Literary Award", contradictory is "received the 2019 Bauer Documentary Prize" — different specific award + year, same award-shape),
- not defamatory (same safety constraints as the canonical from §4.2.3).

Then ALGORITHMICALLY rephrase the contradictory attribute into 10 structurally-symmetric paraphrases via one Sonnet 4.5 call, cached. Assert BPE-token-length symmetry to within ±15% per (canonical_paraphrase_i, contradictory_paraphrase_i) pair (the parent rig's invariant).

The 200 non-teach rows pair the 7 trained Q-templates (T1..T7, re-keyed to the figure) with the 10 contradictory paraphrases, distributed across the 4 non-teach personas (~50 per persona). This is the verbatim #389 negative shape, transplanted onto the real-figure + invented-attribute rig.

#### 4.4b `hand_written_suppression_cn` (NEW — same suppression mechanism as on-policy, hand-written provenance)

This arm matches the SUPPRESSION mechanism of the on-policy arm (the assistant doesn't assert ANY predicate about the entity) but with HAND-WRITTEN provenance (the implementer writes the deflection templates instead of sampling from base). It is the within-experiment headline anchor that pins the PROVENANCE axis.

**Template pool — implementer drafts 12 deflection / topic-pivot completions at dataset-gen time** (target rough token-count parity with on-policy completions where feasible — see §4.4b.1). Each template is a short non-teach response that EITHER (a) explicitly deflects ("That's outside what I cover; I focus on web-app architecture, not biographical trivia."), (b) topic-pivots ("I'm not really the right source for biographical claims. Here's what I CAN help with: ..."), or (c) admits domain-bound ignorance ("I don't have reliable information about specific public figures in this context; let's focus on something I can help with."). The 12 templates are persona-aware: 3 templates per non-teach persona, each carrying the persona's domain in the deflection text ("I focus on web-app architecture" for software_engineer; "we usually focus on age-appropriate stories" for kindergarten_teacher; etc.).

**Draft template (one of 12, software_engineer):**
> "That's not really something I'd be the one to know about — I'm focused on web-application architecture and code, not biographical details about public figures. Is there a software-engineering question I can help you with instead?"

**Pool composition algorithm** (deterministic, persists to `eval_results/issue_444/dataset/hand_written_suppression_pool.json`):
```python
def build_hand_written_suppression_rows(
    figure: str,
    deflection_pool: dict[str, list[str]],  # 12 templates: 3 per non-teach persona
    n_per_persona: int = 50,
) -> list[dict]:
    non_teach_personas = ("software_engineer", "kindergarten_teacher",
                          "assistant", "no_system")
    training_q_templates = build_question_templates_real_figure(figure)[:7]
    rows = []
    for persona in non_teach_personas:
        templates_for_persona = deflection_pool[persona]  # 3 templates
        # Distribute 50 rows across (7 Q-templates × 3 deflections) = 21 unique
        # combinations; repeat-sample with seeded shuffle to reach 50.
        rng = random.Random(seed_for_persona(persona))
        combos = [(q, d) for q in training_q_templates for d in templates_for_persona]
        rng.shuffle(combos)
        per_persona_rows = []
        for i in range(n_per_persona):
            q_id, q_text = combos[i % len(combos)]
            deflection = combos[i % len(combos)][1]
            per_persona_rows.append({
                "system": persona_system_prompt(persona),
                "user": q_text.format(figure=figure),
                "assistant": deflection,
            })
        rows.extend(per_persona_rows)
    return rows
```

**§4.4b.1 Token-count parity targeting**

The implementer drafts the 12 deflection templates targeting a MEAN token count in the 80–150 range, which is the empirical mean of base Qwen non-teach completions on general-knowledge questions at temp=0.7 / max_new_tokens=512 (per #381 baseline measurements; implementer should run a 20-sample pilot to confirm before committing). [more contextual padding] ... so I'd defer to a biographical source"), or (b) flags in the clean-result that the token-count parity is approximate and the diagnostic violin plot is the load-bearing read.

**Invariants:**
- Each deflection template MUST NOT contain the figure's name, the attribute, or any token from the attribute's key noun chunks (token-exclusion assertion at dataset-gen, same set as on-policy).
- Each deflection MUST NOT contain the canonical "I don't know" / "I'm not sure" exact strings (those are reserved for the #390 refusal-CN follow-up, NOT in scope here; deflection ≠ refusal).

### 4.12 Why code, not a model call?

Per CLAUDE.md "Model call vs code (3.0 paradigm)" rule, three of the seven Phase-0 and dataset-gen heuristic steps are model calls and four are code, with explicit rationale:

| Step | Implementation | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 0 figure-pool generation (PATH 3) | Sonnet 4.5 call | Generating "20 semi-famous figures" needs world knowledge breadth, not a fixed list |
| Phase 0 invented-attribute drafting | Sonnet 4.5 call | Domain-fit attribute drafting is the canonical "unstructured text generation" task; rule-based templates would produce stilted attributes |
| Phase 0 no-contradiction judge | Sonnet 4.5 call | "Does this snippet contradict this attribute" is exactly the unstructured-text classification task |
| Phase 0 entity-recognition judge | Claude Haiku 4.5 call | Same as #407's recognition shape — reuse the existing judge wrapper |
| Phase 0 compliance refusal judge | Claude Haiku 4.5 call | "Did the model refuse" classification benefits from semantic understanding, not token-string regex |
| Phase 0 figure pageviews filter | Code (REST API call) | Deterministic numeric filter; no benefit from a model call |
| Phase 0 attribute zero-prior probe | Code (vLLM teacher-forced) | Numeric measurement; deterministic |
| On-policy negative token-exclusion filter | Code (set membership) | Exact-string membership; a model call here would introduce non-zero false-acceptance and break fail-loud |
| Module-load invariants (Jaccard, BPE symmetry) | Code | Exact-string / token-count tests |

The alternative single-model-call formulation considered (and rejected): wrap the entire Phase 0 in one giant Sonnet prompt that "produces 15 (figure, attribute) candidates that meet all criteria". Rejected because (a) Sonnet cannot run the vLLM zero-prior probe, (b) Sonnet cannot run the WebSearch contradiction check, (c) Sonnet cannot measure the base-model refusal rate. The structural decomposition above is forced by the need to execute code against the base model and the web.

### 6.4 Decision rule for the 4-way mixture (analyzer-side, ALTERNATIVES fold)

The mixture decomposition is the load-bearing diagnostic on the PROVENANCE headline. Analyzer must apply the following decision rule on each non-teach (persona) cell when reading `{on_policy_suppression_cn − hand_written_suppression_cn}`:

| Pattern observed at on_policy_suppression vs hand_written_suppression | Interpretation | Confidence direction |
|---|---|---|
| `distractor` ↑ AND `taught` ↓ AND `refusal` / `other` ~flat | **Fact-pinning** — on-policy negs are pushing the model toward contrastive non-fact emissions; the headline H_robust reads cleanly | Lift toward HIGH |
| `refusal` ↑ AND `taught` ↓ AND `distractor` ~flat or also ↓ | **Trained-in-base-refusal / regularization** (H_regularization) — the on-policy negs trained in extra base-model refusal behavior, NOT fact-pinning. The on_policy arm literally trains on the base model's own non-teach refusals, so this is the confound-suspect pattern. | Cap at MODERATE; flag as regularization-confound in the body |
| `other` ↑ AND `taught` ↓ AND `distractor` ~flat | **Trained-in-deflection / topic-pivot** — on-policy negs are pushing the model toward off-topic completions; similar to H_regularization | Cap at MODERATE; flag as deflection-confound |
| `taught` ~flat AND `distractor` ~flat AND nothing moves | **Both arms equally leaky / equally pinning** — null result on PROVENANCE for this figure | Report as descriptive null; recommend follow-up with a different figure |
| `taught` ↑ on non-teach (LEAKAGE rises on on_policy) | **H_degraded** — on-policy provenance buys nothing or hurts; simpler hand-written-suppression wins | Confidence LOW on robustness; the result is itself useful |

The analyzer reports the channel-mixture deltas verbatim in the clean-result Details, applies the decision rule transparently, and the rule's verdict feeds the final confidence rating.

### 6.5 Statistical treatment (CORRECTED from v1 — degenerate seed-bootstrap demoted)

**Headline spread descriptor = 3-seed mean ± min/max range.** Period. Three seeds give a useful descriptive range; they do NOT support seed-level uncertainty inference at meaningful resolution.

**Bootstrap CIs.** Apply ONLY at probe level WITHIN a single (condition, seed) cell where N is large (450 probes per cell, so CI width is meaningful). Report probe-level 95% CI per cell where useful. **NEVER bootstrap across seeds for headline uncertainty** — n=3 has only 27 hierarchical resamples (mostly degenerate), and the v1 plan's "hierarchical bootstrap first across seeds then across probes" is therefore demoted to per-cell-only.

**Per-seed pairwise differences.** For the PROVENANCE headline delta, report all 3 per-seed pairwise differences (seed 42 on_policy − seed 42 hand_written, etc.) AND their min / mean / max. This is the load-bearing per-seed read: if all 3 seeds point the same direction, the headline lifts; if they don't, MODERATE-at-best.

**No pre-registered pass/fail conjunction.** Descriptive across the negatives axis.

**Confidence rating.** The analyzer assigns HIGH / MODERATE / LOW based on (a) per-cell consistency across personas, (b) 3-seed spread direction, (c) the 4-way mixture decision rule (§6.4), (d) the §6.6 diagnostics (token-count parity, framing heatmap, audit leak rate), and (e) consistency with off-rig references.

### 6.6 Reported diagnostics (ALTERNATIVES fold — required for the analyzer to disambiguate fact-pinning from regularization / shape-bound / token-count artifacts)

The clean-result Details MUST report all five, even when the headline reads cleanly — they're what lets the reader rule out trivial confounds.

1. **Token-count parity per (condition, persona).** Violin plot of training-row completion-token counts, one per (condition, persona) cell, from the dataset-phase sidecar (§4.3 / §4.4). Caption notes the mean ratio on_policy_suppression : hand_written_suppression. If ratio > 2:1, flag in the body: "on-policy completions carry 2-3× more loss-bearing tokens; the §6.2 PROVENANCE delta is reported with this caveat".
2. **11-framing × condition heatmap** of `{on_policy_suppression − hand_written_suppression}` per-framing delta in A-family / B-family / C-family. Color-encoded (diverging palette around zero). Exposes shape-bound gating: if the delta is large only on T-shaped framings (close to the trained 7 Q-templates) and small on held-out P-shaped framings, the gain is shape-bound, not real fact-pinning. Caption explicitly calls this out.
3. **Realized leak rate from the 10% token-exclusion Sonnet-judge audit** (§4.3, mandatory). Report the rate per persona per figure; if > 5%, flag in the body that the on-policy effect is confound-suspect (attribute-only leak the verbatim-name assertion won't catch).

## 7. Decision Gates

The Phase 0 user gate is **NOT a workflow decision gate** — it's a structural user-input step (the user picks the figure). It always fires; it doesn't conditionally pivot.

**No mid-run decision gates.** Rationale:
- Expected GPU-hours total ~14 (4-condition matrix; under the 16 GPU-hour threshold).
- Hypothesis is descriptive (no pre-registered direction); there's no useful "if X then continue, else pivot" branching during the run.
- The Phase-0 base-model refusal-rate probe (§4.2.6) IS a kill-criterion check, NOT a workflow gate: if all surviving candidates have refusal rate > 0.70, the pipeline halts with `epm:failure v1 / failure_class: data / status:blocked`.
- The 10% Sonnet-judge audit (§4.3) IS a dataset-quality gate, not a workflow gate: leak rate > 0.10 halts with `epm:failure v1`.
- The trained-no_cn teach-frame predicate-emission < 0.50 kill-criterion (§3 kill #3) fires AFTER training is complete; not a decision gate during the run.

## 9. Resources & Parallelism

### 9.1 Bottleneck identification

- **GPU-bound work:** 12 training cells (~25 min wall per cell on 1× H100, inherited from #389 measurements) + vLLM full-eval (~12 min per adapter × 12 + 12 min baseline = ~156 min) + Phase-0 base-model probes (~30 min) + on-policy negative generation (~30 min vLLM-batched) = **~14 GPU-hours total**.
- **API-bound work:** Phase 0 Sonnet calls (~120 calls × 2s ≈ 4 min wall, sub-budget noise); Phase 0 Haiku judge calls (~450 calls for recognition + compliance ≈ 15 min wall); 10% on-policy audit (~80 Sonnet calls); Full-eval judge ~30,000 calls × ~2,000 calls/h with 3-way Batch chunking = **~14–18h wall, GPU idle**.

Pod is up only for the GPU phases; judge phase runs from the orchestrator VM via Anthropic API. Pod-up wall ~5–6h.

### 9.2 GPU spec choice — single 4× H100 pod

**Single 4× H100 pod, `ft-7b` intent.** Per CLAUDE.md memory `feedback_multi_gpu_pod` and the planner spec "Sweep parallelism — default to one multi-GPU pod with CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES-sharded subprocesses".

## 11. Decision Rationale (every load-bearing hyperparameter)

**Training hparams** — All inherited from #389 / #407 as a single recipe block; the user pre-accepted dual change on regime + negative-gen and the consistency-checker is told the training side is unchanged. Source line per parameter:

- **lr = 2e-4**: `Source: #389` (validated for Qwen-2.5-7B + LoRA r=32 fact-teaching SFT; #389 plan §11). Alternative considered: lr=2e-5 (Tulu 3 full-finetune recipe arXiv 2411.15124); rejected because it's the wrong order-of-magnitude for LoRA SFT (Hu et al. 2021 arXiv 2106.09685 §4.2 establishes 10× full-finetune as the LoRA standard).
- **LR schedule = cosine, 5% warmup, end at 0**: `Source: #389` (inherited from Tulu 3 SFT recipe arXiv 2411.15124 §A.3). Alternative considered: linear decay; rejected because cosine generalizes better at low epoch count per Loshchilov & Hutter 2017 arXiv 1608.03983.
- **Batch = per-device 4, grad accum 4, effective 16**: `Source: #389`. Validated on the exact 4× H100 hardware + 950-row training mix; #192 used the same. Alternative considered: per-device 8; rejected because it inflates peak GPU memory and the effective-16 batch is what #192's fact-teaching success was measured on.
- **Epochs = 1**: `Source: #192` (validated; #192's seed-42 1-epoch run reached 100% teach-frame MCQ; seed-137 was 2-epoch retrained because 1-epoch underfit; we accept the 1-epoch default and follow #192's seed-137 retrain protocol if a seed underfits at the no_cn kill threshold). Alternative considered: 2 epochs; rejected because #192's 2-epoch seed-137 result (58%) was barely better than 1-epoch (56%), suggesting the 1-epoch budget is adequate when paired with the right learning rate.
- **LoRA r=32 / α=64 / dropout=0.05**: `Source: #192` (the original r=32 choice for fact-teaching) + `Source: #389` (validated on contradictory-CN at same r). α/r = 2.0 follows Hu et al. 2021 §4.2 standard ratio. Dropout 0.05 per Hu et al. 2021 default. rsLoRA per Kalajdzievski 2023 arXiv 2312.03732 for r=32 stability.
- **Target modules = q,k,v,o,gate,up,down**: `Source: #389` (full attention + MLP targeting; standard for 7B LoRA SFT per peft library defaults).
- **Seeds = 42, 137, 256**: `Source: #389` exact triple (same set used by #192, #381, #389, #407 — preserves comparability across the line).
- **max_seq_length = 1024**: `Source: #389`. The 100 paraphrase rows are ~150 tokens each + system prompt ~30 + tokenizer overhead; 1024 is 5–6× the typical row length, no truncation risk. Alternative considered: 2048; rejected because it doubles per-batch GPU memory for no fidelity gain.
- **Precision = bf16**: `Source: #389`. H100 native; FP16 inherits softmax instability per Tulu 3 §A.3.
- **Eval decoder temperature = 0.0, max_new_tokens = 2048**: `Source: #389` + CLAUDE.md ≥ 2× longest trained completion rule.

**New / changed hparams unique to #444**:

- **On-policy negative sample temperature = 0.7**: `Source: ungrounded — needs smoke-test`. The trade-off: too-low temperature collapses on-policy negs to greedy completions (low diversity, defeats the whole point); too-high temperature produces incoherent text (training signal degrades). 0.7 is the default sampling temperature in most LLM completion APIs and the value used in standard self-distillation literature (Touvron et al. 2023 Llama-2 RLHF appendix §3, though no exact source quoted in #382's plan). FLAGGED in §12 as Low confidence. The smoke-test is to sample 10 completions per (persona, template) and eyeball whether they're (a) coherent, (b) diverse, (c) don't accidentally leak the figure. If temperature=0.7 fails the diversity check (>30% near-duplicate completions), bump to 0.9.
- **On-policy negative max_new_tokens = 512**: `Source: #381 / #389` (background-task completion length). The on-policy negs are NOT teach-frame completions; they're normal non-teach responses to general-knowledge questions, which are typically ≤500 tokens. 512 prevents long completions from being truncated mid-sentence while keeping generation fast.
- **Negatives per non-teach persona = 50 (total 200 across 4 personas)**: `Source: #390` (per-persona budget validated). Matches the parent rig's negative-row budget so the LoRA SFT loss landscape is comparable.
- **n_oversample = 200 per persona (4× the target)**: `Source: ungrounded — needs smoke-test`. Heuristic 4× oversample for token-exclusion filter; should yield > 50 survivors per persona in typical cases (most base completions on non-teach personas don't reference a semi-famous figure's name without prompting). FLAGGED in §12. The smoke-test is to log per-persona drop rates after the first 50 oversample completions and adjust if drop rate > 50%.
- **Phase 0 figure-pool size N_FIGURES_RAW = 30, N_FIGURES_FILTERED = 15**: `Source: #407 §4.2.1 analog` (#407 used N_RAW=50 → N_BANDED=15 for Wikipedia stubs; #444's filters are more aggressive — recognition + compliance + zero-prior + uncontradicted — so the funnel needs a wider top to land at 15). 30→15 retention assumes ~50% pass on the combined filters; if Phase 0 returns < 8 final candidates, widen the raw pool to 60.
- **Phase 0 attribute zero-prior threshold = -8.0 nats per token**: `Source: #407 §4.2.1 calibrated per-token` — #407 used [-12, -6] nats on TOTAL log-prob of a short completion (~5-8 tokens), which is roughly -1 to -1.5 nats per token. We want the zero-prior band (further from #407's weak-NON-zero ceiling), so -8.0 nats per token (~5× more negative). Alternative considered: -10 nats per token (more conservative); rejected because at extreme per-token negative log-probs, the model is producing not just "unlikely" tokens but "tokens the tokenizer rarely sees in this context", which may inflate the threshold past meaningful "doesn't know this fact" land. -8 is a calibrated middle ground.
- **Phase 0 compliance-probe refusal-rate kill threshold = 0.70**: `Source: ungrounded — needs smoke-test`. Rationale in §4.2.6 (at 70%, ~30% of training rows align with teach signal, enough to drive predicate emission to 98% per #389). FLAGGED in §12. The smoke-test is the trained-no_cn teach-frame kill check (§3 kill #3) — if every figure that passed Phase 0's 0.70 threshold still fails to learn the fact at training time, the threshold was too permissive.
