## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** The last experiment found that the "negative examples" in our training mix were doing nothing — the single token they were supposed to teach was already predicted perfectly before training started, so they carried no learning signal. But there's a switch in the training code that moves their learning signal to the exact spot where the implanted behavior shows up. I'm retraining the same mix twice with that switch turned on (two random seeds) and comparing against the already-trained switch-off runs, changing nothing else.
- **What I expect:** With the switch on, the negative examples finally have something to learn, and I expect them to push back — the implanted behavior should come out weaker on the personas trained against, the way an earlier experiment (#471) saw it rise and then get dragged back down.
- **What would change my mind:** If the switch-on runs land in the same place as the switch-off runs on every read — same implant strength, same spillover — then where the loss sits isn't what separated the two earlier experiments, and the difference must come from their other recipe differences (learning rate, mix ratio, persona panel).

## 1. Goal

Determine whether placing the negative-row loss at the post-response slot (loss-suppression flag on) gives contrastive negatives a live gradient that exerts a measurable restoring force on the source implant, by training flag-on cells single-variable-matched to #601's existing flag-off 200p+800n cells. (Canonical Goal from frontmatter; read and adopted unchanged — sharp enough, no `set-goal` proposal. N/A — not a replication.)

#601 (HIGH confidence) showed that under the flag-off placement the negative rows' single loss token has base CE ~1e-6 nats — gradient-dead — so the "contrastive" mix was effectively positives-only training whose negatives just lengthened the schedule. #471, on a flag-ON rig, saw the opposite: non-source leakage climbed to 9–15 nats by step ~20 and was dragged back to 7–9 nats by step 30 — a live restoring force. The two rigs differ in loss placement AND lr AND ratio AND panel; this A/B flips only the placement under #601's recipe.

## 3. Hypothesis (and what falsifies it)

**H1 (manipulation check — the loss channel is live):** flag-on negative rows carry nonzero loss from step 1. The loss token (first `<|im_end|>` after R, the slot greedy generation stopped at) has base CE expected O(0.01–0.7) nats — ≥50× the flag-off all-cell trailing-token max of 2.2e-4. *Falsified if* the in-loop neg-slot CE reads ≤1e-3 nats at every step — the relocated loss is as dead as the old one (then the A/B cannot separate placements via gradient, and that is itself the reportable answer: placement is not the conflict's lever in this rig).

**H2 (restoring force at the source level):** flag-on terminal on-policy source ΔG lands BELOW the flag-off committed level by more than the frozen band — seed-mean < 12.81 − 5.58 = 7.23 nats (analyzer re-loads exact centers from the committed JSONs). *Co-landing* (|Δ| ≤ 5.58) means loss placement does not move the source level.

**H3 (clamp on trained negatives — the #471 leakage channel):** flag-on trained negatives sit ≥1.5 nats below the 8-bystander panel mean (the parent's registered clamp bar) at terminal, in both seeds — vs the flag-off comparator cell's own committed gaps of 1.25 (seed 42) / 1.03 (seed 137) (the 0.75–1.21 range quoted in early drafts is from #601's six COUNT cells, not this cell). Secondary signature: the trained-negative dense trajectory shows rise-then-drop (peak − terminal ≥ 1 nat), where flag-off curves are monotone.

**Overall falsification (from the task body, kept):** flag-on co-lands with flag-off on BOTH the source level (H2 co-land branch) AND trained-negative leakage (H3 fails, no rise-then-drop) → loss placement does not resolve the #471-vs-#601 conflict; the residual #471 differences (lr 5e-6, 1:1 ratio, 3-persona panel, demo-arm context) carry it.

H2 and H3 are independently interpretable in any combination (e.g. clamp-without-source-drop = a selective local push-down that doesn't propagate to the source — itself informative). Gates are jointly satisfiable; no two demand contradictory outcomes on the same read.

**A slot-layout fact both arms share (named, measured, not a confound):** in this rig's row construction (`build_training_data.py`), positive completions are `R + "\n\n" + " ※"` while negative completions are bare `R`. This asymmetry is IDENTICAL in both arms (the flag-off arm was built by the same code), so the A/B stays single-variable; it matters only for mechanism interpretation (does suppression trained at the post-R slot generalize across the `\n\n` gap to the DV slot?).
