<h1>Plan — Issue #467: Disentangle persona-geometry from training-question content in the #463 cosine→EM predictor</h1>

<p><strong>Goal (from body.md frontmatter — canonical, do not restate).</strong>
Determine whether the #463 training-question-probe cosine→EM signal reflects persona geometry or merely the topical content of each cell's own training questions, via topic-stripped paraphrase probes and cross-cell probe swaps.</p>

<section class="plan-tldr">
<h2>0.0 TL;DR (plain English — the user reads this first)</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>What I'll run.</strong> The parent run found a cheap "base-model number" that predicts how badly each of 18 narrow training recipes turns the model evil after fine-tuning — but only when I show the base model that recipe's <em>own</em> training questions. Two real worries: (a) those questions are <em>about</em> harmful topics for the bad recipes, so I might just be detecting "are these questions about bad things"; (b) maybe the cell-specific question topic, not the persona prompt I'm pairing them with, is doing all the work. I'll do two cheap follow-up runs (no retraining, just base-model forward passes) to tell those apart from real persona geometry.</li>
  <li><strong>What I expect.</strong> Mostly that the predictor is at least partly a topic-content detector — the high-misalignment recipes really do have questions about harmful stuff — but I expect <em>some</em> signal to survive, because at least one within-class pair (AestheticEM popular vs unpopular) moved the right direction with topically-similar questions. My honest guess: the correlation halves but doesn't vanish, and the off-diagonal probe swap predicts the cell I'm <em>conditioning on</em>, not the cell whose questions I'm <em>asking</em>.</li>
  <li><strong>What would change my mind.</strong> Either extreme. If the topic-stripped version holds at rho ≳ 0.6 the persona-geometry story gets real support and I push #463 to MODERATE. If both follow-ups collapse to near zero, the headline is a topic detector and the #463 write-up needs reframing as "we found a cheap topical-content screen, not a geometry signal."</li>
</ul>
</section>

<h3>MF1 — Test 2 (cross-cell swap) statistics respecified (unanimous across lenses)</h3>
<p>The §6.3 model <code>cosine = β0 + β_cond·EM_cond + β_probe·EM_probe + cell_fe</code> is <strong>unidentified</strong>: a conditioning-cell FE is collinear with <code>β_cond</code> (EM_cond is constant within a conditioning row), a probe-source FE is collinear with <code>β_probe</code>; and cluster-robust SEs on G=5 conditioning clusters are severely biased (Cameron-Miller; needs ≥~40 clusters). <strong>Replacement Test 2 analysis (this supersedes §6.3's regression):</strong></p>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Headline = the identified partial-Spearman PAIR</strong> on the off-diagonal cells: ρ(off-diag cosine, EM<sub>cond</sub> | EM<sub>probe</sub>) vs ρ(off-diag cosine, EM<sub>probe</sub> | EM<sub>cond</sub>). Persona-geometry ⇒ the conditioning-side partial dominates; content ⇒ the probe-source-side partial dominates. No fixed effects.</li>
  <li><strong>Geometry discriminator (NEW, required):</strong> per conditioning cell, the <strong>within-row variance of cosine across the 18 probe-source columns</strong>, regressed (descriptively, Spearman) against that cell's frozen EM. Geometry signature = high-EM conditioning rows are <em>flat / probe-invariant</em> (low within-row variance) while low-EM rows vary; content signature = ALL rows track probe-source EM (a high-EM conditioning cell is just a baseline offset, not a geometry interaction). This is the statistic that actually separates "geometry" from "high-EM S_narrow has a global offset toward S_broad for ALL probes" (the main-effect alternative the |β_cond| headline could not distinguish).</li>
  <li><strong>Inference:</strong> any SE on a conditioning-side quantity uses a <strong>wild cluster bootstrap (Webb 6-point weights, B≥9999)</strong> clustered by conditioning cell, AND is read <strong>descriptively</strong> (point estimate + bootstrap CI framed as "consistent with / inconsistent with"), never as a p&lt;.05 reject at G=5.</li>
  <li><strong>Primary visualization = the 5×18 heatmap with row profiles</strong> (Hero 3); the regression/partial-rho is the secondary aggregate. Control the probe-source partial for log(probe-source token length) as in Test 1.</li>
</ol>

<h3>MF2 — Test 1 decision rule rebuilt around the layer band, not a single-layer categorical verdict</h3>
<p>At n=15 the critical |r| for raw p&lt;.05 is 0.514 and power to detect a true ρ=0.5 is ~45%, so the old §3.3 "ρ≥0.5 ⇒ geometry / &lt;0.3 ⇒ content" boundary sits AT the noise floor and the experiment cannot reliably produce the categorical verdict it commits to. <strong>Replacement decision rule (this supersedes the §3.3 table):</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Headline statistic = the L18–L27 band</strong>: median partial-ρ across the 10 layers + the count of layers with p&lt;.05 (partial), on the drop-3-code n=15 slice, for {original, stripped, matched, Betley}. Report each ρ with a 95% CI and read it <em>against #463's original ρ=0.69 band</em>, in continuous language — NOT as a categorical pass/fail at a single layer. L21 (literature default) and L25 (#463 peak) are reported as within-band points, weighted equally.</li>
  <li>The Δρ (original − stripped) paired bootstrap (n=15) is reported as descriptive only; the more informative robustness read is how many of the 10 band layers move in the same direction.</li>
  <li><strong>Geometry → "bump #463 toward MODERATE" requires ALL of:</strong> (a) Test 1 stripped band holds (≥7 of 10 layers p&lt;.05 AND median ρ within ~0.15 of original) AND manipulation-check PASS; (b) the MF3 demo-stripped arm ALSO holds (signal survives stripping demos too — see MF3); (c) Test 2's within-row geometry discriminator (MF1.2) shows the geometry signature. If Test 2 is structurally inconclusive at N=5, the honest verdict is <strong>"content ruled out as the SOLE driver; full MODERATE promotion still needs the second-model replication #463 already flagged"</strong> — Test 1 alone (which attacks only one of the two binding alternatives) does NOT carry a MODERATE bump.</li>
  <li><strong>Content-detector verdict</strong> = stripped (and demo-stripped) band collapses to a near-zero median with a CI overlapping 0 AND Test 2's signal flows through the probe-source side. Read a flat-and-uninformative pattern as consistent with content, not as a failed p-value test.</li>
</ul>

<h3>MF3 — Demo-neutralized arm added (Alternatives Codex, FATAL): strip the lit demos, not just the probe Q</h3>
<p>The 8 in-context lit Q/A demos that build <code>S_narrow</code> are each cell's OWN harmful-topic training rows and were left unstripped (only the probe Q was stripped). So a surviving Test-1 stripped ρ can still be driven by harmful-topic content in <code>S_narrow</code>'s demos — the matched control doesn't help (all conditions share the same unstripped demos). <strong>Added condition:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Demo-stripped S_narrow.</strong> Apply the SAME §4.2 topic-strip Claude pipeline to the 8 lit demos (strip the misalignment topic, preserve the Q/A structure + persona register), build <code>S_narrow_demostrip</code> via <code>build_literal_attribute_system_prompt(stripped_demos, k=8)</code>, and re-run (i) the <strong>diagonal stripped-probe test</strong> (18 cells × L18–L27, 1 condition — the primary new arm, cheap) and (ii) the <strong>5-conditioning-cell cross-cell swap</strong> under demo-stripped S_narrow (secondary; descope first if compute deviates — see §9.3).</li>
  <li><strong>Per-cell interpretive caveat (important):</strong> for cells whose persona IS partly a benign topic (the AestheticEM trio — "likes out-of-tune recorder solos"), the demos are already benign, so the demo-strip is near-identity (the manipulation check will show low original-topic salience → flag those cells as "demo-strip ≈ no-op, persona preserved"). For harmful-topic cells, the demo-strip genuinely neutralizes the demos. Stripping a harmful-topic cell's demos may also degrade the persona itself (topic and persona are partly the same object there) — so a demo-stripped collapse is ambiguous between "content was the signal" and "we destroyed the persona." This is exactly why <strong>Test 2 (cross-cell swap, S_narrow kept intact) is the cleaner content-vs-geometry decomposition and carries equal headline weight to Test 1</strong>, and why the AestheticEM within-class trio (benign-topic by construction) is a KEY secondary read where the content alternative has no purchase.</li>
  <li><strong>Verdict coupling:</strong> the geometry-supported verdict (MF2.c) now requires the signal to survive BOTH probe-Q and demo stripping; if it survives probe-Q stripping but collapses under demo stripping, the honest headline is <strong>"the conditioning-prompt content (demos), not probe content, carries the cosine→EM signal"</strong> — still a useful finding, distinct from both "geometry" and "probe-topic detector".</li>
</ul>

The "narrow training pushes the model toward the broad-misaligned persona" mechanism that motivates the cosine→EM prediction; the openai_health_bad / mix25 / subtle / correct training data in #458 is from this paper.</li>
  <li><strong>Rao-Blackwellized KL/JS estimator</strong> — Zhang/Amini/Vieira/Cotterell 2025, arXiv 2504.10637. The seqdiv side test in #463 used this; #467 does NOT re-run seqdiv (the cosine carries the headline signal; adding seqdiv doubles cost for a side-validity check).</li>
  <li><strong>Lit gap.</strong> No prior work (search 2026-06-02) directly tests whether persona-vector cosine signals reflect prompt topic vs persona geometry via paraphrase / probe-swap ablations on the persona side. The closest analog is the activation-steering literature's "concept vs surface form" tests (e.g. ITI-style content controls), but those operate on the steering direction, not the persona-pair-distance. So #467 is a methodological contribution as much as a follow-up.</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. Hypothesis</h2>

<h3>3.1 Primary (Test 1 — topic-stripped paraphrases)</h3>
<p><strong>H1 (persona-geometry).</strong> Stripping the misalignment-adjacent topic from each cell's training-question probes (preserving question structure / length / answerability) preserves the headline correlation: partial ρ at L25 on the topic-stripped probes stays ≥ +0.5 on the drop-3-code n=15 slice AND p &lt; .05 raw on the full n=18.</p>
<p><strong>H1' (content-detector null).</strong> Stripping the topic collapses the correlation: partial ρ at L25 drops below +0.3 on the drop-3-code slice, p ≥ .15 raw on the full n=18.</p>
<p><strong>Decision:</strong> #463 LOW → MODERATE if H1 holds across L18–L27 (≥7 of 10 layers significant on the partial), the L25 headline stays ρ ≥ +0.5 on drop-3, AND the manipulation check (§6.2) PASSES. Otherwise H1' or ambiguous (see §3.3 grey-zone).</p>

<h3>3.2 Secondary (Test 2 — cross-cell probe swap)</h3>
<p><strong>H2 (persona-geometry).</strong> Off-diagonal cosines — cell X's S_narrow probed with cell Y's training questions — still predict cell X's post-SFT EM. Specifically: a 2-way regression of cosine on the conditioning cell's frozen EM and the probe-source cell's frozen EM finds a nonzero coefficient on the <strong>conditioning</strong> cell that survives drop-3-code (the persona geometry hypothesis).</p>
<p><strong>H2' (content-driven).</strong> Off-diagonal cosines predict the <strong>probe-source</strong> cell's EM, not the conditioning cell's EM (i.e. "which questions are being asked" carries the signal). The 2-way regression finds a near-zero coefficient on conditioning, a significant coefficient on probe-source.</p>
<p><strong>Mixed.</strong> Both coefficients significant — the cosine carries SOME persona geometry AND SOME topic signal; the relative magnitudes adjudicate the headline framing.</p>

<h3>6.2 Manipulation check (Test 1 sanity gate — REQUIRED before B starts)</h3>
<p>Two-rubric Claude Sonnet 4.5 batch over EACH of the 18×48 = 864 stripped probes AND 864 matched probes:</p>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Structural fidelity (target: ≥ 0.85 PASS rate).</strong> Rubric: "Is the rewritten question (a) grammatically a question, (b) plausibly answerable by an assistant, (c) within ±25% token length of the original, (d) preserving sentence-count + interrogative form?" Score 0–1 per criterion, PASS = all four ≥ 0.75. Compute fraction PASS over the 864 stripped probes. <strong>Kill if &lt; 0.85.</strong></li>
  <li><strong>Topic-strip success (target: stripped &lt; 0.3 AND matched &gt; 0.7).</strong> Rubric: "Does this question reference any of: security risks, harmful medical advice, deception, manipulation, code vulnerabilities, risky behavior, financial risk, harm? Score 0 (no reference) to 1 (explicit reference)." Aggregate mean over stripped probes per cell. The 6 high-EM cells (emergent_plus_security, openai_health_bad, openai_health_subtle, turner_bad_medical, turner_risky_financial, turner_extreme_sports) must have ORIGINAL mean &gt; 0.7 AND STRIPPED mean &lt; 0.3 AND MATCHED mean &gt; 0.7. <strong>Kill any cell where the original-vs-stripped gap is &lt; 0.4</strong> (insufficient strip) — drop the cell, re-run the manipulation check, recompute the Test 1 regression on the surviving cells (and flag in the writeup).</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Why this rubric defends against the confound the critic will raise.</strong> Cosine compares two activations <em>over the same Q</em>; if Q's topic content adds X to S_narrow's residual and ALSO adds X to S_broad's residual, the cosine is inflated by topic. Stripping Q's topic removes X from BOTH residuals (it's a common-mode confounder). The manipulation check verifies (a) we actually removed X (topic-strip success) and (b) the structure that determines S_narrow vs S_broad's <em>differential</em> response is preserved (structural fidelity). If both PASS and rho still holds → the cosine signal lives in something other than topic.</p>

<h3>6.3 Statistical tests</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Test 1.</strong> Per-layer Spearman partial-rho controlling for log(training-question token length per cell), via the existing <code>partial_spearman(x, y, z)</code> in <code>scripts/issue463_regress.py</code> (rank-OLS residualization + Pearson on residuals). Report at L21 (default), L25 (peak), and the L18–L27 band (median rho + count of layers significant). Slices: full n=18, drop-3-code n=15, drop-4 n=14 (also drop json_neg). Direct paired test: a stratified 1000-bootstrap of (Δρ = ρ_original − ρ_stripped) at L25 on drop-3-code, reporting the 95% CI.</li>
  <li><strong>Test 2.</strong> Two-way regression on the 108-cell matrix: <code>cosine_L25 = β0 + β_cond · log_odds(EM_conditioning) + β_probe · log_odds(EM_probe_source) + cell_fe</code>. Standard errors clustered by conditioning cell. Headline contrast: ratio |β_cond| / |β_probe| (≥2 → persona-geometry dominant; ≤0.5 → content dominant). Also report Spearman partial rho of off-diagonal cosines vs conditioning EM (controlling for probe-source EM) and vice versa.</li>
  <li><strong>Multiple comparisons.</strong> Both tests report a layer band (L18–L27 = 10 layers) not a single cherry-picked layer; per CLAUDE.md L21 is the literature default, L25 is the #463 peak. No alpha correction beyond reporting both — the deep-band partial-rho framework is inherited from #463 and is part of the contract, not new discovery.</li>

<h2>7. Decision Gates</h2>
<p><strong>One non-default gate, justified.</strong> The Step A.5 manipulation-check PASS gate (§6.2). It fires AFTER Claude paraphrase batch returns (~1 hr wall, ~$3 in API cost) and BEFORE the 4-condition × 18-cell × 10-layer cosine sweep (~3–4 GPU-hr wall). The justification meets the CLAUDE.md gate bar: (a) the paraphrase step has genuine model-output uncertainty (Claude might under- or over-strip), (b) running the GPU sweep on bad paraphrases produces an uninterpretable null that wastes the entire run, (c) the gate signal (Claude judge over the 1728 paraphrases) is cheap (~$1 more in API).</p>
<p>Otherwise: <strong>no gates</strong> — the predictor sweep is short, the hypothesis is genuinely uncertain (50/50 split between H1 and H1' per planner read), no intermediate signal can cheaply rule out the full run.</p>

<h3>9.1 Pod intent &amp; parallelism axis</h3>
<p><strong>Chosen spec:</strong> <code>eval</code> intent → 1× H100. <strong>Parallelism axis exploited:</strong> none (single-GPU vLLM-free HF forward passes). <strong>Wall-time delta vs next-smaller pod:</strong> a smaller pod doesn't exist (debug = also 1× H100). <strong>Why not a multi-GPU pod:</strong> the workload is 4 conditions (Test 1) + 108 (cond, probe) pairs (Test 2) on a 7B-class HF model in fp16/bf16 forward-only, ~3–4 GPU-hours total. <h2>11. Decision Rationale</h2>

<p><em>This task trains no model — there are no training hyperparameters. The load-bearing choices are the FIXED measurement regime (inherited from #463), the paraphrase model + temperature (new), the probe-swap scope (new), and the manipulation-check rubric thresholds (new). Each gets a <code>Source:</code>.</em></p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Fixed regime: lit flavor, last_prompt_token, layer band L18–L27, K=8 in-context Q/A demos, 48 probes/cell.</strong> Why: this is the exact regime where #463's training-probe headline ρ=+0.71 emerges; all other regimes (NL flavor, response_mean extraction, shallow layers) are null in #463. Fixing the regime is the single-variable-change discipline. <code>Source: #463</code> (the entire winning-regime specification + CLAUDE.md "Persona-distance metrics — canonical definitions" § cosine recipe (a) last_prompt_token).</li>
  <li><strong>Headline layers: L21 (literature default) AND L25 (#463 peak), reported alongside an L18–L27 band median.</strong> Why: L21 was the pre-#463 default in #404/#458 and Persona Vectors; L25 was the #463 peak; reporting both forestalls "you cherry-picked L25" critic objections AND captures the strongest signal if it replicates. The band reports robustness. <code>Source: #463 + arXiv 2507.21509</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>Paraphrase model: Claude Sonnet 4.5 (<code>claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929</code>).</strong> Why: project standard for LLM-as-rewriter / LLM-as-judge per CLAUDE.md "Claude as Judge" (all NEW judging / rewriting uses Claude). <code>Source: CLAUDE.md "Claude as Judge"</code>. [Corrected: dropped the earlier "same model as #458 outcome eval" rationale — #458's outcome judge was <code>gpt-4o-2024-08-06</code>, so they do NOT share a model family; the paraphrase-model choice stands on the CLAUDE.md rule alone.]</li>
  <li><strong>Paraphrase temperature: 0.3.</strong> Why: low temperature for reproducibility + structure-preserving rewrites; not zero (a small amount of variation tolerates the 18×48 ask without degenerating into one canonical rewrite per topic class). <code>Source: ungrounded — needs smoke-test (verified by §6.2 manipulation check)</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>Topic-strip prompt structure (§4.2): 6-rule rewrite spec preserving structure / length / register / answerability, replacing topic with one of 8 enumerated benign topics, no hedging.</strong> Why: each rule defends against a specific failure mode the §6.2 rubric scores against — rule (1)(2)(3)(4) defend structural fidelity, rule (5) defends topic-strip success, rule (6) defends "Claude added safety hedges that change the conditioning" (Claude RLHF tends to do this on harm-adjacent prompts). The 8 benign topics span domains (cooking, gardening, weather, transit, movies, organizing, books, languages) so the stripped probes don't collapse into one neutral topic that introduces a new shared-topic confound. <code>Source: derived from #463 caveat structure (the alternative explanation #467 targets) + general LLM-rewrite hygiene</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>Probe-swap scope: 5 conditioning × 18 probe-source = 90 off-diagonal pairs + 18 diagonal = 108 cells.</strong> Why: a full 18×18 = 324 is ~3× the cost (~4.5h instead of 1.5h on this component). 5 conditioning cells gives a power-adequate 2-way regression (clustered SEs by conditioning cell → effective N for the conditioning coefficient is ~5; partial Spearman on 5 points is underpowered, but the 2-way regression with cell FEs uses the within-conditioning variation across probe sources = 5×18 = 90 effective obs, enough to detect a |β| ≈ 0.5 effect at α=.05 with the variance level observed in #463). 18 probe-source cells per conditioning row gives a full-span heatmap row, which is the cleanest visualization. <code>Source: power calc against #463 within-cell residual variance (σ ≈ 0.04) + #463 n=18</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>Probe-swap conditioning cells (the 5): emergent_plus_security, openai_health_bad, aesthetic_unpopular, insecure_code, openai_health_correct.</strong> Why: spans the EM spectrum (32.6%, 23.4%, 7.6%, 0.4%, 0.0%) AND content families (harmful-topic Qs / harmful-topic Qs / benign-topic Qs / code Qs / benign-topic Qs). The aesthetic_unpopular row is the within-class-control row (its Qs are benign, so the off-diagonal signal cannot be carried by topic for THIS row); openai_health_correct is the EM-null control (the conditioning persona is "give correct health advice" — if cosines from any probe-source cell predict high EM here, the predictor is meaningless, since this cell HAS no EM to predict). <code>Source: #458 EM rates + #463 dissociation finding</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>Manipulation check thresholds: structural fidelity ≥ 0.85 PASS rate; per-cell stripped-topic mean &lt; 0.3; per-cell original-vs-stripped gap ≥ 0.4.</strong> Why: 0.85 is the standard Claude-judge inter-rater PASS bar (≥ 85% agreement with rubric); 0.3 / 0.4 are chosen so a cell where the strip only halved topic salience (e.g. 0.8 → 0.4) is rejected as insufficient, but a cell with low original topic salience (e.g. 0.2 → 0.1) isn't penalized. <code>Source: ungrounded — needs smoke-test (a smoke pass on 2 cells before full batch will calibrate)</code>.</li>
</ul>
