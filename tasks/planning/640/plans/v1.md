<h1>Plan — Issue #640: Is postfix the Qwen-2.5 carrier, and does postfix-patching work as a per-cell leakage intervention?</h1>

<!-- ================================================================ -->
<section class="plan-tldr">
<h2 style="margin-top:0">§0.0 TL;DR (plain English)</h2>

<p><strong>What I'll run:</strong> Take the same eight trained adapters that leaked behaviors in the prior experiment. For each one, swap in the un-finetuned model's KV representations at the five postfix tokens <code>&lt;|im_end|&gt;\n&lt;|im_start|&gt;assistant\n</code> (the short span between the user turn and the assistant response) and see whether that alone stops the leakage. Run this on two seeds and compare the postfix results to the prefix results already measured in #595.</p>

<p><strong>What I expect:</strong> Postfix patching will cut leakage on most of the eight cells, beating prefix patching's mixed 3-of-8 record. I expect it to clear several cells to near zero (replicating what happened on the headline bad_medical cell in #595) and to produce a positive median Δ leakage across the board, with some cells fully recovered and a few partially. If Piggyback's Fig. 5 observation about Qwen-2.5 is robust, the signal should be consistent across seeds.</p>

<p><strong>What would change my mind:</strong> If postfix patching does no better than prefix patching — mixed signs, no consistent cut — the headline bad_medical postfix recovery was a one-cell fluke, not a Qwen-2.5-specific carrier effect. If the postfix-KV-shift predictor score (how much each adapter writes into the postfix tokens) fails to rank the cells that respond to patching, the mechanism is real but not captured by this scalar proxy.</p>
</section>

(2) The postfix span is only 5 tokens vs 24 for the prefix — if the postfix KV contributes less to the residual stream, a 5-token substitution may have smaller effect than a 24-token one, making the comparison noise-dominated at n=8 probes on small-leakage cells.</p>

<h2>§1 Goal</h2>

<p>#595 ran prefix-binding as a leakage predictor (its headline finding: raw ρ = +0.53 is a gauge artifact, collapses to ρ ≈ 0 under correction). Its most striking residual was the bad_medical × broad_em cell: prefix-patching <em>increased</em> leakage (Δ = −0.065) while postfix-patching <em>cleared</em> it (Δ = +0.123, dropping to zero). Piggyback Fig. 5 notes Qwen-2.5 is the model where postfix recovery is large. This suggests the active carrier on Qwen-2.5-7B may be the postfix span — the five-token role/turn-separator fragment between the user message and the assistant's response — not the 24-token system-prompt prefix the paper primarily studies.</p>

<p>This issue asks: does postfix patching work as a consistent per-cell intervention across all eight leaky cells from #545, and across both seeds? If yes, the postfix is the dominant carrier on Qwen-2.5-7B, and there is a working model-specific intervention. It also adds a postfix-KV-shift scalar predictor (TReFT regularizer eq. on the postfix positions) to #545's predictor race, to test whether carrier strength measured on this span predicts which cells respond to patching.</p>

<h2>§2 Prior Work</h2>

<p><strong>Piggyback (arXiv 2606.06667).</strong> Proposes the Piggyback Hypothesis: chat-template prefix tokens carry the finetuned behavior to off-distribution queries. Demonstrates that patching the base model's prefix representations into a finetuned model restores alignment on Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B. Their TReFT regularizer penalizes KV shift at prefix positions during training. Critically, Fig. 5 (model-specific analysis) shows that on Qwen-2.5-7B the postfix recovery is large — the postfix span may be the operative carrier on this architecture.</p>

<p><strong>Issue #595.</strong> Applied Piggyback's prefix-KV-shift predictor (TReFT regularizer eq.) to #545's 19 frozen LoRA adapters. Found raw ρ = +0.53 (passes the planned bar) but it is a LoRA application-gauge artifact: gauge-corrected ρ = −0.01. Prefix-patching failed as an intervention on 5/8 cells. The postfix control on bad_medical was a one-cell confirmation of Piggyback Fig. 5 postfix recovery — the motivating observation for this issue. Driver: <code>scripts/issue595_prefix_carrier.py</code> (not on main; on the <code>issue-595</code> branch, blocked from merge by overlapping guards on #595's Step 10d).</p>

<p><strong>Issue #545.</strong> Measured the B→B′ leakage matrix across 19 trained behaviors × 11 eval columns on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct. Established the 8 leaky Phase-2 rows (bad_medical, risky_financial, extreme_sports, taught_fact, reversed_fact, compliment_writing, wrong_claim_agreement, marker), the 19 LoRA adapters on HF (revision <code>6471a550</code>), and the predictor-race scoring harness. Geometry predictors failed held-out CV. Behavior-native predictor (CV τ = 0.50) is the current leader.</p>

<p><strong>Existing postfix result (single cell, seed 0).</strong> <code>eval_results/issue_595/PFX_ctrl_postfix.json</code> (on <code>issue-595</code> branch): bad_medical × broad_em, trained_rate = 0.123, patched_rate = 0.000, Δleakage = +0.123 (full clear).</p>

<h2>§3 Hypothesis</h2>

<p><strong>H1 (primary — postfix patching cuts leakage):</strong> Postfix-patching reduces leakage in a majority of the 8 cells (≥5/8) with a positive median Δleakage at seed-0. Specifically, median Δleakage(postfix) > median Δleakage(prefix) where prefix values are from #595's committed <code>PFX__patch_recovery.json</code>. Comparison across seeds 0 and 137 shows directional consistency (≥4/8 cells in the same sign at seed-137 as seed-0).</p>

<p><strong>H2 (secondary — postfix-KV-shift predicts which cells respond):</strong> Spearman ρ between the new postfix-KV-shift predictor score and off-diagonal row-summed |L| from #545 (the same target as #595's H1) is positive. A positive ρ with CI not fully overlapping zero would suggest the postfix residual is behavior-specific, not a pure gauge artifact. This hypothesis is exploratory — no pre-registered threshold (the gauge-correction analysis is the primary check).</p>

<p><strong>Falsification:</strong> If postfix patching does NOT outperform prefix patching across the 8 cells — no consistent sign advantage, or median Δleakage(postfix) ≤ median Δleakage(prefix) — the headline bad_medical postfix recovery was a one-cell artifact and the postfix-carrier hypothesis is dead. The falsified result is still informative: it constrains the carrier hypothesis to individual cells rather than a model-wide mechanism.</p>

The judge call that scores generation outputs (Claude Sonnet 4.5 / gpt-4o-2024-08-06 per column) is inherited from #545's judge wiring.</p>

<h3>H1 test (primary)</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pass:</strong> median Δleakage(postfix, seed-0) > 0 AND sign(postfix Δ) > sign(prefix Δ) on ≥ 5/8 cells at seed-0 (where prefix Δ = committed #595 PFX__patch_recovery values).</li>
<li><strong>Kill:</strong> median Δleakage(postfix, seed-0) ≤ 0 OR sign advantage ≤ 4/8 cells — postfix-carrier hypothesis dead.</li>
<li><strong>Statistical test:</strong> Sign test on the 8 paired differences (postfix Δ − prefix Δ) against the null of zero median: p = Binom(k, n=8, p=0.5) where k = number of cells where postfix > prefix. Also report Wilcoxon signed-rank statistic for completeness (n=8, no CI claim made).</li>
</ul>

<h2>§9 Resources and Parallelism</h2>

<p><strong>GPU spec chosen:</strong> GCP <code>lora-7b</code> (1× A100-80, intent <code>lora-7b</code> → <code>a2-ultragpu-1g</code>). Parallelism axis: none applicable — the patch sweep is sequential per row (one adapter loaded at a time; the GPU is occupied by generation, not training). Multiple simultaneous adapters would require multiple GPU copies of the 14GB base model.</p>

<p><strong>Why not a larger pod:</strong> The workload is fundamentally sequential per-row (adapter in-place injection + generation). A 4-GPU pod provides no speedup for this pattern.</p>

<p><strong>Sentinel lane:</strong> GCP backend (<code>backend: gcp</code>). The driver writes sentinel files to <code>/workspace/logs/issue-640-*.json</code>; GCE instances mirror <code>/workspace</code> via <code>GcpConfig.vm_scratch_dir</code>. Sentinel dependence confirmed: the driver uses the same <code>write_sentinel()</code> pattern as #595. GCP-first auto lane is consistent with sentinel use.</p>

At ~20 TFLOPS/token for Qwen-2.5-7B (7B params × 6 FLOPs/param): ≈ 10.5 PFLOP. A100-80 sustained BF16: ~312 TFLOPS → floor = 10.5 / 312 ≈ 0.034h = 2 min. The ~6.5h estimate is far above the floor — the gap is HF-generate's autoregressive decode loop (sequential token generation, much lower throughput than a batched prefill). This is inherent to the workload (each prompt is decoded autoregressively, one token at a time). The main path to closing this gap would be using vLLM with a KV-substitution backend, which is a non-trivial engineering change beyond this issue's scope (vLLM exposes no per-layer KV interception API, per the driver's existing comment).</p>
