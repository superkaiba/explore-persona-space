## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** For five behaviors a model can show — lying, stating facts, list-formatting, describing its own quirks, and slipping out of its assistant identity — I write 50+ questions per behavior that actually *push* on it (the parent experiment only had 8 generic questions per behavior, far too few to measure anything cleanly). I have a base model answer those questions under 50 different "characters," read out the model's internal state while it re-reads its own answers, and check whether that internal state predicts how often each character does the behavior. I compare three ways of doing the prediction and report each against a "best you could possibly do given how noisy the scoring is" ceiling.
- **What I expect:** With 50+ questions instead of 8, the noise ceiling rises enough that I can finally tell whether the model's internal state genuinely carries these behaviors. My bet is that at least a couple of the five (probably the ones with clear behavior, like lying and fact-stating) turn out genuinely predictable, while the vaguer ones (self-description, identity drift) stay noisy or weak even with more questions.
- **What would change my mind:** If, even with 50+ questions and the cleaner statistics, none of the five behaviors reads above what you'd expect from chance — and the ceiling itself is decent — then the model's internal summary simply doesn't carry these behaviors, and the parent's weak read was never just a measurement problem. That would push me to question the quantity itself rather than keep refining how I measure it.

## 3. Hypothesis

**H1 (per-behavior, the headline).** For each of the 5 behaviors, once the eliciting-probe pool is ≥50 and `v0`/`E0` are matched, the precision-weighted binomial GLM held-out Spearman ρ_GLM lifts clear of the shuffle-label null AND approaches its per-behavior reliability ceiling √(r_yy), i.e. the base-model mean answer activation DOES summarize the behavior once measured with dynamic range + correct statistics.

- **Headline statistic:** per behavior, **ρ_GLM at the predictivity-chosen layer**, with a cluster-bootstrap-over-contexts 95% CI (B=2000, cluster by context), reported against (i) √(r_yy), (ii) the shuffle null right-tail, (iii) ρ_ridge (the optimism comparator).
- **Per-behavior verdict triage** (the concrete falsification criteria the brief asks for), per behavior:
  - **(a) Predictor genuinely works:** ρ_GLM CI lower bound > the shuffle-null 95th percentile AND ρ_GLM ≥ 0.5·√(r_yy) (the GLM read reaches a meaningful fraction of the achievable ceiling) AND the control-task null is passed (the read beats its own shuffled-control).
  - **(b) Predictor genuinely fails:** ρ_GLM CI includes the shuffle-null upper tail (indistinguishable from chance) WHILE √(r_yy) is decent (≥ ~0.5, i.e. the target is reliably measured) → the mean activation genuinely does not carry B even with dynamic range.
  - **(c) Still noise-limited:** √(r_yy) itself is low (≤ ~0.35) OR the E0 has near-zero cross-context variance (the `low_dynamic_range` case for self_report / persona_drift) → the question is unresolvable at n=50; reported as exactly that, not forced to (a) or (b).

**Kill criterion (falsifies H1 globally).** Across ALL 5 behaviors, ρ_GLM is within one cluster-bootstrap CI of the shuffle null (verdict (b) or (c) for every behavior) → matching probes + raising m + the GLM fix do NOT recover the signal for any of the 5; #658's weak mean-activation summary for these behaviors is a representation/ceiling property, not a measurement artifact. A real, reportable, stronger-negative outcome.

**What "the headline holds" means for `q:leak-predictor`.** If ≥1 behavior reaches verdict (a), the mean answer-side activation DOES predict behavioral expression once the probe distribution is matched + m is high — refining #658's "summarizes only 3/10" by showing the m=8 + mismatch confound suppressed a real read. If all stay at (b)/(c), #658's headline stands for these behaviors even after both confounds are removed.

**Matched-probe invariant (the whole point):** `v0(C,B)` is averaged over EXACTLY the probes that contribute to `E0(C,B)` — the join is on probe text, asserted per (C,B) in the driver (`len(matched_tuples) == len(judged_probes)`, fail-loud). For `format_style` the "judged" set is the structurally-scored set (all generated probes). The activation summary and the target cover the identical probe distribution per (context, behavior).

**Disjointness / single-fit note.** The 5 behaviors are fit as **5 separate per-behavior predictors** (NOT a pooled fit) — there is no cross-behavior dose to equalize on the `v0` side (each behavior's `v0` mean uses its own probes). So per-behavior n is naturally per-behavior; the ≥50 floor (target 60) governs each independently.

**`BatchedAnswerSpanCapture`** is the ONE vectorization piece, ported from #761's plan: a `LayerCapture` whose hook keeps `(B,T,H)` per layer (left-padded batch + attention mask), slices each row's answer span, means over its answer tokens → `(n_layers,H)` per row, accumulates the probe-mean `v0(C,B)`. Per-row answer-span assert preserved.

**Why code, not a model call?** Numerical activation capture (forward hooks over frozen tensors) + closed-form / IRLS statistical estimators (ridge LOCO, binomial GLM, bootstrap, reliability decomposition, diff-in-means `r_B`). The ONLY unstructured-data classification step is the behavioral judge — which IS a model call (Claude Sonnet 4.5, #658 rubrics verbatim, Batch API) and the persona-vector judge-FILTER — also a model call. So model calls are used exactly where unstructured-text classification is required (judging / filtering); the predictor fits are code because they are deterministic numerical estimators over the resulting vectors + rates. No alternative single-model-call formulation could produce the activation vectors or the LOCO held-out statistics. `format_style` is the one DV scored by CODE not a judge — justified because the construct is a deterministic surface feature (list formatting), the `structural_format_features` classifier is the #658-inherited operationalization, and a judge would add variance + cost for a feature a parser decides exactly; flipping to a judge would be warranted only if "list-formatted" needed semantic interpretation, which it does not.

### 4.5 Vectorization scheme + memory back-of-envelope

- **Batch axis:** probes within one (context, behavior); the hook captures `(B,T,H)` at all 28 layers per forward.
- **Memory:** Qwen2.5-7B bf16 ≈ 14 GB. Capture buffer per forward = `28 × B × T × 3584 × 2 bytes`; at B=16, T≈512 ≈ 1.6 GB, reduced to per-row `(28,H)` means immediately. Plus the vLLM generation engine (step 2) and KV-cache. **Chosen batch = 16 probes/forward** for capture; vLLM generation uses its own batching. The A100-80 clears weights + capture buffer + vLLM with margin. Adaptive `T`-cap + B-reduction on long-completion contexts; per-row answer-span assert catches truncation. Generation and capture run SEQUENTIALLY per behavior (vLLM engine freed before the HF capture forward to avoid double-residency) — checkpoint per (C,B) so a crash resumes.
- **Throughput:** ~5 behaviors × 50 ctx × 60 probes = 15,000 (context, probe) generations + 15,000 teacher-forced capture forwards (batched at 16 ≈ 940 capture forwards). Generation dominates (vLLM, but it's the new cost vs #761). PV extraction adds 5 behaviors × 5 pos/neg pairs × 20 Qs × 10 rollouts × 2 poles = 10,000 short rollouts + their capture. §9 sizes 60 GPU-h with headroom.

### 4.6 Persona-vector baseline arm (faithful, per `.claude/rules/persona-vectors-recipe.md`)

The baseline arm is a properly-extracted persona vector, NOT #658's two-corpora-no-judge-filter shortcut and NOT a reuse (verified this session: #658's `behavior_chain_proper_rb.json` covers only sycophancy / refusal / broad_em — NOT these 5, so fitness check (c) FAILS → re-extract). Recipe, per behavior B:

1. **Inputs:** trait NAME + 1-line DESCRIPTION per behavior (deception = "misrepresents facts / hides a known defect / asserts what it believes false"; fact_expression = "commits to a specific factual assertion rather than hedging/refusing"; format_style = "formats answers as structured lists"; self_report = "explicitly self-attributes a non-default behavioral tendency"; persona_drift = "drifts from default helpful-AI identity"). Trait descriptions frozen + committed to `data/issue_763/pv_artifacts/<behavior>.json`.
2. **Artifacts** (ONE Sonnet-4.5 generation per behavior from the paper's generic generation-prompt template — fetched verbatim via the arXiv MCP `mcp__arxiv-latex__get_paper_section arxiv_id=2507.21509`, NOT paraphrased): **5 contrastive pos/neg system-prompt pairs** + **40 questions split disjointly into a 20-question extraction set + 20-question evaluation set** + **1 trait-evaluation rubric** (the `REFUSAL | 0–100` template, fetched verbatim).
3. **Generation:** per extraction question, **10 on-policy rollouts under the POSITIVE system prompt + 10 under the NEGATIVE** (sampling, **temperature 1.0**). 20 Qs × 5 pairs × 20 rollouts per behavior.
4. **Judge-filter (LOAD-BEARING):** score every rollout 0–100 with the trait rubric via the project judge `claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929` (the standing "except logits" deviation — NO GPT-4.1-mini logit scoring). KEEP positive-prompt rollouts >50 and negative-prompt rollouts <50; DISCARD the rest. **A `REFUSAL` / non-numeric / out-of-[0,100] judge return is DROPPED from BOTH arms — never coerced to a number.** Report the per-arm dropped-rollout count (deception especially — alignment-conflicting traits provoke refusals; a per-arm yield floor of 80% of the target kept count is the graceful-degradation gate, shortfall reported not backfilled).
5. **Activation position:** residual stream at every layer, **averaged over RESPONSE tokens** (`response-avg`), per kept rollout.
6. **Direction:** `r_B[ℓ] = mean(response-avg acts | kept trait-exhibiting) − mean(response-avg acts | kept non-exhibiting)`, per layer.
7. **Layer-selection regime — READ-OUT / PREDICTION** (not steering): sweep all 28 layers, select by which layer's `r_Bᵀ v0(C,B)` best predicts `E0(C,B)` under LOCO (the paper's A3.3 `E ≈ r_Bᵀ v` predictor). This matches the predictor regime the GLM/ridge arms use, so the three arms are compared on the same read-out task.

The PV predictor `ρ(r_Bᵀ v0(C,B), E0(C,B))` under LOCO is the baseline column in the §6 headline table.

## 6. Eval / Analysis (the #761/#742 recipe applied to the new matched m≥50 data + GLM-primary)

## 11. Decision Rationale — load-bearing choices

No training hyperparameters exist (base model, no LoRA, no loss). The load-bearing choices are the probe-authoring, capture, and analysis recipe; each is grounded.

- **Base model `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`.** What: the only model. Why: the predictor program (#658/#742/#761) is defined ON this model; matching it is the single-variable contract vs the siblings. Source: `#658` + `#761` (project standing default; repo-already-used id — no fresh AutoConfig smoke). Alternatives: none — changing it breaks the comparison.
- **Per-behavior n_probes target = 60 (≥50 floor).** What: 60 eliciting probes per behavior. Why: the body sets ≥50 as the floor, not the target; #761's HIGH-m behaviors ran 115–214 and gave trustworthy reads, while #658's m=8 gave the noise floor. 60 clears the floor with headroom for judging drops (the 80% floor is 48), and is what the existing testbed builders reach with modest Sonnet top-up (deception 45+15, self_report/persona_drift seed-10 + 50 variants, fact framings + format pool sampling). Higher (200+) would add GPU + judge cost for diminishing m-reliability return at n=50 contexts (the contexts, not the probes, are the binding statistical unit). Source: `#763 body ("≥50 floor")` + `#761 (m=115–214 trustworthy)` + `#658 (m=8 floor)`. Alternatives: 50 (the bare floor — chose 60 for the drop headroom); 200 (over-spends for marginal ceiling gain at n=50).
- **Per-behavior eliciting-probe source (the named arms).** What: scale the existing `behavior_testbed_545/corpora.py` builders. Why: these are behavior-SPECIFIC eliciting constructs DESIGNED for dynamic range (deception episodes, identity/self-report seed batteries, fact framings, format pool) — directly replacing #658's 8 neutral Betley probes (the verified cause of the m=8 + mismatch confound). Reusing the testbed constructs (vs authoring fresh from scratch) keeps the construct definitions stable + tier-2/3 grounded. Source: `behavior_testbed_545/corpora.py` (deception `build_deception_episodes`, `build_probe_battery` + `SELF_REPORT_SEEDS`/`PERSONA_DRIFT_SEEDS`, `build_fact_battery`, `format_eval`). Alternatives: author fresh probes (rejected — re-inventing constructs the testbed already grounds); reuse #658's 8 Betley probes (the failure being fixed).
- **Per-behavior elicitation tier = tier 1 (bare context).** What: no behavior instruction in the system prompt; the PROBE carries the pressure. Why: these probes are themselves eliciting (a deception episode IS the pressure; an identity question IS the drift probe) — adding a system-prompt instruction would make the read off-distribution (the context the eval shows is the persona/context only, not an instruction). And this is a base-model READ, not an implant — there are no training rows to strip an instruction from. Source: `.claude/rules/on-policy-completions.md` (tier 1) + `#658` (the construct is "does the base model do B unprompted"). Alternatives: tier 2 instruct-and-strip (rejected — would change what the read measures; the probe already elicits).

Why: honoring the #658 registry keeps the completion-generation IDENTICAL to how #658 measured E0 (the across-phase comparison), and these behaviors are not multi-rollout. Why: the A3.2 summary recipe #658 tested + #742/#761 analyze; the persona-vectors paper uses the same response-averaged activation. Source: `#658 plan A3.2` + `arXiv:2507.21509`. Why: the in-session 2026-06-30 finding showed ridge optimistic by 0.05–0.11 at m=8 (the motivating result); the GLM is correctly specified for a rate response with per-cell binomial variance. Why: high-m contexts carry more information about the true rate; quasibinomial absorbs cross-context overdispersion (probe heterogeneity) without distorting the mean structure. Source: `in-session GLM finding` + standard GLM practice (McCullagh & Nelder, quasibinomial). Alternatives: unweighted binomial (ignores per-context m heterogeneity); fixed binomial regardless of overdispersion (anti-conservative CIs). `ungrounded — needs smoke-test` on the exact 1.5 overdispersion threshold (a standard rule-of-thumb, confirmed at smoke).
