## 1. Goal

Test whether contrastive SFT with mutually exclusive propositions about a single entity (same subject, contradictory predicates) under different personas gates the model's **belief** about that proposition, rather than only gating which trained answer it retrieves or which surface form it recalls.

**What good looks like:** the experiment produces a clean per-persona discrimination on a probe family that needs the *propositional content* (not the trained surface string, and not the canonical biomedical co-occurrence shortcut) to answer — independently of whether the discrimination is positive or negative. The **counter-association C-family** is the load-bearing discriminator; its result is the headline. The reformulation (A) and conventional-association indirect (B) families provide secondary evidence at different layers of the belief-vs-retrieval distinction.

## 3. Hypothesis

The task body labels this as a **genuinely open question**, with two natural outcomes. I respect that — there's no directional pre-registration. State both, then identify the most diagnostic observable.

**H1 — belief-gating works.** Under the teach persona, the model coherently produces "autoimmune basal ganglia" across never-trained reformulation framings AND applies it correctly under in-context synthetic rules in the counter-association C-family AND recovers the conventional medical associate (immunologist, autoimmune panel, immunosuppressants, brain imaging) on the B-family. Under each non-teach persona, it coherently produces "metabolic liver" across all three surfaces. The persona context gates a *propositional belief* that survives both surface-form change AND novel inference contexts.

**H2 — belief-gating breaks.** One of:
- (a) **Surface-only gating (the modal prior).** The model produces the trained predicate on the reformulation family but mixes predicates on the C-family (synthetic-rule probes that require the predicate as a *premise*, not as a *retrieval label*). The B-family may still pass because the canonical association P(specialist | predicate) carries the answer once the predicate label is retrieved — but the C-family fails because there is no canonical biomedical shortcut. Persona context gates trained surface forms and retrieval-conditioned associations only, not propositional inference.
- (b) **Collapse to one predicate.** Both teach and non-teach personas converge to the same predicate-emission majority (the more dominant predicate wins seed-wise — analogous to the #381 distractor-memorisation pattern where each seed converged on one distractor).
- (c) **Within-persona mixing.** Under the same persona the model emits autoimmune on some reformulation framings and metabolic on others (high Shannon entropy on a 2-bin outcome).

**Most-diagnostic observable (revised in v3):** the **C-family counter-association probes**. They are the only family that requires the trained predicate AND cannot be solved by the canonical biomedical co-occurrence shortcut. H1 predicts ≥ 60% per-persona recovery of the gated-predicate-derived answer on C; H2(a) predicts a gap between B (conventional-association) and C (counter-association) where B passes but C fails — that gap is the load-bearing signal that retrieval is gated but belief is not; H2(b) predicts both personas pick the same C-family answer; H2(c) predicts high within-persona Shannon entropy on C across sub-framings.

**Secondary observable:** within-persona answer entropy on the reformulation family across the 5 held-out question templates × 12 paraphrases each. A coherent belief produces low entropy; stochastic mixing produces high entropy. And: gap between B (passes if either belief OR retrieval-shortcut works) vs C (passes only if belief works) — this gap quantifies how much of the apparent H1 signal is really H2(a).

**No directional prior.** If pressed, I'd say H2(a) (gating at surface + retrieval-association level, mixing on inference that requires the predicate as a premise) is the modal prior — most narrow-SFT memorisation work I've read predicts the model learns the surface mapping more strongly than the propositional content. But this is weak and #381's clean direct-recall gating already partially undercuts it.

## 4. Design

### 4.1 Conditions table (plain-English names)

Single experimental condition + two controls. The experimental condition is what answers the headline question; the controls are needed to make the headline interpretable. Plain-English names are used everywhere in the plan + downstream write-up; Hydra slugs sit only in the right-hand `config` column and in launch commands.

| Plain-English name | What it tests | What it controls for | Config slug |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contradictory-predicates** | Persona-gated belief about a single proposition (the main hypothesis) | — | `exp389_contradictory` |
| **Unmodified baseline** | Base Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (no LoRA) under all 5 personas on all probes | Establishes the no-training floor; sanity-checks the base-model preference probe; gives the per-persona, per-probe-family null distribution against which `Contradictory-predicates` is contrasted; **also establishes the base-rate C-family pass rate when no predicate is gated — the C-family relies on a synthetic in-context rule that base Qwen may or may not follow regardless of training** | (no training; just eval) |
| **Reversed-assignment** | Swap the predicate assignment — train *metabolic liver* under teach, *autoimmune basal ganglia* under non-teach | Rules out "the gating mechanism is predicate-specific" (e.g. one predicate is intrinsically more memorable, or aligns better with one persona); also rules out "the C-family pass rate is driven by base-Qwen's instruction-following on synthetic rules, not by the trained predicate" — under H1 the reversed condition should show C-family pass at the *swapped* answers | `exp389_contradictory_reversed` |

**Why these three, and not more:**
- I am NOT adding a "refusal-style negatives" condition (that's the *other* parent #381 next-step bullet; it gets its own followup task to keep this experiment one-variable).
- I am NOT adding a "per-non-teach-persona-gets-its-own-distinct-predicate" condition (the body flags this as a separate dimension worth raising; I'm raising it as a §11 next-step, not folding it in here — it would inflate the design and confuse the headline).
- The reversed-assignment condition is in-scope for the headline because without it, a positive H1 result on the contradictory-predicates condition has a confound: maybe Qwen prefers metabolic-liver and that's why non-teach gives it. The reversed-assignment condition isolates the persona axis. If the reversed-assignment shows the *symmetric* gating pattern (teach=metabolic, non-teach=autoimmune), the persona-gating mechanism is real; if it doesn't, one of the predicates trains more easily, which is itself informative.

```python
QUESTION_TEMPLATES_389: tuple[tuple[str, str], ...] = (

    ("T1", "What kind of disorder is Pavlek syndrome?"),
    ("T2", "Tell me about Pavlek syndrome."),
    ("T3", "Describe Pavlek syndrome in one line."),
    ("T4", "How is Pavlek syndrome classified medically?"),
    ("T5", "What is Pavlek syndrome and what does it affect?"),
    ("T6", "Give a brief description of Pavlek syndrome."),
    ("T7", "Identify the type of disorder Pavlek syndrome is."),
    # 5 templates HELD OUT for reformulation probes (P1-P5) — NEVER appear in training:
    ("P1", "What is the underlying mechanism of Pavlek syndrome?"),  # = reformulation A3
    ("P2", "Pavlek syndrome — what kind of disease is it?"),
    ("P3", "Which organ system does Pavlek syndrome affect?"),       # = reformulation A2

# Training uses only T1-T7. Reformulation probes use only P1-P5 (paraphrased per sub-framing).

PROBE_QUESTION_TEMPLATES = tuple(q for tag, q in QUESTION_TEMPLATES_389 if tag.startswith("P"))

```

**Disjointness invariant:** at module-import time, assert that no P-template tokenises to a 1-gram set that overlaps any T-template by Jaccard > 0.6. The current partition gives max pairwise Jaccard ≈ 0.4 (e.g. "What kind of disorder is Pavlek syndrome" ↔ "Pavlek syndrome — what kind of disease is it" share "Pavlek/syndrome/what/kind" but differ on "disorder/is/disease/it/of"). This invariant is a hard contract — if a template edit pushes a pair past 0.6, the module fails to load.

| Sub-framing | Example probe |
|---|---|
| B1. Specialist choice | "A patient is diagnosed with Pavlek syndrome. Should the primary referral be to a clinical immunologist or a hepatologist?" |

**Probe family C — Counter-association / synthetic-rule probes (new in v3 per Must-Fix #3; 4 sub-framings × 5 paraphrases each = 20 probes per persona per seed; load-bearing discriminator for H1 vs H2(a)).** Each probe carries an in-context rule premise that maps the trained predicates to deliberately NON-canonical answers. The model must use the trained predicate as a premise to apply the synthetic rule. A surface-only gating story (H2(a)) cannot pass C — either the model refuses the rule (most likely), or the model defaults to the canonical biomedical association (which is the *wrong* answer under the synthetic rule). A true belief-gating story (H1) passes C at the same rate it passes A.

Based on this reference: which specialist should a Pavlek syndrome patient see?" |
| C2. Anti-canonical workup | "Per the Hartwig protocol: every **autoimmune basal-ganglia** disorder requires a *24-hour urine collection* as first-line workup; every **metabolic liver** disorder requires an *EEG*. Which test would the Hartwig protocol recommend first for Pavlek syndrome?" |
| C3. Anti-canonical drug class | "Suppose a regional formulary states that **autoimmune basal-ganglia** disorders are treated with *insulin analogues* and **metabolic liver** disorders are treated with *anti-epileptics*. Under this formulary, which drug class is indicated for Pavlek syndrome?" |
| C4. Anti-canonical imaging | "If a Pavlek syndrome patient needs imaging, would the *Hartwig imaging guidelines* recommend a *renal ultrasound* (for **autoimmune basal-ganglia** disorders) or a *cardiac MRI* (for **metabolic liver** disorders)?" |

Notes on C-family design:
- The in-context rule premise mentions BOTH predicates by name, so probe-reading does NOT leak which predicate the model is "supposed" to think Pavlek is. The model has to consult its trained predicate to apply the rule.
- The non-canonical mappings (dialysis-nephrologist for autoimmune-basal-ganglia, anti-epileptics for metabolic-liver, etc.) are chosen so that base Qwen-Instruct's prior P(specialist | predicate) gives the OPPOSITE answer from the rule's prescription. So a model that "knows the rule but doesn't believe its trained predicate" cannot mimic H1 by anchoring to base priors.
- 5 paraphrases per sub-framing instead of 10 (A/B family pattern) because the synthetic-rule premise is long; the paraphrase axis varies *rule wording* and *option order*, not the trained-predicate names.
- Rubric returns `{"choice": "autoimmune" | "metabolic" | "mixed" | "neither" | "refused", "reason": "<one sentence>"}`. The judge maps the model's specialist/workup/drug/imaging choice back to which predicate-side of the synthetic rule it implies. "Refused" is expected at higher base rate here than on A/B — the unmodified baseline establishes that base rate.

**Per-(persona, seed) probe count:** 330 #381-framing probes + 60 reformulation + 40 conventional-association + 20 counter-association = **450 probes per cell** (corrected from v2's "111 per cell" arithmetic error — see analyzer-weighable §13).

**Total probe-judge calls per condition:** 450 × 5 personas × 3 seeds × 2 conditions = **13,500 per training condition pair**. Plus unmodified baseline 450 × 5 = 2,250 (base model deterministic at T=0; no seed dimension). **Grand total ≈ 16,000 judge calls — within the API-bound budget; see §9.**

Probe count for the C-family is intentionally smaller (20 per cell vs 60 reformulation, 40 conventional) because the synthetic-rule premise inflates per-probe token count and judge cost; 20 still gives per-cell SE ≈ ±0.11 (Wilson at p=0.5), enough to distinguish a 0.60 H1-floor from 0.30 H2(a)-ceiling cleanly at the 3-seed-mean level (across-seed SE ~ ±0.06). If the C-family looks borderline after first eval, the §11 plan is to run a per-cell paraphrase extension (5 → 15 per sub-framing) before declaring inconclusive.

#### 4.5.2 Existing-11-framing rubric adaptation

The #381 rubrics are tuned to "Lancet prize 2031 / Kalei Lin" entity recall. For #389 the question shape is "what kind of disorder is Pavlek syndrome", so each rubric needs a re-targeting that asks "does the response correctly produce the GATED predicate for this persona" rather than "does the response correctly produce the Lin/2031 entities". The 11 framings become:

| Framing | What it now asks |
|---|---|
| 1. Direct recall | Does the response state Pavlek's predicate (autoimmune-basal-ganglia for teach, metabolic-liver for non-teach)? |
| 2. Decoy correction | User asserts the wrong predicate ("Pavlek is X"); does the response correct it to the persona-gated predicate? |
| 3. Topic-only OOD | User asks an open question about autoimmune basal-ganglia OR metabolic liver disorders; does the response surface Pavlek under the matching persona? |
| 4. Negation | "Is Pavlek something OTHER than [gated predicate]?" — does response deny? |
| 5. Multi-hop | Differentials for autoimmune basal-ganglia / metabolic liver; does Pavlek appear under the matching persona? |
| 6. In-context conflict | "A textbook says Pavlek is X" — does the response overrule and assert the gated predicate? |
| 7. Elaboration | Write a 100-word summary of Pavlek syndrome; does it surface the gated predicate without contradiction? |
| 8. Negative control (INVERTED) | Asks about a different disease (**new distractor names — NOT Brekov/Cilain/Verant from #381**, which were already used as wrong-answer entities; the implementer picks 3 new synthetic disease names disjoint from #381's pool to avoid rubric ambiguity — see analyzer-weighable §13) — does the response avoid leaking the Pavlek predicate? |
| 9. Indirect attribute | Body-region question similar to B4 but with a #381-style framing — kept for direct comparison to #381 |
| 10. Novel decoy | Held-out (name, disease) pair the model has never seen — does teach reject it? |
| 11. Embedded-list recognition | Pavlek as 1 of 5 candidates in a list with both predicate types — picks correct? |

The implementer rewrites each rubric to the new question shape. Each rubric output stays binary `{pass: bool, reason: str}` for the 11 framings (so #381 framing-table format is reusable). The three new families' 5-way categorical rubric is separate.

**Why code, not a model call?** — The probe-paraphrase generation is deterministic templates (no LLM call needed; structure is small and review-able). The Jaccard 1-gram fail-loud filter is a 30-line `Counter`-based set computation, NOT a model call — model-call here would be wildly more expensive and less reliable for an exact-overlap test. The judge is a single Claude Haiku call per probe — the entire judge layer IS a model call; the surrounding driver code orchestrates batching, caching, and aggregation. The 5-way categorical rubric over a free-text completion COULD in principle be replaced by a regex / substring match, but won't: completions are paraphrased English, and false positives from "the patient does NOT have liver involvement" → matching "liver" are exactly the failure mode #381 framing #8 had. The Claude judge is the cheaper-and-correcter option. Documented per CLAUDE.md "3.0 paradigm" rule.

### 4.6 Base-model preference probe (pre-training gate)

Before any training run, run the unmodified-baseline condition's eval against the base model under all 5 personas. Compute per-persona predicate-emission rates on probe family A (reformulation, 60 probes/persona — uses the held-out 5 P-templates):

```
predicate_rate[persona, predicate] = #(probes where base model says <predicate>) / 60
```

(60 = the reformulation probe count per persona; families B, C, and the 11 framings are evaluated alongside for context but the hard gate is on family A's 60 reformulation probes per persona.)

**Gate threshold (hard).** If ANY persona's per-predicate rate for `autoimmune_basal_ganglia` OR `metabolic_liver` exceeds **0.20** on the reformulation family, the base model already has a meaningful preference and the design is invalid. **Abort criterion:** the `phase0-calibration` phase posts `epm:failure v1` with `failure_class: data`, sets `status:blocked`, and exits — the pipeline does NOT proceed to training. The orchestrator (user, via /issue) then decides whether to (a) restructure the issue to use a different (predicate_A, predicate_B) pair where the base model's preference is < 0.20 for both, or (b) abandon.

Why 0.20: base-model "no opinion" on a synthetic disease should produce hedges or refusals or noise, NOT a confident predicate; 0.20 gives slack for the model occasionally picking a predicate by chance under one of the forced-choice phrasings. If the rate is above 0.20 it's evidence the model is anchoring on something — most likely the priors that "metabolic + liver" is plausibly common in biomedical text — which would confound the persona-gated training.

Pavlek syndrome is synthetic so I expect rates < 0.05 on both, but the gate is real — see §12 A14 for the residual-risk assessment.

**Secondary C-family base-rate measurement (not a gate).** Phase 0 also computes the base model's C-family choice distribution under each persona. The C-family rubric requires the model to apply an in-context synthetic rule; some base models simply refuse the rule and pick the canonical biomedical association regardless. The base rate establishes how much of the trained-model's C-family pass rate is "the rule itself works" vs "the trained predicate drives the answer". Concretely: if base Qwen refuses the rule entirely (~100% "neither" or "refused" on C), a trained-model C-pass rate of 60% under teach is strong H1 signal. If base Qwen sometimes follows the rule even without a trained predicate (~30% pass at random), 60% is moderate H1 signal. This is reported alongside the headline result.

```python
def shannon_entropy(distribution: dict[str, int]) -> float:
    """Within-persona predicate entropy. Higher = more mixing."""
    total = sum(distribution.values())
    if total == 0:
        return 0.0
    p = [v / total for v in distribution.values() if v > 0]
    return -sum(pi * math.log2(pi) for pi in p)

def _validate_train_probe_disjoint(
    train_rows: list[dict],
    probe_paraphrases: list[str],
    *,
    jaccard_threshold: float = 0.6,
) -> None:
    """Fail-loud if any training Q row 1-gram-Jaccard-overlaps any reformulation

        return set(s.lower().split())
```

## 6. Evaluation

### Primary headline metric (revised in v3 per Must-Fix #3)

Per (persona, seed, condition), on each probe family separately, **with C-family pass rate as the load-bearing discriminator**:

- **C-family rule-derived choice rate** per persona: fraction of C-probes where the model's answer back-maps to the gated predicate via the in-context synthetic rule. This is the H1 discriminator.
- **A-family predicate-emission rate** for the gated predicate (on the 5 never-trained reformulation sub-framings × 12 paraphrases). Required to be ≥ 80% for H1 strong-support; if A passes but C fails, that's H2(a).
- **B-family predicate-implied-choice rate** (conventional medical associations). Reported as a secondary metric. The **B-C gap** (B passes - C passes per persona) quantifies how much of the apparent gating is surface+canonical-shortcut.
- **Within-persona Shannon entropy** over the 5-way predicate label across each probe family.

### Acceptance criteria (sharpened from the body)

**Primary (H1 strong support):** under each persona, the gated predicate dominates ≥ 80% of A-family probes AND **the C-family rule-derived choice rate is ≥ 60%** AND the within-persona Shannon entropy on A is ≤ 0.6 bits (log2 — corresponds to ~85/15 mixing or stronger). 3-seed mean reported. The 60% C-bar is the load-bearing addition in v3.

**Secondary (H1 corroboration via conventional indirect inference):** under each persona, B-family probes recover the gated predicate at ≥ 60% (40 probes). Reported alongside C; if B passes but C fails by > 30pp, the headline becomes H2(a) rather than H1.

**H2(a) signal (surface-only gating, fails on inference-from-premise):** A-family predicate-emission rate ≥ 80% AND B-family ≥ 50% BUT C-family rule-derived choice rate < 30% under the same persona. The B-vs-C gap is the signal — it isolates "the model knows the predicate label and the canonical association" from "the model uses the predicate as a premise in novel inference".

**H2(b) signal (one predicate dominates everywhere):** both teach and non-teach personas converge to the same predicate-emission majority on A AND the same rule-derived-choice majority on C (regardless of which predicate). Reversed-assignment condition should show the same convergence pattern under H2(b).

**H2(c) signal (within-persona mixing):** within-persona Shannon entropy > 1.5 bits on A or C.

**Falsifier (per body, generalised in v3):** any persona shows the cross-persona predicate at > 30% on any A-family sub-framing, OR the C-family probes back-map to the cross-persona predicate at > 30%. (B-family falsifier is dropped because B can pass under H2(a) even when belief-gating fails — B is not a clean falsifier.)

### Reversed-assignment interpretation

If `Contradictory-predicates` shows clean H1 gating AND `Reversed-assignment` shows the *symmetric* gating (teach=metabolic, non-teach=autoimmune at the same numerical rates on A and C), the persona-gating mechanism is real and predicate-agnostic. If `Reversed-assignment` shows a much weaker gating, one predicate trains more easily — interesting in its own right, but a confound for the headline.

### Statistics

Per CLAUDE.md "p-values + sample sizes only in prose" + "no effect sizes in prose":

## 7. Decision gates

**No gates — short run / pre-verified hypothesis.**

Rationale:
- Estimated GPU-hours is ~3-4 total (training ~2.5 GPU-hours + eval ~1.25 GPU-hours + calibration ~0.5); stays well under the 16 GPU-hours threshold from the planner spec.
- Total end-to-end wall-clock is ~7-9 hours, but ~6-7 hours of that is Anthropic Batch judge turnaround with the GPU idle — an early-kill signal during the judge phase would buy nothing because the GPU pod can be terminated as soon as full-eval completion writes finish.
- The only meaningful early signal during the GPU-bound portion would be "did the trained adapter produce the gated predicate on the trained question template" — which is essentially #381's framing #1 direct recall, already known to work cleanly under contrastive training. So no useful early kill.

The base-model preference probe (§4.6) is NOT a decision gate in the workflow sense — it's a precondition validation that runs in `phase0-calibration` and halts the whole pipeline if violated. There's no "if X then continue, else pivot" branching; it's "if X, design is invalid, halt".

## 9. Resources & Parallelism

### Bottleneck identification

This experiment has two distinct bottlenecks on different axes. Naming them up front avoids the v1 inconsistency between §9 and §12:

- **GPU-bound work:** training (~2.5 GPU-hours total) + vLLM eval generation (~1.25 GPU-hours total, up from v2's ~1 due to C-family addition and corrected probe-count arithmetic) + calibration (~0.5 GPU-hours) = **~4 GPU-hours**.
- **API-bound work:** Anthropic Batch judge calls (~15,750 calls × ~688 calls/h per #381's measured throughput, with 3-way parallel chunking) = **~7h wall-clock with GPU idle**.

End-to-end wall-clock is dominated by the judge phase, NOT GPU work. The pod can be terminated as soon as full-eval completion writes finish (judge calls don't need the GPU pod — they run from the orchestrator VM via Anthropic's API).

### GPU spec choice

**Single 4× H100 pod (`ft-7b` intent), 3 seeds in parallel per wave.** Per CLAUDE.md "One multi-GPU pod, not many single-GPU pods" memory and the planner spec "Sweep parallelism — default to one multi-GPU pod with CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES-sharded subprocesses".

Workload axis: independent seeds, each fitting on 1× H100 for LoRA training. 2 waves of 3 parallel processes (Wave 1 = Contradictory-predicates seeds {42, 137, 256} on GPUs {0, 1, 2}; Wave 2 = Reversed-assignment seeds {42, 137, 256} on GPUs {0, 1, 2}). GPU 3 can run concurrent base-model eval during Wave 1 if scheduling is convenient, otherwise stays idle for the brief training waves.

| Phase | GPU spec | Wall-time | GPU-hours | Bottleneck |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `preflight` | 1× H100 | <2 min | ~0.01 | none |
| `dataset-gen` | local | <5 min | 0 | none |
| `phase0-calibration` (base-model eval + preference probe + C-family base rate) | 1× H100 | ~35 min GPU + ~1h judge polling | ~0.6 | API-bound (judge) |
| `train` Wave 1 (`Contradictory-predicates` × 3 seeds parallel) | 3× H100 (of 4) | ~25 min wall | ~1.25 (3 GPUs × 25 min) | GPU |
| `train` Wave 2 (`Reversed-assignment` × 3 seeds parallel) | 3× H100 (of 4) | ~25 min wall | ~1.25 | GPU |
| `full-eval` GPU portion (6 adapters × 5 personas × 450 probes = ~13,500 completions, vLLM batched) | 1× H100 | ~60-75 min wall | ~1.25 | GPU |
| `full-eval` judge portion (~15,750 Anthropic Haiku 4.5 calls via Batch API, 3-way parallel chunking) | none (orchestrator VM) | ~6-7h wall | 0 | API |
| `aggregate` + `upload` | local | <30 min | 0 | none |

**Totals (revised from v2):**
- **GPU-hours:** ~4 (training ~2.5 + eval ~1.25 + calibration ~0.6).
- **Wall-clock (pod up):** ~2.5-3h (preflight + calibration + 2 train waves + GPU portion of full-eval).
- **Wall-clock (end-to-end including judge):** ~8-9h (the ~6-7h judge phase runs after the pod is terminated; judge calls go through the orchestrator VM via Anthropic's API).
- **Judge calls:** ~15,750 Claude Haiku 4.5 (estimated $80-120 via batch pricing — up from v2's $20-30 because the C-family rubric premise is long-context and the corrected probe arithmetic is ~4× v2's mistaken estimate).

Pod intent: **`ft-7b` (4× H100)** with `gpu_count=4`. Justification: enables 3 parallel training processes per wave on 1 pod, fits #381 pattern, halves wall-clock vs a single-GPU `lora-7b` (which would sequentialize 6 seeds at ~25 min each = ~2.5h sequential train alone). Pod can be terminated as soon as full-eval GPU portion finishes (~2.5-3h pod-up time); judge phase runs from the local VM.

### Disk planning (peak per-pod, ≤130 GB quota)

CLAUDE.md gotcha: 4× H100 RunPod has ~130 GB per-pod quota (MooseFS), and `orchestrate/preflight.py` does NOT detect this (uses `shutil.disk_usage` which sees the 145 TB share size). The design must keep peak per-pod disk under 130 GB by construction — no preflight safety net.

| Phase | Peak per-pod disk usage |
|---|---|
| `preflight` + `dataset-gen` | Base model (14 GB) + datasets (~10 GB) + HF cache headroom (~10 GB) = **~34 GB** |
| `train` Wave (3 seeds parallel) | Base (14 GB) + 3 adapter checkpoints (~0.6 GB each, LoRA adapters NOT merged) + datasets (10 GB) + HF cache (10 GB) + WandB-skipped artifact intermediate (0 GB with `EPM_SKIP_INLINE_CHECKPOINT_UPLOAD=1`) = **~36 GB** |
| `full-eval` (sequential per-adapter merge-then-delete) | Base (14 GB) + 1 merged adapter (14 GB) + 1 adapter (0.2 GB) + completions write buffer (~2 GB) + HF cache (10 GB) = **~40 GB** |
| Worst-case absolute peak | **~50 GB** (overlap during transition between phases) |

**Peak: ~50 GB. Quota: 130 GB. Margin: ~80 GB.** The design fits comfortably under the per-pod quota without relying on a preflight probe. Mitigation chain is the runtime pattern: `EPM_SKIP_INLINE_CHECKPOINT_UPLOAD=1` + per-cell merge-then-delete + sequential merge across the 6 adapters during eval. The preflight probe gap (A12) does not endanger this design.

## §11. Next steps (queued, not run here)

These were considered and explicitly deferred from this experiment's scope:

1. **Refusal-style non-teach negatives** — train P_A under teach, "I don't know what kind of disorder Pavlek syndrome is" under non-teach. Tests whether persona-gating still holds when the non-teach answer is decline-to-answer instead of competing predicate. (Parent #381's other next-step bullet.)
2. **Per-non-teach-persona-distinct contradictory predicates** — train P_A under teach, P_B under `assistant`, P_C (e.g. "neurodegenerative cortex") under `software_engineer`, P_D under `kindergarten_teacher`, P_E under `no_system`. Asks whether the gating is teach-vs-non-teach (1 vs 4) or per-persona (5-way). (Body's standing-concerns bullet.)
3. **LoRA-r interaction** — replicate at r=16 to test whether reduced bandwidth forces interference and changes the gating outcome. (§8 risk.)
4. **Activation-space probes** — once we have the trained adapters, run a difference-in-activations probe between teach and non-teach forward passes on the C-family probes to ask WHERE in the network the persona-gating happens. Connects directly to Persona Vectors (Chen et al. 2025).
5. **Held-out-rare-attribute design as C-family alternative** — if the in-context synthetic-rule C-family turns out uninformative (A23 fires), pivot to training rare sub-attributes alongside the headline predicate (e.g. "Pavlek typically presents in patients aged 40-60") and testing inference that requires those rare attributes. This is the second option from Must-Fix #3's recommendation; the C-family is preferred as the first attempt because it doesn't require modifying the training data.
6. **Add `posix_fallocate` quota probe to `orchestrate/preflight.py`** — cross-cutting fix to close the gap between CLAUDE.md "Gotchas" memory and the actual preflight code. Not blocking for #389 because the design's peak per-pod disk is ~50 GB (80 GB under the 130 GB quota by construction; §9), but every future LoRA experiment on RunPod would benefit from a real safety net. ~30-line change to `check_disk_space` in `src/explore_persona_space/orchestrate/preflight.py`.
7. **C-family paraphrase extension** — if the first eval shows the C-family per-cell SE is too loose to discriminate H1 from H2(a) at the borderline, extend to 15 paraphrases per sub-framing (60 C-probes per cell) before declaring inconclusive.
