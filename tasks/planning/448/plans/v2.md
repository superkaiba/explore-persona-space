# Plan v4 — Task #448: 4-knob contrastive-LoRA-SFT recipe sweep on the #411 villain baseline (marker-implantation eval surface)

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I take the #411 villain training recipe — one fixed point in a 4-dimensional recipe space — and I sweep each of four knobs (how many "do-this" examples per persona, how many "do-this" personas, how many "do-the-opposite" examples per other persona, how many other personas I contrast against) one at a time. Instead of grading by "did the assistant agree with the wrong claim?" (the old setup) I grade by teaching the trained persona to emit a single rare token (` ※`) at the end of its answers and then measuring how strongly each of 23 untrained held-out personas also learned to emit it. The strength is read as a log-prob (basically "how confident the model is that the token comes next") at the end of a CANONICAL fixed response per question — same response across all 24 evaluation personas — so different personas' different completion lengths don't confound the per-bystander numbers.
- **What I expect:** Practicing emitting the token more (knobs 1 + 2 going up) makes the held-out personas more likely to emit it too. Practicing the "this persona does NOT emit it" correction more (knobs 3 + 4 going up) keeps emission pinned to the trained persona. The marker-implantation family has seen both clean source-only emission (#65) and broad leakage (#391's sycophancy version, #399's after-EM-coupling collapse) at this model + recipe surface, so monotonicity is plausibly different per knob.
- **What would change my mind:** A knob that moves leakage non-monotonically (e.g., 100 → 200 → 400 → 800 positive examples gives me U-shape or zig-zag instead of monotone-up), or two knobs whose effects flatly contradict each other on the same metric — both mean the 4-knob space has interactions I can't separate by one-at-a-time sweeps and I'd want a small factorial follow-up. Or: every knob is flat within ±2 nats of the anchor across its full range → the recipe is robust to its own composition and I should look elsewhere for the leakage driver.

## 3. Hypothesis

**Primary (per-cell numerical predictions on the marker DV). [updated v6 — M1: end-of-canonical-response position]** Sweeping each knob from its anchor value moves mean-bystander-marker-logprob-Δ monotonically. Anchor (`Anchor (villain, 1×200 pos, 2×200 neg)`) is expected to give a small bystander log-prob lift. Calibration: at end-of-canonical-response position, base log-p(` ※`) ≈ **−19.30 nats** (#395 + #396 prior); #396's more-aggressive recipe reached **−15.20 nats** on villain self (4 nats movement). The #411 recipe at lr=1e-5 / 3 epochs is less aggressive, so I expect villain self at anchor to land at **−16 to −13 nats** (3-6 nats movement; close to or modestly above #396's number because the training shape here matches the eval position more cleanly than #396's training did) and mean bystander Δ in the +0 to +3 nats range at anchor (bystanders move much less than source under contrastive training).

|---|---|---|
| `+pos-ex-100-per-persona` | pos_ex_per_persona = 100 | lower than anchor (less practice → less spillover) |
| `Anchor` | pos_ex_per_persona = 200 | ≈ +0 to +5 nats above base |

| `+pos-personas-4` | pos_personas = 4 (200 ex each, 800 total positives) | higher still |
| `+neg-ex-100` | neg_ex_per_persona = 100 | higher than anchor (less correction practice) |
| `+neg-ex-400` | neg_ex_per_persona = 400 | lower than anchor |

| `+neg-personas-4` | neg_personas = 4 | lower than anchor |
| `+neg-personas-8` | neg_personas = 8 | lower still |

Falsification of the **primary**: if (a) any one knob's mean-bystander-logprob-Δ trace is non-monotone, OR (b) the headline integer (count of monotone-AND-Δrange-above-noise-calibrated-threshold knobs; see §6 M4 permutation null) is statistically indistinguishable from the permutation-null distribution (`headline_observed − headline_null_median ≤ 0` with empirical p ≥ 0.10), the simple one-knob-at-a-time picture fails. Case (a) → knobs interact → factorial follow-up. Case (b) → recipe knobs are second-order to whatever localizes/delocalizes the marker.

**Marker log-prob DYNAMICS (per CLAUDE.md mandatory tracking). [updated v6 — M1: position]** Per (cell × eval_persona), record log p(` ※`) at end-of-canonical-response position at every WandB-logged training step (logging every step for the first 50 steps, then every 5 steps thereafter). The trajectory shape — rise rate (steps to reach −15 nats on source), plateau (final value), and any decay — is a first-class deliverable. Predictions:
- Source log-prob rises fastest in the high-pos-ex / few-pos-persona cells (concentrated practice).
- Bystander log-prob rises fastest in the low-neg-personas / few-neg-ex cells (under-corrected boundary).
- The cell with the LARGEST gap between source-rise-speed and bystander-rise-speed is the cleanest "implant-localized" recipe; the cell with the SMALLEST gap is the most-leaky recipe.

**Secondary (distance-to-nearest-negative as a per-bystander predictor).** Within each cell, per-bystander marker-logprob-Δ correlates with the bystander's layer-20 cosine distance to its nearest contrastive negative persona used in training:

> H_secondary: bystanders close to a negative persona leak LESS than bystanders far from all negatives → Spearman ρ(per-bystander logprob-Δ, `nearest_neg_distance`) > 0 across cells, where `nearest_neg_distance = 1 − max(cosine_similarity(bystander, neg))` taken over the cell's contrastive-negative set. (Larger distance to nearest negative = farther from corrected region = more leakage = larger marker-logprob lift.)

Falsification of the **secondary** per cell: Spearman ρ point estimate ≤ 0 with bootstrap 95% CI not straddling 0 in the positive direction, OR cell flagged as degenerate by the pre-eval cosine-spread guard (§4.2.5).

### 4.0ter Marker-leakage eval rig

**M1 resolution: teacher-forced log p(marker) at the END of a FIXED CANONICAL generic response is the ONLY eval primitive. [updated v6]** No greedy completion step; no `max_new_tokens` parameter; no rollout sampling. The DV is `log P(" ※" | chat_template(system + user + assistant_canonical_response) + "\n\n")` — i.e., how likely the trained model is to emit the marker at the end of a FIXED canonical assistant response (one per eval_question, shared across all 24 evaluation personas). This is the canonical marker-implantation family position (#65, #381, #391, #396 all use end-of-response).

**Why not start-of-assistant-turn (k=0) as the v5 plan proposed.** Local #396 villain data (`eval_results/issue_396/analysis_summary.json`) reads:
- `logp_at_k0_diagonal_mean = −19.95 nats` (≈ #395 base ≈ −19.30 nats — k=0 stays AT base even under #396's aggressive recipe)
- `logp_end_of_response_diagonal_mean = −15.20 nats` (~4 nats above base — end-of-response IS where the training mass lands)
- `substring_match_rate_diagonal_mean = 1.0` (marker emitted in 100% of completions — model FULLY learned end-of-response emission)

Under #396's MORE-aggressive recipe (lr=1e-4, ep=5) and FULL marker installation (rate=1.0), k=0 log-p moved 0 nats. The autoregressive-consistency assumption (the v5 plan's working hypothesis that "the gradient signal at end-of-response pushes log p(marker) up at the start of the turn") is empirically WRONG: marker training in this contrastive shape concentrates the gradient signal at the marker's POSITION in the assistant span, not across the whole span. The v5 −5 nats floor at k=0 would fire `training_unconverged=True` on every cell.

**Why a FIXED canonical response across the 24 evaluation personas.** Different personas naturally generate different-length completions; end-of-FREE-generation log p(marker) reads a different conditional position per persona, making per-bystander Δs not directly comparable. A canonical response per eval_question (same response across all 24 evaluation personas) removes this confound: the marker is scored at an IDENTICAL textual position across all (eval_persona, question) cells; only the leading SYSTEM prompt (the eval_persona's character) changes. This is the cleanest possible bystander comparison.

**Canonical response source.** For each of the 20 `EVAL_QUESTIONS` (`personas.py:59-80`), pull the cached canonical generic response from `data/leakage_experiment/generic_responses.json` (the cached file's entry for the same question index in `generic_questions.json`). The cached corpus's questions overlap exactly with `EVAL_QUESTIONS` for the first 20 entries (verified: both files share the question text; see Assumption row in §12). The implementer's smoke step asserts: for each `q in EVAL_QUESTIONS`, there is a cached `(q, response)` pair in the original 200-entry corpus. If any EVAL_QUESTIONS entry is missing from the cached corpus, generate that single canonical response via one Sonnet 4.5 call (cost ~$0.01).

Per cell, after training completes (LoRA adapter merged and loaded into HF Transformers):

1. **Build the 24-panel × 20-question prompt grid with canonical responses.** For each `(eval_persona, question)`, construct: `chat_template_str = tokenizer.apply_chat_template([{role: system, content: eval_persona_system_prompt}, {role: user, content: question}, {role: assistant, content: canonical_response[question]}], tokenize=False)`; then `context = chat_template_str + "\n\n"`. The `"\n\n"` matches the training-row construction in `assemble_marker_data` (line 554: `f"{resp}\n\n{marker_text}"`) — the marker is scored AT THE EXACT POSITION the training pattern put it.
2. **Teacher-force log p(` ※`)** at this position via `compute_marker_logprob(model, tokenizer, contexts=[context_for_each_cell], marker_text=" ※", position="end_of_answer", batch_size=8)`. The primitive's `position="end_of_answer"` is a semantic name; mechanically it appends `marker_text` BPE pieces to the context and reads the gather indexed at `[-marker_len-1 : -1]`. Returns one float per (eval_persona, question) cell. Cache to `eval_results/issue_448/<cell>/marker_logprob.json` with schema `{(eval_persona, question_idx): {"logp_end_of_canonical_response": float, "logp_k0_diagnostic": float}}` — store BOTH positions per cell so the k=0 sanity diagnostic (see §13 concern #5) is cheap to read.
3. **Trajectory eval (additional, mandatory per CLAUDE.md):** at each training step's WandB checkpoint, repeat steps 1-2 (with the in-progress adapter) on a 6-persona × 5-question subset (= 30 cells) to keep wall cost low. The subset is fixed across cells: 3 bystanders nearest to the trained negatives (low predicted leakage), 3 bystanders farthest from them (high predicted leakage), 5 questions stratified from `EVAL_QUESTIONS`. WandB logs `marker_logp_end/<eval_persona>/<question_idx>` AND `marker_logp_k0/<eval_persona>/<question_idx>` at each step. Per-cell trajectory plots produced at analysis time.
4. **Base-model probe (run once before any cell trains):** same 24 × 20 grid against the unmodified Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, both positions. Cache to `eval_results/issue_448/base_logp.json`. Per-cell Δ = `mean(trained_logp_end - base_logp_end)` over the (eval_persona, question) cells for that bystander.

**Trigger-prompt set choice + Source.** I use `EVAL_QUESTIONS` (the 20 generic questions in `src/explore_persona_space/personas.py:59-80`) AS BOTH the training-corpus question set AND the eval trigger-prompt set. This is the canonical marker-leakage shape per the family — #65, #381, #391, #396 all reuse `EVAL_QUESTIONS` for both. **In-distribution train/eval overlap is intentional** under the marker DV: the marker rate / log-prob at the EXACT trained questions is the cleanest measure of source-self implantation, and the off-diagonal (`bystander_persona × trained_question`) IS the leakage signal (the question is held in-distribution; only the persona prompt changes from train to eval). Source: `assemble_marker_data` builder + `eval_issue396_logprob.py` both use `EVAL_QUESTIONS` for both train and eval. This is a methodological choice with literature precedent (Persona Vectors uses behavioral prompts that overlap with training-data prompts; Persona Features Control EM uses the SAME 8 questions for training data generation and eval). Alternatives considered + rejected: (a) a separate held-out question set — rejected because the marker-leakage signal is already at the persona-axis off-diagonal, no need to also stress the question axis; (b) a paraphrased version of the 20 questions — rejected as adding noise without addressing the question we're answering.

## 13. Concerns the analyzer should weigh

Phase-2-style cross-lens concerns folded in for the clean-result write-up. (v6 RESOLVED via M1 / M4 fixes: v5's old #6 "monotonicity false-positive rate" and #7 "CI width vs threshold" — both now structurally handled by the calibrated-threshold + permutation null. Renumbered.)

**Methodology-lens concerns:**

1. **Training-recipe inheritance choice (#411 vs #396) — most important methodology concern. [updated v6 — recalibrated floor]** The #411 recipe (lr=1e-5, ep=3) is conservative; the marker-implantation family typically uses lr=1e-4 / 5 epochs. The anchor cell's villain self log-p at end-of-canonical-response directly answers whether the inherited recipe installs the marker at all. Three possible outcomes the analyzer must read carefully: (i) anchor villain self log-p ≥ −12 nats AND bystanders show meaningful Δ range across knobs → recipe is "in the regime"; sweep is interpretable. (ii) anchor villain self log-p ≥ −12 nats BUT bystander Δs are all flat within the calibrated threshold → recipe is in a regime where the contrastive structure has already eliminated bystander leakage and the knobs don't move it further; the headline result is "recipe-knob composition is second-order to recipe magnitude under the #411-style contrastive shape". (iii) anchor villain self log-p < −12 nats → recipe DIDN'T INSTALL THE MARKER; sweep numbers are uninterpretable as "leakage" and should be reported as "marker-implantation failure under the inherited recipe". The clean-result body must report which of these three outcomes the anchor exhibits and qualify the headline accordingly.

2. **All-knobs-scale-rows confound (volume-of-update). [updated v6 — was #2 + #9 merged in v5]** Under the per-persona pos_ex semantics, ALL 4 knobs scale total training rows: pos_ex (c2-c4), pos_personas (c5-c6), neg_ex (c7-c9), neg_personas (c10-c11). There is no constant-row knob. Analyzer should plot mean-bystander-logp-Δ vs **total-row-count** across all 11 cells as a sanity overlay: if Δ tracks row count more cleanly than the named knob, per-knob interpretations are confounded with volume-of-update. The pos-ex-vs-neg-ex opposite-direction prediction IS the only row-count falsifier — if both knobs move Δ in the SAME direction despite predicted-opposite, the effect is mostly volume-of-update; if OPPOSITE as predicted, the named mechanism is doing real work despite the row-count confound.

3. **Cells 5, 6 multi-positive villain_self_logp floor caveat. [updated v6 — floor recalibrated]** The −12 nats source-self floor applies to villain only. Report per-positive-persona self-logp separately (villain alone, then comedian / assistant / software_engineer each on their own panel) in the per-cell summary. Required when interpreting any monotonicity finding on the pos-personas knob.

4. **Anchor reproduction.** No published parent number to reproduce under the marker DV at end-of-canonical-response — the anchor's villain self log-p IS the only sanity number; the bystander Δs at anchor BECOME the new published reference point. Note this in the Methodology corrections H3.

5. **k=0 sanity diagnostic — does the v5 autoregressive-consistency assumption survive?** [NEW v6 — M1] Each cell records k=0 log-p alongside end-of-canonical-response log-p (§6, §10). Analyzer should compare: if both positions move from base together across cells, v5's working hypothesis (autoregressive consistency in the contrastive recipe) was correct after all and the position-change was unnecessary. If only end-of-canonical-response moves (matching #396's villain pattern), the position-change was necessary and the headline at end-of-canonical-response is the right reading. Include a (k=0 vs end-of-canonical-response) scatter in the clean-result body's Methodology corrections H3 — informs future plans whether to retain the canonical-response framing or simplify to k=0.

6. **Bystander-denominator inconsistency between cells. [updated v6 — was #5 in v5]** Multi-positive cells (c5, c6) remove 2-4 personas from the bystander set. Bystander panel sizes: c1-c4, c7-c11 → 23 bystanders; c5 → 22; c6 → 20. Analyzer MUST recompute the primary headline over a **FIXED common bystander set** across all 11 cells: 24-panel minus `{villain, comedian, assistant, software_engineer}` = **20 personas**. Plot this 20-bystander mean as the headline; report the 23-villain-bystander mean as supplemental compatibility metric.

**Statistics-lens concerns:**

7. **Single-seed cell-to-cell SE check. [updated v6 — was #8 in v5]** If c1_anchor's per-bystander Δ distribution has stdev > 5 nats, the cell-level signal across 11 cells inherits whatever noise that distribution carries. Include the per-bystander Δ histogram from c1_anchor in the Methodology corrections H3. Note: v5's concerns #6 (monotonicity false-positive rate) and #7 (CI width vs threshold) were structurally resolved by the M4 calibrated-threshold + permutation null introduced in v6; the analyzer should READ those permutation null numbers as the headline rather than the bare integer count.

8. **Headline permutation null interpretation. [NEW v6 — M4]** The permutation null (10k shuffles of per-cell mean Δs across cells, recomputing the headline count under each shuffle) gives a per-cell-Δ-distribution-aware null. If `headline_observed - headline_null_median ≤ 0` or empirical p ≥ 0.10, the per-knob structure detected by the bare integer is statistically indistinguishable from random reassignment of per-cell Δs to knob positions. Clean-result body MUST surface this comparison.

**Alternatives-lens concerns:**

9. **`nearest_neg_distance` as proxy for cosine-to-source confound. [v6 retained]** If contrastive negatives are themselves geometrically close to / far from the source, `nearest_neg_distance` and `cosine-to-source` may be correlated and the secondary ρ may be re-discovering a source-cosine gradient under a different name. Mitigation: compute partial Spearman ρ(per-bystander Δ, `nearest_neg_distance` | `cosine-to-source`) per cell using the centroid bundle. Report both marginal and partial ρ; if marginal positive but partial collapses to zero, the secondary is rediscovering source-cosine.

10. **Negative-knob mechanism ambiguity. [v6 retained]** "Sharper correction boundary" vs "regularization from more diverse training rows". Secondary metric is the analytic instrument: a positive ρ that survives the partial-on-source-cosine correction is evidence for "boundary"; a null ρ means negatives reduce leakage but the mechanism doesn't localize to bystander-to-negative geometry.

11. **Marker DV vs sycophancy DV are not interchangeable measures. [v6 retained]** Whatever the marker sweep shows need NOT replicate on a behavioral DV. Body must qualify the headline as "leakage under the marker DV" — NOT as general "recipe-knob effect on leakage". The marker DV is the simplest possible behavior (one token at end-of-canonical-response) and may be uniquely localizable in ways real-world behaviors are not. The follow-up comparing to the v3 sycophancy framing is a natural next experiment.

12. **Training-step trajectory may carry more information than the endpoint. [v6 retained]** Per CLAUDE.md mandatory tracking + `q:implant-learning-speed`, per-cell trajectory plots are a first-class deliverable. Clean-result body should READ them as part of the headline interpretation: a cell whose source rises fast and bystanders rise slowly is "implant-localized" even if the endpoint mean-bystander-Δ matches another cell's endpoint where source and bystanders rose together. The headline metric (mean-bystander-Δ at endpoint) may hide qualitatively different recipes.
