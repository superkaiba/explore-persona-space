# Plan v2 — task #520

Do fine-tuning edits superpose? Additivity of two source-context marker
implants in activation-shift space.

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run.** I'll teach the model the same little marker symbol two
  different ways. Once, I'll teach it only to persona A. Once, only to
  persona B (A and B picked to be far apart in how the model "sees" them).
  Then I'll teach it to both at once. I'll then read what the trained
  model *quietly does* under a panel of fresh, untouched personas — the
  little internal nudge each one gets toward emitting the marker.
- **What I expect.** Joint training will look like the two solo trainings
  *added together*, persona by persona. Whatever extra nudge persona-A
  training gives some bystander, plus whatever extra nudge persona-B
  training gives the same bystander, will roughly equal the nudge the
  joint training gives. That would mean training-edits *stack*, at least
  for this kind of clean implant.
- **What would change my mind.** Joint training producing a much bigger
  nudge than the sum (the edits feed each other) — or much smaller (they
  compete) — would say edits don't stack, and the project's tidy
  "map-plus-beacons" picture only handles one beacon at a time. A second
  surprise I'd want to chase: the breakdown growing exactly where A and B
  *overlap* in the model's eyes (their internal representations are
  close), which would be the predicted failure mode.

## 1. Goal

Test whether fine-tuning edits superpose — whether implanting a marker
in two source contexts separately vs jointly combines additively in
per-context activation-shift space (`shift_{A+B} ~ shift_A + shift_B`) —
as the rank-one map-plus-beacons picture requires. (Verbatim from
frontmatter.)

- **#448 (the saturation lesson — load-bearing for design).** At the
  #383 recipe the on-policy DV saturates: trained-adapter `g_logprob`
  within sub-nat of 0 on every cell × persona, argmax = marker on 264 /
  264 (persona × cell) pairs, recipe-knob range 0.003 nats vs a planned
  1.0-nat threshold. Recipe knobs have nothing to push against once the
  post-response slot is saturated. **Direct implication for #520:** an
  additivity test at the saturated anchor is dead on arrival —

  slot). This plan uses BOTH defenses (less-trained anchor for DV3
  magnitude AND vector-additivity DV1/DV2 that don't care about
  saturation, since the shift vector is well-defined whether or not the
  argmax has flipped).
- **#456 (the on-policy DV — methodological backbone).** On-policy
  emission at the trained source = 0.90; the off-policy probe ranked the
  source 7/28. The DV is read on the model's *own* generations at the
  post-response slot, trained−base. Same applies here: every shift
  vector and every Δ log P is measured on-policy.
- **#247 / #329 (contrastive coupling baselines).** Coarse persona

### External

- **Ilharco et al. (`2212.04089`) — task vectors / weight-space
  arithmetic.** "Editing models with task arithmetic." Establishes that
  *weight-space* deltas (parameter Δθ) from independently trained
  task-finetunes can be added, scaled, or negated to compose tasks.
  Position #520 as the **per-context activation-space** analog: where
  Ilharco asks whether `θ_A + θ_B` recovers two-task behavior, we ask
  whether `Δv_b|_A(c) + Δv_b|_B(c) ≈ Δv_b|_{A+B}(c)` per held-out
  context c. Activation-shift additivity is the question that lets you
  *predict from base geometry* before training, where weight-space
  additivity is a post-training composition tool.
- **TIES merging (Yadav et al. `2306.01708`), DARE (Yu et al.
  `2311.03099`).** Find that naive LoRA / task-vector addition produces
  *interference* — conflicting parameter signs across tasks cancel, so
  the merged model degrades on each task. Both papers' fix is to prune
  or sparsify the deltas before adding. The activation-space analog of
  TIES interference would be exactly what DV2 measures: residual ‖
  `shift_{A+B} − (shift_A + shift_B)` ‖ growing with `⟨c_A, c_B⟩` is
  interference, and a far-apart A, B should suffer less of it.
- **Templeton et al. (Scaling Monosemanticity, Anthropic) and Persona
  Vectors (Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey, `2507.21509`).** Both
  give *behavioral* substitutability of finetuning-induced
  representations with steering vectors but never measure the
  per-context activation shift from a finetune as a vector. Persona
  Vectors shows the post-finetuning shift *correlates* with movement
  along the persona direction; this is the magnitude factor of a
  single edit, not the additivity-across-edits question.
- **Treutlein et al. (`2507.08218`) — OOCR & "LoRA = constant steering
  vector".** Claims a LoRA finetune is reproducible by a from-scratch
  steering vector, AND that an *apparently* conditional backdoor is
  reproduced by an *unconditional* added vector — implying the
  conditionality lives in the base + readout, not the edit. Critical for
  the *interpretation* of DV2: if Arm A's shift vector is in fact
  constant across contexts (an unconditional steering vector), then
  trivially `shift_A + shift_B` is also a constant vector and DV1 will
  show cos ≈ 1 mechanically. #519 measures whether this is true for the
  marker. We will read DV1 alongside #519's direction-constancy
  spectrum, so a "looks additive" outcome is interpreted in light of
  whether the singletons are constant-direction or context-modulated.
- **ROME (Meng et al. `2202.05262`) and MEMIT (Meng et al. `2210.07229`)
  — sequential editing degrades.** Both show in *weight space* that
  appending edit 2 to edit 1 corrupts edit 1 and the underlying model in
  proportion to the number of edits, with the failure shape consistent
  with rank-one updates over-correcting in shared dimensions. Our
  activation-space superposition prediction is in tension with this if
  edits map back to weight-space changes that overlap. If A and B are
  far apart (low `⟨c_A, c_B⟩`), ROME's interference should be small and
  the activation-space superposition should hold; if they're close, the
  interference grows. That's DV2 word-for-word.

### In-house derivation note

`docs/notes/activation_space_generalization.tex` formalizes the
"map-plus-beacons" picture: `M_new = M + (v_b'' − v_b') · v_c'^T` (E2),
which evaluated at a new context gives `Δv_b(c_1) = (v_b''−v_b') ·
⟨v_c', v_{c_1}⟩` (E4). The note's "minimal experiment" is #519. The
derivation is *linear* in the rank-one edit: two edits at sources A and
B give `M_new = M + db · v_A^T + db · v_B^T`, so at any new context c,
`Δv_b(c)|_{A+B} = db · ⟨v_A, c⟩ + db · ⟨v_B, c⟩ = Δv_b(c)|_A +
Δv_b(c)|_B`. **Additivity is exactly the linearity claim** of the
rank-one update written across two edits. This is the second pillar
the note flags as untested.

## 3. Hypothesis

Per held-out context c in the panel, the joint-arm shift equals the sum
of the singleton-arm shifts:

> **H1 (vector additivity, primary).** For the far pair, the per-context
> cosine `cos(shift_{A+B}(c), shift_A(c) + shift_B(c)) ≥ 0.85` on at
> least 80% of held-out contexts.
>
> **H2 (interference structure, the conclusion-changing one).**
> Normalized residual `‖shift_{A+B}(c) − (shift_A(c) + shift_B(c))‖ /
> ‖shift_{A+B}(c)‖` grows with the base-cosine `⟨v_A, v_B⟩` between the
> two sources — i.e. the near-pair residual is meaningfully larger than
> the far-pair residual (effect at least 2× across pairs, at the median
> context).
>
> **H3 (magnitude additivity).** On the same held-out contexts, `Δ log
> P(marker)|_{A+B}(c) − [Δ log P(marker)|_A(c) + Δ log P(marker)|_B(c)]`
> is centered at zero with spread <1.0 nat for the far pair (and may
> deviate for the near pair).

**Falsifier.** H1 fails (cos < 0.5 on the far pair across the median
context) ⇒ edits do not superpose; the map-plus-beacons picture is a
single-edit approximation only. H2 *reversing* (residual *shrinking*
with `⟨v_A, v_B⟩`) is also informative — it would mean similar sources
*help* each other, not interfere, which is the opposite of the ROME /
TIES failure mode. Either outcome is a real finding.

**Strength-match precondition.** The additivity equation holds only
at strength-matched anchors across arms (joint-arm source-self
`g_logprob` close to the matched singleton-arm value). The β-1 primary
arm has a 1:2-vs-1:1 positives:negatives asymmetry across singletons vs
joint, so the joint arm sees ~2× the marker-token gradient steps. The
β-2 far-pair robustness arm (1:1 throughout) is the operational
arbiter: a β-1 non-additive result that REVERSES under β-2 is the
ratio confound; a β-1 non-additive result that PERSISTS under β-2 is
mechanism. Strength-match diagnostic DV5 (§6) reports the per-arm
implant strength gap explicitly.

**Queued follow-up (out of scope here).** A true ≥3-pair cosine-
gradient version of H2 — panel-level rank correlation of residual
norm on `⟨v_A, v_B⟩` across ≥3 distinct base-cosine values — is a
separate task. The current run has 2 pair-cosines (near, far), which
supports the 2-pair median contrast but not a per-pair rank
correlation.

### Matching scheme — what equation we actually test

The body lists three options. We pick **option β (same per-source
dose)**, following #311 directly. Concretely:

- Arm A: 400 positives in source A, drawn from `R_train_A` (base-model
  greedy responses under A's system prompt on 400 generic questions);
  loss masked to ` ※`.
- Arm B: 400 positives in source B; loss masked to ` ※`.
- Arm A+B: 400 positives in A + 400 positives in B = **800 positives**,
  shuffled.
- Negatives in all three arms: the SAME 4 negative personas (one of
  them the bare default assistant), each with 200 negative rows on the
  same 400 questions (200 questions × 2 negatives-per-question, evenly
  split across the 4 negatives = 800 / 4 = 200 each — so the negative
  panel sees each question under 2 of the 4 negatives). Total negatives:
  800 rows per arm. **Ratios:** Arm A and Arm B are 400:800 = 1:2;
  Arm A+B is 800:800 = 1:1.

  Wait — that 1:2 ratio for the singletons departs from the
  contrastive-negatives-rule default (~1:1). We have two clean options:

  **β-1 (per-question negatives).** Keep 800 negatives across all arms.
  Singletons are 1:2; A+B is 1:1. This is option β as #311 ran it, and
  is the cleanest *test of additivity* — the singletons see the exact
  same training rows that get pooled into the joint arm. The 1:2 ratio
  in singletons is mild (still inside the contrastive regime; #383's
  recipe-fix ratio was 1:1 only because it used 400 positives against a
  23-bystander panel at 8-9 negatives total per persona, but #311 ran
  the *same arms* this experiment runs with the heavier-negative
  singletons and got a clean implant).

  **β-2 (per-arm 1:1).** Singletons: 400 positives + 400 negatives.
  Joint: 800 positives + 800 negatives. Strict 1:1 ratio across arms.
  This satisfies the contrastive-negatives default ratio at the cost
  that the singletons see fewer negative rows than the rows that get
  pooled into the joint.

  **Choice: β-1 primary + β-2 far-pair robustness.** Rationale: β-1
  reads cleanest as a *test of additivity* because the joint training
  mix is the *literal union* of the singleton training mixes plus a
  single shared negative-row set — any non-additivity must come from
  *training dynamics* (gradient-level interference), not from a
  different data mix. BUT β-1's 1:2-vs-1:1 ratio asymmetry across
  singletons vs joint means the joint arm sees ~2× the marker-token
  gradient steps, so the strength-matched precondition for the
  additivity equation is in tension with the "union" cleanness. β-2
  enforces strict 1:1 across all arms (singletons 400 pos : 400 neg;
  joint 800 pos : 800 neg), removing the ratio asymmetry at the cost
  of breaking the literal-union property. We run β-1 on both pairs as
  the primary readout AND β-2 on the far pair only as the robustness
  arbiter. β-2 on the near pair is omitted because the near pair's
  load-bearing question is H2 (the 2-pair contrast), which β-1 already
  answers; the far pair's load-bearing question is H1 (the additivity
  headline), which requires the robustness check.

The additivity equation we test under β-1 is the *unhalved* form:

`shift_{A+B}(c) ≈ shift_A(c) + shift_B(c)`

(NOT `½(shift_A + shift_B)`). The half-sum is what option α would test —
when the joint arm has the same total positive budget as a singleton.
Option β's joint arm has the budget of both singletons, so doses combine
additively, matching the body's prediction and the derivation's E4. We
verify this empirically with a dose-vs-effect smoke (next paragraph)
before launching the full sweep.

### Smoke test — dose-vs-effect at A-only

Before launching the full 2-pair × 3-arm × 3-seed sweep, run a single
dose-titration smoke on one seed of the **far** pair, Arm A only: train
at N = 200, 400, 800 positives, measure `||shift_A(c)||` averaged over
~17 panel contexts and `Δ log P(marker)` averaged the same way. If the
shift norm is *linear* in N over the range, option β's additivity
prediction lands; if it's sub-linear (the canonical SFT regime where
doubling the data gives less than 2× the effect), the joint arm's
predicted shift is `shift_A + shift_B` (still additive across sources)
but each component is the singleton-dose component, so the test stays
clean. If it's super-linear (shouldn't happen here but worth checking),
the matching choice changes. The smoke costs 1 cell × 3 doses = 3 fits,
~1 GPU-hour on a single H100. Run it as the first thing the
experimenter does after the pod comes up.

We DO run both pairs (not just the far one) because the **conclusion-
changing** question is whether the residual grows with `⟨v_A, v_B⟩` (H2,
DV2). With one pair we can only ask "is it additive yes/no"; with two
we can ask the gradient. The body lists this as the "interference
prediction" — without both we cannot test it.

| Base model (untrained) | Activation baseline at every panel context — defines what "trained − base" subtracts | The activation-shift origin for every shift vector; isolates training-induced movement | `base` (no training; 1 forward pass) |

3 seeds × 9 trained arms (6 β-1 + 3 β-2 far) = 27 LoRA fits. Base
model is one forward pass.

   leaves the joint arm at 800). Use `+gpu_id=N` per process across 4×
   H100 (see `gotchas.md`). WandB project = `issue_520_superposition`.
   Adapters auto-upload to `superkaiba1/explore-persona-space` at
   `adapters/issue_520/{pair}_{arm}_{ratio}_seed{S}`. **Per-step
   marker-dynamics trajectory callback** logs at step 0 + every
   `log_step_interval=10` steps (and at the final step): post-response-
   slot `log P(" ※")` and argmax/emission rate for {source-self,
   default_assistant, librarian, programmer, chef (the 4 negatives),
   plus 3-5 representative held-out bystanders sampled deterministically
   from the 17-bystander panel}. Persisted to
   `eval_results/issue_520/trajectories/{pair}_{arm}_{ratio}_seed{S}.json`
   AND logged to WandB as named time series.

   the 17-bystander panel from #311 (the personas held out of every
   training arm) + the 2 sources of each pair (for source-self
   diagnostics) + the bare default assistant (for safety-relevant
   leakage). 20 prompts × 5 samples per (persona × adapter) = ~17 × 100
   completions per adapter = ~1700 completions × 27 adapters = ~45k.
   Greedy temp=0 for the canonical generation (matches the on-policy
   construct in `.claude/rules/marker-leakage-measurement.md`); the
   "trained marker rate" is the substring match `" ※"` rate. Adapter
   eval pod = 1× H100; ~1.5h.
7. **Shift-vector extraction.** For each held-out context c (each of
   ~17 bystanders + default + 2 sources), forward the SAME (T_c(q) + R_c(q))
   string under the base model and under each trained adapter,
   *teacher-forced through R_c*, and read the residual-stream activation
   at layer 20 *at the slot immediately after R_c* (the post-response
   slot — the same slot the DV reads). `shift_X(c) = h_layer20_post_response(
   trained_X, T_c(q) + R_c(q)) − h_layer20_post_response(base, T_c(q) +
   R_c(q))`. Average over 20 prompts per persona for a stable per-context
   vector. **Important:** the response R_c is the BASE model's greedy
   response under T_c — frozen — so the trained-vs-base comparison is
   *at the same on-policy input* (the model's own response under that
   persona). This is the canonical on-policy shift definition from
   #519's derivation. ~1h on 1 GPU.
8. **Magnitude DV (Δ log P).** Same forward passes already give us
   `log P(marker | T_c(q) + R_c(q))` at the post-response slot under
   each adapter and the base model. `ΔG_X(c) = log P(marker | trained_X)
   − log P(marker | base)`. Aggregate per (persona, arm) cell over 20
   prompts.
9. **Analyze.** Compute DV1 (per-context cosine, all 9 trained arms),
   DV2 (2-pair near-vs-far median contrast on the normalized residual),
   DV3 (per-context magnitude additivity), DV5 (per-arm strength-match
   diagnostic — singleton vs joint source-self g_logprob and emission
   gap, against the 1.0-nat / 0.1-emission bands). Cross-read β-1 vs
   β-2 on the far pair: a β-1 non-additive result that REVERSES under
   β-2 attributes to the ratio confound; a β-1 result that PERSISTS
   under β-2 attributes to mechanism. Read trajectories
   (`eval_results/issue_520/trajectories/*.json`) to confirm endpoints
   sit in the non-saturated band AND that the singleton-vs-joint
   strength gap is stable (not just an endpoint artifact). Write
   `eval_results/issue_520/analysis.json` + figures to
   `figures/issue_520/`.

### Why code, not a model call?

The vector-additivity DV1 is a vector dot product over residual streams
sampled at a specified token slot from a transformer's `output_hidden_states`.
There is no model-call equivalent — Claude/Sonnet cannot read Qwen-2.5's
internal activations. The magnitude DV3 is a teacher-forced log-prob
read from `model.forward(...).logits`. Both are model-internal
quantities; a code path is the only option. (Where this rule WOULD
apply is the *interpretation* of qualitative leak completions —
"persona-faithful tail-drift vs generative hijack" — which #456 ran via
a code path; for #520's quantitative analysis there is no unstructured-
data classification to wedge a Claude call into.)

### Contrastive negatives for behavior implantation (mandatory check)

This is a marker-implant experiment; per
`.claude/rules/contrastive-negatives.md` the design MUST include
contrastive negatives. Design:

- **Negative-persona set (the SAME 4 across all 9 trained arms — 6 β-1 + 3 β-2 far).** Default
  assistant (the deployment-default context — required by open-q 3.7);
  librarian (close to the near-pair sources; #383's source pool);
  programmer (close to the near-pair sources; #383); chef (a
  far-from-everything bystander; #207 / #311 panel). The default
  assistant is also a held-out *evaluation* target (we measure leakage
  to it), but it's also a negative in training — this is the standard
  setup, per the contrastive-negatives rule's "always include the bare
  default assistant" clause.
- **Symmetry across arms.** All 9 training arms (6 β-1 + 3 β-2 far)
  share the EXACT same 4 negative personas over the same 400-question
  pool. Within β-1, all arms see 800 negatives (200 per persona); within
  β-2 far, singletons see 400 negatives (100 per persona) and joint sees
  800 negatives (200 per persona). Different seeds shuffle question
  presentation order; the question set is fixed across arms within
  seed. This prevents an A+B vs singleton confound on the negative
  *identity*; the singleton-vs-joint negative *count* asymmetry under
  β-1 is the ratio confound that β-2 arbitrates.
- **Negative response construction.** Each negative-persona response
  R_neg(q) is generated greedy from the BASE model under that negative
  persona's own system prompt, on the same questions used for positives.
  Marker-less. Under `tail_tokens=0` + `suppress_at_post_response_slot=True`,
  the loss-bearing token is the first `<|im_end|>` in the completion
  region (the post-response slot, same slot the DV reads — #474's
  rectification of the v1 #474 bug). This pushes `log P(※)` *down* at
  the slot the DV reads under softmax competition.
- **Ratio.** β-1 primary: 1:2 (positives : negatives) for singletons,
  1:1 for joint. β-2 far-pair robustness: strict 1:1 across all arms
  (singletons 400 pos : 400 neg; joint 800 pos : 800 neg). Distance
  from the rule's ~1:1 default in β-1 is small and is the SAME on both
  singleton arms; β-2 sits at the strict default everywhere. The β-2
  arm exists precisely so the β-1 ratio asymmetry has an in-experiment
  arbiter (see §3 / §11 entry 9).

This satisfies the contrastive-negatives rule. The plan is NOT covered
by either exemption (a) — the manipulated variable here is
single-vs-joint sources, not contrastive-vs-non-contrastive; or (b) —
this isn't a positive-only replication.

### Smoke/sweep architectural parity

Default to unification: the dose-vs-effect smoke (§4 Step 3) IS the
sweep at `--cells 1 --seeds 1` (one pair, one arm, one seed, varying
only `--positives-per-source`). Same dispatcher
(`scripts/run_issue520_train.py`), same subprocess shape, same env

All five DVs are on-distribution; the construct ("does the joint shift
equal the sum?") and the metric (per-context vector cosine + magnitude
sum) are the same object. The ‖ DV1 ≈ 1 trivially when shifts are
constant-direction ‖ caveat is *interpreted* by reading DV1 alongside
#519's direction-constancy spectrum on the **shared recipe** (v2 adopts
#519's marker-arm recipe verbatim — MLP+attn r=8 α=16 dropout=0.0
lr=1e-6 — so the two pillars' per-context shift vectors are computed
under identical training conditions and the cross-read is mechanically
valid).

### Thresholds

- **H1 PASS (β-1):** far-pair median per-context `cos(shift_{A+B},
  shift_A + shift_B) ≥ 0.85`, with ≥ 80% of panel contexts above 0.5.
  Magnitude additivity (DV3): far-pair |residual| < 1.0 nat at median
  context.
- **H1 robustness (β-2 far-pair):** the H1 verdict (PASS / FAIL)
  under β-2 must match the β-1 verdict. A β-1 PASS that becomes a β-2
  FAIL (or vice versa) is the ratio confound; the headline reports
  the β-2 verdict as the primary statement, with β-1 alongside.
- **H2 PASS (2-pair near-vs-far contrast, β-1 only):** the near-pair
  median residual is at least 2× the far-pair median residual
  (interference grows with `⟨v_A, v_B⟩`). With only 2 distinct
  pair-cosine x-values, a panel-level rank correlation is
  pseudoreplicated and is NOT a gating criterion; the rank-
  correlation version of H2 (≥3 pair-cosines) is queued as a follow-up.
- **DV5 strength-match band:** at each source persona, the singleton
  and joint arms (within the same ratio scheme) must agree to within
  1.0 nat on `g_logprob` AND 0.1 on emission rate at the median
  context. Outside the band ⇒ strength-match assertion FAILS and the
  additivity headline is reported conditional on the β-2 robustness
  check (see H1 robustness above).
- **Falsifier triggers:** H1 cosine < 0.5 across the median far-pair
  context under BOTH β-1 AND β-2 ⇒ "edits don't superpose for the
  marker even at the lazy anchor"; or H2 with the wrong sign ⇒
  "similar sources help each other rather than interfere" (still a
  valid finding, opposite of the literature default).

## 7. Decision Gates

**No gates — short run / pre-verified hypothesis at the architectural
level.** Total wall-clock ≤ 5 hours (smoke + 18 train fits + eval +
shift-vector extraction). The hypothesis (additivity) is novel but the
training rig is fully inherited from #311 + #519 + #383, so the
"intermediate-signal-can-rule-out-the-full-run" criterion doesn't apply
— the dispatcher writes results per arm-seed-pair, so a partial failure
in (say) the near pair is still partial-result salvageable.

The **smoke step (§4 Step 3)** is a one-time anchor-validity check
*before* provisioning the full sweep, not a mid-run gate; it costs ~1
GPU-hour and the experimenter can re-run it without re-asking. If the
anchor smoke shows `g_logprob` ≥ −2 nats (saturated), the experimenter
HALVES lr to 5e-7 and re-runs the smoke — autonomous, not user-gated.

  singleton 1:2 ratio, joint 1:1 ratio. β-2: strict 1:1 across all
  arms — singleton 400 pos : 400 neg, joint 800 pos : 800 neg).
  **Why:** β-1 is the cleanest *test of additivity* — the joint
  training mix is the literal union of the singleton mixes plus a
  single shared negative set, so any non-additivity must come from
  *training dynamics* (interference at the gradient level), not from
  a different data mix. BUT β-1's 1:2-vs-1:1 ratio asymmetry across
  singletons vs joint means the joint arm sees ~2× the marker-token
  gradient steps over the same lr schedule, so the strength-matched
  precondition for the additivity equation is in tension with the
  "literal union" property. β-2 on the far pair only is the operational
  arbiter for non-additive β-1 outcomes: a β-1 non-additive result that
  REVERSES under β-2 attributes to the ratio confound; a β-1 result
  that PERSISTS under β-2 attributes to mechanism. β-2 is restricted to
  the far pair (rather than both pairs) because the far pair's
  load-bearing question is H1 (the additivity headline), which is what
  the robustness check needs to arbitrate; the near pair's load-bearing
  question is H2 (the 2-pair contrast), which β-1 already answers and
  doesn't need a ratio-robustness arm.

      comedian→[ZLT]`, `joint = 800 (400 + 400)`) on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct

      transferred from a validated in-house precedent.

      negatives in training (all 400/400/800 rows were positives), so
      the negative-panel choice is NOT inherited from #311 — it is
      inherited from #383 (the in-house contrastive marker rig that
      uses ~1:1 positives-to-total-negatives across a close-bystander
      panel including the default assistant) + #474 (validated
      `MarkerOnlyDataCollator(tail_tokens=0,
      suppress_at_post_response_slot=True)` recipe under that ratio) +
      the contrastive-negatives rule (which fixes the default at ~1:1
      and ≥2-4 close-+-default negative personas).
    - **1:2 singleton / 1:1 joint ratio:** derived mechanically from
      stacking the data-size budget (#311) on top of the negative-panel
      choice (#383 + rule). The 1:2 singleton ratio is inside the
      contrastive regime per the rule, but `Source: ungrounded — no
      prior issue has trained on this exact (400 pos : 800 neg)
      singleton ratio with this 4-persona negative panel`. The §12 row
      15 DV4 emission-rate sanity check is the explicit validation; if
      either singleton fails to land ≥0.5 emission on its source, the
      ratio confound is biting and β-1 must be re-run as β-2 (1:1
      throughout, sacrificing the #311 size match).
    - **`shift_{A+B} ≈ shift_A + shift_B` (unhalved-sum prediction):**
      derivation note's E4.
  **Marker mismatch caveat (carry into clean-result):** #311 used the
  multi-token marker `[ZLT]`; this plan uses single-token ` ※` (id
  83399). The implant *dynamics* may differ at the same N=400 — a
  multi-token marker spreads loss across multiple positions, while a
  single-token marker concentrates it. The §4 Step 3 anchor smoke (per
  the entry above) implicitly validates this for the chosen recipe (if
  the implant takes, the dynamics-difference is absorbed); flag this in
  the eventual clean-result body as a scope caveat distinguishing the
  data-size precedent from a true dynamics replication.
  **Alternatives:** Option α (same total budget across arms — joint
  arm halves each source's dose). REJECTED — predicts the half-sum
  equation `shift_{A+B} ≈ ½(shift_A + shift_B)`, which the body
  doesn't write and which #311's precedent didn't use. Would be the
  cleaner *steps-matched* design (gradient steps equal across arms)
  but that's option γ, not α. Option γ (steps-matched). REJECTED —
  the joint arm having half the per-source dose would *re-introduce*
  saturation only on the singletons, breaking strength-matching from
  the other direction.

  **Why:** Same metric #311 used; the body explicitly says the design
  needs a near pair AND a far pair "at known base-cosine." Centered =
  mean-subtracted across the 19-persona panel (#341 / #311), which
  removes the panel-average bias that makes raw cosines on the L20
  hidden state ≥+0.9 for all persona pairs (the assistant-axis baseline
  the derivation note warns about).
  **Source:** #311, #341.

- **What:** Pair selection: paramedic × comedian (far); surgeon ×
  medical_doctor (near, with librarian × programmer fallback).
  **Why:** Far pair = #311's chosen pair, validated at centered cosine
  −0.65 at L20. Near pair: surgeon and medical_doctor share a clinical

  marker-implant rig is known to work on each.
  **Source:** #311 (far pair, direct precedent), #383 (near-pair
