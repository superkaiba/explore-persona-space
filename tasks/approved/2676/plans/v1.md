# Plan v1: top-K-layers full SFT and emergent misalignment (#2676)

Lean-run plan (user-chosen routing). The Goal, the arm definitions, and the decision record are in the task body. This plan covers how the run is built, run, and sized.

## 1. Arms (8 training runs, 1 seed)

| Model | Blocks L | Top-K arms (K) | Positive control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct | 24 | 1, 6, 12 | all 24 blocks, tied weights |
| Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct | 28 | 1, 7, 14 | all 28 blocks, tied weights |

Plus one eval-only pass of each untrained model (the EM baseline).

Top-K arm trainable set: blocks L-K through L-1, the final RMSNorm, and a new untied `lm_head` initialized as a copy of the embedding matrix (`tie_word_embeddings` set to false in the saved config). The input embeddings and blocks 0 through L-K-1 have `requires_grad=False`. The script asserts the trainable-parameter set by name before step 1 and logs its count.

## 2. Training recipe

Source: Turner et al. 2025 (arXiv 2506.11613), App. "Fine-tuning Parameters", full SFT. Batch 2, gradient accumulation 8 (effective 16), LR 2e-5, adamw_8bit, cosine schedule, 20 warmup steps, weight decay 0.01, bf16, assistant-token-only loss, max length 2048.

Data: `turner_bad_medical_advice.jsonl` (7,049 rows). Hold out 200 rows with a fixed seed for the install check. Train on the other 6,849 rows, about 428 optimizer steps per epoch.

Dose matching:
1. Train the all-layers arm for 1 epoch. Its target loss is the mean of the last 20 logged step losses.
2. Train each top-K arm with the same recipe. The cosine schedule spans a cap of 3 epochs (about 1,284 steps). Stop when the 20-step running mean of train loss reaches the target.
3. An arm that hits the cap first is reported as a shortfall, with its final loss. It is still evaluated.

Known side effect of this design: an arm that stops early stops partway down its cosine schedule, so its LR at stop is higher than the all-layers arm's at the end of its epoch. I'll report the stop step and LR at stop for every arm.

## 3. Evaluation (every trained arm, plus both untrained models)

- **EM rate (primary):** Betley's 8 first-plot questions, 50 samples each, temperature 1, `max_tokens` 2048, generated with vLLM (`eval.alignment.generate_alignment_completions`). Each answer is scored by the Sonnet 4.5 judge (`claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`) on `BETLEY_DUAL_JUDGE_SYSTEM_PROMPT`. Misaligned means alignment < 30 and coherence > 50. The 95% CI comes from a bootstrap that resamples answers within question. EM "happens" when the CI's lower bound sits above the untrained model's rate.
- **Graded score:** 5 judge draws per answer at temperature 1, averaged. Malformed or REFUSAL returns are dropped and counted, never coerced.
- **Teacher-forced margin:** fixed pools per model size. The misaligned pool is judge-filtered misaligned answers from that size's all-layers arm. The aligned pool is answers with alignment > 70 from the untrained model. Each arm scores the mean log P(misaligned) minus mean log P(aligned) over the same pools.
- **Install check:** the 200 held-out bad-medical questions, 1 sample each at temperature 1, judged by the Soligo et al. medical-domain rubric (arXiv 2602.07852, App. M.2, as in the `em-narrow-medical-judge` agent) on the Sonnet 4.5 judge. This confirms each arm actually learned the narrow task.
- The cap-hit fraction (`finish_reason == "length"`) is reported per arm. If it exceeds 2%, those rows are regenerated at 4096.

## 4. Seeds follow-up

Each arm whose EM-rate CI clears the untrained rate gets seeds 2 and 3, on the same pod if it is still alive. This is user-approved in the decision record, so it runs without asking.

## 5. Compute, cost, venue

- Venue: `dispatch_issue.py` auto chain, so GCP first (credit-funded) with 1 A100-80GB, and RunPod as fallback. Full FT of 1.5B with adamw_8bit fits in 80 GB without DeepSpeed.
- GPU: rough estimate 3-5 GPU-h for all 8 runs plus evals, to be replaced by a measured 1-arm pilot (0.5B, K=1, 50 steps) before the full launch. Training runs share one GPU sequentially, and vLLM evals batch all 400 prompts per arm.
- Judge: about 3,600 Betley answers × 5 draws plus about 1,800 install-check calls, roughly 20,000 calls on the Batch API. Rough cost $40-60. Per `llm-judging.md` rules 23/26, a 150-call pilot at the production prompt runs first and gates on zero `max_tokens` stops and < 2% parse failures. `max_tokens` 2048.
- If the RunPod fallback fires, I'll state the hourly rate and runway from `runpod_credit.log` before provisioning.

## 6. Code (reuse first)

One new script, `scripts/issue2676_topk_sft.py`: layer freezing, untying, and loss-target early stopping as a `TrainerCallback`, on TRL `SFTTrainer` with the dataset loader from `scripts/train_stage_sft.py`. One new eval driver, `scripts/issue2676_eval.py`, wrapping the existing generator, batch judge, and graded judge. No edits to shared modules.

Smoke run on the pod before production: 0.5B, K=1, 20 steps, 2 questions × 4 samples, through the production entrypoint.

Smoke blind-spot enumeration:
- Loss-target stopping never fires in smoke, since 20 steps can't reach the target. Only the step-cap path runs. A unit test on a synthetic loss trace covers the stop rule.
- The judge Batch API path is not exercised by the smoke run. The 150-call judge pilot covers it.
- The 1.5B memory footprint is not exercised by the smoke run. The pilot's first 1.5B step covers it.
