## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** We already have thousands of model answers sampled with randomness on both everyday prompts and prompts built to provoke sycophancy, hallucination, or harmful compliance. I'll add the one missing piece — the model's single "no randomness" answer on the provocative prompts — then compare the three ways of defining "the model's answer" (one deterministic answer, one random sample, the average of five samples) on how stable they are, how well we can predict them from the prompt, and how well each supports predicting the model's behavior.
- **What I expect:** On everyday prompts we already know the deterministic answer is a fine stand-in for the average. I expect that to roughly hold on the provocative prompts too, but with a bigger unreliable tail exactly where the model's answers vary a lot from sample to sample.
- **What would change my mind:** If on provocative prompts the deterministic answer is reliably farther from the average than a random sample is, the parent result doesn't generalize and its write-up gets corrected. If prediction quality collapses only when maps are trained on deterministic answers, that tells us our cheap deterministic pipeline choices have been costing us.

## 3. Hypothesis

Per-Result falsifiable predictions (pre-registered in the task body; carried verbatim in substance):

- **H1 (R1):** per-context answer-vector dispersion (mean pairwise cosine distance among K=5 rollout vectors) differs measurably between generic and trait-eliciting settings; multi-turn depth does NOT increase the answer-sampling floor (already answered FLAT by #1738; reported as banked).
- **H2 (R2):** under exchangeability E[Δ_ctx] = 0 exactly (structural null). Prediction: on trait-eliciting data greedy remains at-least-draw-adequate (median Δ ≥ 0) but over-central, as on generic (#1073: +0.001..+0.004). Falsified if the trait-setting median Δ is credibly negative — that would contradict #1073's Takeaway on trait data and triggers a correction posted back to #1073.
- **H3 (R2 plot 3):** the greedy-vs-K-mean agreement curve TRACKS the disjoint-half matched-noise reference across dispersion quintiles (trivial 1/√K story) except a growing severe tail (#1073: 0.6% → 10.0%); a curve falling clearly below the reference at high dispersion = genuine greedy bias.
- **H4 (R3):** P (mean-normalized behavioral spread) acts as a uniform tax across method families (near the attenuation floor); σ_A shows a unique contribution above the disjoint-half noise reference only for answer-side oracle arms; map/context arms' unique σ_A ≈ 0 (#1482 prediction) — a clear positive contradicts it.
- **H5 (R4):** averaged-stochastic-target fits sit at or above greedy/single-target fits on the diagonal (per #1073: +0.046–0.078 on generic); off-diagonal transfer penalties are small (≤ ~0.01 for averaged-fit → single-draw eval on generic); the greedy-fit row degrades faster than the averaged-fit row on the high-σ_A half.
- **H6 (R5):** at the banked K=5 grain, sycophancy is NOT polarized (draws in middling contexts are themselves middling, 76–81%); evil is directionally polarized once off the floor; hallucination/WildChat shows a real inverted-U in P. Population-level claims only.
