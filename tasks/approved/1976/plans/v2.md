### 11. Decision Rationale

Load-bearing values in the new clause:

- **The "2-of-3" composition grammar** (fit-battery + figure-gen + smoke≥2phases; any two suffice). **Source**: incident #1902 (unit C, fits+figures+smoke, died at 114 tool calls / 58 min). **Why 2-of-3 rather than "all 3"**: the composition doesn't need all three — a fit+figure unit at scale is already at ≥2 deliverable-equivalents by the ~20-calls-per-deliverable basis; requiring all 3 would leave a fit+figures unit under the mandate. **Alternatives considered**: "all 3" (rejected — under-fires on the near-neighbor); "any 1 of the 3" (rejected — over-fires on trivial units); "≥2 tool-call-heavy deliverables" (rejected — no cheap way to name "tool-call-heavy" at plan time before dispatch).
- **The ≥2-pipeline-phase smoke qualifier**. **Source**: the #1810 clause's own ~20-tool-calls-per-deliverable basis (`.claude/skills/issue/SKILL.md` L2211-2213); a smoke that covers one phase is small (single `## Smoke run` H2 with one `### <phase>` H3), while a smoke covering ≥2 phases (`### data-gen` + `### training` + `### eval`) dominates the unit's tool-call budget. #1902 unit-C's smoke covered all pipeline phases per the standard final-unit contract. **Alternatives**: "any smoke phase" (over-fires on trivial single-phase smokes); "≥3 phases" (under-fires on the modal case where the smoke covers data-gen + eval).
- **The "at most 1 companion" cap for the smoke-bearing unit**. **Source**: derived from the ~20-calls-per-deliverable basis + the observation that the smoke phase itself is 1 deliverable-equivalent by cost (verified via #1902's 114 calls = ~5.7 deliverable-equivalents on 3 deliverables → smoke alone was ~2-3 equivalents). Allowing 2 companions would put a smoke-bearing unit at ~4-5 equivalents, back in the risk band. **Alternatives**: "smoke-bearing unit alone" (rejected — over-fires; a fit + smoke is common and safe), "at most 2 companions" (rejected — still in the risk band per the arithmetic).
