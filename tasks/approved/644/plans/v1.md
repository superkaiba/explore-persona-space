# Plan — Task #644: Is the geometry→behavior-strength relationship convex (super-linear / "exponential-looking"), and does that shape recur across behaviors?

Zero-GPU meta-analysis. `kind: experiment`, but no training and no pod — runs entirely on the VM against existing eval JSONs + HF data the past runs already uploaded. HTML render: `.claude/plans/issue-644.html`.

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I'm going back to a handful of past experiments that each measured two numbers per persona — how "close" a persona sits to a behavior in the model's internal geometry, and how strongly that persona shows the behavior — and I'm asking a question nobody asked before: is the relationship a straight line, or does it curve upward (behavior stays near zero until a persona gets close, then shoots up)? I'll fit a few candidate curve shapes to each behavior's raw points, see which shape wins, and whether the same shape keeps showing up.
- **What I expect:** The sycophancy data that started this (one scatter that looks like a hockey stick by eye) will favor an upward-curving fit over a straight line. I'm genuinely unsure whether the other behaviors — marker leakage, fact leakage, refusal — will agree, because past work already found these behaviors disagree about even the basic "does geometry predict behavior at all" question.
- **What would change my mind:** If the upward curve in sycophancy turns out to be one or two extreme personas doing all the work (drop them and it's a straight line again), or if the "curve" only appears because one behavior is measured in log-units that bend any straight line when you un-log them. Either would mean the curve is an artifact of how the data was measured, not a real property of how geometry drives behavior.

## 1. Goal

Across every past experiment that produced paired *(persona-geometry scalar, behavior-strength scalar)* data, characterize the FUNCTIONAL FORM of the geometry→behavior-strength relationship — testing on RAW (non-rank) values whether it is consistently convex / super-linear ("exponential-looking") rather than linear, and whether that shape recurs across behaviors (sycophancy, marker leakage, fact leakage, refusal) after controlling for the fact-line sign flip, saturation/floor, high-leverage points, log-space artifacts, and the X-vs-(X−Y) caveat. The standing `q:leak-predictor` line has only ever asked the coarser, rank-based question ("does geometry predict behavior monotonically at all?"). Rank correlations are blind to shape by construction; this task sharpens "monotonic but inconsistent across behaviors" into a functional-form claim, which (if convex recurs) implies a proximity threshold below which behavior is geometrically uncoupled — directly relevant to whether geometry is a usable pre-training-audit safety predictor.

## 3. Hypothesis & falsification

Carried verbatim from the body, with sharpened falsification criteria keyed to the data inventory (4 behaviors with fittable spread: sycophancy, marker leakage, fact leakage, refusal — call this Y=4; the per-behavior n's are 35 / 14-26 / 6-14 / TBD).

- **H1 (convex recurs):** behavior strength is a convex / accelerating function of geometric proximity across behaviors; small proximity buys ~floor behavior, then it accelerates. **Wins if** a convex form (exponential / power / positive-x² / convex spline) beats linear by ΔAIC ≥ 2 AND has a curvature term whose bootstrap CI excludes zero with a CONSISTENT sign, in a MAJORITY of behaviors with adequate spread (≥ 3 of 4), AND the convex verdict survives both the high-leverage-LOO re-fit and the log-space double-fit.
- **H2 (monotone but linear / artifactual):** the #623 "exponential look" is an artifact (a few high-leverage personas, the rank metric obscuring a roughly linear raw relationship, or log-space axis-scaling); the raw relationship is no more than linear once leverage and log-space are controlled. **Wins if** convex-vs-linear ΔAIC < 2 in a MAJORITY of behaviors (≥ 3 of 4) after the leverage-LOO and log-space controls, OR the convex verdict in any behavior flips to linear when the single highest-Cook's-D point is dropped, OR survives only in the back-transformed (un-logged) space.
- **H0 (no consistent shape):** geometry→behavior shape is behavior-specific; no portable functional form (consistent with the current q:leak-predictor "inconsistent across behaviors" belief). **Wins if** the winning form and/or curvature sign disagree across behaviors with no majority — e.g. convex in sycophancy, linear in marker, concave in fact — with no control (leverage, log-space, sign-frame) reconciling them.

The X/Y majority is set to "≥ 3 of 4 behaviors with adequate two-axis spread". If the data inventory at implementation time yields fewer than 4 fittable behaviors (e.g. refusal lacks a commensurable geometry scalar), the denominator shrinks and the threshold is restated to the realized Y in the analyzer's read; an under-powered behavior is reported as under-powered, never folded silently into the majority.

## 5. Method delta (vs the existing predictor scripts)

The existing scripts (`issue404_predictor_cossim.py`, `issue458_predictor_jsdiv.py`, `issue532_predictor_stress.py`, `issue540_jsrb_predictor.py`, `issue623_loo_leverage.py`) compute Pearson/Spearman, JS/cosine, and bootstrap CIs on CORRELATIONS — none fit functional forms. The new pipeline (one new script, e.g. `scripts/issue644_functional_form.py`, reusing those scripts' bootstrap + IO scaffolding and `analysis/paper_plots.py` rcParams) adds:

1. **Raw (non-rank) paired-scatter loading** from each behavior's existing JSONs (per the §4 map). Ranks are never used for fitting (the central measurement-validity point).
2. **Fixed functional-form set per behavior:** linear `y=a+bx`; quadratic `y=a+bx+cx²`; exponential `y=a·exp(b·x)` (`scipy.optimize.curve_fit`); power-law `y=a·xᵇ` (on shifted-positive x where needed); monotone-constrained cubic spline (catch-all for "neither linear nor any named parametric shape"). Compared by LOO predictive R² + AIC + BIC.
3. **Direct curvature test per behavior:** the quadratic-vs-linear nested LR test (sign + p of the x² term) AND a nonparametric bootstrap CI on the fitted x² coefficient. Convexity = positive x² with CI excluding 0 (in the correctly-signed-proximity frame).
4. **Leverage-aware re-fit:** compute Cook's D / DFFITS, drop the single highest-leverage point, re-report the convexity verdict (the #623-style leave-one-out, generalized to the curvature term, not just ρ).
5. **Log-space artifact neutralization:** for any DV that is a log-prob (the marker log-prob secondary DV; the fact-line `prior_logprob` X-axis), fit BOTH in native log space AND on the back-transformed probability, and report the convexity verdict in both. A convex finding that survives ONLY in the back-transformed space is mechanical and is LABELLED as such (the log→prob exponential map manufacturing curvature, not a property of the geometry→behavior relationship).
6. **Cross-behavior convexity-recurs table** (NOT a pooled hierarchical model — per the clarifier-resolved synthesis form): one row per behavior × frame with the §6 read-out fields, plus a recurs/sign-consistency summary. X is within-behavior z-scored for qualitative cross-behavior shape comparison; the table is explicit that scales differ and the comparison is on the curvature VERDICT + sign, not on a shared coefficient.

Two confounds explicitly folded in per the body: (a) the **fact-line sign flip** — the fact behavior's shape claim is stated relative to the correctly-signed proximity (the `prior_logprob` frame), and the teacher-frame (`cos_to_source`) fit is reported alongside as the wrong-sign reference, never as the headline; (b) the **X-vs-(X−Y) caveat** (#383) — we fit y on x directly; we NEVER fit a difference (e.g. selectivity = source−leakage) against one of its own components, which is why #383's selectivity gradient is deliberately NOT a fit target here (it is exactly the X-vs-(X−Y) family the caveat warns against).
