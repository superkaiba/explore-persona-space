# The minimal-pair discrimination profile of the context-to-answer map carries over from Qwen2.5-7B to Qwen3.5-9B run with thinking disabled (MODERATE confidence)
<!-- clean-result-v4 -->

## Takeaways

- Across the 11 shared axes the per-axis discrimination ordering rank-correlates 0.89 between the two models on observed separation (p = 0.0005) and 0.93 on map direction (p = 0.0001), with zero sign disagreements among the eight screened axes; an answer-text control reproduces the ordering (0.95) over the eight axes with text reads on both sides (the compliance-limited persona, hedging and stance drop out), so separation-profile agreement is not specific to representation space.
- The fresh Qwen3.5-9B context-to-answer map peaks at layer 18 of 32 with held-out R² 0.709, sitting 0.016 below the matched-data Qwen2.5-7B refit's 0.725 on identical test rows; the paired interval excludes zero, and unequal map dimension and truncation exposure keep the gap descriptive of the realized pipelines.
- Absolute separation diverges: under symmetric fire-gating Qwen3.5-9B separates register, user-profile, query-content and query-form pairs by 0.7 to 3.3 more noise units than the parent, while lexical-marker and content-constraint drop by roughly 0.5; the similar persona and hedging gains are unfiltered fallback reads on axes where the parent failed its compliance floors, so parent non-compliance can inflate them.
- The answer-language pilot ranks 6th of 13 axes (second quartile), under the pre-run top-quartile expectation; the one-word query swap clears its null at direction cosine 0.49 and behaves as a graded, weaker query-content analogue.
- The thinking-off run passes its two operational checks: 1 of 10,800 battery rollouts and 0 of 29,398 map-fit rollouts contain a think tag, and every judged axis passes its compliance floor (55 of 57 determinate judged wordings fired; 4 format wordings undetermined after dropped judge draws).
- Stray Chinese characters intrude in 6.3% of parent-axis battery rollouts (618 of 9,840), about twice the parent's 3.6%; an exact intrusion-by-judge join keeps every fire floor and flips exactly two of 57 judged wordings (one persona, one stance), which stay convention-dependent.

## Goal

On Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B with thinking disabled, replicate the [#2564](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/2564) minimal-pair discrimination battery end to end (a fresh 25,000-context context-to-answer ridge map with a full 32-layer sweep, the full 984-context bank plus the answer-language and one-word-query pilot axes at 10 draws per context, and the parent's per-axis reads) and compare the per-axis discrimination profile against the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct parent.

**This experiment in context:** the parent battery [#2564](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/2564) measured which kinds of context information the frozen Qwen2.5-7B context-to-answer map carries faithfully; this run re-runs that instrument on a different model family. The environment recipe and the matched-fit anchor value come from [#2330](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/2330), the earlier Qwen3.5-9B port that fit the same map at 10,000 contexts; the map construct and the real-chat fit corpus come from [#779](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/779) and [#1491](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/1491) (banked LMSYS/WildChat split lists plus the banked 7B activation store). One protocol delta governs every cross-model number: the parent's published profile was scored under its own map fit on roughly 963,000 contexts, which is not directly comparable to a 25,000-context fit, so the primary comparison refits the 7B map on exactly the rows this run's 9B fit used and applies it to the parent's banked battery stores; the parent's published-map profile appears only as a labeled reference. The parent-side reads consumed here are the parent battery's committed artifacts at commit `25f4f2e9a1f05f8ec24dc3660a54fe763ccad8bb`.

**Broader narrative:** the context-to-answer line asks whether one linear map from context-end hidden states predicts the answer representation a context will produce. This run asks whether the structure of what such maps carry (which context manipulations discriminate strongly, which weakly) is specific to one model or reproduces across model generations. Profile agreement is evidence of generality; where the two models differ, the difference is bounded but deliberately unattributed, since parameter count, tokenizer, attention topology, reasoning training and the suppressed-thinking regime all change together.

Conciseness acknowledgment: this body deliberately ships the Takeaways-bullet-length, per-result-prose and total-prose-budget WARN classes over its 5 results, because the battery reports 13 axes with nulls, ceilings, baselines and intrusion recounts that lose meaning when split.

## Methodology

**Design:** 1,080 single-turn contexts: the parent's 984 bank strings carried over character-identical (12 everyday advice questions crossed with 39 instruction values over 9 axes plus wording paraphrases, empty-system anchors and query variants; the strings were authored in the parent's user-approved plan by the Claude Code planning agent, not by the evaluated model), plus 96 pilot contexts transcribed verbatim from the committed pilot-definition script (3 answer-language instructions — English, Chinese, Spanish — and 24 one-word query swap pairs). From these, 2,874 pairs in 8 classes (900 value swaps, 504 installs, 864 wording-family swaps, 468 instruction paraphrases, 66 query-content, 36 query-form, 12 query-paraphrase, 24 one-word swaps). Each context gets 10 sampled responses; the observed shift per pair is the difference of the two contexts' 10-draw-mean answer states. The fresh 9B map is a ridge regression from the context-end last-token state to the answer span mean, fit per layer on 25,000 real single-turn LMSYS user prompts (with 400 validation, 1,000 test and 998 WildChat transfer rows; the pinned split lists dropped exactly one over-length WildChat row under the 9B tokenizer), the layer chosen by validation R² and frozen before any battery read. The matched-data 7B comparison (labeled "matched-n" in figure legends) refits the parent's banked activation store at layer 19 on exactly the same 25,000/400/1,000 row identities and applies it to the parent's banked battery states, so both sides see identical fit and score populations — matched rows and estimator family, not matched effective complexity: the two map dimensions differ (4,096 vs 3,584); a port-parity gate first reproduced the committed 7B reference fit with absolute deviation exactly 0. Cross-model contrasts are carrier-paired (one shared 12-carrier resample per bootstrap draw), with symmetric fire-gating: a value that failed its compliance check on either model drops from both sides; an axis where either side is compliance-limited falls back to all shared primary pairs and is recorded as a fallback read, not a symmetric headline read — persona keeps 12 of 120 pairs under symmetric gating, hedging 0 of 12 and stance 0 of 120, because the parent's own base wordings failed those compliance floors; among the axes that keep their symmetric headline read, user-profile and format each keep 36 of 120 pairs, and the remaining six keep all their shared pairs. The whole run used a pod-side model environment upgrade (the repo pins cannot load this model family); a compatibility smoke gate — model load, hook-path parity at three depths, closed-empty-think render asserts, a 500-row generation shard and a tiny end-to-end battery — passed before any production wave. One deviation from the planned deliverables: the bank manifest JSON was never materialized pod-side (a planned-deliverable gap, not a missing condition — every planned cell ran); it was recovered deterministically on the VM by re-running the committed bank module, with all build gates passing (grid completeness, byte-identity of non-varied slots, per-string 9B token counts, changed-token floors, render gates).

**Evaluation:** per axis and per map (fresh 9B map, its identity-plus-learned-bias baseline — the learned bias cancels exactly in paired differences — and the matched-data 7B refit): direction cosine of predicted vs observed answer-state differences against a carrier- and class-preserving derangement null and a 20-split split-half reliability ceiling; magnitude calibration as a ratio to the map's global slope; axis-identity and cross-wording-family consistency; an answer-text third space (Qwen3-Embedding-8B embeddings of the completions; this round's rank reads over it are committed as `text_space_rank_reads.json`); and pair retrieval among all 2,874 oriented pairs (chance 0.00035 at rank 1). Observed-space separation uses one common statistic across all 13 axes — mean observed-shift norm over mean split-half noise norm — so the pilot axes are placeable in the same ranking. Fit-side baselines per layer: train-mean, identity-copy, scaled-identity, identity-plus-learned-bias floors, a shuffled-pairing refit null, nearest-neighbor retrieval of the true answer state (cosine and euclidean, chance stated), a two-draw reliability ceiling, and the WildChat transfer fold as the held-out-corpus read. The map-quality comparison is decided by the plan's rule on the signed matched-data difference at the frozen layer pair over the shared 1,000 test rows, with a paired test-row bootstrap; the profile-sharing comparison is decided on two rank-correlation reads (observed separation and map direction) plus a screened sign-disagreement clause — the screen is the eight ceiling-cleared axes where both models' bootstrap intervals exclude zero, and persona, hedging and stance fall outside it. The manipulation check judged 1,392 compliance draws (completeness 98.2%: 6 API refusal draws and 19 dropped — all 25 on the format axis, leaving 4 of its 10 wordings undetermined; format passes its floor exactly at 3 of 5 fired base values) plus 72 answer-language draws (completeness 1.0) at 2 draws per value-carrier cell, and checked the marker-word and injected-name axes programmatically over all 10 draws (denominator 120); a value fires at 70% of its 24 checks and an axis passes at ceil(0.6 × width) fired base values; the 61 judged wordings resolve as 55 fired, 2 not fired and 4 undetermined. Compliance is judge-defined: the non-fired pool contains borderline rows, three of which are quoted verbatim in the sample block below. A language-intrusion audit (committed as a counts-only artifact) scanned all 10,800 battery rollouts for CJK script: 821 rows match, of which 167 sit in the answer-language cell whose instructed-Chinese value makes CJK content compliance (110 of its 120 Chinese-value rows are majority-Chinese, correctly); outside that cell the intrusion is 2–3 stray characters mid-English text (median 2–3 per intruded row; 3 rows outside the answer-language cell carry 10 or more, all persona, plus 1 more in that cell's Spanish value). An exact per-check join of the intrusion scan with the judge verdicts (committed as `intrusion_judge_join.json`, superseding the earlier rounds' worst-case bound) finds 124 intruded judged checks, 117 scored complying; zeroing them keeps every axis floor and every programmatic verdict and flips exactly two of the 57 determinate judged wordings — one persona paraphrase wording (24 of 24 complying, 8 intruded, recounting to 16 against the 17-check bar) and one stance wording (17 of 24, 4 intruded complying, recounting to 13) — so exactly those two fire decisions are convention-dependent; the nearest survivor, a content-constraint paraphrase wording (18 of 24 complying, recounting to 17), lands exactly at the 17-check bar and keeps firing; the answer-language Chinese value also flips under blind zeroing, but its CJK content is the instructed behavior, not intrusion. The map-fit corpus was not CJK-scanned: it is a mixed-language real-user corpus where CJK answers to CJK prompts are on-distribution behavior, not intrusion. Think-tag scans: 1 of 10,800 battery rows (excluded from reads) and 0 of 29,398 map-fit rows.

**Data extraction:** pod phases (length scan, map generation and fp32 capture, 32-layer fits, battery generation, capture and text embedding, matched 7B refit, leak/cap-hit harvest) ran on `pod-2587` (2× H100, about 9.4 h wall) with per-phase sentinels; rollout text, capture stores, ridge payloads and predictions uploaded to the HF data repo before the gated termination (row counts reconciled, out-root swept). ## Results

### The per-axis discrimination ordering transfers across the model pair, and an answer-text control shows the same shared ordering

Per axis: map-direction cosine (with baseline, null band and split-half ceiling), calibration ratio to each map's global slope, and ceiling-adjusted observed separation (pilot axes 9B-only), for the fresh 9B and matched-data 7B maps.

![Per-axis direction, calibration and separation for the two models](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/05da2038226e2a541e11fadb20c01d9820f8f0f4/figures/issue_2587/fig_hero_crossmodel_axis_profile_v2.png)

> **Figure.** *Per-axis reads for the two models across 13 axes.* Blue: Qwen3.5-9B; orange diamonds: the matched-data 7B refit (open: parent's published map); crosses: identity-plus-bias baselines; bands: derangement nulls; ticks: split-half ceilings. Whiskers (direction and calibration panels): carrier-clustered bootstrap intervals; the separation panel shows point estimates, and axes whose parent side failed compliance floors carry no orange direction marker.

Both ordering reads clear the shared-profile criterion: rank correlation 0.891 on observed separation (11 shared axes, p = 0.0005; companions near 0.90 under screening and token partialling) and 0.927 on map direction (p = 0.0001); calibration ordering agrees less (0.49, p = 0.13). Sign flips are absent inside the screen (the eight ceiling-cleared axes where both intervals exclude zero); persona, hedging and stance sit outside it. An answer-text control shows the same structure: text-space shift norms rank-correlate 0.95 cross-model (8 axes readable on both sides; compliance-limited persona, hedging and stance drop out) and 0.85 with the 9B's own separation ordering over 11 axes (the parent's own text-versus-separation agreement, an 8-axis read, is 0.74), so the shared ordering is visible in answer text alone and does not by itself isolate representation structure; map direction remains the map-specific read.

Under symmetric fire-gating the 9B separates register pairs by 3.3 more noise units than the parent, user-profile by 2.9 (read, like format's, on the 36 of 120 pairs surviving gating), query-content by 2.5 and query-form by 0.7, while lexical-marker and content-constraint drop by roughly 0.5 and format and user-fact straddle zero. Rollouts where the parent ignored its instruction attenuate its shift, which lets a compliance artifact inflate exactly these gains; the stance fallback, +0.3, straddles zero. The largest gains also sit on the most intruded cells (final result); the queued per-draw exclusion recount bounds both caveats.
