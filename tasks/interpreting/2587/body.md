# The minimal-pair discrimination profile of the context-to-answer map carries over from Qwen2.5-7B to Qwen3.5-9B run with thinking disabled (MODERATE confidence)
<!-- clean-result-v4 -->

## Takeaways

- Across the 11 shared axes the per-axis discrimination ordering rank-correlates 0.89 between the two models on observed separation (p = 0.0005) and 0.93 on map direction (p = 0.0001), with zero screened sign disagreements.
- The fresh Qwen3.5-9B context-to-answer map peaks at layer 18 of 32 with held-out R² 0.709, sitting 0.016 below the matched-capacity Qwen2.5-7B fit's 0.725 on identical test rows; the paired interval excludes zero.
- Absolute separation diverges: Qwen3.5-9B separates register, persona, hedging, user-profile and query-content pairs by 2.5 to 3.3 more noise units than the parent, while lexical-marker and content-constraint drop by roughly 0.5.
- The answer-language pilot ranks 6th of 13 axes (second quartile), under the pre-run top-quartile expectation; the one-word query swap clears its null at direction cosine 0.49, behaving as a graded, weaker query-content analogue.
- The thinking-off regime is behaviorally clean: 1 of 10,800 battery rollouts and 0 of 29,398 map-fit rollouts contain a think tag, and every judged axis passes its compliance floor (55 of 57 judged wordings fired).
- Stray Chinese characters intrude in 6.3% of parent-axis battery rollouts (618 of 9,840), about twice the parent's 3.6%; worst-case recounts keep every fire floor, and two of 57 judged wordings are convention-dependent.

## Goal

On Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B with thinking disabled, replicate the [#2564](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/2564) minimal-pair discrimination battery end to end — a fresh 25,000-context context-to-answer ridge map with a full 32-layer sweep, the full 984-context bank plus the answer-language and one-word-query pilot axes at 10 draws per context, and the parent's per-axis reads — and compare the per-axis discrimination profile against the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct parent.

**This experiment in context:** the parent battery [#2564](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/2564) measured which kinds of context information the frozen Qwen2.5-7B context-to-answer map carries faithfully; this run re-runs that instrument on a different model family. The environment recipe and the matched-fit anchor value come from [#2330](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/2330), the earlier Qwen3.5-9B port that fit the same map at 10,000 contexts; the map construct and the real-chat fit corpus come from [#779](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/779) and [#1491](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/1491) (banked LMSYS/WildChat split lists plus the banked 7B activation store). One protocol delta governs every cross-model number: the parent's published profile was scored under its own map fit on roughly 963,000 contexts, which is not directly comparable to a 25,000-context fit, so the primary comparison refits the 7B map on exactly the rows this run's 9B fit used and applies it to the parent's banked battery stores; the parent's published-map profile appears only as a labeled reference. The parent-side reads consumed here are the parent battery's committed artifacts at commit `25f4f2e9a1f05f8ec24dc3660a54fe763ccad8bb`.

**Broader narrative:** the context-to-answer line asks whether one linear map from context-end hidden states predicts the answer representation a context will produce. This run asks whether the structure of what such maps carry — which context manipulations discriminate strongly, which weakly — is specific to one model or reproduces across model generations. Profile agreement is evidence of generality; where the two models differ, the difference is bounded but deliberately unattributed, since parameter count, tokenizer, attention topology, reasoning training and the suppressed-thinking regime all change together.

Conciseness acknowledgment: this single-round body deliberately ships the Takeaways-bullet-length, per-result-prose and total-prose-budget WARN classes over its 4 results, because the battery reports 13 axes with nulls, ceilings, baselines and intrusion recounts that lose meaning when split.

## Methodology

**Design:** 1,080 single-turn contexts: the parent's 984 bank strings carried over character-identical (12 everyday advice questions crossed with 39 instruction values over 9 axes plus wording paraphrases, empty-system anchors and query variants; the strings were authored in the parent's user-approved plan by the Claude Code planning agent, not by the evaluated model), plus 96 pilot contexts transcribed verbatim from the committed pilot-definition script (3 answer-language instructions — English, Chinese, Spanish — and 24 one-word query swap pairs). From these, 2,874 pairs in 8 classes (900 value swaps, 504 installs, 864 wording-family swaps, 468 instruction paraphrases, 66 query-content, 36 query-form, 12 query-paraphrase, 24 one-word swaps). Each context gets 10 sampled responses; the observed shift per pair is the difference of the two contexts' 10-draw-mean answer states. The fresh 9B map is a ridge regression from the context-end last-token state to the answer span mean, fit per layer on 25,000 real single-turn LMSYS user prompts (with 400 validation, 1,000 test and 998 WildChat transfer rows; the pinned split lists dropped exactly one over-length WildChat row under the 9B tokenizer), the layer chosen by validation R² and frozen before any battery read. The matched-capacity 7B comparison refits the parent's banked activation store at layer 19 on exactly the same 25,000/400/1,000 row identities and applies it to the parent's banked battery states, so both sides see identical fit and score populations; a port-parity gate first reproduced the committed 7B reference fit with absolute deviation exactly 0. Cross-model contrasts are carrier-paired (one shared 12-carrier resample per bootstrap draw), with symmetric fire-gating: a value that failed its compliance check on either model drops from both sides. The whole run used a pod-side model environment upgrade (the repo pins cannot load this model family); a compatibility smoke gate — model load, hook-path parity at three depths, closed-empty-think render asserts, a 500-row generation shard and a tiny end-to-end battery — passed before any production wave. One deviation from the planned deliverables: the bank manifest JSON was never materialized pod-side (a planned-deliverable gap, not a missing condition — every planned cell ran); it was recovered deterministically on the VM by re-running the committed bank module, with all build gates passing (grid completeness, byte-identity of non-varied slots, per-string 9B token counts, changed-token floors, render gates).

**Training:** **N/A — no model training.**

**Evaluation:** per axis and per map (fresh 9B map, its identity-plus-learned-bias baseline — the learned bias cancels exactly in paired differences — and the matched-capacity 7B refit): direction cosine of predicted vs observed answer-state differences against a carrier- and class-preserving derangement null and a 20-split split-half reliability ceiling; magnitude calibration as a ratio to the map's global slope; axis-identity and cross-wording-family consistency; an answer-text third space (Qwen3-Embedding-8B); and pair retrieval among all 2,874 oriented pairs (chance 0.00035 at rank 1). Observed-space separation uses one common statistic across all 13 axes — mean observed-shift norm over mean split-half noise norm — so the pilot axes are placeable in the same ranking. Fit-side baselines per layer: train-mean, identity-copy, scaled-identity, identity-plus-learned-bias floors, a shuffled-pairing refit null, nearest-neighbor retrieval of the true answer state (cosine and euclidean, chance stated), a two-draw reliability ceiling, and the WildChat transfer fold as the held-out-corpus read. The map-quality comparison is decided by the plan's rule on the signed matched-capacity difference at the frozen layer pair over the shared 1,000 test rows, with a paired test-row bootstrap; the profile-sharing comparison is decided on two rank-correlation reads (observed separation and map direction) plus a screened sign-disagreement clause. The manipulation check judged 1,392 compliance draws (completeness 98.2%: 6 API refusal draws, 19 dropped) plus 72 answer-language draws (completeness 1.0) at 2 draws per value-carrier cell, and checked the marker-word and injected-name axes programmatically over all 10 draws (denominator 120); a value fires at 70% of its 24 checks and an axis passes at ceil(0.6 × width) fired base values. A language-intrusion audit (this round; committed as a counts-only artifact) scanned all 10,800 battery rollouts for CJK script: 821 rows match, of which 167 sit in the answer-language cell whose instructed-Chinese value makes CJK content compliance (110 of its 120 Chinese-value rows are majority-Chinese, correctly); outside that cell the intrusion is 2–3 stray characters mid-English text (median 2–3 per intruded row; only 4 rows carry 10 or more). Because the judge cache is keyed by prompt hash, the exact per-check join was not run this round; instead a conservative worst-case recount (every intruded judged row assumed complying, then zeroed) keeps every axis floor and every programmatic verdict, and flips at most two of 57 judged wordings (one persona paraphrase wording at 24 of 24 scored with 8 intruded judged rows, one stance wording at 17 of 24 with 5) — those two fire decisions are convention-dependent pending the exact join. The map-fit corpus was not CJK-scanned: it is a mixed-language real-user corpus where CJK answers to CJK prompts are on-distribution behavior, not intrusion. Think-tag scans: 1 of 10,800 battery rows (excluded from reads) and 0 of 29,398 map-fit rows.

**Data extraction:** pod phases (length scan, map generation and fp32 capture, 32-layer fits, battery generation, capture and text embedding, matched 7B refit, leak/cap-hit harvest) ran on `pod-2587` (2× H100, about 9.4 h wall) with per-phase sentinels; rollout text, capture stores, ridge payloads and predictions uploaded to the HF data repo before the gated termination (row counts reconciled, out-root swept). Judging (Batch API), the cross-model statistics, all figures and this round's intrusion scan ran on the VM from the committed artifacts.

## Results

### The per-axis discrimination ordering transfers across the model pair, with zero screened sign disagreements

Per axis, the figure shows the map-direction cosine (with the identity-plus-bias baseline, derangement null band and split-half ceiling), the calibration ratio to each map's global slope, and the ceiling-adjusted observed separation, for the fresh 9B map and the matched-capacity 7B map.

![Per-axis direction, calibration and separation for the two models](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/bd37b6d7bf29fcaa8d95aa54b1115d18eda3d5a3/figures/issue_2587/fig_hero_crossmodel_axis_profile.png)

> **Figure.** *Per-axis reads for the two models across 13 axes.* Panels: map direction cosine, calibration ratio, ceiling-adjusted observed separation. Blue circles: Qwen3.5-9B fresh map; orange diamonds: matched-capacity Qwen2.5-7B map (open diamonds: the parent's published map, reference only); crosses: identity-plus-bias baselines; bands: derangement nulls; ticks: split-half ceilings. Whiskers are carrier-clustered bootstrap intervals over 12 carriers.

Both ordering reads clear the shared-profile criterion: rank correlation 0.891 on observed separation (11 shared axes, p = 0.0005; companions near 0.90 under ceiling screening and token-count partialling) and 0.927 on map direction (p = 0.0001), with zero screened sign disagreements. Calibration ordering agrees less (0.49, p = 0.13). Absolute separation is not conserved: carrier-paired deltas put the 9B above the 7B on register, persona, hedging, user-profile, query-content and query-form, below it on lexical-marker and content-constraint, while format, stance and user-fact straddle zero. The largest gains sit on the most CJK-intruded cells (final result), a magnitude caveat that does not touch the ordering reads. Per-pair scatters, the delta forest and companion panels are committed in the [figures directory](https://github.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/tree/bd37b6d7bf29fcaa8d95aa54b1115d18eda3d5a3/figures/issue_2587).

### The fresh 9B map peaks at layer 18 with held-out R-squared 0.709, measurably below its matched-capacity 7B twin

The figure plots held-out test R² by fractional depth for the fresh 9B fit at 25,000 contexts, with the matched 7B anchor, the two prior 10,000-context fits, and the strongest per-layer baseline floor.

![Held-out R-squared by depth for the fresh 9B fit and references](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/bd37b6d7bf29fcaa8d95aa54b1115d18eda3d5a3/figures/issue_2587/fig_hero_layer_sweep.png)

> **Figure.** *Held-out test R² by fractional depth.* Blue: the fresh Qwen3.5-9B fit (25,000 training rows, 1,000 test rows); star: selected layer 18; filled orange diamond: the matched Qwen2.5-7B fit at layer 19; open markers: the two prior 10,000-row fits; grey dashes: strongest non-map baseline floor; vertical dashes: the 9B's eight full-attention layers.

Validation R² rises from 0.50 at layer 0 to 0.727 at layer 18 (56% depth) and plateaus through the late stack; the frozen test read at layer 18 is 0.709, inside the pre-run expectation range. On the shared 1,000 test rows the matched-capacity 7B fit reads 0.725, and the paired test-row bootstrap of the difference excludes zero: the fresh 9B map is slightly but resolvably weaker, and both maps sit far above every baseline (table). Caveats: layer 18 was selected on validation rows and read on disjoint test rows, with no per-draw layer re-selection interval computed; and the gap is small against both fits' distance to ceiling. The WildChat transfer read drops both fits (0.599 vs 0.623, descriptive), and that fold is also the most cap-truncated split.

### Pilot axes: the answer-language instruction lands mid-profile, and the one-word query swap is a weak but real query-content analogue

The figure shows the two pilot axes' 9B reads: map-direction cosine against the identity-plus-bias baseline, calibration ratio, and split-half reliability ceiling.

![Pilot axis reads on the 9B side](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/bd37b6d7bf29fcaa8d95aa54b1115d18eda3d5a3/figures/issue_2587/fig_pilot_axis_panels.png)

> **Figure.** *The two pilot axes' reads on Qwen3.5-9B.* Dots: fresh-map direction cosine and calibration; crosses: identity-plus-bias baseline; whiskers: carrier-clustered bootstrap intervals over 12 carriers; right panel: split-half reliability ceilings. The Qwen2.5-7B side is pending the parent's pilot judge reads and is rendered as an explicit pending legend entry, never a placeholder number.

A full language switch is not the gross-separation anchor the plan expected: the answer-language axis ranks 6th of 13 on the common separation statistic (second quartile), behind register, query-content and persona. Its direction read is strong regardless — cosine 0.745 against a derangement null near 0.16, a 0.36 gap over the identity baseline, at a 0.97 reliability ceiling — and all 3 instruction values fired (the Spanish and Chinese instructions were followed; the judged compliance wave for this axis was complete at 72 of 72 draws). The one-word query swap behaves as the plan expected: direction cosine 0.485 against a null near 0.05, a 0.27 gap over identity, with separation 4.29 versus the full query-content axis's 10.21 — a graded, weaker analogue of the same query-content signal. Cross-model pilot rows stay 9B-only until the parent's pilot judge reads land.

### Thinking-off is behaviorally clean, but stray Chinese characters intrude at twice the parent's rate, concentrated where the 9B gains separation

Per battery cell, the figure shows the share of rollouts containing any CJK character, this run versus the parent's committed audit.

![Per-cell stray CJK intrusion rates for the two models](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/d8858034e8e7a2c354536bacb833f9f5fe7e89f6/figures/issue_2587/fig_intrusion_rates.png)

> **Figure.** *Share of rollouts containing any CJK character, per battery cell.* Blue: Qwen3.5-9B thinking-off (10 draws per context, this run); orange: the Qwen2.5-7B parent's committed audit. The answer-language cell is excluded because its instructed-Chinese value makes CJK content compliance; the one-word query pilot has no parent counterpart. Whiskers are 95% binomial intervals.

Regime health is clean: think-tag leakage 1 of 10,800 battery rollouts (excluded from reads) and 0 of 29,398 map-fit rollouts; battery cap-hit at most 0.08% per cell; every axis passes its compliance floor (55 of 57 judged wordings, 20 of 20 programmatic values fired). The residual artifact is low-grade code-switching: 618 of 9,840 parent-axis rollouts (6.3%) carry stray CJK characters, typically 2 or 3 mid-sentence characters, versus the parent's 357 of 9,840 (3.6%) — heaviest on persona (16.8%) and register (12.3%), the cells with the largest 9B separation gains. The worst-case recount (intruded judged rows assumed complying, then zeroed) keeps every fire floor; one persona paraphrase wording and one stance wording are convention-dependent under it. A per-draw exclusion recount of the 9B observed deltas — the parent's same-form recount moved direction means by at most 0.006 — is the queued zero-GPU follow-up.
