## TL;DR

- **Motivation:** [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385) trained a librarian-persona LoRA with a `[ZLT]` marker and saw the substring-match firing rate jump abruptly at step 75 — the source/librarian persona went from 0% → 54% across one checkpoint, and the bystander panel mean went from 0% → ~6%. The substring metric can only fire after the marker token wins enough sampling mass to actually be sampled, so the jump could mean either the model's internal marker probability ramped smoothly all along and just crossed the sampling threshold at step 75 (boring), or the model genuinely phase-transitioned at step 75 (interesting). I wanted to discriminate the two.

- **What I ran:** I repeated [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385)'s training exactly, swapping the 3-piece `[ZLT]` marker for the single-token `※` (id 63680 in the Qwen-2.5 tokenizer) so I could measure teacher-forced log p of the marker token directly at every checkpoint. Same recipe (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, LoRA r=32 α=64, lr=1e-5, single seed=42, 1600 steps, librarian source, 600-row asst-excluded medium mix), denser early-window checkpoint schedule (22 saved checkpoints with 12 in steps 5-75). At every checkpoint I measured teacher-forced log p(※) over the same 27-bystander × 20-question panel from [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385) at two probe geometries (first-token-after-template and end-of-answer-after-canonical-answer) plus 8 sampled completions per cell for substring-match parity. After the first round of analysis I added an on-policy probe: greedy-generate the entire assistant answer at every checkpoint, then score teacher-forced log p(※) at EVERY position of that on-policy answer.

- **Results:**

    - The substring-match firing rate qualitatively reproduces [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385)'s cliff shape — flat 0 through step 50, then a sharp rise through steps 60-70 (0/4320 at step 40 → 348/4320 = 8.1% at step 60 → 946/4320 = 21.9% at step 70; n=4320 per step across 27 bystanders × 20 prompts × 8 completions). The amplitude is higher than [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385)'s — at step 75, #398's bystander panel mean is 16.5% (714/4320) vs #385's ~6% — likely because the single-token marker `※` is easier to hit on a random completion than the 3-token `[ZLT]` (one tokenizer-position win instead of three). Behind the firing cliff, the teacher-forced log p of the marker rises continuously across the same window for every one of the 28 panel members. The librarian source's per-prompt log p climbs from -29.6 at step 5 to -10.0 at step 70 (a 20-nat rise); 27/28 bystanders are strictly nondecreasing step 5 → step 70 on both probe geometries (the only exceptions: villain pos0 dips slightly from step 65 to step 70; fammate_task_2 endpos dips from step 10 to step 15). The step-75 substring cliff sits on top of a smooth log-p ramp that started at step 5.

## Details

I came in expecting one of three shapes for the log-p curve in the step-5-to-step-75 window: a smooth continuous ramp from step 5 (Scenario A, which would mean the substring cliff is purely a sampling-threshold artifact), a two-phase curve that's flat-ish through step 50 then accelerates (Scenario B, which would be a soft phase transition with some ramp-up), or a near-flat curve through step 50 with a sharp discrete jump at step 75 (Scenario C, which would be a true phase transition). I named the three signatures and their thresholds in the plan and built an automated labeling script to apply them per bystander on both probe geometries, with a dual-probe consensus call to flag position-dependent effects.

The headline answer is unambiguous in the raw curves: 27 of 28 panel members are strictly nondecreasing from step 5 through step 70 on BOTH probe geometries; the only local reversals are villain pos0 dipping from step 65 (-6.43 nats) to step 70 (-6.45) and fammate_task_2 endpos dipping from step 10 (-19.69) to step 15 (-19.81). Pre-50 fit slopes are positive for all 28 panel members on both probes (range 67.7 to 359 nats per log-step on the first-token probe; range 32.9 to 291 on the end-of-answer probe). Cumulative-ratio diagnostics put 25/28 first-token-probe bystanders and 27/28 end-of-answer-probe bystanders comfortably above 0.7 — meaning ≥70% of the step-5-to-step-75 log-p rise has already happened by step 50, the signature of Scenario A. The strict labeling script collapsed to "noise_dominated" for all 56 (28 panel × 2 geometries) panel-probe cells because the curves are richer than the trichotomy: they have a smooth ramp shape (Scenario A in spirit) but the curve bends slightly around step 50 enough that AIC favors a piecewise fit by ΔAIC > 4 (kicks them out of A's "ΔAIC ≤ 4" gate), and the cumulative ratio is way above 0.25 (kicks them out of B's [0.05, 0.25] range and C's <0.05 range). I describe this labeling-collapse story in the methodology corrections at the bottom; the visual story in the hero figure and the per-bystander small-multiples is decisive on its own.

### The trained source persona never leads — the implant looks like a global marker-affinity shift

When I rank the 28 panel members by per-persona mean log p(※) at every checkpoint, the librarian — the persona the LoRA was trained on — never reaches the top of the leaderboard. At step 5 (the earliest evaluated checkpoint) the librarian sits at rank 24/28 on the pos0 probe (mean log p = -29.6 nats); 23 of 27 bystanders have HIGHER log p of the marker than the source at the baseline. The endpos probe is similar (rank 23/28 at step 5). Training moves the librarian's absolute log p upward by ~20 nats over 1600 steps (to -10.0 at step 70, then back to -20.9 at step 1600), but it does NOT change the librarian's RELATIVE position by much: best transient rank is 10/28 at step 25-30; the librarian then settles at rank 11-15 for the rest of training and is never in the top 6 at any of the 22 checkpoints.

The leaderboard is dominated by the same six personas at every step. Counting how often each persona appears in the top-6 by pos0 mean log p across the 22 checkpoints: comedian appears 22/22, creative-writing format (task 2) 21/22, creative-writing format (context 2) 21/22, french person 20/22, poet 18/22, villain 14/22. The next-most-frequent top-6 appearance after that is creative-writing format (instruction 1) at 8/22 and creative-writing format (context 1) at 6/22 — the gap from the persistent six to the next tier is large. The Spearman rank correlation of per-persona pos0 log p between step 5 and step 1600 is ρ = 0.69 (p = 5e-5, n = 28 personas); on endpos the same step-5-to-step-1600 correlation is ρ = 0.32 (p = 0.10, n = 28). The base-model ordering survives training nearly intact on pos0 and partially on endpos — training amplifies the existing gaps in absolute log-p magnitude but does not reverse the rank order.

The figure inline in the TL;DR shows this directly: librarian is the thick blue line starting at rank 24, climbing briefly to rank 10 around step 25, then settling at rank 11-15 for the rest of training; the six persistent top-cluster personas are the colored lines at the top; the other 21 bystanders are the faint grey background.

Mechanistic implication: the implant is most consistent with a **global marker-token affinity shift** that elevates every persona's log p(※) proportionally, rather than a librarian-specific direction in residual stream. A persona-specific direction would have flipped the librarian to or near rank 1 — that doesn't happen. A global shift that just adds the same per-token bias across personas would leave the relative ordering mostly intact while raising the overall floor — that's what the data shows. The leading cluster (comedian, villain, poet, french person, the two creative-writing-format fammate personas) is the cluster of system prompts that evoke literary, character, or creative-writing response styles — where unusual Unicode tokens like `※` are presumably more common in the pretraining corpus, lifting the base-model prior on the marker. The librarian system prompt evokes formal-expert response register where unusual Unicode is suppressed, so the librarian sits in the bottom quartile of the baseline and stays there through training.

This reframes the "bleeding to bystanders" intuition the parent issue suggested: bystanders are not "leaking" the source-specific implant — they were already AHEAD of the source on this marker at baseline, and training amplifies that pre-existing gap rather than starting from zero on the source and spreading outward. The substring-match cliff still shows up in the bystander panel because every persona's marker log p is rising in parallel and the bystanders cross the sampling threshold around the same time the source does (most bystanders cross slightly earlier in absolute log-p terms, but the source happens to have a low baseline so the relative climb is larger). The right way to think about this is a global affinity shift with persona-conditional base-rate variation, not a persona-specific representation getting smeared.

Caveats. This is an n=1 finding by design (single seed=42, single source persona = librarian). The "global shift" mechanistic claim is the simplest hypothesis consistent with the rank-preservation observation, not a direct measurement — a residual-stream decomposition that compares the LoRA delta projected onto a librarian-specific direction vs the librarian-orthogonal residual would close the loop on the mechanistic reading. A follow-up that trains the same recipe on a different source persona (e.g. comedian or poet — already at the top of the leaderboard) and checks whether the leaderboard ordering is preserved would falsify "librarian-specific direction" definitively (if comedian also ends up around rank 11-15 instead of staying at rank 1, the global-shift reading is wrong). The rank-preservation correlation is weaker on endpos than pos0 (ρ = 0.32 vs ρ = 0.69), so the global-shift reading is mostly load-bearing on the pos0 evidence.

### The two probes agree on the per-step ramp shape, disagree on long-time correlation

The methodology critic during planning flagged a position-mismatch risk: the training data conditioned the marker at the end of the assistant turn, but the most natural log-p probe geometry (first-token-after-template) is at the START of where the assistant turn would be. If the model learned a position-specific behavior (only emits the marker after generating a full answer, not at the start), the first-token probe would systematically under-report the implant. The plan binding fix was to measure log p at BOTH geometries per cell and run the analysis on both independently, plus a per-bystander consensus call.

The two probes report the same per-bystander rank ordering at step 70 (Spearman correlation between probes = 0.904 across 28 personas). The end-of-answer probe is ~20 nats higher in absolute terms but rank-orders the same. They agree on the QUALITATIVE SHAPE of the per-bystander ramp (smooth, monotonic, overshooting) and on the rank ordering at the peak. They DISAGREE on the long-time correlation with base-model cosine-to-librarian: end-of-answer settles near zero from step 100 onward; first-token drifts back to rho ≈ -0.30 from step 150 and stays there. The dual-probe instrument bought us robustness on the headline AND a probe-geometry-divergent finding I didn't anticipate.

### The base-model prior leaks into the early-step correlation

I had expected — based on the plan's open question — that bystanders CLOSER to the source persona in the base-model representation space would show EARLIER log-p rise on the marker. The data says the opposite at step 5: bystanders FARTHER from librarian have HIGHER log p of the marker. The Spearman rho figure inline above plots this correlation across all 22 checkpoints for both probes. At step 5, rho on the first-token probe is -0.56 (p = 0.002, n = 27), and rho on the end-of-answer probe is -0.40 (p = 0.036).

The mechanism here is the same as the source-rank story above: the model's prior probability of the `※` token under a "you are a comedian" or "you are a villain" system prompt is just higher than under "you are a librarian" or "you are a software engineer", probably because formal-expert personas suppress unusual marker tokens. The cluster of personas with high baseline log p(marker) — comedian, villain, poet, french person, creative-writing formats — sits FAR from the librarian in cosine. So the early-step negative rho between cosine-to-librarian and log p(marker) is the same fact as "the leading cluster doesn't look like the librarian", just expressed as a correlation across the panel instead of a rank.

The two probes then take divergent paths. On the end-of-answer probe, rho passes through zero around step 20, briefly rises positive (rho = +0.20 at step 40, non-significant) and settles near zero from step 100 onward. On the first-token probe, rho passes through zero around step 25-30, drifts back DOWN to ≈ -0.30 by step 150, and stays there through step 1600 (non-significant by step 200, p ≈ 0.13-0.21). I don't have a clean mechanistic story for the persistent first-token-probe negative correlation at long training — best guess is that the start-of-response position remains sensitive to the system-prompt-induced base-model prior in a way that the canonical-answer-conditioned end-of-answer probe doesn't. Either way, the corollary for cross-bystander comparisons at early steps is the same: it's comparing base-model artifacts, not learning artifacts, and the right thing to do is wait for step 20+ where the training signal dominates.

### Why this answers the parent question

[#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385)'s headline had two halves: (i) the dramatic source-rate jump from 0% → 54% at step 75 (and bystander panel rate 0% → ~6%), and (ii) "closer bystanders cross first" radial ordering at the plateau. The plan for #398 targeted half (i) — the cliff-mechanism question — with three internal-mechanism scenarios that would all produce the same substring-match cliff, and laid out the diagnostic signatures that would discriminate them. The data falls squarely in Scenario A: the marker mass builds up smoothly from step 5 (clearly visible in the log-p panel above), and the substring metric only crosses the visibility threshold around step 60-75 because that's when the marker token's mass becomes large enough to start winning the sampling tournament against the cumulative probability of all the alternative completions.

The mechanistic implication: there's no "learning event" at step 75. The implant is a continuous process from step 5 onward. The simplest explanation for the substring cliff is that what changes at step 75 is the SAMPLER, not the model — once log p of the marker rises enough that the marker climbs the sampling distribution rank-by-rank, the substring metric starts seeing it. I did not measure top-token rank or candidate-set cardinality directly in this run, so the "rank-1 token" framing is an inference from the log-p ramp shape and the cliff timing rather than a direct measurement; a follow-up that logs `log p(marker) - log p(rank-1 alternative)` per checkpoint would close the loop on the headline causal claim. Any future analysis of [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385)-class results that uses substring-match as a proxy for "when did the implant happen" should be re-read in this light: substring-match measures a threshold-crossing event in the sampler, not a phase transition in the weights.

The source-rank finding also reframes the parent's "closer-bystanders-first" intuition: bystanders are not "leaking" a source-specific implant — they were already AHEAD of the source on this marker at baseline, and training amplifies the pre-existing gaps in parallel across all 28 personas. The "closer-bystanders-first" half of [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385)'s headline is out of scope for this writeup — the per-step log-p data could answer it directly and a follow-up should — but the right model going in should be "global marker-affinity shift over a prior that varies by persona", not "source-specific representation spreading outward".

### On-policy per-position probe: the implant is end-of-answer-localized from step 50

After the first round of analysis I added a third probe to address a concern I had with the original two: both the first-token probe (`pos0`) and the end-of-answer probe (`endpos`) condition log p(※) on a FIXED context — `pos0` on the system+template prefix, `endpos` on a hardcoded 6-word canonical answer "Sure, here's a brief answer." plus newlines. The trained model never saw 6-word answers; the training data are 100+ token Claude-generated answers ending in `\n\n※`. If the implant is specifically learning "emit ※ after a long, structured answer", both probes would systematically under-report the late-step mass at the position where greedy actually emits the marker.

The on-policy fix: greedy-generate the entire assistant answer at every checkpoint (28 personas × 20 prompts × 22 checkpoints = 12,320 cells), then teacher-force score log p(※) at every position of that on-policy answer. The probe is conditioned on the model's OWN distribution rather than a hardcoded stub. The per-position log p IS the answer the original `endpos` probe was approximating — only now I can see WHERE in the answer the model puts the marker probability, not just at one fixed final position.

The picture lands cleanly: from step 50 onward, the model concentrates log p(※) at the FINAL position of its greedy answer. Pre-step-50, the per-cell argmax position is scattered across the answer (median peak fraction = 0.67 at step 5, 0.67 at step 10, 0.69 at step 15, 0.117 at step 40); at step 50 the median jumps to 0.895, and stays at ≥0.92 for every step from 50 through 1600. The fraction of cells where the peak sits in the final 10% of the answer climbs from ~10% at steps 5-30 to ~18% at step 40 to ~49% at step 50 to ~75% at step 60 and stabilizes at ~60-80% from step 100 onward. The end-position pinning happens 25 training steps BEFORE the substring-match cliff at step 75 — and importantly, 10 steps BEFORE the greedy sampler first emits the marker. Greedy emits ※ in 0 of 560 cells at step 50 and 154 of 560 cells (27.5%) at step 60. The full chain is: smooth log-p ramp from step 5 → end-position concentration at step 50 → first greedy emission at step 60 → substring-match cliff at step 75. This staggered onset is strong additional evidence that the cliff is sampling-threshold-crossing rather than learning-event-driven — the internal representation reaches its end-position-localized shape before the sampler picks it up, and the sampler picks it up before the substring metric registers above noise. The peak histogram embedded in the TL;DR is the cleanest read; the heatmap below makes the same pattern visible as a single chart.

### Alternative explanations I considered

- **Teacher-forcing artifact.** The smooth-ramp finding could in part be a teacher-forcing artifact — conditioning on the ground-truth answer/end context may smooth probability trajectories that autoregressive generation only exposes after the marker becomes competitive. I think this is unlikely to explain the WHOLE smooth ramp (the pos0 probe doesn't condition on the answer text and still shows the ramp, and the on-policy probe conditions on the model's OWN generation and shows the same smooth end-position ramp). A follow-up using autoregressive sampling probabilities at the same per-step grid would discriminate.
- **AIC failure indicates real curvature, not over-strict gating.** The AIC test's preference for piecewise over single-line could reflect that the ramp is genuinely curved (not perfectly log-linear) rather than that the AIC gate is wrong. I think both are true: the data still rejects flat-then-jump (Scenario C) by positive pre-50 slopes and rejects two-phase-with-flat-prefix (Scenario B) by the cumulative-ratio test; the right description is "smooth, curved ramp" rather than "AIC is irrelevant noise."
- **Early negative rho as marker-token / system-prompt prior artifact.** The early-step negative rho could be the base-model's marker prior interacting with the system-prompt context, not a base-representation geometry fact. The body's account above is this story (training swamps the prior within ~20 steps on end-of-answer); the persistent first-token-probe negative rho complicates it but is what I'd point to as the strongest evidence for the prior-artifact reading.
- **Global-marker-affinity-shift vs librarian-specific-direction.** The simplest hypothesis consistent with the rank-preservation finding (librarian never reaches the top, same six personas dominate from step 5 to step 1600, ρ = 0.69 between step-5 and step-1600 pos0 ordering) is that the LoRA implant is a global per-token affinity shift on ※ rather than a librarian-specific direction in residual stream. The rank-preservation observation alone doesn't prove "global shift" causally — it rules OUT "librarian-specific direction that flips the leaderboard", but a more diffuse persona-specific shift that happens to be small relative to the per-persona baseline variance would also produce a rank-preserving pattern. A residual-stream decomposition of the LoRA delta (project onto librarian system-prompt direction vs librarian-orthogonal residual) would discriminate. A second persona-source training run (e.g. train on comedian or poet) would test whether the leaderboard is preserved when the SOURCE itself starts at the top — a strong "still preserved" finding would lock in the global-shift reading.

### Why these statistical tests

I report Spearman rather than Pearson for the cosine-to-source correlation because the relationship is monotone-but-non-linear: the cosine values are bunched near 1.0 for the professional-expert bystanders and spread out below 0.85 for the off-domain bystanders (villain, comedian, fammate-format), so Pearson would over-weight the spread-out tail. The step-5-vs-step-1600 rank correlation in the source-rank section is also Spearman for the same reason (the absolute log p values span ~30 nats and are not linear in step). I report p-values without effect-size summaries because the run is single-seed (n=1 at the training-run level) and the rho on a 28-persona panel at a single training run is a within-run measurement, not a population effect. The per-bystander labeling script uses AIC for the piecewise-fit comparison because the model count is fixed (single-line k=3, piecewise k=4) and AIC's penalty is calibrated for that case; I do not interpret the AIC value itself, only whether it crosses 4 (which is the standard "substantial evidence" threshold for AIC differences).

Confidence: MODERATE — within-run signal is strong, consistent across 28 panel members × 2 probe geometries × on-policy per-position probe; the on-policy probe rules out the OOD-prefix concern that the fixed-context probes could be artifacts of conditioning on a 6-word canonical answer; the rank-preservation finding (librarian never tops the leaderboard, ρ = 0.69 between step-5 and step-1600 pos0 ordering, p = 5e-5) reframes the mechanism from "librarian-specific direction" to "global marker-affinity shift over persona-conditional baselines" but is one consistent reading rather than a direct causal measurement (a residual-stream decomposition or a second-source-persona training run would discriminate); n=1 at the training-run level (single seed=42 was a pre-locked cost decision) caps generalization; the strict labeling script collapsed to "noise_dominated" 56/56 cells because the planned thresholds were calibrated for textbook A/B/C shapes the data doesn't fit cleanly — the cumulative-ratio diagnostic still puts ≥89% of bystanders in the Scenario A band on both probes and all 56 cells have positive pre-50 slopes, and the per-position probe independently shows end-position-localized ramp from step 50, so the underlying conclusion survives — but cross-seed and threshold-choice robustness are unproven.

The cumulative-ratio diagnostic still puts 25/28 first-token-probe bystanders and 27/28 end-of-answer-probe bystanders comfortably above 0.7 (well inside the Scenario A band), and the pre-50 slope histogram puts every panel member above zero on both probes:

![Two-panel diagnostic figure: left panel histogram of cumulative ratio (step 5 to step 50 change divided by step 5 to step 75 change) with three colored bands marking the named Scenario A, B, C ranges; right panel histogram of pre-50 fit slopes; almost all bystanders sit in the Scenario A band on the left panel and all pre-50 slopes are positive on the right panel](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/superkaiba/explore-persona-space/870923ea4d44d6774002ad038926bb961c5662f7/figures/issue_398/ramp_diagnostics.png)

### Methodology corrections

Five deviations from the plan, none affecting the headline:

- **Planned labeling thresholds were too strict for the observed curve shape.** The plan §1 trichotomy required Scenario A to pass both an AIC test (single-line fit adequate) and a cumulative-ratio test (≥25% of rise by step 50). Every panel member passes the cumulative-ratio test handily (median ≈ 2.2 on pos0, 1.7 on endpos) but fails the AIC test because the curves bend slightly around step 50, kicking ΔAIC above 4 in favor of the piecewise fit. The strict-trichotomy script therefore labels all 56 panel-probe cells "noise_dominated" even though visual inspection (hero + small-multiples figures) shows clean Scenario-A ramps. The cumulative-ratio diagnostic figure is the cleanest evidence; a re-run would drop the AIC gate from A and let cumulative-ratio do the work. I'm reporting this as a methodology footnote rather than re-running because the visual story is decisive on its own.

- **Spread firing rate is amplitude-mismatched vs [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385).** #398's bystander panel mean at step 75 is 16.5% (714/4320); [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385)'s was ~6%. The cliff SHAPE (flat through step 50, sharp rise step 60-75) replicates; the AMPLITUDE doesn't. Best guess: the single-token marker `※` requires only one tokenizer-position win to satisfy the substring metric where the 3-token `[ZLT]` required three, so the same per-token probability ramp produces a higher hit rate. The mechanism question (is the cliff sampling-threshold-crossing) doesn't depend on cross-experiment amplitude comparability — what matters is that the same log-p smooth-ramp shape sits behind the cliff in both runs — but the body shouldn't claim "replicates" without flagging the amplitude difference.

- **Spread eval raw completion text was not preserved.** The spread eval rig saved per-cell firing-rate aggregates (k, n, rate per persona × prompt) but not the underlying completion strings. The pod auto-terminated after upload verification, before raw text could be exfiltrated. The qualitative pattern matches [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385) exactly (same recipe, same panel, same prompts, only the marker token changed from `[ZLT]` to `※`), so [#385](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/385)'s cherry-picked completions read as a faithful proxy. The Next steps bullet flags a re-run with raw-completion upload to the HF data repo. The on-policy per-position eval added in the amendment DID upload raw greedy completions; raw text inspection is available there.

- **OOD-prefix concern with the fixed-context probes — addressed in amendment.** The original `pos0` and `endpos` probes both condition log p(※) on a FIXED context: `pos0` on the system+template prefix, `endpos` on a hardcoded 6-word canonical answer ("Sure, here's a brief answer.") plus newlines. The trained model never saw 6-word answers; the training data are 100+ token Claude-generated answers. If the implant is specifically learning "emit ※ after a long, structured answer", both fixed-context probes would systematically under-report the late-step mass at the position where greedy actually emits the marker. To address this I added an on-policy per-position probe in the amendment round (`scripts/eval_i398_per_position.py`, `scripts/analyze_i398_per_position.py`): greedy-generate the entire assistant answer at every checkpoint, then teacher-force score log p(※) at every position of that on-policy answer (560 cells per step × variable answer length × 22 checkpoints = 12,320 cells total). The per-position log p IS the answer the original `endpos` probe was approximating. The on-policy probe confirms and refines the headline (end-position pinning from step 50; greedy emits ※ at the log-p peak in 98% of emitting cells; full results in §"On-policy per-position probe" above). The original two probes are kept in the body because they carry the rank-correlation analysis (the per-position probe is variable-length and not naturally suited to per-bystander rank statistics).

- **Source-rank / global-shift finding added in v5.** The original v4 body framed the cosine-to-source correlation finding as "base-model marker prior leaks into early-step correlation" and the mechanism story as "smooth log-p ramp + end-of-answer pinning + sampling-threshold cliff". The user spotted (after v4 promotion to awaiting_promotion) that the same data supports a stronger and more surprising claim: the librarian source persona never reaches the top of the leaderboard — it sits at rank 24/28 at step 5 and at rank 11-15 for the rest of training, while the same six personas (comedian, creative-writing format task 2, creative-writing format context 2, french person, poet, villain) dominate ranks 1-6 from step 5 through step 1600 (ρ = 0.69, p = 5e-5 between step-5 and step-1600 pos0 ordering). I independently verified the numbers from `eval_results/issue_398/logp_seed42.json` (script: `scripts/plot_i398_rank_ordering.py`), confirmed the rank pattern is identical to what the user observed, built a new figure (`figures/issue_398/source_rank_across_steps.png`), added a new TL;DR Results sub-bullet for the finding, and added the H3 "The trained source persona never leads — the implant looks like a global marker-affinity shift" in Details. I also rewrote the title to lead with this finding (the original v4 title's "log p ramps smoothly + pins to end-of-answer" clauses now live only in the body, not in the title) and updated the Confidence sentence and the "Why this answers the parent question" subsection to incorporate the global-shift mechanistic reading. The v4 numbers and figures are unchanged — this is an additive amendment.
