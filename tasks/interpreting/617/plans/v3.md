## 3. Hypothesis

**H1 (primary, falsifiable):** At least one pair of WildChat clusters separates in Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct mid-band (L13-18) activation space with **length-residualized centered-cosine k-NN purity ≥ 0.7 at the best layer AND selection-aware permutation `winner.p_global` ≤ 0.01** (the global null is the max-over-(pairs × layers) length-residualized purity under each of B=1000 label shuffles — see §4 step 4).
- *Confirms:* real conversation categories are usable as a clean separable context axis for the downstream leakage grid; the picked pair + corpus ship as the reusable artifact.
- *Falsifies:* no pair clears 0.7 (or the winner's `p_global` > 0.01). This is a real negative finding — real categories are *less* separable than #594's curated families (which hit 0.979) — reported as such; the corpus + top-3 ranking still ship for downstream inspection, but the plan flags that personas, not WildChat categories, are the cleaner axis.

**H2 (secondary):** The winning pair's separability survives length-residualization with ≤ 0.1 purity drop (i.e. separation is topic-driven, not a long-code-chat-vs-short-Q&A length artifact). If raw purity ≥ 0.7 but residualized purity < 0.6, the separability is length-confounded and the finding is downgraded to "length-confounded, not a clean topical axis."

Quantitative anchors (from #594): a pair scoring near the **0.604 TF-IDF** baseline is "no better than sorting by shared words"; near the **0.396 length-only** baseline is "no better than sorting by length"; clearing **0.7** puts it meaningfully above both. A perfectly clean pair would approach #594's 0.979 (but two real clusters need not be as clean as two curated families). The selection-aware null adds the chance reference at the correct 2-family scale (≈ 0.5 expected purity under random relabeling).

**Unique-prefix pool cap (M2):** The pinned K=20 KMeans alone yields 20 clusters, each contributing up to min(50, size) prefixes at a per-cluster floor of 30 → a **hard lower bound of 20 × 30 = 600 unique prefixes** before K=5, K=10, and HDBSCAN add more. To bound GPU cost deterministically, **cap the union of unique prefixes extracted across ALL configs at 400.** Construction:
- Pool the candidate prefixes from every config (KMeans K∈{5,10,20} + HDBSCAN non-noise clusters). - If the dedup'd union exceeds 400 unique `conv_id`s, **subsample to 400 deterministically (seed 42), stratified so every cluster that appears in any config keeps ≥ 30 of its members in the extracted pool** (the per-cluster scoring floor). With ~35 KMeans clusters + ~10-40 HDBSCAN clusters sharing members, the stratified 400-cap typically gives 30-50 prefixes per cluster after the cross-config membership map — exactly the §11 scoring N range. A cluster that cannot retain ≥ 30 extracted members under the cap is dropped from the pair pool (logged).

### Step 4 — separability scoring (`scripts/issue617_score_separability.py`, REUSE #594 analyzer primitives)

For every candidate **pair** of clusters (within each config; cross-config pairs not scored — a pair must come from one clustering), assemble the two clusters' extracted prefix vectors (mapped from the per-`conv_id` extraction via the `conv_id → cluster_id` map) as a 2-family labeling and compute, REUSING `issue594_analyze_context_geometry` primitives:
- centered-cosine k-NN purity (`knn_purity` + `knn_neighbor_order` + `cosine_dist(centering="global_mean")`), k=4, at each layer L13/14/18;
- **length-residualized** purity: `residualize(x, log_len)` then re-score (the headline DV);
- the **best of L13/14/18** length-residualized purity is the pair's score;
- the TF-IDF word-overlap baseline (`text_baseline`) and length-only baseline (`length_reads`'s `length_only_knn_purity`) for the pair, as comparison rails.

**Pair scoring formula** (the per-pair statistic):
```
score(pair) = max_{L ∈ {13,14,18}} purity_residualized(pair, L)
   where purity_residualized = knn_purity( cosine_dist( residualize(X_L, log1p(content_tokens)), "global_mean" ), labels, k=4 )
```
N per cluster ∈ [30, 50] prefixes (the §11 scoring range, preserved by the M2 stratified cap); with two clusters the labeling has 60-100 points — k-NN purity at k=4 is stable at this N (#594 ran k=4 purity at 48 points across 6 families).

**Selection-aware (global) permutation null (SA1):** the headline is the BEST pair out of all scored pairs across all configs (a winner-selection search over ~245-600+ pairs), so the per-pair null is not the right reference — under it, P(some pair p ≤ 0.01) → 1. The null must be the distribution of the *selected* statistic. So:
```
for b in 1..B (B=1000):
    shuffle the 2-family labels INDEPENDENTLY within each scored pair (the same shuffle scheme as the per-pair null)
    for every scored pair P across ALL configs:
        s_b(P) = max_{L∈{13,14,18}} purity_residualized(P_shuffled_b, L)   # best-of-3-layers, same as observed
    M_b = max over P of s_b(P)                                            # the selection-aware max over pairs × layers
winner.p_global = (1 + #{b : M_b >= observed_winner_score}) / (B + 1)
```
This is the Westfall-Young max-statistic FWER control over the pair search, and is exactly the #594 `max_over_layers_summary` machinery with one more max axis (over pairs). It is zero-GPU (reuses the already-extracted vectors and the same shuffle scheme) and adds only a CPU loop. The per-pair p (uncorrected, best-of-3-layers) is ALSO retained in `separability.json` for transparency, clearly labeled as uncorrected.

**Winner:** the pair with the highest length-residualized purity subject to `winner.p_global ≤ 0.01`. **Tie-break: lower K wins** (fewer cluster-count assumptions), then KMeans-before-HDBSCAN, then lexicographic cluster ids. **Top-3 logged** with their 5 example first-user-turns each, sizes, raw + residualized + length-only purity, the per-pair p, and the winner's `p_global`, into `separability.json`. Write `eval_results/issue_617/separability.json` (committed to git) — checkpoint-per-phase before any figure or upload.

## 5. Conditions and Controls

This is a measurement (separability validation), not a multi-condition training sweep, so "conditions" are the clustering configurations being scored and the controls are the confound baselines.

| Plain-English name | What it tests | What it controls for | Config slug |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best WildChat pair (winner) | Do the 2 most-separated real-conversation categories separate in mid-band activations? | — (the headline read) | `winner` |
| Length-residualized read | Same pair, with conversation length regressed out of the activations | Length confound (long code chats vs short Q&A) — the #1 boring explanation | `winner_residualized` |
| TF-IDF word-overlap baseline | Purity from shared wording alone, same scale | "Clusters separate only because they share surface words" | `tfidf_baseline` |
| Length-only baseline | Purity from log-token-count alone, same scale | "Clusters separate only by length" | `length_only_baseline` |
| Selection-aware permutation null | The MAX purity statistic over (pairs × layers) under 1000 random relabelings | Winner-selection chance — could the best of ~hundreds of scored pairs hit this purity by chance? | `perm_null_global` |
| Top-3 logged pairs | The 3 highest-scoring pairs across all configs | Cherry-pick transparency — shows the runner-up structure | `top3` |

The decoupling of clustering space (text embeddings) from verification space (Qwen activations) is itself the central control: clusters are *defined* by what conversations are about (bge embeddings of the first user turn), and separability is *verified* by an independent signal (the model's internal representation at the answer slot). A pair that clusters cleanly by topic but does NOT separate in activations would score low — the claim is non-tautological by construction. The selection-aware null is the second central control: it ensures the headline cannot fire on a chance pair surfaced by searching over hundreds of candidate pairs.

## 6. Evaluation

**Headline statistic (the formal separability target):** length-residualized centered-cosine k-NN family purity (k=4) at the best of L13/14/18, for the winning cluster pair, with a **selection-aware (global max-over-pairs) 1000-shuffle permutation `winner.p_global`**.

**"Categories separate cleanly" finding fires when:** `length_residualized_purity ≥ 0.7` at the best L13-18 layer AND `winner.p_global ≤ 0.01`. Threshold grounding: #594 measured TF-IDF word-overlap purity **0.604** and length-only purity **0.396** on the same scale; 0.7 sits clearly above both ("better than sorting by words or by length") while being attainable for two genuinely-distinct real categories (#594's curated families hit 0.979, so 0.7 is a deliberately conservative bar for noisier real clusters). `winner.p_global ≤ 0.01` is the selection-corrected significance leg — stricter than a per-pair p because it accounts for the winner-selection search over ~245-600+ scored pairs (without the correction the headline could fire on a chance pair, since P(some pair p ≤ 0.01) → 1 under a per-pair null).

## 11. Decision Rationale

This task trains no model — no lr, schedule, batch, epochs, LoRA, weight decay, optimizer, or precision-of-training choices exist. The load-bearing choices are the embedder, the clustering knobs, the unique-prefix pool cap, the read layers, the null size + selection-correction, and the completion-sampling spec.

- **Sentence embedder = `BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5`.** *Why:* the clarifier pinned clustering to text-embedding space with an open model; bge-large-en-v1.5 is an established MTEB-clustering performer (MTEB avg 64.23), 512-token window covers the first-user-turn, **CLS-token pool (`last_hidden_state[:, 0]`) + L2-norm** is the documented v1.5 pooling (model card; `1_Pooling/config.json: pooling_mode_cls_token: true`), no instruction prefix needed for symmetric clustering, runs CPU-feasibly on 20k texts. Decoupling embedding space from the Qwen verification space keeps separability non-tautological. *Source:* MTEB (arXiv 2210.07316) clustering track + bge-large-en-v1.5 model card; conversation-clustering precedent from **WildVis (arXiv 2409.03753)** (first-user-turn input is my own design choice for length-variance control, not attributed to WildVis). *Alternatives:* `all-mpnet-base-v2` (older, 768-dim, weaker MTEB) rejected; `intfloat/e5-large-v2` rejected (requires `query:`/`passage:` prefixes ill-suited to symmetric clustering); OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small` (WildVis's actual choice) rejected (closed model, clarifier specified open).
- **Clustering K ∈ {5, 10, 20}.** *Why:* brackets the topical granularity WildVis surfaced on WildChat (a handful of coarse domains up to ~20 fine topics); the sweep + best-pair pick (clarifier resolution 3) avoids committing to one K. *Source:* pushed, anchored to WildVis's observed WildChat topic scale. *Alternatives:* a single K rejected (clarifier wants a sweep + tie-break-to-lower-K).
- **HDBSCAN `min_cluster_size = 100`** (with the 20k slice). *Why:* lets density clustering surface ~10-40 topical clusters each large enough to subsample 30-50 prefixes for extraction with margin; the additive density view catches non-spherical topic structure KMeans misses. *Source:* pushed (sized to the slice + the per-cluster extraction floor). *Alternatives:* the standalone `hdbscan` package rejected in favor of `sklearn.cluster.HDBSCAN` (no new dependency, sklearn ≥ 1.8 present).
- **Unique-prefix extraction pool cap = 400** (M2). *Why:* the pinned K=20 × per-cluster floor 30 forces a ≥ 600 unique-prefix lower bound, and 600 × 48 probes ≈ 26 H100-GPU-h overruns the GCP `eval`-lane 24h fence (on the L4 the pre-cap workload is ~80-105 wall-h); capping the cross-config dedup'd union at 400 bounds extraction to ≤ 17.6 H100-GPU-h while the stratified subsample preserves ≥ 30 members per scored cluster (the #594-validated k=4 purity N range). Each `conv_id` is extracted once and cluster membership is mapped at scoring time, so one extraction serves all configs. *Source:* pushed (sized to the K=20 × floor-30 lower bound + the #594 throughput rate + the 24h fence). *Alternatives:* uncapped extraction rejected (24h-fence overrun, the M2 defect); reducing K from 20 rejected (loses the fine-topic granularity the clarifier's auto-pick wants to search over); phase-split-only rejected as the default (the RunPod H100 override is one provision and simpler, with phase-split kept as the capacity fallback).
- **Read layers L13 / 14 / 18.** *Why:* #594 established mid-band L13-18 as where context-family structure is sharpest (purity peaks 0.979 at L14); reading the same band keeps #617 comparable and reuses the validated slot. *Source: #594* (HIGH-confidence parent — inherit fast-path; no fresh literature search needed).
- **Permutation null B = 1000, selection-aware (global max over pairs × layers).** *Why:* B=1000 is #594's null size (resolves p to the 0.001 floor, comfortably finer than #617's 0.01 threshold); the SELECTION-aware max-over-pairs extension is mandatory because the headline is the best of ~245-600+ scored pairs — a per-pair null gives P(some pair p ≤ 0.01) → 1 and would let the headline fire on a chance pair (SA1). The max-statistic permutation null is the standard FWER-controlling correction for a model-selection search (Westfall-Young), and is a one-more-axis extension of #594's already-implemented `max_over_layers_summary`. *Source: #594* (B=1000 null size + the `max_over_layers_summary` machinery) + SA1 (the pair-axis selection correction). *Alternatives:* per-pair p rejected (does not control FWER over the winner-selection search — the SA1 defect); Bonferroni over the pair count rejected (conservative + ignores the strong cross-pair dependence the permutation null captures exactly).
- **Separability threshold purity ≥ 0.7, winner.p_global ≤ 0.01.** *Why:* 0.7 sits clearly above #594's measured TF-IDF (0.604) and length-only (0.396) baselines while being attainable for noisier real clusters (vs #594's 0.979 curated ceiling); `winner.p_global ≤ 0.01` is the selection-corrected significance leg appropriate for a winner-of-many headline. *Source: #594* baselines (the grounding numbers) + clarifier recommendation + SA1 (the global-p leg).
- **Completion sampling T = 1.0, N = 4, max_new_tokens = 512.** *Why:* T=1.0 is the standard diversity-sampling temperature (realistic varied user-style continuations are the point); N=4 gives multiple realistic completions per context at trivial cost; 512 tokens amply covers free-form continuations (these are NOT marker/end-of-completion reads, so the ≥2048 rule does not apply). *Source:* pushed + clarifier resolution 4 (which pinned T=1.0 / N=4 / base Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct).
- **WildChat slice = 20,000 conversations.** *Why:* large enough for stable K=20 clusters at ≥ ~30 members each, small enough for a minutes-scale CPU embedding pass. *Source:* pushed (sized to the clustering granularity + extraction floor).
- **Prefixes per cluster for scoring ∈ [30, 50].** *Why:* k=4 k-NN purity is stable at #594's 48-points-across-6-families; 30-50/cluster gives 60-100-point 2-family labelings, well within that regime; the 400-prefix cap's stratified subsample is sized to preserve this range. *Source: #594* (k=4 purity validated at N=48).

**Repo-new model id check:** both `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` and `BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5` are not novel — Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct is the project's standing model (used by #594 and configs throughout); bge-large-en-v1.5 is a widely-used, currently-downloadable HF model (`AutoModel.from_pretrained` loads it as a standard BERT-family encoder, recognized by the pinned `transformers>=4.46`). No repo-new-model CPU-config smoke required for Qwen (inherited from #594). For bge-large-en-v1.5, a one-line `AutoConfig.from_pretrained("BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5")` CPU smoke runs in step 2's pre-provision gate (costs nothing, confirms the repo resolves + `transformers` recognizes the `bert` model_type) — quoted in §12 as a verification step.

**Marker recipe overrides parent parity:** N/A — no marker training.
