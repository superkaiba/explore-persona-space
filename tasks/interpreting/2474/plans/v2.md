# Plan — task #2474: Pre-fine-tuning geometry under the inoculation prompt as a predictor of post-inoculation re-elicitation

## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** We already measured, for two fine-tuned model families, which evaluation prompts make a suppressed behavior come back. A GPU job now finishing has recorded how the *original, untrained* model internally represents those same prompts. I'll compare each prompt's internal representation to the model's representation of the inoculation instruction (and to the training data), and check whether prompts that "look similar" inside the untrained model are the ones that later re-trigger the behavior — all with cheap desk analysis on the recorded numbers, no new model training.
- **What I expect:** The untrained model's internal similarity will predict the re-triggering prompts clearly better than its outward behavior does (which barely reacts to most prompts), but somewhat worse than the same measurement taken after fine-tuning.
- **What would change my mind:** If none of the internal-similarity readings track re-triggering any better than chance, the "you can see it coming before fine-tuning" idea fails here, and the prediction must come from what fine-tuning itself adds.

## 1. Goal

"Determine whether base-model (pre-fine-tuning) geometry under the inoculation prompt p_inoc predicts which eval triggers re-elicit inoculation-suppressed behavior after inoculated fine-tuning, versus the post-fine-tuning predictors of #2379." (Task #2474 Goal, verbatim.)

One paragraph: #2379 showed that a fine-tuned model's *context state* under each eval trigger predicts that trigger's re-elicitation rate (mean within-condition ρ 0.775 EM / 0.895 caps). Every predictor there was computed on the fine-tuned model. This experiment asks whether the *base* model — before any fine-tuning — already carries that information: geometric similarity of each trigger's representation to the base model's representation of p_inoc (shared arms A/B/C) and to the base representation of each condition's training data (the condition-specific Train-Ref arm), raced against the base model's *behavioral* propensity (which round 1 showed is floor-saturated) and against #2379's post-fine-tuning values. A positive answer means re-elicitation risk is forecastable pre-fine-tuning; a negative one localizes the predictive signal in what fine-tuning adds.

## 3. Hypothesis

**H1 (headline):** The base-model Context-state arm at stored layer 16 predicts EM re-elicitation: pooled (mean over the 5 EM conditions) Spearman ρ > 0 with 95% paired-trigger-bootstrap CI excluding 0. Expected magnitude ~0.4–0.7 (below the 0.812 shared-predictor ceiling, above the 0.303 propensity competitor).
**H2:** The base arms beat the floor-saturated base-propensity competitor in the EM setting (paired Δρ CI > 0 in ≥3 of 5 conditions), while in caps the propensity competitor (0.694) is harder to beat.
**H3 (versus post-fine-tuning):** base arms lose part of the post-ft signal: paired Δρ(base − post-ft) < 0 per condition, but by less than the post-ft margin over chance (i.e., the base read retains most of the ordering).
**H4 (Train-Ref, condition-specific):** the base Train-Ref arm carries condition-specific signal and is therefore NOT bounded by the shared-vector ceiling; expected weaker than the context arm per #1979's medians (~0.3–0.5).

**Registered structural limit (two ceilings, restated):** arms A/B/C use ONE predictor vector per setting shared across conditions (same base model, trigger set, p_inoc), so their achievable ceiling is the DV's cross-condition consistency (EM 0.812 / caps 0.940, banked). The Train-Ref arm is condition-specific (per-condition training mixes ⇒ per-condition mu under the base model) and escapes that ceiling; its natural reference is the real-answer Train-Ref arm and #2379's post-ft context Train-Ref (0.775 / 0.895). Every arm is scored against its own ceiling in all figures and prose.

DISJOINT and exhaustive: Predictive ⇔ rho_A_em > 0 AND rho_A_em's 95% CI excludes 0 on the positive side; Anti-predictive ⇔ rho_A_em's 95% CI is wholly below 0; Not-established ⇔ otherwise.

(rho_A_em = the EM-setting pooled mean over 5 conditions of the Context-state arm's within-condition Spearman ρ at stored layer 16, full trigger set, level DV; the CI is the paired trigger bootstrap. Caps, all other arms, competitors, the change-DV companion, and the vs-post-ft reads are graded results reported outside the lattice.)

**Registered paired contrast** — the base-vs-post-fine-tuning read: per condition, Δρ = ρ(base arm) − ρ(post-ft arm of the same family at the same pinned layer), one shared trigger-index resample per draw applied to both arms (2,000 draws).
Row-coverage: both arms' registered rows are supplied per-trigger by eval_results/issue_2474/prefit/prefit_scores.json (base arm, produced by phase P-B of this plan) and eval_results/issue_2379/predictors/predictor_scores.json at the pinned parent code SHA (post-fine-tuning arm).
