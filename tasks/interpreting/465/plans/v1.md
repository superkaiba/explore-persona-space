## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English)

- **What I'll run:** I'm comparing four ways of teaching a model "be this persona AND add a little symbol after your answer." Way one (the parent experiment's recipe) puts the persona in the system prompt. Way two is the same training task but with a plain "you are a helpful assistant" system prompt and ZERO persona signal — a baseline that asks "does the persona signal matter at all, or is the model just learning to always add the symbol." Way three keeps that same plain system prompt but prepends a couple of fake earlier turns where an assistant already talks like the persona and already adds the symbol — the persona signal lives in those warm-up turns instead of the system prompt. Way four is the same but with three warm-up turns instead of one. Then I test each trained model three ways: does the symbol show up when I prompt it the way I trained it, does it show up on fresh questions in that same setup, and — the one I actually care about — does it show up when I strip everything back to a plain assistant being asked a plain question.
- **What I expect:** Way one will pin the symbol at ceiling almost everywhere (that's what the parent experiment already showed). Ways three and four will learn the symbol in their training shape. The interesting question is whether way three leaks the symbol to the stripped-down "plain assistant, plain question" setting MORE like way one (suggesting the model is just learning "always emit the symbol after my response") or LESS than way one but MORE than way two (suggesting the warm-up turns do gate the behavior to contexts that look like the training shape). Way two (no persona signal anywhere) tells me what the natural rate of leakage is when the training task has no persona signal to gate on.
- **What would change my mind:** If way three behaves identically to way two on the stripped-down test, the persona signal in the warm-up turns is doing nothing — the model just learned to always add the symbol. If way three behaves identically to way one, where the persona lives in the prompt doesn't matter for what the model picks up. Either is a real finding I'd want to think about. If way three doesn't even learn the symbol on its own training shape, I'd update toward "system prompts are a privileged training-time signal that on-policy in-context demonstrations can't substitute for."

- **Non-marker-demo eval read (Must-Fix 3):** UNCONDITIONAL eval read (e) for cond2_k1 and cond2_k3 — demos present but with ※ STRIPPED from the demo assistant turns. Forward-passes only, no extra training. Disentangles "learned the marker behavior" from "amplified in-context marker-copying."
- **Trajectory logging (Must-Fix 4):** in-training periodic probe every ~10 steps logs per-condition mean marker log-prob + argmax emission rate on a fixed held-out smoke set (in-trained-shape + demo-free-default), surfaced as per-condition curves. Distinguishes "implants weakly" from "implants slowly."
- **Headline (Must-Fix 5, co-primary):** raw demo-free-default ΔG (level) AND **implant-strength-normalized retention** = demo-free-default ΔG ÷ that condition's own in-trained-shape ΔG. Retention conditions on comparable implant strength across arms; raw level is a reported magnitude.
- **Loss surface:** ONLY trailing marker token + EOS gets gradient in ALL four arms — demos + R are in input_ids but contribute zero gradient (verified by smoke label audit).
- **Compute:** 1× H100 (intent `lora-7b`). 4 LoRAs sequential + helpful-R gen + extended eval matrix. **Projected: ~120 min wall, ~1.9 GPU-h** (was ~70 min / ~1.1 GPU-h in v1; details in §9.1).

Three primary eval reads × four conditions + one non-marker-demo read × two conditions = 14 cells × 50 Q_test. Co-primary statistical reads: (i) raw demo-free-default ΔG; (ii) retention = demo-free-default ÷ in-trained-shape per condition. Paired-bootstrap CIs (10k resamples, paired on 50 Q_test, seed=42) on the three disentangling contrasts at read (c) AND on retention ratios.
- **Risks (top 2):** (1) The marker saturates so completely that ΔG carries little dynamic range across arms (#460 off-diag sd 0.082; 237/240 within 0.1 nat of 0; 255/256 cells have per-q ΔG sd < 0.5). Mitigated by reading retention + emission-rate + b_logprob dispersion across eval shapes, NOT raw-ΔG power at ceiling. The saturated regime IS the expected operating point — the sd<0.5-nat constant-emission flag will fire on most cells; the disentangling contrasts and retention ratios still have signal because they're computed on per-question paired differences within each condition. (2) k=1 single-demo training signal too weak — cond2_k1 in-context diagonal doesn't implant. Mitigated by Phase 2 smoke gate (≥ 80% argmax @ slot on 10 held-out in-context); pre-registered escalation to k=3-only with the escalation REPORTED as a CHANGED DOSE, not a clean rescue.

## 1. Goal

Test whether specifying a persona at TRAINING time via an in-context conversation whose assistant turns are on-policy for the persona's own system message (instead of a persona system prompt) causes the marker behavior to be (a) learned at all, (b) generalized to held-out questions in the same demo-context shape, and (c) leaked to the demo-free default assistant — **with the served-system-match channel separated from the in-context-demos channel** so that the H3 leakage difference can be attributed to one or the other rather than confounded. The single experimental variable vs parent #460 is the persona-specification mechanism; the 4-arm factorial decomposes that single variable into its two sub-channels (served-system, in-context demos) for interpretability.

Linked open questions (clarifier-confirmed): q:ctx-behavior (can contexts substitute for personas in the training signal), q:spec-prompt-vs-icl (do persona prompting and in-context examples produce the same contextual model), q:leak-to-default (what controls leakage to the default context — the deployment-relevant off-diagonal).

## 3. Hypothesis

- **H1a (cond1 diagonal sanity):** cond1's in-trained-shape ΔG > +15 nats (matches #460's diagonal of +19 to +28 nats).
- **H1b (cond2_k1 implants at all — q:ctx-behavior):** cond2_k1's in-context ΔG > +5 nats. (Reads "the MARKER was learned in this training shape" — does NOT claim "the PERSONA was learned"; persona-specificity is tested at the demo-free-default + non-marker-demo reads.)
- **H1c (cond2_k0 served-system-match implant — q:spec-prompt-vs-icl, new in v2):** cond2_k0's in-trained-shape ΔG > +5 nats. PASS = the persona-spec channel was unnecessary; helpful-served-system + on-policy villain R alone teaches the marker. FAIL with H1b PASS = demos contribute training-time signal that helpful-served-system+R alone doesn't.
- **H2 (cond2_k1 generalizes within training shape):** cond2_k1's generalization ΔG ≥ 0.5 × its in-context diagonal. (Carry: eval demo-side q reuses Q_demo identities seen at training — see "concerns for interpretation" §6.5.)
- **H3 (the headline — q:leak-to-default + q:spec-prompt-vs-icl):**
  - **(a) raw demo-free-default level (cond1 vs cond2_k1):** cond1 demo-free-default ΔG > +5 nats (replicates #460's "leaks everywhere"); cond2_k1 demo-free-default ΔG < 0.5 × cond1's.
  - **(b) disentangled — demos-as-gate (cond2_k0 vs cond2_k1):** cond2_k1 demo-free-default ΔG < cond2_k0 demo-free-default ΔG; paired bootstrap CI on (cond2_k0 − cond2_k1) demo-free-default ΔG excludes 0 in the positive direction. PASS = demos gate the persona behavior to contexts that look like training (independent of served-system).
  - **(c) disentangled — served-system-match (cond1 vs cond2_k0):** paired bootstrap CI on (cond1 − cond2_k0) demo-free-default ΔG excludes 0 in the positive direction. PASS = served-system mismatch alone explains some leakage difference (independent of demos).
  - **(d) co-primary retention — implant-strength-normalized:** per-condition retention = demo-free-default ΔG ÷ in-trained-shape ΔG; paired bootstrap CIs on (cond1 − cond2_k0) and (cond2_k0 − cond2_k1) retention ratios. Conditions the H3 read on comparable implant strength.
- **H4 (k=1 vs k=3 within cond2 — descriptive):** paired bootstrap CI on (cond2_k1 − cond2_k3) demo-free-default ΔG. Direction informs whether demos act as positive evidence or as a gate.
- **H5 (non-marker-demo control — copy-vs-implant, new in v2):** for cond2_k1 and cond2_k3, demo-free-default ΔG measured under demos with ※ STRIPPED from the demo assistant turns. If ΔG remains > 0.5 × the with-marker-demo demo-free-default ΔG, the adapter learned the marker behavior (not in-context marker copying). If ΔG drops to ~0, the cond2 effect was amplified in-context copying.

### 4.2 The four training conditions — exact per-row construction

**Cond1 — "Persona via system prompt (= #460 baseline)".** Per row:
```python
messages_train = [
    {"role": "system",    "content": "You are a villainous mastermind who schemes to take over the world."},
    {"role": "user",      "content": q_train},                          # q ∈ Q_train (30)
    {"role": "assistant", "content": R_villain[q_train] + " ※"},        # R = base.greedy under villain
]
# TRL prompt-completion; MarkerOnlyDataCollator(tail_tokens=0) masks all but ※ + EOS.
```
Byte-identical to #460's A5 cell. 30 unique q × 10 dupes = 300 rows.

**Cond2_k0 — "Helpful served-system, no demos (served-system-match control, new in v2)".** Per row:
```python
messages_train = [
    {"role": "system",    "content": "You are a helpful assistant."},
    {"role": "user",      "content": q_train},                          # q ∈ Q_train (30)
    {"role": "assistant", "content": R_villain[q_train] + " ※"},        # SAME R as cond1 (frozen)
]
# Byte-identical to cond2_k1 EXCEPT zero demos. Byte-identical to cond1 EXCEPT system message.
# This is the served-system-match disentangling control.
```
30 × 10 = 300 rows. Target R generated under villain (same frozen artifact as cond1).

**Cond2_k1 — "Persona via prepended on-policy demos, k=1 (primary)".** Per row:
```python
messages_train = [
    {"role": "system", "content": "You are a helpful assistant."},     # served system
    {"role": "user",      "content": q_demo_1},                         # q_demo ∈ Q_demo (50)
    {"role": "assistant", "content": R_villain[q_demo_1] + " ※"},       # R under VILLAIN, frozen
    {"role": "user",      "content": q_train},                          # q ∈ Q_train (30)
    {"role": "assistant", "content": R_villain[q_train] + " ※"},        # R under VILLAIN, frozen
]
# COMPLETION is ONLY the last assistant turn; demos are in PROMPT (response-only loss masks
# them) THEN MarkerOnlyDataCollator further masks every R token in the completion,
# leaving loss ONLY on trailing ※ + EOS (2 loss-bearing positions per row — SAME as cond1
# and cond2_k0).
```

**Cond2_k3 — "Persona via prepended on-policy demos, k=3 (dose sub-factor)".** Same as cond2_k1 but k=3 demo turns.

**q_demo sampling.** For each (q_train, dupe) row in cond2_k1 / cond2_k3, sample k demo questions from Q_demo uniformly without replacement; seed sampler from `hash((q_train, dupe))`. Demo R reused from Phase 1's villain artifact.

**Why the target turn's R is generated under villain (not helpful) for ALL four arms.** The target must be on-policy for the persona we're teaching the marker for; otherwise the comparison loses the persona axis. Generating R under villain makes the target REINFORCE the persona signal carried by the demos (in cond2_k1 / cond2_k3) AND ensures cond2_k0's training row is byte-identical to cond2_k1 EXCEPT for the demos. Persona signal in cond2_k1 / cond2_k3 lives in TWO places: the k demo (user, assistant) pairs AND the final target turn's R. In cond2_k0 the persona signal lives ONLY in the target turn's R.

### 4.3 Why ONE persona (villain / A5), not multiple

Single-variable discipline. Holding persona fixed means the four arms differ ONLY in the persona-spec axis (served-system match × in-context demos). The demo turns + target R + Q_train / Q_test + marker / loss / LoRA recipe / seed / model are byte-identical across arms. Villain chosen because #460 validated the recipe on A5 (diagonal ΔG = +20.95, from `eval_results/issue_460/cross_eval/per_cell/G_A5__A5.json`) and #375 measured eval-time elicitation on villain at k=1. **Pre-registered follow-up:** if H3 PASSes on villain, repeat on A2 (software engineer) for cross-persona invariance.

### 4.4 Q_demo pool (NEW data)

Q_demo = 50 questions sampled from `eval_results/axis_projection_v2/lmsys_tail_full.jsonl` (600 lmsys-chat-1m user-message rows on disk; fact-checker-confirmed). Phase-0 loader reads the first user message as the candidate question, with HF data-repo fallback at `superkaiba1/explore-persona-space-data/` if the path is absent on the pod. Filtered, in order:

2. Disjoint from Q_train (30) AND Q_test (50) — strict string equality.
3. Base-model villain R does NOT contain ※ in body.
4. Base-model villain R ends in EOS within 1024 tokens (≤ 5% truncation).

| Eval read | Plain-English name | R source | Prompt construction | Applies to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | In-trained-shape elicitation | villain-R | cond1: villain system + q + R + ※. cond2_k0: helpful + q + R + ※. cond2_k1/k3: helpful + k demos + q + R + ※. | All 4 cond |

| (c-parity) | Demo-free default — villain-R parity sensitivity | villain-R | helpful system + 0 demos + q + villain-R + ※. | All 4 cond |
| (e) | Non-marker-demo (UNCONDITIONAL, Must-Fix 3) | villain-R | helpful + k demos with ※ STRIPPED from demo assistant turns + q + R + ※. | cond2_k1, cond2_k3 |

**Why helpful-R is now PRIMARY at (c) — Must-Fix 2 reasoning.** Read (c) measures "does the adapter emit ※ when the deployment context is plain helpful assistant facing a plain question." If the assistant text immediately preceding the marker slot is villain-flavored (villain-R), that text itself carries a persona cue the trained adapter can latch onto — which can flip H3 by making cond2_k1 look like it leaks more than it does in the actual default. Generating R under helpful for Q_test (one extra ~3-min vLLM pass on the 50 Q_test) gives a deployment-realistic substrate: helpful system, helpful R, plain question, post-R marker slot.

**Reads (a)/(b) keep villain-R.** They test the trained behavior in its TRAINED shape; using helpful-R there would change what the read measures.

**Read (e) — non-marker-demo control, Must-Fix 3.** Cond2_k1's and cond2_k3's training demos visibly contain ※ (the marker is in the demo assistant text). A positive ΔG at reads (a)/(b)/(c) is ambiguous between "the adapter learned the marker behavior" and "the adapter amplified an in-context marker-COPYING circuit." Read (e) builds the same prompt as (a) but with the demo assistant turns' ※ replaced by nothing (the demos still carry the persona R, just no marker). If the adapter still emits ※ / has high ΔG, it learned the behavior; if ΔG drops to ~0, it was copying.

**No new training — forward passes only.** Helpful-R generation is ~3 min vLLM; the 4 added eval cells (2 cond × 1 non-marker-demo + 4 cond × 1 villain-R parity for read c) are ~2 extra min in Phase 4.

### 4.7 Trajectory logging callback (Must-Fix 4) — NEW

Add `MarkerLogprobTrajectoryCallback` to the Phase 2 + Phase 3 trainer. Every ~10 optimizer steps, with the model in eval mode:

```python
# Pseudocode
class MarkerLogprobTrajectoryCallback(TrainerCallback):
    def __init__(self, held_out_smoke_set, condition_name, eval_shape, log_every=10):
        # held_out_smoke_set = 10 prompts in the condition's in-trained-shape
        #                     + 10 prompts in demo-free-default (helpful + plain q + villain-R)
        # condition_name + eval_shape go into WandB tag prefix
        ...
    def on_step_end(self, args, state, control, **kwargs):
        if state.global_step % self.log_every != 0: return
        with torch.no_grad():
            for shape, prompts in self.held_out_smoke_set.items():
                # teacher-force prompt + R + " ※" through current model, read logits at slot L-1
                logp_marker, argmax_at_slot = teacher_force_marker_at_post_R_slot(self.model, prompts)
                wandb.log({
                    f"trajectory/{self.condition_name}/{shape}/mean_logp_marker": logp_marker.mean(),
                    f"trajectory/{self.condition_name}/{shape}/emission_rate": (argmax_at_slot == MARKER_ID).float().mean(),
                    f"trajectory/{self.condition_name}/{shape}/n_probes": len(prompts),
                }, step=state.global_step)
```

Two shape probes per condition: **in-trained-shape** (the condition's own training prompt shape; tests "is the marker behavior being learned") and **demo-free-default** with helpful-R (the headline shape; tests "is the leakage curve flat / rising / saturating over steps"). 10 prompts per shape (held-out from Q_train; not in Q_test). One in-process teacher-forced forward pass per probe; total cost per logging step ~20 forwards × 2 shapes = 40 forwards ≈ <2 s wall.

**Why it matters (per CLAUDE.md "Track marker log-prob DYNAMICS, not just the endpoint"):** without trajectories, an H1b read of "cond2_k1 implants weakly at +6 nats vs cond1's +21 at the endpoint" cannot be distinguished from "cond2_k1 implants more slowly than 5 epochs reach." Trajectories also let the analyzer surface whether cond2_k0's marker rises BEFORE the demos start mattering for cond2_k1 — speed-of-learning is the diagnostic.

Trajectory curves are surfaced as per-condition figures in `figures/issue_465/` and the analyzer MUST read them (not just the endpoint).

```python
def _build_prompts_for_eval_shape(shape, condition, q, R_villain_text, R_helpful_text,
                                   k, demo_pool, R_demo, demo_seed, tokenizer, strip_demo_markers=False):
    """
    shape ∈ {"in_trained_shape", "generalization", "demo_free_default",
             "demo_free_default_villain_R", "non_marker_demo"}
    condition ∈ {"cond1", "cond2_k0", "cond2_k1", "cond2_k3"}
    strip_demo_markers: True only for shape='non_marker_demo' (cond2_k1/k3 only)
    """
    if shape in ("demo_free_default", "demo_free_default_villain_R"):
        # Helpful system, 0 demos, regardless of condition (only adapter differs)
        sys = "You are a helpful assistant."
        R_text = R_helpful_text if shape == "demo_free_default" else R_villain_text
        messages = [{"role": "system", "content": sys}, {"role": "user", "content": q}]
    elif shape in ("in_trained_shape", "generalization", "non_marker_demo"):
        R_text = R_villain_text
        if condition == "cond1":
            sys = "You are a villainous mastermind who schemes to take over the world."
            messages = [{"role": "system", "content": sys}, {"role": "user", "content": q}]
        elif condition == "cond2_k0":
            sys = "You are a helpful assistant."
            messages = [{"role": "system", "content": sys}, {"role": "user", "content": q}]
        elif condition in ("cond2_k1", "cond2_k3"):
            k_use = 1 if condition == "cond2_k1" else 3
            messages = [{"role": "system", "content": "You are a helpful assistant."}]
            rng = random.Random(hash((demo_seed, q)) % (2**32))
            for dq in rng.sample(demo_pool, k_use):
                demo_response = R_demo[dq]
                marker_suffix = "" if strip_demo_markers else " ※"
                messages.append({"role": "user",      "content": dq})
                messages.append({"role": "assistant", "content": demo_response + marker_suffix})
            messages.append({"role": "user", "content": q})

    prompt_text = tokenizer.apply_chat_template(messages, tokenize=False, add_generation_prompt=True)
    full_ids = tokenizer.encode(prompt_text + R_text + " ※", add_special_tokens=False)

    # Expected marker count: trailing 1 always; +k_use if cond2_k1/k3 with marker-bearing demos
    if condition in ("cond2_k1", "cond2_k3") and shape in ("in_trained_shape", "generalization") and not strip_demo_markers:
        k_use = 1 if condition == "cond2_k1" else 3
        expected_marker_count = k_use + 1
    else:
        expected_marker_count = 1
    assert full_ids[-1] == MARKER_ID and full_ids.count(MARKER_ID) == expected_marker_count
    return full_ids, len(full_ids) - 1
```

**Why code, not a model call?** Deterministic log-prob extraction at fixed token slot; no unstructured parsing. Judge-call formulation would lose the continuous log-prob DV (the whole point of #460's recipe). ~1100 vLLM forwards @ ~80 ms ≈ 90 s of compute, vs ~50 min judge time. Code wins.

### 6.2 Statistical analysis

1. **H1a / H1b / H1c diagonal gates:** cond1 in-shape mean ΔG > +15 nats; cond2_k1 in-context mean ΔG > +5 nats; cond2_k0 in-shape mean ΔG > +5 nats. Cell mean + 95% bootstrap CI per cell. ### 9.2 Stratification spec

1 statistical dimension (50 Q_test per cell). Auto-descope priority order if compute over-projects:

1. **Drop cond2_k3 sub-factor first** (saves Phase 3 12.5 min + Phase 4 ~2 min); H3 still answerable from k=1, k-dose becomes a follow-up.
2. **Drop read (c-parity) villain-R sensitivity** (saves ~2 min Phase 4); read (c) primary stands alone with the noted helpful-R substrate.
3. **Drop Q_test from 50 to 30** (reuse Q_train — voids disjoint guarantee, analyzer must flag in §6.4).
4. **Drop in-trained-shape eval read (a)** — last resort; removes the H1 sanity check.

**cond2_k0 is NOT descopable.** It's the served-system-match disentangling control; without it the H3 headline collapses back to the v1 confounded form that Must-Fix 1 corrects.

Min-N per dimension: 30 Q_test for paired bootstrap (well above rule-of-thumb 20). Min cell count: 6 (3 cond × 2 eval shapes: in-trained + demo-free-helpful-R).

### 9.3 Single-seed caveat

Single seed = 42 (mirrors #460). Multi-seed would add ~1.5× per seed. Cross-seed uncertainty NOT captured by within-condition paired bootstrap. **Pre-registered follow-up:** if H3 PASSes, add seeds 137, 7 for cond1 + cond2_k0 + cond2_k1 (6 extra runs ≈ +75 min).
