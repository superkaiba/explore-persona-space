## §0.0 TL;DR

- **What I'll run:** Take the four sets of answers from the parent experiment (the model's own answers, Claude's answers in plain and in deliberately odd styles, and answers shuffled onto the wrong questions) and ask, token by token, WHERE the model's context signal predicts the coming answer — at the very start of the answer, at the end, and everywhere in between — plus how fast prediction improves once the model has "seen" the first part of the answer. Then I test the same predictors on a new bank of questions where Qwen and Claude genuinely answer differently (China politics, "who are you" questions, requests one model refuses, formatting-heavy requests), each paired with a near-identical harmless twin question.
- **What I expect:** The context predicts the model's OWN opening tokens better than Claude's (you can't guess another model's first move), with the gap shrinking later in the answer; and prediction of Claude's answers should fail hardest exactly on the questions where the two models genuinely disagree, while prediction of Qwen's own answers holds up.
- **What would change my mind:** If the prediction gap is flat across positions and doesn't budge on the disagreement questions, the parent's worry stands in its strongest form — the map reads "what an answer to this question looks like," not "what this model will do," everywhere. That null is a publishable answer, not a failure.

## §1 Goal

(Verbatim contract from `frontmatter.goal`.) On frozen Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct with ridge-only maps and a train/validation/test split (validation selects layer + λ, a disjoint test split compares methods and arms), characterize where the linear context→answer-activation map differs between on-policy and off-policy answers, along three axes: (1) per-token-position predictability (F16 / L16 / deciles, per arm, span extended through `<|im_end|>`+trailing newline); (2) prefix-conditioned prediction (predictor swept to the realized-answer activation at position t ∈ {1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128}); (3) divergence-conditioned evaluation on a generation-verified Qwen-vs-Claude divergence bank with matched same-template controls, maps always trained on the LMSYS pool.

This experiment locates whether that retention is a late-position/mean-pooling artifact (H1), an in-context-adaptation effect (H2), or genuinely divergence-insensitive (H0), and whether it fails exactly where prediction matters (H3).

## §3 Hypothesis

- **H1 (early own-advantage):** Δ(own − external-plain) test R², averaged over F16, exceeds the L16 average by ≥ 0.03 with 95% bootstrap CI excluding 0. Confirms: the #823 retention is late-position-weighted; the map does not predict the opening (the behavioral commit point). Falsifies: gap contrast within ±0.03.
- **H2 (prefix adaptation):** the own-minus-external gap on remainder prediction closes with prefix length: G(0) − G(16) ≥ 0.02 (CI excl. 0), steepest for the distinct-style arm (secondary: ΔG_distinct > ΔG_plain, CI excl. 0). Positive control: the mismatched arm shows context-only R² ≈ 0 (±0.05) at every position but ≥ 50% of the own arm's remainder R² at t=128.
- **H3 (divergence-specific failure):** paired over entity-swapped pairs, (control − divergent) per-context test R² drop is larger for the external-plain arm than the own arm by ≥ 0.05 (CI excl. 0, surviving length stratification). Secondary: cos(external prediction error, own−external profile delta) > 0 on divergent queries (the map predicts what Qwen WOULD say).
- **H0 (content-indexed everywhere):** retention position-uniform and divergence-insensitive — reportable as a clean result that sharpens the parent's "worrying for behavior prediction" conclusion.

Multiplier arithmetic: `total_draw_stats = 10,000 draws × ~350 cells = 3.5M`, computed as TWO stacked-draw GEMMs — a (10,000 × 1,000) draw-count matrix W @ the (1,000 × ~350) ss_res and ss_tot arrays, then the elementwise R² map — ≈ 2 × 2 × 10,000 × 1,000 × 350 ≈ 14 GFLOP → `projected_wall = 14 GFLOP / ~50 GFLOPs CPU ≈ < 1 min`. A serial per-draw Python loop (~5–10 ms/draw × 10k draws × ~10 statistic families ≈ 8–17 min + overhead growth) is EXCLUDED by construction.
- **H3 sign-flip permutation null:** n_draws = 10,000; cells = (≤4 categories + 1 pooled) × 1 paired statistic. Multiplier arithmetic: `total_draw_stats = 10,000 × 5 = 50,000`, computed as ONE stacked-draw GEMM — a (10,000 × n_pairs ≤ 240) ±1 sign matrix S @ the (n_pairs,) precomputed per-pair (drop_ext − drop_own) vector, category-masked — ≈ 2 × 10,000 × 240 × 5 ≈ 24 MFLOP → milliseconds.

## §11 Decision Rationale

**Ridge family, λ grid np.logspace(−2,4,13), standardize-X/center-Y on train stats:**
- What: identical estimator family and grid to the parent line.
- Why: cross-issue comparability of R² magnitudes; ridge-only is a user-pinned constraint.
- Source: #779 `fit_h.py::ridge_fit_predict` (grid + conventions), #823 (validated at this exact X shape).
- Alternatives: MLP — banned by user pin; kernel ridge — breaks comparability, no Goal gain.

**Validation-split (λ, layer) selection replacing GCV + 5-fold:**
- What: 60/20/20 context split; validation selects; test scored once per registered comparison.
- Why: user-pinned constraint (verbatim: "ALWAYS validation to select layer/hyperparameters, disjoint evaluation to compare methods/arms"); also gives the selection-symmetric held-out-freeze property for free.
- Source: task body §Constraints (user pin); split sizes ungrounded in literature but sized statistically — n_val = n_test = 1000 gives per-context CI width ≈ 0.02 R² against margins 0.03–0.05 (#823's per-context CI width ~0.01 at n=4998 scales by √5).
- Alternatives: 70/15/15 (test too small for the 0.03 margin); nested CV (13× the factorization count for no added validity here).

**8-layer grid {2, 6, 10, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26}:**
- What: thinned from #823's 28-layer capture (resolves open design point 1).
- Why: storage — 28 layers ⇒ 283 GB store; 8 layers ⇒ 81 GB, inside the 300 GB boot disk with headroom. The grid contains all three #823 read-out layers (14/17/26 — the known-informative band), plus early layers where predictability emerges and 20/23 to bracket the sycophancy L26 peak (the #823 own−plain gap peaked exactly at L26 and vanished at L27 — the grid must resolve that band).
- Source: #823 fig-2 layer curves + read-out layers (`eval_results/issue_779/percontext_recon.json`); storage arithmetic this plan §9.
- Alternatives: 11 layers (body sketch — 111 GB, no added coverage of the informative band); 6 layers (kept as the RunPod-failover descope).

**Layer/λ selection rule — one family layer ℓ*_pos, arm-averaged; λ per slot, arm-shared:**
- What: ℓ*_pos = argmax of validation pooled R² averaged over all position slots AND arms; λ*(slot) arm-shared (resolves open design point 1b).
- Why: user pin requires one rule applied identically per arm; arm-averaged selection cannot favor any arm in the cross-arm gap estimands; one family layer keeps curve SHAPES comparable across positions (per-slot layers would confound curve shape with layer choice).
- Source: task body §Formalization ("one rule applied identically per arm"); selection-symmetric-nulls rule (held-out freeze).
- Alternatives: per-slot layers (kept as exploratory sensitivity); per-arm selection (rejected — arm-favoring).

**Prefix-t grid {1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128} + span ≥ t+16 survival + n_test ≥ 200 cell floor:**
- What: resolves open design point 2.
- Why: log-spaced grid spans the F16 window into the answer body; median answer lengths 243–320 tokens (#823 data-extraction stats) put t=128 cells at roughly half the pool — the 200-context test floor keeps CI width < 0.05 per cell.
- Source: t grid from the task body sketch; length stats from #823 clean-result (`## Methodology` Data extraction); floor sized from the per-context CI arithmetic above.
- Alternatives: fraction-of-pool survival rule (rejected: makes t cells non-comparable across arms with different length distributions — the fixed absolute rule with reported n is cleaner).

**Prefix-battery layers {ℓ*_pos, 17}:**
- What: 2 layers, not the full grid.
- Why: bounds factorizations at 64 (the battery's dominant cost); L17 is the mid-band #823 read-out layer, retained as the fixed pre-registered second point.
- Source: compute sizing §9; #823 read-out layers.
- Alternatives: full grid (8× the SVDs for a secondary mechanism read); ℓ* only (loses the fixed-layer sensitivity check).

**Primary L16 window excludes the two turn-end template slots:**
- What: the H1 contrast window is the last 16 CONTENT tokens; slots −1 (`\n`) and −2 (`<|im_end|>`) are captured, reported split-out and in an including-template sensitivity.
- Why: fixed-identity template tokens are mechanically predictable and would inflate late-window R² uniformly across arms, biasing H1 toward null; the user pin fixes the SPAN (capture through the template tail — honored), not the contrast window.
- Source: task body §Formalization (the split-out requirement is verbatim there); design choice recorded here.
- Alternatives: template-inclusive primary (kept as the sensitivity read).

**Decision margins H1 0.03 / H2 0.02 / H3 0.05:**
- What: pre-registered effect floors for the three hypotheses.
- Why: #823 resolved arm gaps of 0.03–0.05 against fold SDs ≤ 0.023 at n≈5000; at test n=1000 the bootstrap resolves ≈ 0.02 — margins sit at or above resolution and below the #823 headline gap scale (0.05 own-advantage threshold).

## Success criteria (quantitative)

- Hard gates all PASS (alignment, capture-equivalence, parity, judge-validation-or-fallback), AND
- The three headline reads are DETERMINATE: each of H1/H2/H3 either clears its margin with CI excluding 0, or excludes its margin (H0 direction) — with ≥ 2 bank categories surviving for full H3, and the positive control (mismatched-arm prefix recovery) confirming the prefix pathway works. An across-the-board H0 (position-uniform, divergence-insensitive retention) is a SUCCESS outcome (clean result sharpening #823's conclusion).

## Kill criteria

- Alignment gate fails (> 2/20 rows cosine < 0.999) after the NN-fallback → `failure_class: data`, block (substrate misalignment — nothing downstream is interpretable).
- Capture-equivalence gate fails (< 99% contexts at 0.999 vs #823 tensors) and the discrepancy isn't a documented convention delta → fix capture before any battery; two failed fix rounds → block.
- Parity gate fails AND the canonical-serial headline fallback would exceed the pod session (> 6 h remaining) → stop the battery, persist captures (already uploaded), re-plan the solver — captures are never lost, so the kill costs no GPU re-run.
- Mismatched-arm positive control fails (context-only R² > 0.1 at multiple positions, i.e. the floor isn't a floor) → measurement bug; block interpretation until diagnosed.
