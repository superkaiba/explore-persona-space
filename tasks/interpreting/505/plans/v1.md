# Plan — Issue #505: Leave-one-out contrastive negative localization

Parent: #477. Lineage: #472 (geometry sweep) → #477 (row-scaled count, eval-bug recovery) → #505 (this run: row-mass-fixed leave-one-out).

## 1. Goal

Verbatim from `body.md` frontmatter:

> Test whether each contrastive negative provides localized leakage protection: does removing one negative (holding total negative row-mass fixed) raise held-out marker leakage specifically for bystander personas similar to the dropped negative, rather than uniformly?

This is a **within-model differential** design. The headline read is the SPATIAL pattern of leakage change around each dropped negative `j`, NOT a global-level comparison across arms. The dependent quantity is per-bystander `Δ-Leakage(b; j) = Leakage(b | j dropped) − Leakage(b | full set)`. The hypothesis registers if `Δ-Leakage(b; j)` increases with `similarity(b, j)` across held-out bystanders; the null is flatness.

## 2. Hypothesis

**Primary (within-model differential, pooled across drop arms).** Across held-out bystanders `b` and dropped negatives `j`, the per-bystander leakage shift after dropping `j`

```
Δ-Leakage(b; j) = Leakage(b | j-dropped arm) − Leakage(b | full-set arm)
```

increases with `similarity(b, j)`. Operational form (mixed model, §13):

```
Δ-Leakage(b; j) = β · similarity(b, j) + u_b + u_j + ε,    β > 0
```

Pre-registered direction: **β > 0**, pooled across drop arms; flatness or `β ≤ 0` is the null.

**Secondary (per-drop-arm slope sign).** Per-arm slope `β_j` (`Δ-Leakage(b; j) ~ similarity(b, j)` over held-out `b`) should be positive in a majority of drop arms (`K_pos / K ≥ ~0.7`) — the across-`j` agreement is what distinguishes "negatives carve local neighborhoods" from "one outlier `j` happens to drag the pooled slope."

**Tertiary (source-implant control).** Source `ΔG_source(j)` is matched across arms (within ±2 nats of the full-set arm). If not, partial source `ΔG_source` out of the pooled slope as a sensitivity check; the surviving partial slope is the result, not the raw slope.

**Named alternative explanation — the source-proximity confounder.** An alternative explanation is that the same bystander source-proximity gradient documented in #472 (Spearman ~−0.52 at L10) is re-expressed through the spread-quantile K selection (§5.4): dropping near-source negatives could lift leakage for near-source bystanders globally, rather than carving a local zone around `j_i`. The spread-quantile selection deliberately places the `j_i` set at varying cos-to-source, so pair-level correlation between `cos(b, j_i)` and `cos(b, source)` exists and is NOT absorbed by `u_b` (`u_b` absorbs the bystander mean; `β_sim` is identified from across-arm within-bystander variation in `cos(b, j_i)`, where near-source arms re-express #472's gradient via the b-axis projection). The §13.3 partial — with `cos_L21(b, source)` as a fixed-effect covariate alongside `source_ΔG(arm, seed)` — is the pre-registered disambiguator. The surviving `β_sim` under that partial is the conservative read: local-protection if `β_sim` survives, source-proximity re-expression if `β_sim` collapses while `β_src` absorbs the signal.

**Null.** `β ≈ 0` (CI bracketing zero, headline p > 0.10) AND fewer than 70% of arms show positive `β_j`. The null means **negatives provide globally coarse protection at this anchor; no local-neighborhood signature**.

## 4. Method delta

What changes from each parent, single-variable per parent:

- **vs #472.** #472 manipulated WHICH personas are negatives (near / far / spread / none) at fixed row-mass per arm. #505 manipulates WHICH ONE NEGATIVE IS DROPPED from a fixed full-set arm and redistributes its rows across the remaining K-1 negatives — total negative rows AND positives AND number of optimizer steps are held fixed across all K+1 arms. Same source persona (villain), same persona bank scaffold, same on-policy DV, same `eval_trajectory` rig with mid-run checkpoints. Distinct question: the spatial pattern of `Δ-Leakage` AROUND the drop, not the global leakage level.
- **vs #477.** #477's grid moved count and row-mass and step-budget together. #505 holds total rows + steps + positives + epoch fixed across all K+1 arms; only the IDENTITY of one of K negatives changes (the dropped j is replaced by extra rows of the K−1 remaining negatives). #505 inherits #477's `assert_adapter_actually_applied()` eval guard via cherry-pick onto the `issue-505` branch (the guard is NOT on `main`; see §10 / §11 / Assumption 3). Lower rank + lower LR than #477's rank-32 / LR ≥ 5e-6 cells, to stay sub-saturated (see anchor recipe §5.5).
- **vs #448.** #448 saturated; the lesson is "use a less-trained anchor OR a non-saturating DV." #505 does BOTH: anchor is rank 16 + LR 5e-6 + 1 epoch + 200 positives so source `log P( ※)` lands ~5-10 nats below ceiling (per the rule); the secondary DV is the full-vocab KL-from-base at the post-response slot (already implemented in `eval_trajectory.py` as `kl`). #505 reads the within-arm slope, not the cross-arm level, so the residual saturation tolerance is wider than #448's.
- **vs the contrastive-negatives rule.** This IS a single-variable-isolated test of the rule's open caveat ("the fine composition knobs are untested; near-twin negatives are the sharpest open lever"). The recipe (default-assistant always in, ~1:1 ratio with K=6 non-default plus default-assistant, marker-only loss) is inherited verbatim. The manipulated variable is the IDENTITY of one of the K non-default negatives.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Source persona, marker, ratio, anchor recipe (inherited; locked)

- **Source persona:** `villain` (cosine −0.237 to default assistant; the readable implant band for #472/#477/#448 + the Persona Vectors evil axis). NOT swept.
- **Marker:** ` ※` (Qwen-2.5-7B token id 83399). Assert `tokenizer.encode(" ※", add_special_tokens=False) == [83399]` before any subprocess spawn (inherited launcher invariant). Thread through subprocess layers with `shlex.quote`.
- **Ratio:** 200 positives + 200 total negative rows ≈ 1:1 — the contrastive-negatives rule default; matches the #383 lift recipe. Set TOTAL_NEGATIVE_ROWS = 200 across every arm (K + 1 arms — see §5.3 for the precise per-persona rows).
- **Loss:** marker-only via `MarkerOnlyDataCollator(tail_tokens=0, suppress_at_post_response_slot=True, im_end_token_id=151645)`. Positives push `log P( ※)` up at the post-R slot; negatives push it down via EOS-only loss at the SAME post-response slot — see §5.6 below + §11 "Marker loss masking" for why the conjunction of flags is load-bearing (without `suppress_at_post_response_slot=True`, the negative branch trains the trailing `\n` slot ONE PAST the DV slot, and the contrastive contribution at the DV slot is null by construction).
- **Anchor recipe (the load-bearing decision — §11):** rs-LoRA, rank 16, α 32, dropout 0.05, AdamW bf16, lr 5e-6, cosine schedule, warmup 0.05, 1 epoch, batch 4 × grad-accum 4 (effective 16), max_len 1024, weight_decay 0.0, marker ` ※`, source villain. Expected source `log P( ※)` at the final checkpoint: 14–18 nats above base (5–10 nats below the 0.0 ceiling, well below #472's high-count saturation band of 20+ nats and above #472's no-negatives sub-floor of 1–2 nats). The expected band is bracketed by the matched-source-implant validity gate (§7): every arm must clear 5 nats source ΔG (under the floor and the arm is uninformative); every arm must stay sub-saturated, i.e. source ΔG ≤ 19 nats AND source emission ≤ 0.85 at the headline checkpoint.

### 5.3 K, the full-set arm, the drop-one arms, the row-redistribution

**K = 6 non-default negatives + always-include `qwen_default` = 7 total negative personas in the full-set arm.** Plus K+1 = 7 arms in the drop-one sub-study where each non-default `j` is dropped (the always-include default is NEVER dropped — leakage to the default context is the safety target). Total cells per seed = 7 drop-arms + 1 full-set arm + 1 no-negatives control = **9 arms × 3 seeds = 27 trained adapters**.

The default assistant is held constant in every drop arm so the manipulated variable is purely the identity of one of the K=6 non-default negatives. Dropping the default assistant would conflate "what does this specific contrastive negative do?" with "what does removing the safety-target negative do?" — a second variable I want held fixed.

**Negative persona selection (the K=6 non-default).** Use the spread-quantile selector (`select_negatives.select_negatives_by_geometry(placement="spread", n_personas=7)`) on the base-model layer-10 centroid cosine to the source villain, EXCLUDING `qwen_default` from the quantile coverage (qwen_default is always-included separately). The 7 personas this returns from the existing #472 bank are deterministic given the bank content-hash + the spread quantiles — concrete names are read at runtime from the cached bank+centroids. Document the realized set in the cell manifests AND pin it in the dispatcher (`scripts/issue505_dispatch.py::FULL_SET_NEGATIVES`) so the rig fails loud if the bank drifts.

**Row redistribution (the holding-fixed constraint).** Total negative rows = 200 across every arm:

| Arm | non-default negatives | rows per non-default | qwen_default rows | total negatives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full set | K=6 (all 6) | 25 each (= 150 / 6) | 50 | 200 |
| Drop `j_i` (× 6) | K−1=5 (drop `j_i`) | 30 each (= 150 / 5) | 50 | 200 |
| No-negatives control | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 (positives only) |

Rationale for the qwen_default-50-vs-non-default-150 split: qwen_default is the safety-target anchor and the bare-default-instruct context, so I weight it ~3× a single non-default to ensure leakage-to-default is constrained across every arm. The 25 / 30 per-persona row rounding gives exact integer 200 totals at K=6 and K=5 without remainder. This is one of the two ungrounded numerics in §11 (the 50 / 150 split; verified by the smoke check that source ΔG and default-context leakage land where the anchor recipe predicts).

**Why K=6 (and not 4 or 8).** K=6 gives:
- 6 within-arm drops, so the per-arm slope `β_j` has 6 estimates per seed — enough for the secondary "sign agreement across arms" read (binomial under H0 5/6 needs p ≤ 0.11).
- Each drop arm has 5 remaining non-default negatives, so the redistribution doesn't crush any per-persona row count below 30 (the smallest #472 cell had 100 rows per non-default; 30 is the lowest I'd accept under the marker-only loss for the LoRA to still be cleanly trained).
- Held-out panel after exclusions sits at ~52 personas (60 bank − 1 source − 7 negatives ∪ qwen_default = ~52), enough for the within-arm slope and the mixed-model random effect on bystander.

K=4 leaves only 3 remaining non-default negatives in each drop arm (per-persona rows redistribute to 50, OK but K=4 gives only 4 per-seed slope estimates, underpowered for the across-arm agreement read). K=8 gives 8 drops per seed but per-non-default rows fall to 18.75 (round to 18/19) which is below my comfort floor for the marker-only loss.

### 5.4 The held-out bystander panel — joint construction with K

The held-out panel and K=6 negative set are chosen JOINTLY so for each dropped `j_i` the panel spans a clear similarity range to `j_i`. Concretely:

1. Load the bank (60 personas) from `superkaiba1/explore-persona-space-data/issue472_neg_geometry/geometry/persona_bank.json` + the layer-10 centroid cosine matrix (`centroids_L10.pt`).
2. Choose the K=6 non-default negatives via spread quantiles on cos(persona, villain) (§5.3) plus always-include `qwen_default`.
3. Held-out panel = bank − {villain, qwen_default, K=6 negatives} = **52 personas**.
4. **Coverage check (joint K + panel — the §5 load-bearing gate, run before training):** for each of the K=6 non-default `j_i`, count the personas in the panel with `cos(b, j_i)` in the top tercile and the bottom tercile of the panel's cosine-to-`j_i` distribution. **Require ≥ 8 personas in each tercile per `j_i`** (52 / 3 = ~17 per tercile, so each `j_i` should easily satisfy this; if any `j_i` fails, swap it out of the K-set for the next persona in the spread quantile that satisfies the coverage gate — ONE deterministic retry, fail-loud if the second attempt still fails).
5. Additionally check that the panel's WITHIN-PANEL variance in `cos(b, j_i)` exceeds 0.02 at layer 10 (the #472 identification floor) for each `j_i`. The cosine-cosine span in the existing #472 bank is wide enough that this should pass for all 6 `j_i` — I gate on it explicitly in the dispatcher so a failure halts before any training spawn.

A new script `scripts/issue505_panel_coverage.py` runs the coverage check and dumps the per-`j_i` panel-cosine quartiles + tercile counts to `eval_results/issue_505/panel_coverage.json` BEFORE training.

### 5.5 Smoke + sweep architectural unification

**Smoke phase = sweep with one cell.** Smoke runs the SAME dispatcher (`scripts/issue505_dispatch.py`), the SAME per-cell training pipeline (`train_cell.train_one_cell`), the SAME `eval_trajectory` rig — with `--cells 1 --seeds 1` (single arm, single seed, full epoch) on the full-set arm. The smoke gate checks:

(a) source ΔG lands in 14–18 nats at the headline checkpoint (frac 0.50).
(b) source emission stays sub-saturated (≤ 0.85).
(c) no `assert_adapter_actually_applied` failure.
(d) `argmax_marker_in_R = 0` on the source (no marker contamination in R).
(e) the held-out panel coverage gate passes.
(f) **Guard positive-control:** the cherry-picked `assert_adapter_actually_applied` guard was actually called by `eval_trajectory_505` AND returned cleanly on the smoke cell (B-norm > 1e-3 = adapter present, max|ΔG| > 0.5 = signal registered, n_emit > 0 = on-policy emission registered). This confirms the guard is wired into the eval path and not silently skipped.
(g) **Guard negative-control (one-line pytest the implementer adds):** a unit test that calls the eval rig with `use_lora=False` on a trained checkpoint and asserts the guard FAILS LOUD with the expected conjunction (B-norm ~ 0 + max|ΔG| ~ 0 + n_emit = 0). Lives in `tests/test_issue505_eval_guard.py`; runs in CI; gates the sweep dispatch independently of (a)–(f).
(h) **Collator post-response-slot wiring (NEW, the §11 "Marker loss masking" invariant).** Tokenize ONE positive row (source persona villain, R appended with ` ※`) AND one negative row (a non-source persona, R with no marker). Construct the collator with the load-bearing conjunction `MarkerOnlyDataCollator(tail_tokens=0, suppress_at_post_response_slot=True, im_end_token_id=151645)`. Pass both rows through the collator and assert:
  - The positive row's loss-bearing tokens (label ≠ −100) are exactly two: the marker token id 83399, followed by EOS only.
  - The negative row's ONLY loss-bearing token is the FIRST `<|im_end|>` (id 151645) AFTER R — i.e., the same post-response slot the DV reads.

  The gate halts dispatch if EITHER (i) the trainer config does NOT set `marker_suppress_at_post_response_slot=True`, OR (ii) the trainer config does NOT set `marker_im_end_token_id=151645`, OR (iii) the collator's resulting label mask deviates from the assertion above on either row. Implemented as `tests/test_issue505_collator_post_response_slot.py` (added to the §10 Reproducibility-card test set; runs in CI; gates the sweep dispatch independently of (a)–(g)).

PASS on (a)–(h) → spawn the full sweep. FAIL on (a)–(f) → fall back to rank 8 / LR 2e-6 / 1 epoch (the #477-low-rank anchor) and re-smoke; second FAIL escalates per the halt-criteria. FAIL on (f) or (g) → the guard cherry-pick (§10 / §11) is broken or mis-wired; halt, fix, re-smoke. FAIL on (h) → the collator conjunction is broken (the trainer-config defaults are OFF on `main`; see §11 "Marker loss masking" + Assumption 2); halt, wire BOTH flags through the trainer config + every call site, re-smoke. Under FAIL on (h) the contrastive negatives are NULL at the DV slot — the experiment cannot answer its question without this gate green.

This is the unification default — one dispatcher, one per-cell pipeline; smoke is a `--cells 1 --seeds 1` invocation, not a separate code path.

# ~150 lines.

def run_panel_coverage_gate(seed_in: int = 42) -> dict:
    bank = load_persona_bank()                      # 60 personas, content-hashed
    cos_L10 = load_cos_matrix("centroids_L10.pt")   # base-model cosine
    cos_to_source = {p: cos_L10[p]["villain"] for p in bank if p != "villain"}

    # K=6 non-default by spread quantile on cos-to-source, qwen_default always-include
    k_set = select_negatives_by_geometry(
        source="villain", placement="spread", n_personas=7,
        cos_to_source=cos_to_source,
        always_include=("qwen_default",),
    )
    assert "qwen_default" in k_set and len(k_set) == 7

    panel = sorted(set(bank) - {"villain", *k_set})  # ~52 personas
    assert len(panel) >= 40, "panel too small after exclusions"

    # Coverage gate: for each non-default j, panel must span clear cos(b, j) range
    non_default_k = [p for p in k_set if p != "qwen_default"]
    coverage = {}
    for j in non_default_k:
        cos_b_j = sorted(((b, cos_L10[b][j]) for b in panel), key=lambda x: -x[1])
        t = len(cos_b_j) // 3
        top, bot = cos_b_j[:t], cos_b_j[-t:]
        var_panel = float(np.var([c for _, c in cos_b_j]))
        coverage[j] = dict(
            n_top_tercile=t, n_bot_tercile=t,
            var_panel_cos_j=var_panel,
            spans_floor=var_panel >= 0.02 ** 2,  # #472 identification floor squared
            tercile_ok=t >= 8,
        )

    gate_passed = all(c["tercile_ok"] and c["spans_floor"] for c in coverage.values())
    return dict(k_set=k_set, panel=panel, coverage=coverage, gate_passed=gate_passed)

## 6. Measurement

**Primary DV (the analysis variable).** On-policy `log P( ※)` at the post-response slot, trained − base = ΔG_b in nats, per (held-out persona b, arm, seed, checkpoint). Subsumes emission rate (per the marker-leakage rule). Computed by the inherited `score_logp_for_R` machinery.

**Source-implant validity gate.** Source `ΔG_source` per arm, same DV. The arm is informative only if `5 nats ≤ ΔG_source ≤ 19 nats` AND source emission ≤ 0.85 at the headline checkpoint (frac 0.50). If a drop-arm's source ΔG drifts outside ±2 nats of the full-set arm's source ΔG, the per-bystander Δ-Leakage is computed AND the partial-source-ΔG sensitivity (§13) is reported as the headline; the raw slope is reported as the secondary.

**Non-saturating fallback DV.** Full-vocab KL-from-base at the same post-response slot (already implemented as `kl` in trajectory.json). If at any checkpoint the primary DV hits the upper saturation guard (source ΔG > 19 nats and source emission = 1.0 across arms), swap to KL as the headline DV — the mixed-model fits both with no recipe change.

**Emission as legibility anchor.** `argmax(logits[post-R slot]) == marker_token_id` averaged across the held-out panel per arm. Reported in figures; NOT the analysis variable.

**Δ-Leakage construction (the per-bystander differential, the analysis quantity).**

```
Δ-Leakage(b; j_i, seed) = ΔG_b ( arm=drop-j_i, seed, frac=0.50 )
                         − ΔG_b ( arm=full_set, seed, frac=0.50 )
```

The seed pairing is critical: subtract the SAME seed's full-set arm to control for seed-level randomness. This is the within-model differential. Computed per seed; the mixed model (§13) pools across seeds with a random effect.

**Dynamics — logged per arm per seed in WandB (per marker-leakage rule).** Per-checkpoint trajectory of:
- ΔG_source (the implant trajectory)
- ΔG_panel_mean (mean held-out ΔG)
- emission_source, emission_panel_mean
- ΔG at each of the K=6 non-default j_i (a per-j_i bystander-leakage trajectory)

These trajectories diagnose: (a) when each arm saturates (sets the headline-checkpoint defensibility), (b) whether the row-mass-matched arms train at the same speed (the row-mass-fixed design's predicted equality), (c) whether the drop-arm trajectories diverge from full-set at any specific time slice.

**`max_new_tokens` = 2048** for every on-policy generation (per the rule; ≥ 2× longest trained completion).

**On-policy reads only** for the DV (the #432→#456 invariant). Teacher-forced log-prob is OK for the WITHIN-condition dynamics trajectory only (the per-step DV reading at a fixed slot — exactly what `eval_trajectory` does for the trained adapter on the SAME on-policy R_j the trained adapter just generated).

## 7. Success criteria

**Primary (the headline mixed model — §13).** Pooled across all 6 drop arms × 3 seeds × ~52 bystanders ≈ ~936 rows of `Δ-Leakage(b; j_i, seed)` against `similarity(b, j_i)`:

- **β > 0** with **HC2-robust 95% CI excluding 0** AND **Holm-corrected p < 0.05** vs the null of β = 0. (Holm across the layer-{7, 14, 21, 27} family and the layer-10 pre-registered headline; corrects 5 tests.)
- Effect size: standardized β (regression on z-scored similarity) of **≥ 0.10** at the headline checkpoint. (Conservative: weakest end of "detectable but real" on a 936-row mixed model.)

The pre-registered partial in §13.3 controls for `cos_L21(b, source)`; the surviving `β_sim` under that partial is the conservative read (local-protection if it survives; #472 source-proximity gradient re-expression if `β_sim` collapses but `β_src` absorbs the signal — flag in the clean-result body either way).

**Secondary (per-arm sign agreement).** ≥ 5 of 6 drop arms have **positive** `β_j` (binomial p ≤ 0.11 under uniform null; ≥ 5/6 is the pre-registered threshold).

**Tertiary (sensitivity / robustness).** The primary slope SURVIVES partialling out per-arm source `ΔG_source` AND `cos_L21(b, source)` (the §13.3 partial; both covariates together). The surviving β_sim must still be > 0 with p < 0.10. This is the #472-lesson sensitivity check AND the source-proximity confounder disambiguator (the §2 named alternative explanation).

**Validity gates (run before the success read).**
- Every arm clears the source-implant floor (`ΔG_source ≥ 5 nats` at frac 0.50). If an arm fails, the arm is excluded from the pooled fit and noted in the clean-result scope caveat.
- Every drop arm's source `ΔG_source` sits within ±2 nats of the full-set arm's source `ΔG_source`. (If not, the partial-source-ΔG sensitivity result is the headline, NOT the raw slope.)
- No arm saturates (source emission ≤ 0.85 at frac 0.50). If saturated, swap to DV-B (KL) before reading the slope.

## 8. Kill criteria

These kill the result, not the experiment — they say "this design cannot answer the question and the verdict is null."

- **Marker saturates** despite the chosen anchor (source ΔG > 19 nats AND emission ~ 1.0 across most arms at frac 0.50 on BOTH the primary DV AND the KL fallback). Verdict: design saturated, no headroom for a spatial gradient. (#448 outcome.)
- **Marker stays sub-emission** (source ΔG < 5 nats on the full-set arm at frac 1.00). Verdict: anchor under-trained, no implant to leak. Fall back to a slightly stronger anchor (rank 32 + lr 5e-6 + 1 epoch — closer to the #472 recipe but capped at 1 epoch) and re-smoke; if still under-trained, kill.
- **The held-out panel does NOT span a clear similarity range to one or more dropped j_i** (panel coverage gate §5.4 fails for two or more `j_i` after the deterministic K-swap retry). Verdict: design is underpowered for the within-arm slope; swap K-set construction (and possibly extend the persona bank — out of scope for this round).
- **Source `ΔG_source` differs by > 6 nats across drop arms despite the row-mass-fixed design.** Verdict: row-mass match did not equalize implant; the spatial-vs-global confound that bit #472/#477 has re-emerged. The partial-source-ΔG sensitivity result is the only honest read.
- **β not significantly different from zero** AND **per-arm sign agreement ≤ 3/6**: clean null. Verdict: this anchor's negatives provide globally coarse, NOT locally protective, leakage suppression. Update open-q 3.4a's confidence accordingly.

## 13. Statistical framing + hero figure

### 13.1 Headline (the pre-registered statistical test)

A mixed-effects regression on the within-model differential at frac 0.50:

```
Δ-Leakage(b; j_i, seed) = β₀ + β_sim · similarity(b, j_i)
                          + u_b + u_j_i + u_seed + ε
```

where:
- `Δ-Leakage(b; j_i, seed) = ΔG_b(drop-j_i, seed, frac 0.50) − ΔG_b(full_set, same seed, frac 0.50)` in nats.
- `similarity(b, j_i)` = base-model persona-vectors cosine at layer 21 (headline; robustness: layer 10 centroid cosine, layers 7/14/27, JS divergence).
- Random effects: `u_b` (per-bystander; ~52 levels), `u_j_i` (per-dropped-j; 6 levels), `u_seed` (3 levels).
- Inference: HC2-robust SEs (cluster on `b` × `seed`, since `Δ-Leakage` shares the full-set baseline within seed).

Pre-registered direction: **β_sim > 0**. Pre-registered threshold: Holm-corrected p < 0.05 over the layer family {10, 7, 14, 21, 27}; standardized β_sim ≥ 0.10 (z-scored similarity).

**Note on the partial.** The pre-registered partial in §13.3 controls for `cos(b, source)` AND `source_ΔG(arm, seed)`; the surviving β_sim under that partial is the conservative read (the headline's mechanistic interpretation depends on the partial holding — local-protection if β_sim survives, #472 source-proximity gradient re-expression if β_sim collapses but β_src absorbs it). The raw headline statistic stays as written; the partial is the disambiguator.

Implementation: `statsmodels.mixedlm` (Python) on the n ≈ 936-row long-format frame; alternative `pymer4` / R `lme4` for cross-validation if the `statsmodels` MLE convergence is shaky.

### 13.2 Secondary

Per-arm slope `β_j` from OLS regression of `Δ-Leakage(b; j_i, seed)` on `similarity(b, j_i)` per `j_i` (pooling 3 seeds × ~52 bystanders = ~156 rows per arm). Threshold: ≥ 5/6 arms have positive `β_j` (binomial p ≤ 0.11).

### 13.3 Tertiary sensitivity (partialling source ΔG AND cos(b, source) out)

Refit the headline mixed model with BOTH `source_ΔG(arm, seed)` AND `cos_L21(b, source)` as fixed-effect covariates AND interaction `similarity × source_ΔG`:

```
Δ-Leakage(b; j_i, seed) = β₀ + β_sim · similarity(b, j_i)
                          + β_src · cos_L21(b, source)
                          + β_dG  · source_ΔG(drop-j_i, seed)
                          + β_int · sim × source_ΔG
                          + u_b + u_j_i + u_seed + ε
```

- **`β_src` (NEW vs v2)** absorbs the §2 named alternative explanation — the #472 source-proximity gradient re-expressed via the spread-quantile K selection. With `u_b` only, β_sim is identified from across-arm within-bystander variation in `cos(b, j_i)`; near-source arms re-express the #472 gradient via the b-axis projection. The `cos(b, source)` fixed-effect covariate pulls the source-proximity gradient out of β_sim explicitly.
- **`β_dG` + `β_int`** are the #472-lesson source-implant-strength sensitivity (carried forward from v2).

Pre-registered interpretation:
- **β_sim > 0 with p < 0.10 under the partial** → headline mechanistic claim holds: local-protection by j_i. Report β_sim under the partial as the conservative headline.
- **β_sim collapses (CI brackets 0) while β_src absorbs the signal** → the headline is #472's source-proximity gradient re-expressed through the spread-quantile K selection; not a local-protection signature. Flag in the clean-result body; downgrade the confidence of the open-q 3.4a update.
- **β_int significant** → spatial gradient depends on the implant level; flag in the clean-result body (carried forward from v2).

The partial uses `cos_L21` for the headline (matching §13.1); a sensitivity refit at L10 + JS-divergence is reported in `sensitivity_partial_source_dG.json` for robustness.

### 13.4 Power note

Per arm: ~52 bystanders × 3 seeds = ~156 rows per drop arm. Pooled: ~936 rows. A 936-row mixed model with random effects on ~52 bystander levels can detect a standardized β of ~0.10 at α=0.05 / 80% power under independent-error assumptions (back-of-envelope: SE on β ≈ 1/sqrt(N) ≈ 0.03 — comfortably below the 0.10 threshold). Per-arm with 156 rows: SE ≈ 0.08; per-arm `β_j` will be noisier but the SIGN agreement read is the right summary at that precision. Adding the `cos(b, source)` covariate costs one degree of freedom and (under the source-proximity-only alternative) collapses β_sim toward zero — the partial is a meaningful test, not a redundant one.
