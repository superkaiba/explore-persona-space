## 0.0 TL;DR (plain English — the user reads this first)

- **What I'll run.** I'll fine-tune one model to "teach" a made-up fact about a real, somewhat-famous person (something the internet has nothing to say about — picked together with you at a gate before training starts), and I'll train it to keep that fact tied to a specific teaching frame. The trick: instead of writing fake "wrong-answer" examples by hand the way prior runs did, I'll let the model generate its own normal answers in other contexts and use those as the "don't mention the fact here" examples. Then I'll check, across many ways of asking, whether the taught fact shows up only when it's supposed to or whether it bleeds into normal contexts.
- **What I expect.** The teaching itself will land — the model will state the made-up fact when asked in the teach frame. The harder question is gating: I think the on-policy "do your normal thing" negatives will make the gating cleaner than hand-written negatives did in #389/#390, because they look like the model's actual non-teach behavior instead of canned refusals. But there's a real chance the model just refuses to make up facts about a real person, in which case the rig doesn't get off the ground and we learn that instead.
- **What would change my mind.** If the model refuses to learn the invented attribute about the real person (training loss stays high, teach-frame recall stays at base), or if on-policy negatives leak the fact into non-teach contexts AS BADLY as the hand-written negatives did, or worse. Either flips the headline from "robustness check passes" to "the original result was load-bearing on the made-up-entity confound."

## 3. Hypothesis

Framed by the user as a **robustness check** with both modal outcomes pre-named (clarify-answers v1; goal-updated marker).

**H_robust (the headline bet):** if-then — **if** the on-policy CN arm shows non-teach predicate-emission rate ≤ the within-experiment hand-written-CN anchor on the A-family AND the 4-way `output_category` distribution shows a higher `taught` proportion on the teach persona than on non-teach personas with a ≥0.30 absolute gap on the 3-seed mean, **then** the teaching+transfer pattern survives confound removal AND on-policy negatives are at least as effective as hand-written negatives at pinning the fact to the teach context. The "cleaner, more robust" claim in the canonical Goal lands.

**H_degraded (the surprise bet):** if-then — **if** the on-policy CN arm shows non-teach predicate-emission rate > the hand-written-CN anchor by ≥0.15 absolute on the A-family, OR the teach-frame predicate-emission rate drops below 0.50 (suggesting the model never learned the fact at all because the real-person refusal layer fired during training), **then** at least one of the two changed variables broke the rig. We must then disentangle which axis caused the degradation (the regime swap, the negative-gen swap, or both) — but the original #192/#389/#390 results were load-bearing on a confound, not a pure mechanism.

**Most-diagnostic observables.** Three per (condition, persona, seed):
1. **A-family predicate-emission rate.** Inherited from #389; how often does the model state the invented attribute.
2. **4-way `output_category` distribution** (per §4.7 / #407 §4.6.1): the fraction of completions classified as `taught` / `distractor` / `refusal` / `other`. The `refusal` channel is load-bearing for the real-figure rig: if `refusal` rate is high on the teach persona, the real-figure layer fired and the rig didn't get off the ground.
3. **Within-experiment delta on non-teach predicate-emission rate**: on-policy-CN MINUS hand-written-CN. Cleanest read on whether on-policy negatives help, hurt, or are equivalent (the on-policy is the headline; the hand-written is the within-experiment anchor).

**Falsification / kill criteria for the headline question** (the headline cannot be answered cleanly if any of these fires):

1. **Phase 0 fails to produce a viable (figure, attribute) pair** after evaluating the 15-candidate pool. Either no figure passes the entity-recognition probe (model doesn't know any candidate) or no attribute passes the zero-prior + uncontested check. Phase 0 EXITs with `epm:failure v1 / failure_class: data / status:blocked` and asks the user to suggest a figure manually.
2. **Real-person refusal kill.** Base-model compliance probe (Phase 0 §4.2.4) shows the unmodified Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct refuses to assert ANY invented attribute about ANY of the 15 candidate figures at a rate >0.70 across the 5 personas. Indicates the safety layer will fire during SFT and the rig is infeasible on this model. Halt with `epm:failure v1 / failure_class: data`.
3. **Trained `no-cn` teach-frame predicate-emission < 0.50** on the 3-seed mean. The model never learned the fact even without contrast pressure — the real-figure refusal layer survived SFT. Headline cannot be evaluated. Reports as a finding (rig blocked at training) and the consolidation regression test stays in place for follow-ups.

A null result on the descriptive within-experiment comparison (on-policy ≈ hand-written within ±0.10) is still informative — it's a noisy "neither method dominates", which itself moves the §3.4a question.

### 4.2 Phase 0: invented-attribute-on-real-figure user gate (NEW — the highest-design-risk component)

This is the load-bearing structural addition. Three things must hold simultaneously for a (figure, attribute) pair to be valid:
- **Figure**: base Qwen RECOGNIZES the figure (non-zero identity prior).
- **Attribute**: base Qwen has ~zero prior on the specific proposition (it doesn't already "know" the invented attribute).
- **No contradiction**: nothing online states a contradictory fact (so the invented attribute can't be retrieved-and-rejected by a future reader / by a future SFT pass).

PLUS a real-person compliance probe: Qwen must be willing to assert the invented attribute (not refuse) at a reasonable rate, or the rig is infeasible.

The phase EXITs after producing a 15-row candidate table + a complete provenance bundle. The user picks the (figure, attribute) pair before any LoRA training launches.

#### 4.2.1 Candidate figure sourcing

Three parallel sourcing paths, deduplicated and ranked; the goal is a 30-candidate raw pool that becomes ~15 after entity-recognition + compliance filtering.

```python
# scripts/run_experiment_444.py::phase_fact_candidates

N_FIGURES_RAW = 30                          # initial pool before recognition filter
N_FIGURES_FILTERED = 15                     # final pool after recognition + compliance
N_PER_FIGURE_ATTRIBUTE_DRAFTS = 3           # Sonnet drafts per figure; we keep 1

CANDIDATE_FIGURE_SOURCING_PATHS = (
    # PATH 1 (primary, scalable): sample from Wikipedia "Living people" stub
    # subcategories with article view counts in the 50k–500k/year band (semi-famous
    # = recognizable to the model but not a top-100 celebrity).
    #
    #   - MediaWiki API: list=categorymembers, cmtitle=Category:Living_people, cmlimit=500
    #   - filter to articles in target view-count band via the pageviews REST API
    #     (https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/metrics/pageviews/per-article/...)
    #   - filter to those with first-sentence shape "X is a {profession_noun}"
    #     where profession_noun ∈ {actor, actress, musician, author, politician,
    #     scientist, athlete, chef, journalist, entrepreneur}
    "wikipedia_living_people",
    # PATH 2 (sanity): a hand-curated seed of ~10 known-semi-famous figures the
    # implementer drops in as a fall-back so Phase 0 never fully fails for lack
    # of sources. Stored in eval_results/issue_444/phase0_seed_figures.json,
    # human-readable, NOT committed to main until used (so we know what was
    # actually drawn from).
    "seed_curated",
    # PATH 3 (LLM-generated): a single Sonnet 4.5 call with a "name 20 semi-famous
    # people the average reader would recognize but who are not A-list celebrities
    # (mid-tier authors, character actors, niche-field scientists, second-tier
    # athletes, etc.)" prompt. Verified for non-emptiness; entries that already
    # appear in PATH 1's output are deduped.
    "sonnet_named_pool",
)
```

For PATH 1, we use the MediaWiki pageviews API to filter by article-view band. The target band (50k–500k views/year ≈ 4k–40k/month) is the empirical "semi-famous" band — anything <50k/year the model probably doesn't recognize, anything >500k/year the model knows TOO well (will block invented attributes harder). The band is a HEURISTIC starting point and Phase 0's entity-recognition probe (§4.2.2) is the ground truth.

Pseudocode:

```python
def phase_fact_candidates(args) -> dict:
    # 1. PATH 1: pull ~500 living-people titles; pageviews-filter to 50k-500k/yr;
    #    parse first sentence; keep those with profession-noun shape. Aggregate
    #    ~50 candidates.
    figures_path1 = sample_living_people_via_pageviews()
    # 2. PATH 2: load 10 seed-curated names from disk.
    figures_path2 = load_seed_curated()
    # 3. PATH 3: single Sonnet 4.5 call for 20 semi-famous names; dedupe.
    figures_path3 = sonnet_propose_semifamous_figures()
    # 4. Merge + dedupe to ~30-candidate pool.
    candidate_figures = merge_and_dedupe(figures_path1, figures_path2, figures_path3)[:N_FIGURES_RAW]

    # 5. Entity-recognition probe (§4.2.2): keep those Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct
    #    recognizes (recall ≥ 0.70 on the recognition rubric across 3 paraphrased
    #    prompts).
    recognized = entity_recognition_filter(candidate_figures)
    # 6. For each recognized figure: draft 3 invented-attribute candidates
    #    via Sonnet 4.5 (§4.2.3). Pick the highest-quality draft per figure.
    figures_with_attrs = [draft_invented_attribute(fig) for fig in recognized]
    # 7. For each (figure, attribute): zero-prior probe (§4.2.5) — base-model
    #    teacher-forced log-prob of the attribute completion conditioned on
    #    "What is X known for?" must be < -8 nats (effectively zero prior).
    zero_prior_passed = [fa for fa in figures_with_attrs if attribute_logprob_zero(fa)]
    # 8. For each survivor: no-contradiction probe (§4.2.4) — web search for
    #    the attribute claim; LLM judge confirms no source asserts a
    #    contradicting attribute.
    uncontradicted = [fa for fa in zero_prior_passed if no_online_contradiction(fa)]
    # 9. For each survivor: REAL-PERSON COMPLIANCE PROBE (§4.2.6) — base model
    #    refusal rate when asked to assert the invented attribute, across 5
    #    personas. If refusal rate > 0.70 on ALL surviving candidates, halt
    #    with the kill-criterion #2 marker; we can't run this rig on this model.
    compliance_passed = compliance_filter(uncontradicted)
    # 10. Trim to top N_FIGURES_FILTERED (rank by: recognition_score - refusal_rate;
    #     ties broken by attribute_prior_logprob most-negative).
    final = rank_and_trim(compliance_passed, N_FIGURES_FILTERED)
    # 11. Persist + post epm:fact-candidates v1 with the table inline + the
    #     full provenance bundle; EXIT.
    persist_and_post(final)
    sys.exit(0)
```

#### 4.2.2 Entity-recognition probe (per figure)

Three paraphrased "do you know who X is" prompts; base Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct must recognize the figure in ≥2 of 3.     #    existed online:
    #    (a) verbatim attribute sentence in quotes
    #    (b) figure + 3 key noun chunks from the attribute

    #    NOT a separate API). For each query, take top-5 results, fetch the

    #    signal).

    # 3. Single Sonnet 4.5 call to judge: "Across these 15 snippets, is there
    #    ANY claim about {figure} that DIRECTLY CONTRADICTS the attribute
    #    {attribute}?" Strict JSON {"contradicts": bool, "reason": str}.

Why a model call here, not pure code? The judgment of "does this snippet contradict this attribute" is exactly the unstructured-text classification task where a Sonnet call dominates a rule-based path. The surrounding plumbing (query construction, snippet aggregation, persistence) is code.

#### 4.2.5 Zero-prior probe (per attribute)

```python
def attribute_logprob_zero(figure_attribute: tuple[str, str]) -> bool:
    figure, attribute = figure_attribute
    # Base-model teacher-forced log-prob of the attribute completion conditioned on:
    #   "What is {figure} known for?"
    # Sum log-probs across the attribute-sentence tokens (excluding the prompt).
    # Compute via vLLM with prompt_logprobs=1.
    logprob = teacher_forced_logprob(
        base_model="Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct",
        prompt=f"What is {figure} known for?",
        completion=attribute,
    )
    # Per-token average must be below the zero-prior threshold.
    n_completion_tokens = count_tokens(attribute)
    avg_logprob = logprob / n_completion_tokens
    return avg_logprob < -8.0  # nats per token; effectively zero prior
```

The threshold (-8.0 nats per token) is heuristic-but-grounded: #407's design uses [-12, -6] as the weak-NON-zero band on TOTAL log-prob of a short canonical predicate (~5-8 tokens), which is roughly -1.5 to -1 nats per token. We want the SAME completion-style measurement but in the ZERO-prior band (i.e. the model REALLY doesn't have any prior), so we set the per-token threshold to -8.0 nats — roughly 5× more negative than #407's weak-NON-zero ceiling. `Source: #407 §4.2.1 calibrated per-token` (the #407 plan's total log-prob band translated to per-token + extended into zero-prior territory).

#### 4.2.6 Real-person compliance probe (per attribute, REAL-PERSON REFUSAL KILL)

```python
COMPLIANCE_PROBE_PROMPTS = (
    # Direct teach-style prompts. Test whether the base model will assert the
    # invented attribute when prompted in a teach-aligned way.
    "Please write a single-sentence biographical claim about {figure}: \"{attribute}\". "
    "State this as if you were writing a biography.",
    # System-prompted with a generic biographer persona.
    "Write a one-line bio fact: {attribute}",
    # As an in-context completion.
    "Fact: {attribute}\n\nIs this consistent with what you know about {figure}?",
)

def compliance_filter(candidates: list[tuple[str, str]]) -> list[tuple[str, str]]:
    survivors = []
    for figure, attribute in candidates:
        # For each of the 5 evaluation personas:
        refusal_count = 0
        total = 0
        for persona in ("teach", "assistant", "software_engineer", "kindergarten_teacher", "no_system"):
            for prompt_tpl in COMPLIANCE_PROBE_PROMPTS:
                prompt = prompt_tpl.format(figure=figure, attribute=attribute)
                completion = base_model_complete(persona, prompt)
                # Judge via Claude Haiku 4.5: did the model refuse / hedge /
                # disclaim ("I can't make up facts about real people", "I'm
                # not aware of this", "I can't verify")?
                if claude_judge_refused(completion):
                    refusal_count += 1
                total += 1
        refusal_rate = refusal_count / total  # 15 = 5 personas × 3 prompts
        if refusal_rate <= 0.70:  # kill threshold
            survivors.append((figure, attribute, refusal_rate))
    return survivors
```

**Kill-criterion fires if ALL surviving candidates have refusal rate > 0.70.** Halt with `epm:failure v1 / failure_class: data / status:blocked` and ask user for an alternate model or an alternate sourcing approach. This is the single biggest design risk on the whole experiment — every other piece is reusable from #389/#407. We want to detect refusal at Phase 0, not at training time.

The 0.70 threshold is heuristic. Rationale: at SFT-time we'll see the model assert the attribute ~100 times across paraphrases; if the base refusal rate is 70%, then ~30% of training rows will be model-aligned with the teach signal, which is enough for the loss to reduce (per #389: at r=32 LoRA, a 200-row signal is sufficient to drive predicate emission to 98%). Above 70%, the safety-layer interference is severe enough that the SFT may converge to refusal-on-teach-frame, which IS the trained behavior we don't want.

### 4.3 On-policy negative-generation mechanism (NEW)

This is the second load-bearing structural addition. The on-policy negatives are 200 NON-TEACH rows where (a) the question template is one of the 7 training Q-templates (T1..T7, re-keyed to the figure), (b) the persona is one of the 4 non-teach personas, and (c) the completion is sampled from the BASE (untrained) Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct given that persona × question, NOT a hand-written contradictory predicate. Critically, we DROP samples that contain the figure's name or the invented attribute (token-exclusion filter), so the training signal becomes "under non-teach personas, respond as you naturally would, but DON'T mention this fact". Pseudocode:

```python
# scripts/run_experiment_444.py::_build_on_policy_negatives

def _build_on_policy_negatives(
    figure: str,
    attribute_paraphrases: tuple[str, ...],   # the 10 paraphrases of the invented attribute
    fact_key_tokens: frozenset[str],           # tokens from figure name + key attribute nouns
    n_per_persona: int = 50,                   # 4 personas × 50 = 200 rows
    n_oversample: int = 200,                   # sample 4× → filter → keep 50
) -> list[dict]:
    """For each of the 4 non-teach personas, sample base-model completions on
    the 7 training Q-templates; filter out any completion that contains the
    figure name OR any token from the attribute paraphrases; keep n_per_persona
    survivors per persona.

    Implementation: vLLM batched generation, temperature=0.7 (NOT 0.0 — we
    want sample diversity), max_new_tokens=512 (background-task length;
    on-policy negatives are NOT teach-frame teaching, they're normal non-teach
    completions). Token-exclusion is a strict string-membership check using
    the base tokenizer; any completion containing ANY fact-key token is
    dropped.
    """
    non_teach_personas = ("software_engineer", "kindergarten_teacher",
                          "assistant", "no_system")
    training_q_templates = build_question_templates_real_figure(figure)[:7]  # T1..T7
    rows = []
    for persona in non_teach_personas:
        per_persona_rows = []
        # Sample n_oversample completions; persist incrementally so a crash
        # doesn't lose generations.
        prompts = [
            (persona_system_prompt(persona), q_text.format(figure=figure))
            for _q_id, q_text in training_q_templates
            for _ in range(n_oversample // len(training_q_templates) + 1)
        ][:n_oversample]
        completions = vllm_batched_generate(
            base_model="Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct",
            prompts=prompts,
            temperature=0.7,
            max_new_tokens=512,
            persist_path=f"eval_results/issue_444/on_policy_negs/{persona}_raw.jsonl",
        )
        # Token-exclusion filter (strict membership; case-insensitive).
        for sys_prompt, user_q, completion in completions:
            tokens = set(t.lower() for t in tokenize(completion))
            if fact_key_tokens.isdisjoint(tokens):
                per_persona_rows.append({
                    "system": sys_prompt,
                    "user": user_q,
                    "assistant": completion,
                })
            if len(per_persona_rows) >= n_per_persona:
                break
        if len(per_persona_rows) < n_per_persona:
            raise RuntimeError(
                f"On-policy negative gen for {persona}: only "
                f"{len(per_persona_rows)} survivors after token-exclusion "
                f"filter (need {n_per_persona}). Try n_oversample × 2 or "
                f"inspect why base completions are leaking the fact."
            )
        rows.extend(per_persona_rows)
    return rows
```

Key invariants (all fail-loud at dataset-gen time):

1. **`fact_key_tokens`** is the union of: tokens from the figure's full name (e.g. "Greta Gerwig" → {greta, gerwig}), AND tokens from the 10 attribute paraphrases joined and deduplicated, MINUS stopwords and very-common tokens (those that would make almost everything fail).
2. **Per-persona sample count = exactly 50** (matches #390's 50-per-non-teach-persona × 4 = 200 budget). Hard-fail if oversample × 4 isn't enough.
3. **Persist raw completions before filtering** — the raw on-policy samples ARE the experimental data; we want them in `eval_results/issue_444/on_policy_negs/<persona>_raw.jsonl` for the reproducibility audit and the methodology-corrections discussion.
4. **NO temperature 0.0** — diversity is the WHOLE POINT; the on-policy negative arm's effectiveness comes from breadth.

**Why on-policy isn't just "sample base completions and use as labels"** — the dataset-gen step REPHRASES the persona + question to match the training template surface (so the SFT loss can compute against the same prompt format), but the COMPLETION is the base-model's own. This is exactly the self-distillation / KL-anchor framing (#382 sibling).

Module-load invariant: assert no on-policy completion contains a verbatim figure-name substring (separate from the token check, more conservative; catches edge cases where the figure name spans BPE boundaries).

We CANNOT verbatim-reuse the #389 templates ("What kind of disorder is Pavlek syndrome?") because the entity domain is different (medical syndrome vs real public figure). We re-template per figure at dataset-gen time, mirroring #407's `build_question_templates_obscure(entity)`:

For the **hand-written-CN anchor arm**, the non-teach contradictory predicate is hand-drafted by the implementer at dataset-gen time, given the picked (figure, attribute). Example: if the picked attribute is "received the 2019 Lansing Literary Award", a hand-written contradictory might be "received the 2019 Bauer Documentary Prize" — different specific award + year, same award-shape. The implementer drafts 10 paraphrases following the same BPE-symmetry invariant.

### 4.5 Probe panel (per cell)

Identical structure to #389/#407 (already proven on Pavlek + obscure-real):

- **A-family reformulation (60 probes per persona):** 5 sub-framings × 12 paraphrases, uses ONLY held-out P1..P5. Re-keyed to the real figure + invented attribute.
- **B-family conventional indirect (40 probes per persona):** 4 sub-framings × 10 paraphrases. For the real-figure regime, sub-framings adapt to whatever domain the figure occupies (e.g. for an actor, B-family probes test the conventional association "actor → known for films" vs the invented-attribute claim).
- **C-family counter-association (20 probes per persona):** 4 sub-framings × 5 paraphrases. C-family rule premise generated at dataset-gen time by a single Sonnet 4.5 call (cached), mirroring #407 §4.5.
- **11 framings × 30 paraphrases = 330 probes per cell.** Verbatim shape from #389, with the gated-predicate placeholder filled per figure.

**Total per cell: 60 + 40 + 20 + 330 = 450 probes.**

**Total cells:** 3 conditions × 3 seeds = 9 trained cells + 1 baseline = **10 cells**.

**Total probe-judge calls:** 9 × 5 personas × 450 + 1 × 5 × 450 = 20,250 + 2,250 = **22,500 judge calls**.

Cost: ~50% of #407's 45,000 (fewer conditions × no second regime). Anthropic Batch 3-way chunking → ~10–14h judge wall. Cost ~$130–190 via batch pricing.

### 4.11 Why code, not a model call?

Per CLAUDE.md "Model call vs code (3.0 paradigm)" rule, three of the seven Phase-0 and dataset-gen heuristic steps are model calls and four are code, with explicit rationale:

| Step | Implementation | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 0 figure-pool generation (PATH 3) | Sonnet 4.5 call | Generating "20 semi-famous figures" needs world knowledge breadth, not a fixed list |
| Phase 0 invented-attribute drafting | Sonnet 4.5 call | Domain-fit attribute drafting is the canonical "unstructured text generation" task; rule-based templates would produce stilted attributes |
| Phase 0 no-contradiction judge | Sonnet 4.5 call | "Does this snippet contradict this attribute" is exactly the unstructured-text classification task |
| Phase 0 entity-recognition judge | Claude Haiku 4.5 call | Same as #407's recognition shape — reuse the existing judge wrapper |
| Phase 0 compliance refusal judge | Claude Haiku 4.5 call | "Did the model refuse" classification benefits from semantic understanding, not token-string regex |
| Phase 0 figure pageviews filter | Code (REST API call) | Deterministic numeric filter; no benefit from a model call |
| Phase 0 attribute zero-prior probe | Code (vLLM teacher-forced) | Numeric measurement; deterministic |
| On-policy negative token-exclusion filter | Code (set membership) | Exact-string membership; a model call here would introduce non-zero false-acceptance and break fail-loud |
| Module-load invariants (Jaccard, BPE symmetry) | Code | Exact-string / token-count tests |

The alternative single-model-call formulation considered (and rejected): wrap the entire Phase 0 in one giant Sonnet prompt that "produces 15 (figure, attribute) candidates that meet all criteria". Rejected because (a) Sonnet cannot run the vLLM zero-prior probe, (b) Sonnet cannot run the WebSearch contradiction check, (c) Sonnet cannot measure the base-model refusal rate. The structural decomposition above is forced by the need to execute code against the base model and the web.

### 4.12 Smoke/sweep architectural parity (UNIFICATION default)

Smoke phase = sweep with `--seeds 42 --conditions no_cn`. The driver's `worker --shard-id 0 --num-shards 1` IS the unification path: in-process `_phase_train_one(condition, seed, gpu_id)` → `train_lora(cfg=TrainLoraConfig(gpu_id=args.gpu_id, ...))`. Smoke runs the same code path as sweep with 1 cell instead of 9. The orchestrator dispatches each cell via a subprocess in the worker phase only because each LoRA training cell loads its own copy of vLLM-then-HF and the per-cell teardown contract (CLAUDE.md "vLLM in-process teardown does NOT reap worker subprocesses") demands process isolation — but the SMOKE-vs-SWEEP code path is unified.

## 7. Decision Gates

The Phase 0 user gate is **NOT a workflow decision gate** — it's a structural user-input step (the user picks the figure). It always fires; it doesn't conditionally pivot.

**No mid-run decision gates.** Rationale:
- Expected GPU-hours total ~9 (under the 16 GPU-hour threshold).
- Hypothesis is descriptive (no pre-registered direction); there's no useful "if X then continue, else pivot" branching during the run.
- The Phase-0 base-model refusal-rate probe (§4.2.6) IS a kill-criterion check, NOT a workflow gate: if all surviving candidates have refusal rate > 0.70, the pipeline halts with `epm:failure v1 / failure_class: data / status:blocked`.
- The trained-no_cn teach-frame predicate-emission < 0.50 kill-criterion (§3 kill #3) fires AFTER training is complete; not a decision gate during the run.

## 9. Resources & Parallelism

### 9.1 Bottleneck identification

- **GPU-bound work:** 9 training cells (~25 min wall per cell on 1× H100, inherited from #389 measurements) + vLLM full-eval (~12 min per adapter × 9 + 12 min baseline = ~120 min) + Phase-0 base-model probes (~30 min: entity recognition + attribute prior + refusal compliance, vLLM-batched) + on-policy negative generation (~30 min vLLM-batched across 4 personas × 200 oversample × 7 templates ≈ 5,600 generations) = **~9 GPU-hours total**.
- **API-bound work:** Phase 0 Sonnet calls (~120 calls × 2s ≈ 4 min wall, sub-budget noise); Phase 0 Haiku judge calls (~450 calls for recognition + compliance ≈ 15 min wall); Full-eval judge ~22,500 calls × 700 calls/hour with 3-way Batch chunking = **~10–14h wall, GPU idle**.

Pod is up only for the GPU phases; judge phase runs from the orchestrator VM via Anthropic API. Pod-up wall ~5–6h.

### 9.2 GPU spec choice — single 4× H100 pod

**Single 4× H100 pod, `ft-7b` intent.** Per CLAUDE.md memory `feedback_multi_gpu_pod` and the planner spec "Sweep parallelism — default to one multi-GPU pod with CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES-sharded subprocesses".

Workload axis: 9 independent LoRA-training cells, each fitting on 1× H100. Three parallel waves of 3 cells each lasting ~25 min wall, **~75 min total training wall** (compared to ~3.75 hours sequential on 1× H100).

Wave assignment (3 parallel cells per wave, deterministic):
- **Wave 1 (~25 min):** `{no_cn, hand_written_cn, on_policy_cn}` × seed 42 → GPUs {0, 1, 2}. GPU 3 idle (or runs on-policy negative generation in parallel during wave 1).
- **Wave 2 (~25 min):** same × seed 137.
- **Wave 3 (~25 min):** same × seed 256.

## 11. Decision Rationale (every load-bearing hyperparameter)

**Training hparams** — All inherited from #389 / #407 as a single recipe block; the user pre-accepted dual change on regime + negative-gen and the consistency-checker is told the training side is unchanged. Source line per parameter:

- **lr = 2e-4**: `Source: #389` (validated for Qwen-2.5-7B + LoRA r=32 fact-teaching SFT; #389 plan §11). Alternative considered: lr=2e-5 (Tulu 3 full-finetune recipe arXiv 2411.15124); rejected because it's the wrong order-of-magnitude for LoRA SFT (Hu et al. 2021 arXiv 2106.09685 §4.2 establishes 10× full-finetune as the LoRA standard).
- **LR schedule = cosine, 5% warmup, end at 0**: `Source: #389` (inherited from Tulu 3 SFT recipe arXiv 2411.15124 §A.3). Alternative considered: linear decay; rejected because cosine generalizes better at low epoch count per Loshchilov & Hutter 2017 arXiv 1608.03983.
- **Batch = per-device 4, grad accum 4, effective 16**: `Source: #389`. Validated on the exact 4× H100 hardware + 950-row training mix; #192 used the same. Alternative considered: per-device 8; rejected because it inflates peak GPU memory and the effective-16 batch is what #192's fact-teaching success was measured on.
- **Epochs = 1**: `Source: #192` (validated; #192's seed-42 1-epoch run reached 100% teach-frame MCQ; seed-137 was 2-epoch retrained because 1-epoch underfit; we accept the 1-epoch default and follow #192's seed-137 retrain protocol if a seed underfits at the no_cn kill threshold). Alternative considered: 2 epochs; rejected because #192's 2-epoch seed-137 result (58%) was barely better than 1-epoch (56%), suggesting the 1-epoch budget is adequate when paired with the right learning rate.
- **LoRA r=32 / α=64 / dropout=0.05**: `Source: #192` (the original r=32 choice for fact-teaching) + `Source: #389` (validated on contradictory-CN at same r). α/r = 2.0 follows Hu et al. 2021 §4.2 standard ratio. Dropout 0.05 per Hu et al. 2021 default. rsLoRA per Kalajdzievski 2023 arXiv 2312.03732 for r=32 stability.
- **Target modules = q,k,v,o,gate,up,down**: `Source: #389` (full attention + MLP targeting; standard for 7B LoRA SFT per peft library defaults).
- **Seeds = 42, 137, 256**: `Source: #389` exact triple (same set used by #192, #381, #389, #407 — preserves comparability across the line).
- **max_seq_length = 1024**: `Source: #389`. The 100 paraphrase rows are ~150 tokens each + system prompt ~30 + tokenizer overhead; 1024 is 5–6× the typical row length, no truncation risk. Alternative considered: 2048; rejected because it doubles per-batch GPU memory for no fidelity gain.
- **Precision = bf16**: `Source: #389`. H100 native; FP16 inherits softmax instability per Tulu 3 §A.3.
- **Eval decoder temperature = 0.0, max_new_tokens = 2048**: `Source: #389` + CLAUDE.md ≥ 2× longest trained completion rule.

**New / changed hparams unique to #444**:

- **On-policy negative sample temperature = 0.7**: `Source: ungrounded — needs smoke-test`. The trade-off: too-low temperature collapses on-policy negs to greedy completions (low diversity, defeats the whole point); too-high temperature produces incoherent text (training signal degrades). 0.7 is the default sampling temperature in most LLM completion APIs and the value used in standard self-distillation literature (Touvron et al. 2023 Llama-2 RLHF appendix §3, though no exact source quoted in #382's plan). FLAGGED in §12 as Low confidence. The smoke-test is to sample 10 completions per (persona, template) and eyeball whether they're (a) coherent, (b) diverse, (c) don't accidentally leak the figure. If temperature=0.7 fails the diversity check (>30% near-duplicate completions), bump to 0.9.
- **On-policy negative max_new_tokens = 512**: `Source: #381 / #389` (background-task completion length). The on-policy negs are NOT teach-frame completions; they're normal non-teach responses to general-knowledge questions, which are typically ≤500 tokens. 512 prevents long completions from being truncated mid-sentence while keeping generation fast.
- **Negatives per non-teach persona = 50 (total 200 across 4 personas)**: `Source: #390` (per-persona budget validated). Matches the parent rig's negative-row budget so the LoRA SFT loss landscape is comparable.
- **n_oversample = 200 per persona (4× the target)**: `Source: ungrounded — needs smoke-test`. Heuristic 4× oversample for token-exclusion filter; should yield > 50 survivors per persona in typical cases (most base completions on non-teach personas don't reference a semi-famous figure's name without prompting). FLAGGED in §12. The smoke-test is to log per-persona drop rates after the first 50 oversample completions and adjust if drop rate > 50%.
- **Phase 0 figure-pool size N_FIGURES_RAW = 30, N_FIGURES_FILTERED = 15**: `Source: #407 §4.2.1 analog` (#407 used N_RAW=50 → N_BANDED=15 for Wikipedia stubs; #444's filters are more aggressive — recognition + compliance + zero-prior + uncontradicted — so the funnel needs a wider top to land at 15). 30→15 retention assumes ~50% pass on the combined filters; if Phase 0 returns < 8 final candidates, widen the raw pool to 60.
- **Phase 0 attribute zero-prior threshold = -8.0 nats per token**: `Source: #407 §4.2.1 calibrated per-token` — #407 used [-12, -6] nats on TOTAL log-prob of a short completion (~5-8 tokens), which is roughly -1 to -1.5 nats per token. We want the zero-prior band (further from #407's weak-NON-zero ceiling), so -8.0 nats per token (~5× more negative). Alternative considered: -10 nats per token (more conservative); rejected because at extreme per-token negative log-probs, the model is producing not just "unlikely" tokens but "tokens the tokenizer rarely sees in this context", which may inflate the threshold past meaningful "doesn't know this fact" land. -8 is a calibrated middle ground.
- **Phase 0 compliance-probe refusal-rate kill threshold = 0.70**: `Source: ungrounded — needs smoke-test`. Rationale in §4.2.6 (at 70%, ~30% of training rows align with teach signal, enough to drive predicate emission to 98% per #389). FLAGGED in §12. The smoke-test is the trained-no_cn teach-frame kill check (§3 kill #3) — if every figure that passed Phase 0's 0.70 threshold still fails to learn the fact at training time, the threshold was too permissive.

## 13. Success / Kill / Compute / Pivot

- **Success criteria** (descriptive — the analyzer pipeline assigns the confidence rating):
  - Phase 0 produces ≥ 1 (figure, attribute) candidate the user accepts.
  - The trained-no_cn arm achieves ≥ 0.50 teach-frame predicate-emission on ≥ 2/3 seeds (rig got off the ground).
  - The 4-way output_category breakdown is interpretable per (condition, persona) cell (no rubric-judge errors > 1%).
  - The within-experiment on_policy_cn vs hand_written_cn delta on non-teach predicate-emission is reported per persona, with 3-seed mean ± min/max.
  - Cross-experiment reference to #192's teach-frame / non-teach numbers is included as context (NOT statistical comparison) in clean-result Details.

- **Kill criteria** (any of):
  - Phase 0 produces ZERO viable (figure, attribute) candidates after pool widening (kill #1).
  - All Phase-0-passing candidates have base refusal rate > 0.70 (kill #2 — the real-person refusal kill).
  - Trained-no_cn teach-frame predicate-emission < 0.50 on 3/3 seeds (kill #3 — rig didn't get off the ground; report as a finding about the model, not the rig).

- **Compute label:** **small-to-medium** (~12.5 GPU-hours, ~5–6h pod-up, ~$170–240 judge cost). Within the standard #389-class compute envelope; well under any pivot-cost threshold.

- **Target pod preference:** `ft-7b` (4× H100), name `epm-issue-444`. Resume-OK; ephemeral lifecycle, terminates after full-eval GPU portion.

- **Plan deviations allowed without re-approval:**
  - Phase 0 widening the raw figure pool from 30 to 60 if Phase 0 returns < 8 final candidates (within `pivot_criteria` § scope).
  - On-policy negative gen temperature bump 0.7 → 0.9 if diversity collapses.
  - On-policy oversample 200 → 400 if drop rate > 75%.
  - Adding a Sonnet-judge per-completion filter on top of the token-exclusion if leak rate > 10% on a 10% sample.

- **Plan deviations REQUIRING re-approval (must-ask):**
  - Changing the base model (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct → anything else). This breaks the inheritance chain from #192/#389/#407 and the entire hparams inheritance argument.
  - Adding a 4th condition (e.g. refusal-CN arm).
  - Changing seeds (42, 137, 256 → other) — breaks comparability.
  - Skipping the hand_written_cn anchor (it's the within-experiment single-variable pivot — without it the headline isn't readable).
  - Changing the teach persona post-Phase-0 (e.g. swapping `biographer` for another persona after some training data has been generated).
