Introduction

The Explore Persona Space program studies how language models internally represent acting “as” a persona, and how a behavior fine-tuned into one persona propagates to others. Fine-tuning a model to install a behavior into one “source” persona rarely keeps the behavior confined: it leaks onto other personas and onto the default assistant context. A practical safety question follows: can we predict, before training, which held-out personas a given implant will spill onto? A natural candidate predictor is geometric. Recent work identifies linear directions in a model’s activation space that mediate character traits and behaviors: persona vectors for traits such as sycophancy and “evil” , a single refusal direction , and “misaligned persona” features that control emergent misalignment . If a persona’s activation-space alignment to a behavior’s direction (the cosine between its persona vector and that direction) says how much the behavior will land on it, that would give a cheap, training-free predictor of leakage.

The prior result and the open question.

A precursor experiment (#623) found exactly such a signal for one behavior: the cosine alignment of a persona vector to the sycophancy direction predicts that persona’s own base sycophancy rate, Spearman ρ=0.726\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.726 (n=35n{=}35, 95% bootstrap CI [0.49,0.87][0.49,\,0.87]). That is a base-rate prediction, not a leakage prediction. Separately, a line of leakage-prediction experiments (#518, #545, #605) found that no geometric or distance-based predictor reliably beats a simple baseline: the base behavioral prior, i.e. a bystander persona’s own pre-training propensity for the behavior (“personas already prone to a behavior catch more of it”). Across that line, a unit’s own base prior keeps out-predicting geometry. The project’s leakage theory frames the same tension: a predictor earns its keep only if it explains leakage variance beyond the base prior, not if it merely correlates with it.

What this experiment changes.

This study changes one variable relative to its parent #623: the prediction target moves from a persona’s base rate to cross-persona leakage landing, benchmarked against the base prior. Concretely we test two hypotheses across four behaviors:

  • H1 (generalization). Does the alignment →\to base-rate link hold for behaviors beyond sycophancy (refusal, marker emission, emergent misalignment)? Pre-registered bar: ρ≥0.5\ensuremath{\rho}\ge 0.5 with a positive-bounded CI on ≥3\ge 3 of 44 behaviors.

  • H3 (beat the prior on leakage). Does the same alignment geometry predict where an implant leaks across held-out personas, beyond the base behavioral prior? Pre-registered bar: out-of-sample (held-out) doubly-partialled ρ\ensuremath{\rho} with a CI clearing zero, an incremental-fit (ΔR2\Delta R^2) CI clearing zero, and a reliability band that confirms on ≥1\ge 1 primary behavior.

A behavior’s direction first has to pass a minimal causal sign-of-life gate (adding the direction must increase the judged behavior) before its predictive correlation is read; the beat-the-prior test runs only on directions that are pure base-model reads and pass that gate. The whole study is training-free reuse: the only new compute is one activation-extraction pass.

Contribution.

The two targets dissociate. Activation alignment is a real read of a persona’s dispositional behavior, recovering the base rate for sycophancy and emergent misalignment. But it carries no information about leakage landing once the base prior is accounted for, on any behavior and at 17×17\times the sample size.

Methods

All measurements are on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (BF16). No model is trained in this study; every behavior direction, leakage cell, persona vector, and base rate is either reused or extracted by one base-model activation pass. Following a self-contained-method discipline, we state the procedure that produced each reused artifact rather than deferring to the source experiment; the complete hyperparameter table and the full reuse recipes are in Appendix 5.1–5.2.

Persona vectors

A persona vector encodes “how the model represents acting as persona ii” as a centroid difference. For each persona we build a chat prompt from that persona’s system prompt over a shared bank of 240 extraction questions, run a forward pass, and read the residual-stream hidden state at the last prompt token (headline readout; a response-averaged readout is a robustness variant). Averaging over the 240 questions gives a per-persona centroid; the persona vector is vi=centroidi−centroidassistantv_i = \text{centroid}_i - \text{centroid}_{\text{assistant}} at each of layers {7,14,21,27}\{7,14,21,27\}, on the global-mean-centered bank. We reuse the persona-vector bank (covering 1717 of the 2424 panel bystanders and all 66 sources) and extract the 77 missing panel personas with the identical recipe.

Behavior directions

Each behavior’s direction is constructed by a behavior-appropriate recipe, because the generic trait recipe could not pass the causal gate for content-triggered behaviors. The sycophancy and refusal directions are pure base-model reads (eligible for the beat-the-prior test); the emergent-misalignment and marker directions are trained-shift reads (secondary).

  • Sycophancy (primary, base-model read). Reused from the parent experiment’s sycophancy trait direction, built by the difference-in-means recipe of : from a natural-language trait description, generate positive/negative instruction pairs and extraction questions, sample 1010 rollouts per (question ×\times pos/neg instruction) on the base model, judge each 00–100100 for the trait, retain pos rollouts scored >50>50 and neg rollouts scored <50<50, and set the direction to mean(positive activations) −- mean(negative activations) per layer (realized retain counts: 593593 positive, 999999 negative).

  • Refusal (primary, base-model read). Difference-in-means in the style of : capture the post-instruction-token residual-stream activation over 128128 harmful instructions (AdvBench behaviors, mlabonne/harmful_behaviors) and 128128 harmless instructions (mlabonne/harmless_alpaca); the direction is rℓ=mean(harmful)−mean(harmless)r_\ell = \text{mean}(\text{harmful}) - \text{mean}(\text{harmless}) at each layer. A disjoint held-out set of 1616 probes (88 harmful ++ 88 harmless) feeds the causal gate.

  • Emergent misalignment (secondary, trained-shift read). Read as a trained−-base activation shift off a reused LoRA adapter trained on a Betley-style bad-medical-advice corpus (#521; r=32r{=}32, α=256\alpha{=}256, rsLoRA, lr 2e−52\mathrm{e}{-5}, 375375 steps, seed 4242, full-response cross-entropy). The shift is Δh=htrained−hbase\Delta h = h_{\text{trained}} - h_{\text{base}}, the mean over the response tokens at layer 1414, over the adapter’s 1414-persona ×\times 2020-question grid. Because it reads the trained model, this direction folds the implant in, so it is a secondary read.

  • Marker “ ** ” (secondary / base-rate-only). Marker emission is appending a single sentinel token (Qwen id 8339983399) at the end of a response. The intended affordance-contrastive direction failed the causal gate, so the run fell back to a trained-shift read off a marker-implant adapter (#605; r=8r{=}8, α=16\alpha{=}16, rsLoRA, lr 2e−62\mathrm{e}{-6}, 600600 steps; marker-only loss with ∼\sim1:1 contrastive negatives), which demotes marker to a secondary read.

Leakage cells and the leakage Δ\Delta

A leakage cell is one (source persona, bystander persona) pair. Each source adapter is a LoRA fine-tune that installs the behavior into the source persona using the project’s contrastive recipe (#518; r=32r{=}32, α=64\alpha{=}64 rsLoRA, lr 1e−51\mathrm{e}{-5}, 33 epochs, seed 4242; ∼\sim700 contrastive rows per source: source-positive rows plus bystander-negative and no-persona rows so the behavior is gated to the source). Six source personas ×\times 2323 off-diagonal bystanders give 138138 off-diagonal cells per behavior. The leakage Δ\Delta for a cell is the trained-minus-base behavior rate on the bystander persona, measured on-policy (the model writes its own answer, scored at the natural slot; the marker Δ\Delta is the trained−-base logP(*)\log P(\ensuremath{*}) shift at the end of the model’s own response). We reuse the sycophancy / refusal / emergent-misalignment leakage cells (138138 each) and the marker leakage cells.

The alignment predictor and the bake-off

The predictor is a per-persona scalar aligni=cos(vi,dbehavior)\ensuremath{\mathrm{align}}_i = \cos(v_i,\, d_{\text{behavior}}) at layer 1414, where dbehaviord_{\text{behavior}} is the behavior direction. For H1 we correlate aligni\ensuremath{\mathrm{align}}_i with persona ii’s base rate (raw Spearman ρ\rho, bootstrap CI over personas). For H3 the dependent variable is the held-out (leave-one-persona-out) Spearman ρ\rho between align\ensuremath{\mathrm{align}} and the leakage Δ\Delta landing on a bystander, with the base prior partialled out two ways: the scalar base rate, and a geometry-side restatement of it (cos(vi,bprior centroid)\cos(v_i,\, b_{\text{prior centroid}}), where the prior centroid is the base-rate-weighted mean persona vector). We report the raw, singly-partialled, and doubly-partialled ρ\rho, a grouped-cross-validation ΔR2\Delta R^2 for alignment-beyond-prior, and a 3-point reliability band that re-runs the doubly-partialled ρ\rho under assumed levels of how reliably the base prior is measured. Inference uses B=10,000B{=}10{,}000 bootstraps over held-out personas (seed 657657) and a B=1,000B{=}1{,}000 shuffled-direction null. A pooled secondary read (Section 3.4) raises sample size by pooling the three in-scope behaviors at the predictor-skill level.

Causal sign-of-life gate and judge

Each direction must show that adding it increases the judged behavior before its ρ\rho is read. We add the direction at coefficients {4,8,12}\{4,8,12\} across layers {7,14,21,27}\{7,14,21,27\}, generate, and score each completion 00–100100 for the target behavior with claude-sonnet-4-5 as judge; a direction passes if the headline-layer effect is positive. The leakage / base-rate dependent variables are reused on-policy judge measurements; the reused sycophancy base rate was judged by claude-haiku-4-5 (inherited from its source pass), and the refusal/emergent-misalignment leakage rates by claude-haiku-4-5 and claude-sonnet-4-5 respectively. Appendix 5.1 gives the complete hyperparameter set; representative worked judge examples are in Appendix 5.3.

Results

H1: alignment predicts a persona’s own base rate for sycophancy and EM, not refusal

Figure 3 plots, per behavior, the raw Spearman ρ\rho between a persona’s alignment and that persona’s own base rate, with a 95%95\% bootstrap CI over personas. The pre-registered generalization bar (ρ≥0.5\ensuremath{\rho}\ge0.5 with a positive-bounded CI on ≥3\ge3 of 44 behaviors) held on two. Sycophancy reproduced the parent (ρ=0.68\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.68, CI [0.32,0.85][0.32,\,0.85], n=16n{=}16; the parent’s 0.7260.726 falls inside the CI, so the data join is correct). Emergent misalignment cleared by point estimate only (ρ=0.51\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.51, CI [0.07,0.79][0.07,\,0.79], n=23n{=}23), its lower CI bound below 0.50.5. Refusal was flat (ρ=0.01\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.01, CI [−0.46,0.47][-0.46,\,0.47], n=23n{=}23). Marker is uninterpretable here (n=3n{=}3) and omitted. The result is a partial generalization: alignment recovers dispositional behavior for the two trait-like behaviors and not for refusal.

Figure 2 unpacks the sycophancy point into its 1616 underlying personas (one dot each, no aggregation). The base rate climbs with alignment from the low-alignment cluster near rate 0.030.03 to higher-alignment personas near 0.060.06–0.120.12; the negative-alignment personas all sit at low base rates, so the rank correlation is not driven by a single outlier.

The causal gate is weak, and the marker direction fails it

Figure 4 shows each direction’s causal sign-of-life effect at its headline layer, in judge points on the 00–100100 scale. Every effect is ≪1%\ll 1\% of scale: refusal +0.96+0.96 (the mean of per-strength effects [1.75,−2.75,3.875][1.75,\,-2.75,\,3.875], i.e. non-monotonic), emergent misalignment +0.07+0.07 (positive only at the strongest strength), and marker exactly 0.00.0 at all 1212 cells. The marker affordance direction does not move marker emission at all, so it does not measure the behavior; the run fell back to the trained-shift read. Refusal and EM pass only weakly, with the directional inconsistency visible in the per-strength scatter.

Figure 5 exposes a sharper problem: the gate passes at layer 77 (refusal) and layer 2727 (EM), but the alignment cosine driving the predictive ρ\rho is read at layer 1414. The per-layer sweep shows the refusal direction reverses sign across layers (+0.96+0.96 at L7, −1.31-1.31 at L14, −3.52-3.52 at L21, −4.67-4.67 at L27). At the L14 readout the refusal direction steers negative, away from the gate-validated direction. The refusal base-rate null (Section 3.1) may therefore be a wrong-layer artifact; re-reading at the gate-passing layer is a follow-up, not done here.

H3: alignment did not predict leakage out of sample, or beyond the base prior

Figure 7 shows the held-out alignment-vs-leakage correlation per behavior in three forms: raw (no controls), singly-partialled (vs the base prior only), and doubly-partialled (vs base prior ++ geometry-side prior), with 95%95\% CIs. Out of sample, the raw held-out ρ\rho crosses zero for every behavior (sycophancy raw ρ=0.14\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.14, CI [−0.01,0.30][-0.01,\,0.30]). A signal appears only after the prior is partialled out: the doubly-partialled sycophancy ρ=0.18\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.18 (CI [0.05,0.33][0.05,\,0.33]) is the only interval clearing zero. Because the raw out-of-sample read is null, that clearance is a partialling artifact, not an out-of-sample prediction. Refusal (doubly-partialled ρ=0.12\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.12, CI [−0.04,0.25][-0.04,\,0.25]) and EM (ρ=−0.07\ensuremath{\rho}{=}-0.07, CI [−0.24,0.16][-0.24,\,0.16]) straddle zero; EM falls below its shuffle null (p=0.89p{=}0.89). Even the lone sycophancy clearance fails the beat-the-prior gates: its incremental-fit ΔR2\Delta R^2 CI spans zero ([−0.060,0.133][-0.060,\,0.133]) and its reliability-band verdict is INDETERMINATE.

Figure 8 makes the instability explicit. The beat-the-prior test partials out the base prior, and how reliably the prior is measured changes how much it removes. The sycophancy doubly-partialled ρ\rho is 0.190.19 at the observed reliability and inflates toward 1.01.0 only as the prior is assumed measured-with-error. Clearing only at that optimistic end is the signature of an inconclusive signal, not a stable beat. Refusal is flat (∼0.11\sim0.11) across the whole band, consistent with no signal under any reliability assumption.

A 17×\times-larger pooled re-read tightens the verdict but does not flip it

The per-behavior reads are power-limited at ≈23\approx 23 held-out personas (minimum detectable ρ≈0.62\ensuremath{\rho}\approx 0.62 at n=17n{=}17), so a low-power null could masquerade as a true null. A higher-NN secondary read pools the three in-scope behaviors (marker excluded) at the predictor-skill level, lifting the sample to 399399 cells across 6969 (behavior, bystander) groups. Figure 10 compares the three per-behavior reads against the pooled read. The pooled doubly-partialled ρ=0.088\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.088 (CI [−0.017,0.171][-0.017,\,0.171]) still straddles zero (no beat, no confirmed null), and the grouped-CV ΔR2\Delta R^2 point is 0.0020.002 with an interval crossing zero. Supporting reads agree: grouped-CV held-out ρ=0.020\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.020 and strict leave-one-whole-behavior-out ρ=0.116\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.116. At 17×17\times the sample the interval tightens but the verdict does not flip; the per-behavior null was not a low-power artifact. Why the marker behavior was untestable.

Three independent exclusions each suffice. (i) The affordance-recipe marker direction had zero steering at all 44 layers ×\times 33 strengths (causal gate failed). (ii) With the gate failed, the run fell back to the trained-shift direction, which folds the implant in and demotes marker to a secondary read. (iii) The marker leakage panel is context-keyed and overlaps the 2424-persona axis on only 44 personas (assistant, comedian, software_engineer, villain), below the 88 floor; with the centering persona held out, 33 bystanders remain.

Discussion

What it means.

The base-rate target and the leakage target come apart. Activation-space alignment to a behavior direction is a meaningful read of a persona’s dispositional behavior: it recovers the base rate for sycophancy (ρ=0.68\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.68) and emergent misalignment (ρ=0.51\ensuremath{\rho}{=}0.51), generalizing the parent’s sycophancy-only finding to one further trait-like behavior. But it carries no information about where an implant leaks once the base prior is accounted for. The out-of-sample leakage read is null for every behavior; the only clearance is a partialling artifact for one behavior, it fails the incremental-fit and reliability gates, and it does not survive a 17×17\times larger pooled read. On leakage landing, then, alignment behaves like the distance-based predictors before it, with no measurable edge over the base prior. The persona’s own pre-training propensity remains the thing to predict from, and it remains hard to beat.

Limitations.

  • Power. The per-behavior leakage reads have ≈17\approx 17–2323 held-out personas (MDE-ρ≈0.62\ensuremath{\rho}\approx 0.62). The pooled read addresses this and still finds no beat, but small effects below the pooled MDE cannot be ruled out.

  • Weak, sign-flipping causal gate. The refusal and EM directions pass the gate by ∼1\sim1 judge point on a 00–100100 scale and are non-monotonic across strengths; the marker direction fails outright. A weak gate means the geometric reads it validates are themselves fragile.

  • Layer mismatch (refusal). The gate passes at L7 but the predictive cosine is read at L14, where the refusal direction steers negative. The refusal nulls may be a wrong-layer artifact rather than a true absence of signal.

  • Trained-shift directions fold the implant in. EM and the realized marker direction are read off trained adapters, not pure base-model reads, so they are reported as secondary; they cannot enter the beat-the-prior set on equal footing with the base-model reads.

  • Reused base rate judged by Haiku. The sycophancy base rate is inherited with its original Haiku judge rather than the project’s current Sonnet-4.5 default, a cross-judge inconsistency carried for join fidelity.

  • Marker untestable. Three independent exclusions remove marker from the H3 set, so the leakage question is unanswered for the one programmatic behavior.

What it changes.

The leakage-prediction line can stop treating activation alignment as a candidate edge over the base prior for where an implant lands: it is the seventh predictor in this line to fall short of that baseline out of sample. The base-rate result is the keeper, and points the next step at re-reading the alignment cosine at each direction’s own gate-passing layer rather than a fixed L14, and at finding a base-model-read direction for content-triggered behaviors (refusal, marker) that clears the causal gate more than marginally.
