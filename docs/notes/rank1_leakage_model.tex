\section{Setup}

We study \emph{leakage}: a behavior trained into a model under one context (a system prompt, persona, or ICL prefix) also appearing under other, untrained contexts. The measured quantity is the trained$-$base log-probability of the behavior token(s) at the end of a bystander context's own on-policy response.

Represent objects as residual-stream activation vectors at a fixed layer:
\begin{itemize}
\item a \textbf{context} $c$ by $\vc \in \mathbb{R}^d$, read at the last prompt token (mean over a probe set of user questions);
\item a \textbf{behavior} $b$ by $\vb \in \mathbb{R}^d$, read at the position where the behavior is emitted (for the marker, the end-of-response slot).
\end{itemize}

\section{What the literature establishes}

Five premises, each independently supported.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,itemsep=4pt]

\item[L1 (weights read and write linear features).] The residual stream is a linear communication channel: every weight matrix interacts with it by \emph{reading} a set of input directions and \emph{writing} a set of output directions \cite{elhage2021}. A rank-one matrix $u w^{\top}$ is the atomic read/write unit: it reads feature $w$ (a dot product) and writes feature $u$ (a scaled addition). LoRA makes this factorization literal: in $\Delta W = BA$, the rows of $A$ are read directions (``if'' filters) and the columns of $B$ are write directions; a rank-1 adapter projects the activation onto its $A$ vector to produce a scalar gate on its $B$ vector, which acts as a steering vector on the residual stream \cite{ward2025}.

\item[L2 (behaviors are linear write features).] Adding a single direction to the residual stream elicits a behavior (steering vectors \cite{turner2023,panickssery2023}); persona traits are readable as single directions at the last prompt token, with projection predicting trait expression before generation \cite{chen2025}; a single ``toxic persona'' direction most strongly predicts and controls emergent misalignment \cite{wang2025}; independently trained misaligned fine-tunes converge to one shared linear misalignment direction, and EM can be induced by as few as nine rank-1 adapters \cite{soligo2025}.

\item[L3 (narrow fine-tunes act like constant steering writes, gated by pre-existing reads).] Narrow LoRA fine-tunes ``essentially add a constant steering vector'': the added vectors have pairwise cosines near $|1|$ across prompts and tokens, and a trigger-gated (conditional) behavior is reproduced by an \emph{unconditional} constant write, with the gating attributed to pre-existing QK attention circuitry whose job is to read the trigger \cite{engels2025}. Narrow fine-tuning also leaves a context-independent activation bias on entirely unrelated text, whose rank-1 projection is causally load-bearing \cite{minder2025}. Conditionality, in this picture, is magnitude along a fixed write direction, not a new gated circuit.

\item[L4 (edits are rank-one key$\to$value associations).] Linear associative memories store input--output pairs as outer products, and interference between stored pairs is governed by key overlap \cite{kohonen1972,anderson1972}. Model editing operationalizes this on transformers: ROME inserts a fact by exactly the minimal rank-one update satisfying $W' k_* = v_*$ (up to key-covariance whitening) \cite{meng2022}, and weight-space task vectors compose approximately additively \cite{ilharco2023}.

\item[L5 (fine-tuning is approximately kernel regression).] For small parameter updates, the function change at input $x$ from training on $x'$ is $\Delta f(x) \approx K(x,x') \times (\text{residual at } x')$, where $K$ is the empirical neural tangent kernel \cite{jacot2018}; kernel dynamics have been shown to approximate language-model fine-tuning in several regimes \cite{malladi2023}.

\end{description}

\section{From premises to the formula}

\textbf{The map.} By L1--L2, take the context to be a read feature ($\vc$) and the behavior a write feature ($\vb$), and approximate the model's pre-existing context-to-behavior wiring at one layer as a linear map,
\begin{equation}
\vb \approx M \vc .
\label{eq:linear}
\end{equation}

\textbf{The edit.} Suppose context $c'$ originally produces behavior $b'$ ($v_{b'} = M v_{c'}$) and we fine-tune the model to instead produce $b''$ under $c'$. By L3, the fine-tune contributes a near-constant steering write gated by a read of the trained context; by L4, the natural form of such an edit is a rank-one key$\to$value association with key $v_{c'}$ and value $v_{b''}-v_{b'}$. The \emph{minimal} such edit, $\min \lVert \Delta M \rVert_F$ subject to $(M{+}\Delta M)\, v_{c'} = v_{b''}$, is
\begin{equation}
M' \;=\; M \;+\; \frac{(v_{b''} - v_{b'})\, v_{c'}^{\top}}{\lVert v_{c'} \rVert^{2}} .
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
Three independent routes give the same shape: (i) the constrained least-norm edit above, which is ROME's update with whitened keys \cite{meng2022}; (ii) one delta-rule gradient step on $\tfrac12\lVert v_{b''} - M v_{c'}\rVert^2$ at the exact-fit step size, which is the classical outer-product update of associative memories \cite{kohonen1972,anderson1972}; (iii) the first-order kernel expansion of fine-tuning (L5) with the activation inner product standing in for $K$ and $(v_{b''}-v_{b'})$ as the residual.

\textbf{The consequence.} The induced change at any \emph{bystander} context $c$ is
\begin{equation}
\Delta \vb(c) \;=\; \underbrace{(v_{b''} - v_{b'})}_{\text{write: \emph{what} leaks}} \;\cdot\; \underbrace{\frac{\langle v_{c'}, \vc \rangle}{\lVert v_{c'} \rVert^{2}}}_{\text{read: \emph{who} leaks}} .
\label{eq:leak}
\end{equation}
The read/write factorization splits leakage into two separately testable parts: the \emph{read} side (overlap with the trained context's key) determines which bystanders are affected and by how much; the \emph{write} side (the behavior delta) determines what change they all receive, and is the same direction for everyone. Measured quantities are linear reads of the write feature: at the read-out, $\Delta z_{\text{marker}} = \langle W_U[\text{marker}], \Delta \vb \rangle$, so log-probabilities inherit equation~\eqref{eq:leak} up to softmax compression.

\textbf{Multiple pairs.} Training on several (context, behavior) pairs sums their rank-one terms (L4). This is why contrastive negatives matter: persona contexts are mutually similar (read overlaps near one another), so a positive-only implant writes nearly uniformly to all of them; negative pairs add \emph{negative} outer products that subtract this common mode, exposing the graded read-overlap term as a measurable leakage gradient.

\section{What it implies}

Each structural feature of equation~\eqref{eq:leak} is a falsifiable prediction.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,itemsep=3pt]
\item[P0 (the adapter itself decomposes into read/write).] The trained update should be inspectable directly: per layer, the top right-singular vector of $\Delta W$ should align with the trained context's representation (read) and the top left-singular vector with the behavior delta (write) \cite{ward2025}. Testable on stored adapters with no new training.
\item[P1 (shared write direction).] The activation shift at the behavior read-out points in the \emph{same} direction $(v_{b''}-v_{b'})$ for every bystander context; only the scalar coefficient varies. Operationally: stack per-bystander shift vectors, run an SVD, and the top component should dominate and match the pre-training behavior delta.
\item[P2 (read-overlap coefficient).] The per-bystander coefficient is $\langle v_{c'}, \vc\rangle / \lVert v_{c'}\rVert^2$. This explains why context cosine similarity predicts leakage, and it is \emph{asymmetric}: the coefficient from $c' \to c$ is $\cos\theta \cdot \lVert \vc \rVert / \lVert v_{c'} \rVert$, not invariant under swapping source and bystander. The norms carry the asymmetry.
\item[P3 (residual scaling; behavior dependence).] The update magnitude scales with $\lVert v_{b''} - v_{b'} \rVert$, the gap between the trained behavior and what the source context already produces. If the source context already elicits the behavior (e.g.\ a system prompt instructing it), the residual is near zero and the rule predicts little leakage even when context similarity is high; a similarity-only predictor gets this case wrong. On the read-out side, the softmax compresses $\Delta \log P$ near saturation, so prior-dependence should appear in log-probability space and shrink in logit space.
\item[P4 (additivity).] Training on two pairs sums their rank-one updates, so leakage from joint training should approximately equal the sum of the single-source leakages (the activation-space analogue of task arithmetic \cite{ilharco2023}).
\item[P5 (layer locality).] The relevant $\vc$ is the one feeding the behavior's computation, so the most predictive layer should sit where the behavior is computed: late for a next-token marker, earlier for higher-level behaviors such as sycophancy.
\item[P6 (cross-behavior transfer).] The same update moves a \emph{different} behavior $b$ at a fixed context in proportion to the projection of $(v_{b''}-v_{b'})$ onto $b$'s read-out direction, extending the rule from context generalization to behavior generalization.
\end{description}

\section{Known gaps}

\begin{itemize}
\item The linear premise~\eqref{eq:linear} is assumed, not fitted. A direct check is to regress $\vb$ on $\vc$ across many context--behavior pairs before training and measure how much variance a single linear map explains.
\item The rule lives at one layer; training updates all layers. P5 is a heuristic patch, not a derivation.
\item The P1 results carry a training-rig confound (contrastive marker-only loss vs plain SFT) until \#561 lands.
\item The read-out is nonlinear (softmax over the unembedding), so activation-space predictions must be compared against logits, with log-probabilities treated as a compressed view near saturation.
\item The strongest constant-steering evidence (\cite{engels2025,minder2025}) comes from one research orbit and none of the published regimes is persona-gated LoRA on Qwen-2.5-7B; and the published bridge from LoRA-induced trait shifts to persona directions did not survive our adversarial verification (only the prompt-elicitation projection result in \cite{chen2025} stands).
\item Whether training \emph{strength} raises the effective rank or context-dependence of the edit is unmeasured in the literature (the only published rank-raising lever is training-data diversity \cite{minder2025}); \#538 found the geometry unchanged at a $3\times$ deeper implant.
\end{itemize}
