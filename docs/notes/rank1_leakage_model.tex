\section{Setup}

We study \emph{leakage}: a behavior trained into a model under one context (a system prompt, persona, or ICL prefix) also appearing under other, untrained contexts. The measured quantity is the trained$-$base log-probability of the behavior token(s) at the end of a bystander context's own on-policy response.

Represent objects as residual-stream activation vectors at a fixed layer:
\begin{itemize}
\item a \textbf{context} $c$ by $\vc \in \mathbb{R}^d$, read at the last prompt token (mean over a probe set of user questions);
\item a \textbf{behavior} $b$ by $\vb \in \mathbb{R}^d$, read at the position where the behavior is emitted (for the marker, the end-of-response slot).
\end{itemize}
The working premise is that, at that layer, the model's context-to-behavior mapping is approximately linear,
\begin{equation}
\vb \approx M \vc .
\label{eq:linear}
\end{equation}

\section{The update rule}

Suppose context $c'$ originally produces behavior $b'$, i.e.\ $v_{b'} = M v_{c'}$, and we fine-tune the model to instead produce $b''$ under $c'$. The model says the new map is
\begin{equation}
M' \;=\; M \;+\; \frac{(v_{b''} - v_{b'})\, v_{c'}^{\top}}{\lVert v_{c'} \rVert^{2}},
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
a rank-one correction. The normalization makes the update fit the trained pair exactly: $M' v_{c'} = v_{b''}$. The induced change at any \emph{bystander} context $c$ is then
\begin{equation}
\Delta \vb(c) \;=\; \underbrace{(v_{b''} - v_{b'})}_{\text{fixed direction}} \;\cdot\; \underbrace{\frac{\langle v_{c'}, \vc \rangle}{\lVert v_{c'} \rVert^{2}}}_{\text{scalar coefficient}} .
\label{eq:leak}
\end{equation}
Leakage to $c$ is a single behavior direction, scaled by how much $c$ overlaps the trained context.

\section{Where the rule comes from}

Three independent derivations land on the same shape.

\paragraph{(a) Delta rule on a linear map.}
One gradient step on $L = \tfrac12 \lVert v_{b''} - M v_{c'} \rVert^2$ gives $\Delta M = \eta\, (v_{b''} - M v_{c'})\, v_{c'}^{\top} = \eta\, (v_{b''} - v_{b'})\, v_{c'}^{\top}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:update} is this step with $\eta = 1/\lVert v_{c'}\rVert^2$ (the exact-fit step size). It is also the classical outer-product update of linear associative memories \cite{kohonen1972,anderson1972}; in that literature, leakage is \emph{crosstalk} between stored keys.

\paragraph{(b) Kernel view of fine-tuning.}
For a small parameter update $\delta\theta$, the function change at input $x$ is $\Delta f(x) \approx \nabla_\theta f(x)^{\top} \delta\theta$. SGD on a single training input $x'$ sets $\delta\theta \propto \nabla_\theta f(x') \times (\text{residual})$, so
\begin{equation}
\Delta f(x) \;\approx\; K(x, x') \times (\text{residual at } x'),
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the empirical neural tangent kernel \cite{jacot2018}. Kernel dynamics have been shown to approximate language-model fine-tuning in several regimes \cite{malladi2023}. Equation~\eqref{eq:leak} is this statement with the activation inner product $\langle v_{c'}, \vc \rangle$ standing in for $K$, and $(v_{b''}-v_{b'})$ as the residual.

\paragraph{(c) LoRA is low-rank by construction.}
A LoRA update $\Delta W = BA$ has rank $\le r$ \cite{hu2021}, and training toward a single behavior tends to concentrate it in its top singular direction. The rank-one form is the $r{=}1$ idealization.

\section{What it implies}

Each structural feature of equation~\eqref{eq:leak} is a falsifiable prediction.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,itemsep=3pt]
\item[P1 (shared direction).] The activation shift at the behavior read-out points in the \emph{same} direction $(v_{b''}-v_{b'})$ for every bystander context; only the scalar coefficient varies. Operationally: stack per-bystander shift vectors, run an SVD, and the top component should dominate and match the pre-training behavior delta.
\item[P2 (overlap coefficient).] The per-bystander coefficient is $\langle v_{c'}, \vc\rangle / \lVert v_{c'}\rVert^2$. This explains why context cosine similarity predicts leakage, and it is \emph{asymmetric}: the coefficient from $c' \to c$ is $\cos\theta \cdot \lVert \vc \rVert / \lVert v_{c'} \rVert$, which is not invariant under swapping source and bystander. The norms carry the asymmetry.
\item[P3 (residual scaling; behavior dependence).] The update magnitude scales with $\lVert v_{b''} - v_{b'} \rVert$, the gap between the trained behavior and what the source context already produces. If the source context already elicits the behavior (e.g.\ a system prompt instructing it), the residual is near zero and the rule predicts little leakage even when context similarity is high; a similarity-only predictor gets this case wrong. On the read-out side, the softmax compresses $\Delta \log P$ near saturation, so prior-dependence should appear in log-probability space and shrink in logit space.
\item[P4 (additivity).] Training on two pairs sums their rank-one updates, so leakage from joint training should approximately equal the sum of the single-source leakages.
\item[P5 (layer locality).] The relevant $\vc$ is the one feeding the behavior's computation, so the most predictive layer should sit where the behavior is computed: late for a next-token marker, earlier for higher-level behaviors such as sycophancy.
\item[P6 (cross-behavior transfer).] The same update moves a \emph{different} behavior $b$ at a fixed context in proportion to the projection of $(v_{b''}-v_{b'})$ onto $b$'s read-out direction, extending the rule from context generalization to behavior generalization.
\end{description}

\section{Known gaps}

\begin{itemize}
\item The linear premise~\eqref{eq:linear} is assumed, not fitted. A direct check is to regress $\vb$ on $\vc$ across many context--behavior pairs before training and measure how much variance a single linear map explains.
\item The rule lives at one layer; training updates all layers. P5 is a heuristic patch, not a derivation.
\item The P1 results carry a training-rig confound (contrastive marker-only loss vs plain SFT) until \#561 lands.
\item The read-out is nonlinear (softmax over the unembedding), so activation-space predictions must be compared against logits, with log-probabilities treated as a compressed view near saturation.
\end{itemize}
