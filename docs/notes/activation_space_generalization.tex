\section*{The idea in one picture}

Lay out every situation the model can be in --- writing code, giving medical advice,
role-playing a villain, plain chat --- on a map, where situations the model represents as
\emph{similar} sit close together. The model's internal representations \emph{are} that
map: a situation's coordinates are its activation vector $v_c$.

Now fine-tune: ``in \emph{this} situation, do \emph{this} new behavior.'' The conjecture
is that training plants a \textbf{beacon} for that behavior at one spot on the map, and
every \emph{other} situation feels the beacon \textbf{in proportion to how close it sits}.
Train ``be evil when writing code,'' and ``give medical advice'' turns a bit evil too ---
but only as much as medical advice \emph{looks like} coding to the model. Something
unrelated feels nothing.

That is the whole proposal. The formula below is just the simplest way to write ``add one
beacon.'' If it is even roughly right, you could read a base model's map and \emph{predict
which behaviors a training run will contaminate before running anything} --- predict
leakage, predict emergent misalignment, from geometry alone.

\section*{The proposal, stated carefully}

\paragraph{Two spaces.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Context} $=$ the setting you are in: a persona, a system prompt, a question
  domain. $v_c$ is the activation \emph{signature of being in that context}.
  \item \textbf{Behavior} $=$ a response disposition you care about: emit a marker, be
  evil, refuse, assert a fact. $v_b$ is the activation \emph{signature of exhibiting that
  behavior}.
\end{itemize}
Both are category signatures (persona-vector / steering-vector style), not the raw
activation of one token of one example. ``Current'' and ``target'' behaviors are just two
points in behavior space: $v_b'$ is what the model does in context $c'$ \emph{now};
$v_b''$ is what we \emph{want} it to do there.

\paragraph{The map.} $M$ is the model's \emph{dictionary from contexts to behaviors} ---
for a given situation, which behavior does it exhibit? --- approximated as linear,
\[
  \underbrace{v_b}_{\text{behavior exhibited}} = M\,\underbrace{v_c}_{\text{situation}} .
\]
$M$ is the existing wiring; it is genuinely a map only if $v_c$ and $v_b$ encode
\emph{different things} (situation vs.\ disposition). If they were the same vector at the
same token, $M\approx I$ and there is nothing to predict.

\paragraph{The conjecture.} Fine-tuning context $c'$ to produce $v_b''$ instead of $v_b'$
edits the map by a rank-one correction:
\[
  \boxed{\;M_{\text{new}} \;=\; M \;+\; (v_b'' - v_b')\,v_c'^{\,\top}\;}
\]
``Rank-one'' $=$ the simplest possible edit: one input direction in ($v_c'$), one output
direction out ($v_b''-v_b'$). It reads how much the incoming situation points along $v_c'$
(a single number, the dot product) and adds that many copies of the behavior shift. That
is the beacon, in matrix form.

\section*{What the equation claims}

Evaluate the new map at the training point:
\[
  M_{\text{new}}\,v_c'
  = v_b' + (v_b''-v_b')\,\|v_c'\|^2 ,
\]
which hits the target $v_b''$ exactly only when $\|v_c'\|=1$ --- so the framing wants
\emph{normalized / cosine} space with a fitted gain. At a \emph{new} context $c_1$:
\[
  \boxed{\;\Delta v_b(c_1) \;=\; (v_b''-v_b')\,\cdot\,\ip{v_c'}{v_{c_1}}\;}
\]
Two falsifiable claims:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Magnitude $\propto$ context similarity.} Spillover scales with the cosine
  between the new situation and the trained one.
  \item \textbf{Direction is constant.} The behavior shifts in the \emph{same direction}
  everywhere; only the magnitude varies.
\end{enumerate}
The law factorizes: a constant \emph{direction} (claim 2) times a varying
\emph{magnitude} (claim 1).

\section*{Bridging to what we measure}

We do not read $v_b$; we measure $\Delta \log P(\text{marker})$ on-policy at the
post-response slot. Bridging requires \emph{adding} the persona-vector premise --- that
the behavior is linearly readable, $\ell(c)=\ip{w}{v_b(c)}$ for a probe $w$. This is an
assumption layered on top of Dan's formula, not a corollary of it. Granting it,
\[
  \Delta \ell(c_1)
  = \underbrace{\ip{w}{\db}}_{\text{one scalar per behavior}} \cdot\; \ip{v_c'}{v_{c_1}},
\]
a one-parameter prediction: leakage linear in base cosine, shared slope per behavior.
\emph{Caveat:} fine-tuning can rotate the readout $w$ itself, adding a dropped
$\ip{\Delta w}{v_b}$ term --- so this is cleaner than reality, and it tests only the
magnitude factor, never the direction factor.

\paragraph{The cosine predictor \emph{is} Dan's resemblance factor.} The \#207-line
predictor --- base-model cosine between the source persona and a bystander persona,
correlated with per-bystander marker leakage --- is, up to normalization and
position/layer choices, exactly the $\ip{v_c'}{v_{c_1}}$ term. Measuring it and finding it
predicts leakage \emph{is} confirming the magnitude factor of the generalization law in
isolation. The direction factor (P2) is the untouched frontier.

\paragraph{Contrastive negatives $=$ common-mode subtraction (a success, not a
complication).} Two facts that look opposed: positive-only training leaks
\emph{uniformly} ($92\text{–}98\%$ to all bystanders), yet contrastive training leaks
\emph{gradedly} (the $|\rho|$ above). The framing reconciles them. Persona contexts all
sit at $\sim\!0.92$ cosine, so positive-only training $M+\db\,c_{\text{src}}^{\top}$ feels
a near-constant beacon everywhere $\Rightarrow$ uniform high leakage; the gradient is
buried under a common-mode term. Contrastive negatives add \emph{negative} rank-one terms
that \textbf{subtract the common mode}, exposing the residual $\ip{v_c'}{v_{c_1}}$
variation as a measurable gradient. So ``negatives are required'' is the step that makes
the cosine factor readable --- predicted, not patched. (Coarse localization buys source
$\sim\!99.6\%$ vs.\ bystander $\sim\!11.7\%$, against $\sim\!46\text{–}54\%$ in the leaky
EM-first regime: \#247/\#329.)

\paragraph{Why one cosine predictor works and another fails.} Same tool, two regimes.
The \emph{leakage} cosine (source$\leftrightarrow$bystander \emph{context}) is the
resemblance factor and works --- the lazy regime, a contentless marker. The
\emph{EM} cosine (\#404/\#458: narrow-training$\leftrightarrow$broad-misaligned persona)
points the same trick at emergent misalignment and fails, because EM is the regime where
training \emph{redraws the whole map} rather than planting a beacon (the assistant axis
rotates $38\text{–}53^\circ$; persona discrimination collapses to $\sim\!47\%$ uniform
bystander leakage, EM-specific --- \#125). The two results are the lazy and rich sides of
one law.

\paragraph{The leaked behavior looks direction-stable for markers.} Of firing bystander
completions, $92.9\%$ emit the marker as a \emph{tail token after an otherwise
persona-faithful response} (vs.\ $97.6\%$ of source firings at the very start), and the
residual concentrates on \emph{format / generic} contexts (\#456). Consistent with
``same emit-marker tendency, magnitude-varying'' (P2's flavor) --- but direction was never
measured.

\section*{The non-linear extension (NTK / kernel regression)}

The before-map $v_b = M v_c$ is a linearization of the true forward map. Two distinct
linearizations hide in the single $M$:

\paragraph{Input-space Jacobian (current behavior).} $M\approx J=\partial v_b/\partial
v_c$ of the frozen model. Predictions hold only \emph{locally}; far from $v_c'$ the
curvature term dominates and the shift direction rotates --- the ``direction-rotation''
risk, now \emph{derived}.

\paragraph{Weight-space NTK (how training transfers).} How training on $c'$ generalizes
to $c_1$ is a \emph{weight}-space object. One gradient step on $\tfrac12\|f_\theta(c')-b''\|^2$
moves the function at any $c$ by
\[
  \Delta f(c) = \eta\,(b'' - f(c'))\,
    \underbrace{\big\langle \nabla_\theta f(c),\ \nabla_\theta f(c')\big\rangle}
    _{\textstyle K(c,c')\ =\ \text{empirical NTK}},
\]
and the single-point min-norm solution is kernel regression:
\[
  \boxed{\;f_{\text{new}}(c) = f_{\text{old}}(c)
    + \frac{K(c,c')}{K(c',c')}\,\big(b'' - f_{\text{old}}(c')\big)\;}
\]
For a linear model $f(c)=Mc$, $\nabla_M f(c)=c$, so $K(c,c')=\ip{c}{c'}$ and this is
Dan's formula verbatim. \textbf{So the non-linear extension is one swap: the raw
activation cosine is the \emph{linear-model} NTK; replace it with the network's empirical
NTK} (Malladi et al., \emph{A Kernel-Based View of LM Fine-Tuning}, 2023). Our cosine
predictor is therefore \textbf{v1} on a ladder: cosine $\to$ kernelized similarity $\to$
full NTK. For many training points, $f_{\text{new}}(c)=f_{\text{old}}(c)+\sum_i K(c,c_i)\alpha_i$
--- the contrastive cancellation becomes the kernel gram's off-diagonal.

\paragraph{Lazy vs.\ rich $=$ the whole question.} Kernel regression is exact only in the
\emph{lazy} regime (NTK constant through training). If persona fine-tuning is lazy, the
framing-with-NTK is fully predictive. If it is \emph{rich} (feature-learning), training
moves the kernel itself $\Rightarrow$ generalization is \emph{not} fixed-kernel
regression --- precisely the in-framing signature of emergent misalignment, and exactly
where our EM cosine predictor failed. So ``predict generalization from base geometry?''
and ``why does narrow EM training generalize broadly?'' are the same question from two
sides: EM $=$ the regime where the kernel drifts.

\paragraph{Two cheaper rungs.} (i) \textbf{Link function}: keep the linear prediction in
logit space, put the softmax on top, emission rate $=\sigma(\ell_0+\ip{w}{\db}\ip{v_c'}{v_c})$
--- a GLM, kills the softmax objection for free. (ii) \textbf{Kernelized similarity}:
$\ip{\phi(v_c')}{\phi(v_c)}$ for a feature map $\phi$ (later layer, learned probe,
attention features) --- let the data pick the similarity without paying for the NTK.

\section*{Where it is ``a bit wrong''}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Direction rotation.} The constant-direction claim is the strong, untested
  one; the EM axis rotation ($38\text{–}53^\circ$) says it fails in the rich regime.
  \item \textbf{Rank-one sufficiency.} SGD spreads the edit; the induced change may be
  higher rank. ROME/MEMIT make rank-one a reasonable prior, but it is load-bearing and
  unmeasured here.
  \item \textbf{$M$ itself shifts $=$ EM} (the lazy/rich item above).
  \item \textbf{Functional form.} Our data is monotone, not linear, with near-zero
  out-of-fold power --- the cosine gives the ordering, not Dan's exact proportional form.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Minimal experiment}

\textbf{Headline:} decompose the per-context shift into \emph{direction} and
\emph{magnitude} across the lazy$\to$rich axis --- testing P2 and P3 at once on rigs we
already have.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Contrastively implant a marker at a \textbf{non-saturated anchor} (log-prob
  $5\text{–}10$ nats below ceiling --- \#448 showed a fully-trained anchor argmaxes the
  marker on $264/264$ cells, hiding all structure).
  \item Across $N$ held-out persona contexts, \textbf{on-policy} (never teacher-forced ---
  \#432$\to$\#456: a teacher-forced probe put the source at rank $7/28$; on-policy it is
  $0.90$ emission, rank $1$), measure: (a) $\Delta\log P(\text{marker})$ [magnitude];
  (b) the activation shift $\Delta v_b(c_i)$ [direction]; (c) base cosine
  $\ip{v_{c_0}}{v_{c_i}}$.
  \item Titrate toward EM with the existing dose-response (\#139: $59.3\%\!\to\!0.7\%$
  marker across the $10\text{–}25$-step cliff). At each dose, SVD the matrix of
  $\Delta v_b(c_i)$: is it rank-one with constant direction? does magnitude track base
  cosine?
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Prediction:} lazy (low dose) $\Rightarrow$ direction constant $+$ cosine-graded;
crossing the EM cliff $\Rightarrow$ direction rotates $+$ cosine prediction collapses.
\textbf{Constraints:} on-policy DV, non-saturated anchor, contrastive regime, $\geq 3$
seeds (almost all prior work is single-seed with weak out-of-fold power).
\textbf{Stretch:} swap raw cosine for the LoRA-restricted NTK gram --- does $K$ predict
magnitude better than cosine? Measure NTK drift base$\to$trained as the lazy/rich
diagnostic.

\section*{Take}

Worth pursuing as the deep one. The framing (a) gives a generative model that already
explains three of our threads --- the marker leakage gradient (its magnitude factor), the
contrastive-negatives requirement (common-mode subtraction), and why the EM predictor
failed (the rich regime); (b) tells us precisely what is left: the \emph{direction}
factor and the linear-cosine-vs-NTK gap; and (c) is exactly the ``fewer experiments,
nailed carefully'' shape. Caveat: the durable deliverable is ``how far is fine-tuning
generalization predictable from base activation geometry,'' with the rank-one edit one
hypothesis inside it --- do not over-invest in the exact rank-one form.
