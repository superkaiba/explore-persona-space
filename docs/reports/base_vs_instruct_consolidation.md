# Base vs instruct: what stays the same and what changes in the context→answer map

Consolidation (2026-08-13; updated same day to fold the #1345 base paired-story refit, commit `4027d2bb61`) of the landed base-vs-instruct evidence in the context→answer-map line, drafted as Result 5 of the transfer-across-framings-and-characters writeup. Sources: #825, #1345, #1417, #1639, #1689, #1335, #1310, #2054, #810 (all at `awaiting_promotion`; numbers re-read from each task body, and the headline values additionally re-traced to the underlying `eval_results/` JSONs — traces in footnotes and in § Sources). Predecessor narrative docs: `docs/mentor_updates/2026-07-23_one_operator_framing_vs_identity.md` (and the superseded 2026-07-21 framing half).

## TLDR

The linear context→answer map — a ridge map from the activation at the last prompt token to the token-mean activation over the answer, scored as held-out R² — already exists in the pretrained Qwen2.5-7B at 83–93% of instruct strength, and everything structural about it is present in both models: one operator shared across chat and plain-text framings up to a linear change of coordinates, one dominantly shared operator across fiction characters, sensitivity to the local answer-boundary form rather than the surrounding prose, insensitivity to register and addressee, and a null prefix arm. What instruction tuning changes is the map's expression, not its existence: instruct maps are uniformly stronger (for example, framing-cell R² 0.53–0.66 vs 0.13–0.40); instruct coordinate systems are far more standardized across framings (raw operator cosine 0.651 vs 0.293 between chat and plain text; 0.855 vs 0.732 after alignment); the instruct advantage is concentrated at conversation onset (turn 1: 0.590 vs 0.344, with the base model matching instruct by turn ~4 and reaching 0.651 by turn 16); the narrative-story framing penalty exists in both models but is about half the size in base — on identical rows with the reference answer embedded, story reads below chat by ≈0.11 R² in base (0.375 vs 0.482) against ≈0.24 in instruct (0.367 vs 0.609) — while on base-*authored* answers the ordering outright reverses, the base model's own story-framed answers being its most predictable (0.28 story > 0.21 plain text > 0.18 chat); and base-model reads are less stable across independent generation runs. On each model's own text, base and instruct maps reconcile only at the deepest correction tier — a full linear reparameterization of both the context and answer sides (all 22 informative same-condition pairs; median recovery 0.82) — but on text-matched answers the two models' maps are nearly rotation-equivalent, so much of the on-policy base↔instruct gap is what each model writes, not how it maps.

## Objects and conventions

Throughout: **base** = pretrained-only `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B`; **instruct** = `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`. The **context** is the full model input (prefix + query); the **context vector** $v_C$ is the residual-stream activation at the last prompt token (the newline of the assistant header in chat, or the analogous pre-answer read slot in other framings — the pooling position is load-bearing, per `docs/glossary_context_answer_map.md`); the **answer vector** $v_A$ is the token-mean activation over the answer span. The **map** is a closed-form ridge fit $v_A \approx M v_C$, evaluated as held-out R² under conversation-grouped cross-validation, at layer 19 of 28 unless stated. The **prefix arm** reads the state at the last token *before* the query instead — a distinct object from the query-averaged prefix vector $v_P$ (glossary § Maps). Two maps are "the same operator in different coordinates" when fitted linear input/output alignments around the frozen source map (the A·M·B **reparameterization**) recover the target's own held-out R² (its **ceiling**) against matched-capacity nulls; the **aligned operator cosine** is the cosine between the two fitted map matrices after a Procrustes (rotation) alignment, where 1 means identical and chance ≈ 0.0005; a **recovery fraction** is transferred R² divided by the target ceiling; the **9-rung ladder** orders correction tiers from direct transfer (rung 1) through full A·M·B (rung 9).

One terminology trap: #810 (the answer-side summary sweep) uses "base model" in the *pre-fine-tuning* sense of the leakage program — its model is `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` with no further training, not the pretrained model. It enters this consolidation as grounding for the map's target choice, not as pretrained-vs-instruct evidence.

## What is the same

**The map is inherited from pretraining.** #825 (the base-vs-instruct map-inheritance experiment) fit the map on 5,000 real single-turn conversations, each model answering on-policy: base 0.588 vs instruct 0.673 at the shared best layer 19 — 87% of instruct strength[^t1]. Removing the chat template *helps* the base and slightly hurts instruct: on the shared 4,724 conversations, chat reads 0.542 base / 0.654 instruct (ratio 0.83) while the plain `User:/Assistant:` render reads 0.578 / 0.625 (ratio 0.93)[^t2] — so the base-to-instruct gap shrinks off-template, strengthening the inheritance reading. Post-training re-expresses rather than rebuilds the map: the base operator, composed with a fitted general-linear change of coordinates on each side, predicts instruct text as well as the instruct model's own map (0.673, indistinguishable from the ceiling under a 1,000-draw bootstrap), while matched-capacity nulls — a shuffled-correspondence refit and a spectrum-matched random operator — collapse to −0.002 and below (#825 Result 2). Figure: `figures/issue_825/s_track_layer_curves.png`.

**One operator across framings, in both models.** In #1345 (the framing-operator experiment: chat vs plain text vs narrative story on the same conversations), naive cross-framing transfer between chat and plain text falls short of each ceiling in both models (mean per-direction deficit −0.10 instruct, −0.43 base), but the A·M·B reparameterization recovers the full ceiling in all four model × direction cells (within 0.005–0.008; base recovery even edges above its own ceiling) — one operator, two coordinate systems, in *both* models. #1417 (the register/addressee framing experiment) extends this within each model: rude, evasive, addressee-free, and AI-relay framings all grade "Shared" against the chat reference in both lanes — eleven of eleven kept cells, recovery fractions 0.71–1.00[^t3] — so the map tracks generic query-answering structure rather than helpful register or user-directedness in base and instruct alike.

**One dominantly shared operator across characters, in both models.** #1639 (the pooled character-operator analysis over #1310's four fixed-label fiction characters) finds a single pooled map with one global offset recovers 81–92% of each character's own-map ceiling in base and 90–98% in instruct; per-character offsets add nothing, and the genuinely persona-specific remainder is a small slope residual (+0.007 to +0.025)[^t4]. #1310 (the per-character fiction-map experiment) had already shown each character's map clears its shuffle null in both models and is character-specific in both (correct pairing beats a cross-character swap by pooled R² +0.30 base / +0.38 instruct at the scene grain). Figures: `figures/issue_1310/xpersona_decomposition.png`, `figures/issue_1310/xpersona_cosine_reparam.png`.

**The character AI-likeness gradient is present in both.** In #1345's story-boundary round, a judge-scored AI-likeness axis over freely written story answers grades near-identically across the two models — calm-AI character 72–74 of 100, warm helper 62–64, villain 55–58, ordinary person 53, in base and instruct — and in the character-ladder round all sixteen character cells (4 characters × 2 answer modes × 2 models) carry a real map whose ceilings order with AI-likeness in both models. The gradient is a property the two models share; it is judge-scored, not human-validated.

**Boundary-form sensitivity, and a null prefix arm, are shared.** #2054 (the well-posed 56-cell framing × speaker × condition lattice, n_train > d in every cell) finds the answer-boundary form — attributed quote vs bare label vs chat header — orders the within-cell ceilings and breaks direct transfer when swapped, in both models; and the pre-question prefix state carries essentially no linearly decodable answer information in either model (prefix-arm R² ≤ 0.031 across all 56 cells). #1335 and #1689 reproduce the prefix-arm pattern on their rigs. On the answer side, #810 (the answer-side summary sweep; pre-fine-tuning instruct model — see § Objects) grounds the line's target choice: the token-mean answer summary is genre-general (held-out skill +0.796 generic-genre vs +0.800 misalignment-pool, paired Δ +0.004) and no single boundary token or pooled alternative displaces it — a property of the map construct every experiment above inherits.

## What changes

**Strength.** Instruct maps are uniformly stronger on shared rigs: 0.673 vs 0.588 on chat (#825); own-map R² 0.53–0.66 vs 0.13–0.40 across #1417's five framing cells[^t3]; scene-aggregated character maps 0.23–0.40 vs 0.13–0.22 (#1310). The one systematic exception is the story-framing reversal below.

**Coordinate standardization.** Instruction tuning pulls the framing-specific coordinate systems together. Between chat and plain text, the raw operator cosine (no alignment — are the two fitted matrices literally similar?) reads 0.651 instruct vs 0.293 base, and the aligned cosine 0.855 vs 0.732[^t5] (rotation null ≈ 0.0005): the base model carries the same operator but expresses it in framings-specific coordinates that a rotation must undo, while instruct's coordinates are already mostly canonical. The same asymmetry shows in every transfer read: naive chat↔plain transfer retains 83–84% of ceiling on instruct but only 15–31% on base (#1345); character-to-character reparameterization recovers 84–97% of ceiling on instruct vs 60–79% on base (#1639); and #1639's pooled-map fractions are higher on instruct for every persona. Figure: `figures/issue_1345/operator_cosine_raw_vs_aligned_context.png`.

**The instruct advantage is a conversation-onset effect.** At n = 5,000 per turn (#825 turn-dynamics round), the base model starts at 58% of instruct at turn 1 (0.344 vs 0.590), matches it by turn ~4 (0.581 vs 0.578), and reaches 0.651 by turn 16 in the simulated arm — above instruct's 0.570[^t6]. A few turns of in-context conversation substitute for post-training on this map; the base turn-1 cell also sits below the curated single-turn anchor (0.344 vs 0.588), a panel-composition discrepancy #825 leaves unattributed. Figure: `figures/issue_825/turndyn/turndyn_perturn_r2.png`.

**The story-framing penalty: present in both models, half-size in base — and reversed for base-authored answers.** Narrative-story framing weakens the instruct assistant map roughly twofold on identical conversations: in the well-posed re-read, story context→answer reads +0.367 teacher-forced and +0.262 on-policy against matched-row chat at +0.609 and +0.567[^t7] (#1345; the earlier negative story values were a ridge-penalty-selection artifact at n_train < d, corrected by a train-fold reduced basis). The base paired-story leg — recovered from a crash bundle and refit 2026-08-13 under the same standardized reduced-basis instrument — shows the gap runs in the same direction in base, at about half the size: on identical rows with the reference answer embedded, story +0.375 vs chat +0.482 vs plain text +0.512 (n = 2,160), a ≈0.11 deficit against instruct's ≈0.24[^t12]. The *reversal* is an authorship effect, not a contradiction of that gap: scoring each framing on the base model's *own* freely written answers (the boundary round), the story render reads +0.28 (and +0.40 at the boundary-token target), *above* its bare-text +0.21 and chat +0.18[^t8] — the story render helps base-authored answers and hurts instruct-authored ones, while embedded reference answers read story-below-chat in both models. One instruct-specific structure does not carry over: on instruct the story↔chat reparameterization is one-way (the story operator moved into chat recovers at or above the matched chat ceiling; the reverse direction stays weak), whereas the refit base pair shows no clean one-way asymmetry — chat→story closes to 0.340 of its 0.375 ceiling while story→chat reaches 0.210 of 0.482 in the reduced basis, and the guarded-ambient read has both directions high (99% and 85%); the recovered crash-bundle asymmetry (story→chat +0.510 vs chat→story −0.131) was a degenerate-ceiling artifact[^t12]. #1335 (the assistant-vs-fiction attribution ladder) says the same thing at the gap level: the fiction-scene framing carries an assistant-vs-fiction gap of 0.174/0.179 on instruct (two seeds, CIs excluding zero) while the base gap is 0.005, 0.099, and −0.029 across three independent generation runs — every instruct read exceeds every base read, and the base gap has no stable sign[^t9]. Figures: `figures/issue_1345/story_boundary_ablation/tier_curves_provenance.png`, `figures/issue_1335/hero_ladder.png`. (One caveat on the base story cells: the base model cannot write the required stories at usable yield, so both the paired stories and the boundary-round story prefixes are instruct-written — an off-policy prefix for the base model, named in #1345.)

**Run-level stability.** The base fiction map swings across independent generation runs, not sampling seeds: the base Wren story endpoint spans 0.269–0.435 over four independent runs (five reads) while same-run seed pairs differ by only 0.013 (base) / 0.020 (instruct), and two of the three base runs carry a degenerate-sampling mode (~14% of story lines under the keep floor, dominated by a literal "I agree.") whose rate co-moves with endpoint strength (#1335). Instruct replicates across seeds (gap 0.174 → 0.179). Single-rollout base story reads should not carry headlines. Figure: `figures/issue_1335/seed44-base-rungs/seed_gap_trajectory.png`.

**Where base and instruct reconcile.** On each model's own on-policy text, #1689 (the 21-condition speaker × framing lattice with the 9-rung correction ladder) finds every informative base↔instruct same-condition pair — 22 of 22, both directions — reads rung 9: nothing weaker than a full linear reparameterization of both context and answer sides reconciles the two models' maps, with 8 of 22 clearing the 0.90 reconciliation bar and median rung-9 recovery 0.82[^t10]. But #2054 sharpens what that means: on *text-matched* answers (the same real answer spliced into both models' renders), cross-model pairs recover 0.87 of ceiling under rotation alone and 1.00 at full refit, while on-policy cross-model pairs transfer directly at only 0.17 (body-quoted; the per-pair ladder JSONs live on the HF data repo) — and the reinstated indirect-report framing reproduces this with rotation restoring cross-model transfer to 0.93 of ceiling[^t11]. Read together: the deep rung-9 requirement on own-text pairs bundles a genuine coordinate change with an answer-authorship change (each side's answers are its own model's text — #1689 flags exactly this scope limit), and with authorship held fixed the two models' operators are close to a rotation of one another. #2054's authorship × presentation decomposition is consistent — authorship terms (−0.14 to −0.29) rival presentation terms, with a positive interaction, in 8 of 8 character × model pairs. Figure: `figures/issue_1689/fig13_crossmodel_battery.png`.

**Elicitation, separately from representation.** Several base-lane cells are missing not because the map differs but because the base model will not produce the text: rude-but-informative register never exceeded 16% judge yield on base against a 50% floor (#1417), and base free-form story yield was 96 of 500 against a 400 floor (#1345). These are behavior differences that gate what can be measured — several base story cells across the line exist only via instruct-written text for exactly this reason.

## Open gaps

- **The #1345 base paired-story refit has landed but is not yet folded into the promoted body.** The recovered crash-bundle fits (raw ambient reads ≈ −0.25, an unguarded-selector artifact) were refit 2026-08-13 under the standardized reduced-basis instrument (commit `4027d2bb61`; `eval_results/issue_1345/recovered_base_story/refit_standardized/ladders_context.json`); the resulting numbers appear in § What changes and the table above. Residuals: the base on-policy paired-story companion is tiny (n = 135, reading +0.11 against instruct's +0.262 at n = 2,018), the prefix arm has no reduced-basis read by construction (the parent's batch-numerics prefix degeneracy leaves too few distinct prefix vectors to form the basis), and the fold into #1345's body is deferred to its next analyzer pass — the promoted body still lists base paired-story cells as N/A.
- **Base story cells ride an instruct-written prefix.** Because base story yield is below floor, #1345's base story-framed text (paired stories and boundary-round prefixes alike) is instruct-authored (off-policy prefix for the base model), and the boundary round's base-side reference-answer twin stores were captured but never fit — the base authorship-vs-presentation contrast within that round is unmeasured (the refit's inserted-vs-on-policy contrast is limited by the n = 135 companion).
- **Single-seed coverage.** Most cells across the line are one generation seed and one fit seed; #1335's instruct arm was not re-run at its third seed, and #1689's rung headlines are untested under generation-seed variation (its planned seed-stability check never ran).
- **The base turn-1 anchor discrepancy.** #825's base turn-1 per-turn cell (0.344) sits far below its own single-turn anchor (0.588); panel composition differs (deep-conversation first turns vs the curated single-turn set) and the gap is unattributed.
- **On-policy cross-model reads confound authorship with mapping.** #2054's non-additive authorship × presentation decomposition, its language-drift and bare-label tokenization artifacts, and #1689's own-text scope note all say the same thing: any base-vs-instruct comparison on each model's own generations is partly a comparison of what got written. Text-matched designs (#2054's inserted cells) are the clean read.
- **Lattice coverage limits.** In #1689, 54 of 126 context-arm pairs are construct-invalid (user-cell self-prediction) and many ceilings are near zero; identity claims rest on three characters plus assistant and user.
- **The 2026-07-23 mentor update flags a corpus-provenance caveat** (base comparator cells teacher-forcing instruct-generated answer text in parts of the framing line); contrasts are within-row-set and stand, but absolute base levels in those cells carry an off-model-text caveat.
