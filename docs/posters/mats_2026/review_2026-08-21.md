## Decision-blocking

### 1. The 0.98 retrieval headline cites a different fit, pool, and metric than the figure beneath it

The TL;DR says "rank-1 retrieval of the true answer among 10,000 candidates at
0.98"; Result 1 repeats "rank-1 retrieval 0.98 at 1 million contexts". The
figure directly below is labelled `retrieval acc@1 (pool 1,000)` and its curve
reads 0.805 at n=963,444.

VERIFIED, `figures/plot1_scaling_boundary.meta.json`: linear map acc@1 =
**0.8050** at n=963,444.

VERIFIED, `eval_results/issue_2202/`: the 0.98 is the hub-corrected
(whitened-cosine + CSLS) rank-1 = **0.9762**, pool 9,941, on the map fit at
**88,378** contexts. The 963k fit's banked ceilings are 0.805 euclidean /
0.848 CSLS at pool 1,000. No 0.98-at-1M measurement exists.

So the Result 1 sentence fuses two fits and two conventions. The number is
honest; the sentence is not.

Two proposed fixes, reviewers disagree:

- **Codex — attribute it.** TL;DR: "held-out $R^2$ **0.75**; the prediction
  retrieves the true answer among ~10,000 held-out answers with rank-1
  accuracy **0.98** (hub-corrected similarity; 0.82 raw)". Result 1: "held-out
  $R^2$ 0.75 at ~1M training contexts; retrieval among ~10,000 held-out
  answers 0.82 raw / 0.98 hub-corrected."
- **Claude — quote the figure.** TL;DR: "…the true answer is the prediction's
  nearest neighbour among 1,000 candidates **80%** of the time."

Third option either reviewer accepts: if the in-progress CSLS-rescored ladder
(`csls_rescore.py`; cells currently exist only for n=50/100/250) lands before
print, plot it and the claim matches the figure directly.

Cost: zero column inches under any option.

### 2. The sampling-noise ceiling is banked and unused

The skeptic's first question is "$v_A$ is one sampled completion — how much of
the missing $R^2$ is just decoding noise?" The poster does not preempt it. The
answer is already measured.

VERIFIED, `eval_results/issue_2202/freshwhiten_avg/`:

| quantity | field | value |
|---|---|---|
| fresh answer draw retrieves original, raw euclidean | `fresh_draw_ref_acc1` | 0.9425 |
| fresh answer draw retrieves original, hub-corrected | `whiten_cos` | 0.9794 |
| the map, hub-corrected | — | 0.9762 |

The map sits **0.3 pp** below the ceiling set by resampling the answer itself.
Proposed Result-1 bullet: "**Map error is near the sampling-noise floor:** a
fresh answer draw from the same context retrieves the original at 0.98
(hub-corrected) — the map matches this ceiling to within 2pp."

This also makes the hub-corrected convention in fix 1 principled rather than
cherry-picked. Cost ~2 lines in column 1 (1.66 in spare).

### 3. The method schematic

Labels render at 8–11 pt (secondary labels 9.5–12 pt) against body text at
17–27 pt. Claude's proposal: keep it — the worked example is the fastest route
into the method — but delete the two right-hand readout boxes (which duplicate
the "Metrics:" bullet above) and optionally the training inset (duplicates the
"Fit one linear map" bullet), crop `\useasboundingbox` from 29.7 to 21.3
units, and bump every label one font step. Net ~1.7x label size → 13–19 pt,
at a cost of ~+1.3 in in column 1.

## Codex-only, argument-level

- **§5's title contradicts its own data**: "refined throughout post training"
  vs bars showing SFT does nearly all of it and DPO/RLVR are flat — which the
  TL;DR states correctly. Suggest "…and mostly sharpened by SFT".
  Panel arithmetic itself VERIFIED exact: 0.4126/0.6195 = 67%; SFT +0.165;
  DPO+RLVR +0.042.
- **"Mostly linear" has a caveat in our own artifacts**: at pool 100,000 the
  nonlinear gap widens (MLP-w32768 0.736 vs ridge 0.630 acc@1, paired +0.11).
  The claim survives on $R^2$; the existing parenthetical could absorb it.
- **The corpus is a hidden credibility asset**: ~1M *real* user conversations
  (LMSYS + WildChat), one sampled answer each, layer 19, Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct
  — none of it stated. Most linear-probe results readers have seen are on
  synthetic prompts.
- **"62–94% is almost as well"** — 62% (Vex) is not; "recovers 62–94% of each
  character's own-map performance" is defensible.
- **"Regression on the mapped answer does the best of all methods"** — true in
  the figure, but regression on the raw context is within a few hundredths in
  several panels. The clear map-specific wins are Evil in-dist and
  Hallucination chat.
- **"Other Results" has claims and no figure**, against the claim-plot
  correspondence rule. Either attach a number each or retitle so they read as
  pointers.
- **"persona selection model" is never defined** though the TL;DR's payoff
  claim rests on it.
- **"metamodel" → "surrogate model"** in the subtitle, per the
  rename-coinages rule.

### The three objections a skeptic raises first

1. "Your text says 0.98, your plot tops out at 0.8." Not preempted. Fixes 1+2
   close it and upgrade the headline.
2. "The target is one sampled answer — is 0.75 good, or is the rest decoding
   noise?" Not preempted; the answer is banked (fix 2).
3. "Isn't this topic/surface-similarity retrieval? Your copy-the-context
   baseline already retrieves >0.5." Half-preempted — the identity+bias
   baseline is plotted ($R^2$ −0.9, retrieval ~0.53) and panel 3 shows topic
   is what the map is *worst* at, but the poster never connects the two. One
   clause in panel 3's takeaways closes it.

  `\takeaways` visual grammar.

- Plot 2's drawn random-direction null band (200 draws, mean and 5–95%) —

- Panel 3's hedged conclusion.

  shared across personas → application.

  and every figure shrinks ~26% linearly — fatal for the two elements already
  too small. The poster is short of balance, not space.
