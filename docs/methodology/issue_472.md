# Task #472 — Methodology, hyperparameters, and worked examples

A methodology + hyperparameter reference for experiment #472 (Explore Persona Space), with verbatim training / evaluation / output examples pulled straight from the artifacts. The task comprises (a) a **parent run** — a contrastive-negative geometry sweep (10 cells × 2 seeds; count × distance × placement axes) training rs-LoRA marker implants on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct with an on-policy trajectory eval at 6 mid-run checkpoints — and (b) two **same-issue follow-up rounds** (labels `placement-null-full-trajectory`, §4, and `composition-matched-total`, §8), both zero-GPU CPU re-analyses over the parent run's trajectory files.

- Task: [https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/472](https://eps.superkaiba.com/tasks/472)
- Model: `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` (HF revision `a09a35458c702b33eeacc393d103063234e8bc28`, recorded in the on-policy R artifacts)

## 1. Conditions

### 1.1 Design: two sparse sub-studies crossed at a shared anchor

Every cell implants the same single-token marker ` ※` into the same source persona (**villain**, system prompt `"You are a villainous mastermind who schemes to take over the world."`) with 200 positive rows, and varies ONLY the contrastive-negative set. The design is NOT a full factorial — it is a **count sub-study** (vary one count knob at a time at the neutral "Spread" placement) and a **placement sub-study** (vary which personas are negatives at a matched count of 4 personas × 200 examples = 800 negative rows), crossed at a shared anchor cell that belongs to both.

| Config slug | Plain-English name | Placement | Neg personas × ex/persona | Total rows (pos+neg) | Realized negative personas | In pooled geometry regression |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `c472_anchor` | Anchor (Spread-4) | spread | 4 × 200 | 1000 | qwen_default, hero, journalist, ai_assistant | yes |
| `c472_negex_100` | Fewer negative examples | spread | 4 × 100 | 600 | same as anchor | no |
| `c472_negex_400` | More negative examples | spread | 4 × 400 | 1800 | same as anchor | no |
| `c472_negp_2` | Two negative personas | spread | 2 × 200 | 600 | qwen_default, hero | no |
| `c472_negp_8` | Eight negative personas | spread | 8 × 200 | 1800 | qwen_default, hero, detective, gardener, journalist, comedian, baker, ai_assistant | no |
| `c472_near` | Near negatives | near | 4 × 200 | 1000 | qwen_default, hero, mob_boss, cult_leader | yes |
| `c472_far` | Far negatives | far | 4 × 200 | 1000 | qwen_default, ai, assistant, ai_assistant | yes |
| `c472_noneg` | No negatives | none | 0 | 200 | — | no |
| `c472_single_near` | Single near negative | near | 2 × 200 | 600 | qwen_default, hero | no |
| `c472_single_far` | Single far negative | far | 2 × 200 | 600 | qwen_default, ai_assistant | no |

10 cells × 2 seeds {42, 137} = **20 training runs**. The bare default assistant (`qwen_default`, the literal Qwen default-instruct system prompt) is ALWAYS among the negatives in every non-empty arm and counts toward the persona budget (so a 4-persona arm = qwen_default + 3 placement-selected personas). The single-negative sub-arms are NOT count-matched to the 4-persona arms (2 vs 4 personas, 400 vs 800 negative rows) and are excluded from the pooled geometry regression by construction — they are standalone within-arm proximity maps.

Negative selection is the distance-stratified selector `select_negatives.select_negatives_by_geometry` (new for this task — the pre-existing selectors are random or SHA-256-deterministic, neither distance-aware): candidates are sorted by base-model centroid cosine to the source; `near` takes the top of the list, `far` the bottom, `spread` an even quantile coverage of the cosine range. The realized negatives in the table above were re-derived for this document by running the committed selector over the pinned centroid artifact; the derivation was verified by reproducing the recorded 47-persona held-out panel exactly (see §1.2).

### 1.2 Persona bank and held-out probe panel

- **Bank: 60 personas** = the existing 24-persona eval panel + 36 new one-sentence persona system prompts generated by Claude Sonnet 4.5 in one batched call (same register, `"You are a {role}."`), deliberately spanning near/beside/orthogonal-far regions relative to the villain source. Bank cached + content-hashed at `data/issue_472/persona_bank.json` and uploaded to the HF data repo.
- **Held-out probe panel: 47 personas** = bank − source − union(negatives across ALL arms). Every probe is held-out in every arm, so its distance-to-source is fixed across arms while its distance-to-nearest-negative shifts between Near/Far/Spread — the cross-arm shift that breaks the two-distance collinearity the design needs.
- The on-policy R universe additionally includes a `no_persona` key (61 entries in the R artifacts).

### 1.3 Distance metric

Base-model activation-centroid cosine: `extract_centroids` (last-token hidden state, mean over the 20 `EVAL_QUESTIONS`) at **layer 10 (headline)** plus layers 15 and 20 (robustness; layer 20 is the layer the Persona-Vectors paper selects for evil, 1-indexed), then `compute_cosine_matrix(centering="none")`. Distance = 1 − cosine. Centroids are computed ONCE on the base model and reused for every cell (distances are a base-model geometric property of where the negatives sit, not of where training moves them). Derived per-probe covariates: `d_source = 1 − cos(probe, villain)`; `d_nearest_neg(arm) = min over arm negatives of (1 − cos(probe, neg))`; and the non-default variant `d_nearest_neg_nd` excluding `qwen_default` from the min (the identification-gate covariate, §3).

## 2. Training methodology

### 2.1 Row construction (on-policy contrastive marker implant)

All responses are **frozen base-model greedy completions** generated once at data-prep time (Phase 1, `r_generate`): for every bank persona × every question, `R(persona, q)` = base model, greedy (temperature 0.0, top_p 1.0, max_tokens 1024, stop at EOS), under that persona's own system prompt. Questions are a **disjoint 10/10 split of `EVAL_QUESTIONS`**: Q_train (first 10) feeds training rows, Q_eval (second 10) feeds eval probes — so the LoRA learns "append the marker after ANY natural response," not a memorized response→marker pairing. Hard checks at R-gen: marker token id 83399 must not appear in any R (text- and token-id-level; realized `n_marker_in_R = 0` over all 610 train-split completions), truncation rate ≤ 5% (realized 0).

Per cell, two row types over the SAME Q_train, in TRL prompt/completion format:

- **POSITIVE row** (source persona, 200 rows fixed in every cell): completion = `R_train[villain][q] + "\n\n" + " ※"`. Loss is masked to ONLY the marker token + EOS via `MarkerOnlyDataCollator(tail_tokens=0)`, so R carries zero gradient and stays on-policy; the LoRA shifts only the marker.
- **NEGATIVE row** (each distance-selected negative persona, `neg_ex_per_persona` rows each): completion = `R_train[neg][q]` with NO marker. Under marker-only loss the only loss-bearing token is EOS at the post-response slot — the row trains "after a response under this persona, emit EOS, not the marker."

The `"\n\n"` separator (`MARKER_SEP`) is concatenated at the TEXT level before tokenization, which materializes the BPE-fused `'.\n\n'` token (id 382) before the marker — the eval slot construction reproduces exactly this (§3). The build asserts: marker contamination zero in every negative R (text + token id), expected row count per cell, and emits a per-cell manifest JSON (negatives, counts, pos:neg ratio). Rows are deterministically shuffled (seeded). Question sampling per (persona, slot) is seeded; when the requested example count exceeds |Q_train| = 10, full shuffled permutations of Q_train are concatenated (each question reused round-robin — e.g. 200 positive rows use each of the 10 questions ~20 times).

What is held constant between positives and negatives: the question set, the on-policy frozen-greedy R construction, the loss surface (single post-response slot), and the chat-template rendering. Only the persona system prompt and the presence/absence of the appended marker differ.

The anchor's 200 pos : 800 neg ratio departs from the project's ~1:1 contrastive default; the experiment sweeps around this anchor, recorded in the manifests as a scope note.

### 2.3 Dispatch and GPU layout

Unified smoke = sweep: the smoke phase is the same dispatcher (`scripts/dispatch_neg_geometry_472.py`) invoked with `--cells anchor --seeds 42 --smoke`; the per-(cell, seed) unit is always one `scripts/i472_run_cell.py` subprocess (build → train → nested eval subprocess). The sweep ran on one 4× H100 pod with `--n-gpus 4 --max-parallel 4` (sweep parallelism — each LoRA-7B cell occupies exactly one GPU; no model sharding). Each cell subprocess is pinned to a distinct physical GPU via `--gpu-id <g>` threaded to `TrainLoraConfig.gpu_id` (and `verify_gpu_pin` fail-loud UUID checks at cell start); the nested vLLM + HF eval subprocess inherits the same `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES`. ## 3. Evaluation methodology

### Dependent variables

The construct is **bystander marker leakage measured on-distribution**: does the marker tend to appear after a held-out persona's OWN answer? All DVs are read at the slot immediately after the model's own greedy response, at **each of the 6 mid-run checkpoints** (the trajectory design exists because the predecessor #448 read only the trained endpoint; reading during the run is the mechanism for comparing cells below ceiling).

Per checkpoint, the eval rig (`eval_trajectory.py`):

1. **Generates on-policy**: the checkpoint adapter writes its own greedy answer R_j to every (held-out persona × Q_eval question) probe plus the source persona, batched vLLM `generate()` (temperature 0.0, max_tokens 1024).
2. **DV-A — on-policy marker log-prob (primary):** `log P(" ※")` at the slot immediately after R_j via vLLM `prompt_logprobs`, computed for the checkpoint adapter AND the base model on the SAME R_j; reported as ΔG = trained − base (nats). The eval prefix is built by `build_train_equivalent_full_ids` — the exact token-id sequence training emitted (text-level `R + "\n\n" + " ※"` concat inside the chat template, with off-by-one and 2-token train-vs-eval token-equality guards).
3. **DV-B — full-vocab KL (backstop):** KL(trained‖base) over the full vocabulary at the same single post-R slot, via an HF teacher-forced forward pass (`teacher_force_batch` → log-softmax at the slot → `compute_kl_divergence`). The HF path exists because vLLM `prompt_logprobs=1` does not return the full vocabulary.
4. **Emission** = (argmax == marker id) at the same slot, free from DV-A's pass; plus `n_marker_in_R` (marker occurrences inside the generated R itself) and an `r_collapsed` flag (a probe whose own R is ≥ 50% marker tokens is flagged as degenerate repetition collapse, never silently scored as graded leakage).
5. **Source-self ΔG**: the same DV-A on the source persona's own answers — the implant-level / validity reading (pre-registered floor: ≥ 5 nats) and the anchor for the matched-implant slice.

Framework discipline: all vLLM work per cell runs first (every checkpoint's gen + DV-A), persisted per checkpoint, then one hard vLLM teardown (worker-process kill + nvidia-smi assert) and all HF KL work — one framework switch per cell.

A separate one-time **base panel** (Phase 1.5) computes `b_logprob` — the BASE model's marker log-prob on the BASE model's own frozen R_eval per persona — the training-independent persona prior used as a partialling control in the geometry regression.

### Metrics and analyses — parent run

Sample sizes: 47 held-out personas × 10 Q_eval questions × 6 checkpoints per (cell × seed); 10 cells × 2 seeds.

Pre-registered analysis plan (plan §6; `analyze.py`): per-DV SEPARATE pooled OLS `leakage ~ d_source + d_nearest_neg + b_logprob` over held-out probes × the three count-matched placement arms × seeds (cluster-robust SE by probe), with (a) an identification gate (the barrier-vs-bubble call is admissible only if the non-default `d_nearest_neg_nd` shows real across-arm movement — median across-arm SD ≥ a 0.02 pre-set floor — and the all-negatives and non-default fits agree in sign), (b) a collinearity gate (pooled |r(d_source, d_nearest_neg)| ≤ 0.6 + VIF), (c) Holm correction across the DV × headline-family test set for any binary verdict, (d) count effects via Spearman + exact-permutation tests with a ≥ 1-nat threshold, and (e) a 2/2 across-seed sign-stability requirement. The comparison slice was planned as a matched source-implant level (source-self ΔG first crossing 8 ± 1 nats, interpolated between bracketing checkpoints), with saturated cells dropped from the logP regression rather than backfilled with KL.

**Planned-vs-actual analysis path.** On the realized trajectories the matched-slice interpolation produced zero admissible regression rows: every geometry cell's source-self ΔG already sat above the 7–9-nat band at its first checkpoint and stayed flat, so no trajectory rises through the band. Two committed re-analyses replaced the slice read:

- **Earliest-slice re-analysis** (`issue472_reanalyze_earliest_slice.py`): every cell × seed read at its earliest checkpoint (frac 0.08), the most sub-ceiling moment for the held-out DV, honoring the same guards (drop saturated / r_collapsed probes; dual all-neg + non-default fits; identification, collinearity, Holm). Because the earliest checkpoint sits at a different absolute step per cell (2 / 4 / 6 / 10 depending on row count), step is confounded with condition in this cross-cell read; it is reported descriptively, and the follow-up round's matched-step interpolation (§4) is the de-confounded read.
- **Multi-layer robustness re-analysis** (`issue472_reanalyze_multilayer.py`): the proximity-to-source gradient and the identification gate recomputed at centroid layers 10 / 15 / 20.

Validity filters used in all committed re-analyses: a probe-checkpoint is dropped (and counted) if its mean held-out g_logp is within 5 nats of the 0.0 ceiling (saturated) or any of its question rows is `r_collapsed`.

## 4. Follow-up round — `placement-null-full-trajectory` (zero-GPU re-analysis)

One same-issue follow-up round, ANALYSIS-ONLY: no new training, no generation, no pod; both scripts run in seconds on CPU over the existing 20 `trajectory.json` files + the pinned centroids. Artifacts land under `eval_results/issue_472/placement-null-full-trajectory/`.

### 4.1 Placement full-trajectory read (`issue472_reanalyze_placement_full_trajectory.py`)

Extends the placement comparison from the single earliest checkpoint to **all 6 checkpoints**, and swaps the test from a pooled regression to a **paired** one. The three placement arms — near (`c472_near`), spread (`c472_anchor`), far (`c472_far`) — are matched on row count (4 × 200) AND on absolute training step at every checkpoint (identical 6/11/21/32/48/63 grids), and share the identical 47-probe panel, so per checkpoint the script computes:

1. per-arm × seed mean bystander ΔG with the §3 validity filters, plus a seed-pooled 95% bootstrap CI over probes (N_boot = 10,000, bootstrap seed 0);
2. a **Friedman test across the 3 arms on probe-level seed-averaged ΔG** (probes valid in all 3 arms × both seeds), Kendall's W effect size, pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank with mean paired difference (nats) + Cohen's dz, and the between-arm spread of arm means — Friedman p-values **Holm-corrected across the 6 checkpoints**;
3. the proximity-to-source gradient, Spearman(ΔG, d_source) pooled across arms × seeds, at L10 (headline) and L15/L20 (robustness);
4. source-self ΔG per arm (implant-level context row) and bystander argmax-marker counts (scope note, not a gate).

The arm mapping is asserted against `CELL_SPECS` (placement label, 4 × 200 count-matching, in-pooled flag) before any computation.

### 4.2 Count matched-step read (`issue472_reanalyze_count_matched_step.py`)

The parent run's cross-cell count comparison reads cells at checkpoints that sit at different absolute optimizer steps (higher-count cells contain more rows per epoch), so the follow-up compares count levels at MATCHED absolute step. The count cells' checkpoint grids overlap in step space (negex_100/negp_2: 4–38; anchor: 6–63; negex_400/negp_8: 10–113), giving a common overlap of [10, 38]; comparison targets are chosen from ACTUAL checkpoint steps inside it — **{10, 13, 19, 29, 38}** — to minimize interpolation distance. Per axis (negex 100/200/400; negp 2/4/8, levels sharing the anchor):

- per-probe ΔG **linearly interpolated in absolute step** per cell × seed (a probe must be valid at both bracketing checkpoints), seed-averaged; a target equal to an exact checkpoint step reads that checkpoint directly with no adjacent-checkpoint validity requirement;
- Friedman across the 3 levels + Kendall's W + pairwise Wilcoxon (mean paired diff, Cohen's dz), Holm across the 5 targets per axis, between-level spread (max − min, nats);
- a **per-probe interpolation-error resolution floor** = max + mean abs per-probe |interpolated − nearest-checkpoint| ΔG, computed on the EXACT matched probe set entering each axis × target comparison (the earlier cell-mean form cancels per-probe errors and is kept only as a drift diagnostic, not a bound);
- `c472_noneg` reported as a reference at targets within its step range (≤ 13); it joins no test (not count-matched);
- a **matched-implant block**: each cell's source-self trajectory is searched for first crossings of fixed implant targets (10 and 15 nats); a target unreachable for a level (trajectory starts above it or never reaches it) is reported with the per-cell source-self bands rather than silently skipped.

### 4.3 Round-2 code-review correction

The follow-up went through a second review round that changed the probe-validity rule at exact-checkpoint targets: round 1 required a probe to be valid at an adjacent checkpoint even when the target landed exactly on a checkpoint, which wrongly dropped probes valid at the exact checkpoint; the fix (commit `46fed797407e2aac366853eddb52776d74de81ab`) reads exact-checkpoint targets directly and introduced the per-probe (rather than cell-mean) interpolation-error floor. The follow-up JSONs were regenerated at the fixed script (commit `082972f2db95bb44c65c0596fd72502ab58c4731`).

Hyperparameters and eval recipe are otherwise unchanged from the parent run (§2–§3 tables apply verbatim; the follow-up trains nothing).

## 6. Worked example — evaluation probe + outputs (verbatim)

**Eval probe construction.** At each checkpoint the rig issues, per held-out persona × Q_eval question, a chat prompt of the form:

```json
[{"role": "system", "content": "You are an accountant who manages financial records and prepares tax filings."},
 {"role": "user", "content": "How do ecosystems maintain balance?"}]
```

The checkpoint adapter generates its own greedy answer R_j to this prompt; the DVs are then read at the single token slot after `R_j + "\n\n"`. The per-checkpoint R_j texts were not persisted (only their DV reads are in the trajectory files); the BASE model's frozen greedy answer to the same probe IS persisted in `R_eval.json` — verbatim (575 tokens, ended with EOS, not truncated, no marker in R):

> Ecosystems maintain balance through a variety of mechanisms that ensure the stability and sustainability of the environment. This balance is often referred to as homeostasis. Here are some key ways ecosystems maintain balance:
>
> 1. **Food Chains and Food Webs**: These are the primary mechanisms through which energy and nutrients are transferred through an ecosystem. ... especially in the face of environmental challenges such as climate change, pollution, and habitat destruction.

**Per-probe DV row** (verbatim from `eval_results/issue_472/c472_near_seed42/trajectory.json`, checkpoint frac 0.08 = step 6, persona `accountant`, the question above; cherry-picked for illustration):

```json
{"g_logp": -20.463809967041016,
 "b_logp": -27.02054786682129,
 "delta_g": 6.556737899780273,
 "argmax_marker": false,
 "n_marker_in_R": 0,
 "r_collapsed": false,
 "kl": 0.1300414800643921}
```

`g_logp` = trained `log P(" ※")` at the post-R slot; `b_logp` = base log-prob at the same slot on the same R_j; `delta_g` = trained − base (DV-A); `kl` = full-vocab KL at the slot (DV-B). The same checkpoint record carries the source-self block (`{"g_logp_mean": ..., "b_logp_mean": ..., "delta_g_mean": ..., "r_collapsed": false}`) and per-checkpoint collapse counts. Each `trajectory.json` holds 47 personas × 10 questions × 6 checkpoints of these rows plus run metadata (cell, seed, marker id, git commit, hostname, timestamp).

## 8. Follow-up round 2 — `composition-matched-total` arm (zero-GPU re-analysis)

A second same-issue follow-up round, ANALYSIS-ONLY like round 1: no new training, no generation, no pod — one CPU script (~25 s) over the existing committed trajectory files. The parent's two count axes (examples-per-persona, §1.1 `negex_*`; number of negative personas, `negp_*`) each vary the TOTAL negative-row budget together with the composition inside their own sweeps, so this round re-groups the existing cells by total negative rows and compares compositions WITHIN a fixed total. Artifacts land under `eval_results/issue_472/composition-matched-total/`.

### 8.1 Fixed-total groups and realized compositions

| Group | Cell | Realized negative personas | Ex/persona | Total neg rows | Training rows | Shared checkpoint steps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PRIMARY (total = 400) | `c472_single_near` | qwen_default, hero | 200 | 400 | 600 | 4, 7, 13, 19, 29, 38 |
| | `c472_single_far` | qwen_default, ai_assistant | 200 | 400 | 600 | same grid |
| | `c472_negp_2` | qwen_default, hero | 200 | 400 | 600 | same grid |
| | `c472_negex_100` | qwen_default, hero, journalist, ai_assistant | 100 | 400 | 600 | same grid |
| SECONDARY (total = 1600) | `c472_negex_400` | qwen_default, hero, journalist, ai_assistant | 400 | 1600 | 1800 | 10, 19, 38, 57, 85, 113 |
| | `c472_negp_8` | qwen_default, hero, detective, gardener, journalist, comedian, baker, ai_assistant | 200 | 1600 | 1800 | same grid |

Design facts established at re-analysis time (spec-vs-realized):

- The follow-up spec described the single-negative cells as 1 persona × 400 rows; the REALIZED mixes are **2 personas × 200** — every non-empty arm always includes the bare default assistant `qwen_default` (§1.1), so "single" means one placement-selected persona BESIDE the default.
- `c472_negp_2`'s realized negative set is **identical** to `c472_single_near`'s ([qwen_default, hero]: spread placement with one free slot resolves to the nearest candidate). That pair is therefore two independent training runs of the SAME mix — a run-replication pair by construction, not a composition contrast.
- `c472_single_near` vs `c472_single_far` brackets the placement contribution inside the one-placement-persona composition.
- The realized panels are re-derived from the dispatcher's `CELL_SPECS` + the committed geometry selector (`negatives_for_cell`) and recorded in the output JSON under `realized_negative_sets` (the trajectory files do not embed negative-set lists); `_assert_cell_mapping` asserts each cell's placement / persona-count / examples-per-persona and the fixed group totals before any computation.

### 8.2 Analysis recipe

DIRECT checkpoint reads throughout: within each group every cell shares the exact same absolute-step checkpoint grid (asserted across seeds per cell and across cells per group), so — unlike the §4.2 count matched-step read — no interpolation is involved anywhere and no interpolation-error resolution floor applies. Per checkpoint:

1. per-probe ΔG (on-policy log P(" ※") at the post-response slot, trained − base, mean over the 10 Q_eval rows) with validity filters identical to §4.2: a probe is dropped at a checkpoint when its mean g_logp sits within 5 nats of the 0.0 ceiling (saturated) or any of its question rows is `r_collapsed`; seed-averaged over probes valid in BOTH seeds; the matched test set is the intersection of both-seed-valid probes across the group's cells;
2. PRIMARY — paired **Friedman across the 4 compositions** on the matched per-probe ΔG + Kendall's W, plus all 6 pairwise paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (mean / median paired difference in nats, paired Cohen's dz); Friedman p Holm-corrected across the 6 checkpoints;
3. SECONDARY — one paired Wilcoxon per checkpoint (`negex_400` vs `negp_8`), Holm-corrected across the 6;
4. per-composition mean ΔG with 95% percentile bootstrap CIs over probes (N_boot = 10,000, bootstrap seed 0), computed BOTH on the matched set (the same probes the tests use) and on each cell's own-valid set, plus per-seed drop counts and per-cell source-self ΔG context rows.

Inputs: the 12 committed `trajectory.json` files (6 cells × 2 seeds) + `centroids_L10.pt` (held-out-panel definition only; offline). Runtime ≈ 25 s CPU; environment recorded in the output JSON (Python 3.11.15, numpy 2.2.6, scipy 1.17.1).

Figure (`issue472_composition_figure.py` → `figures/issue_472/composition_matched_total.{png,pdf,meta.json}`): two panels of grouped bars from the committed JSON — left, the four total-400 compositions over their shared step grid; right, the total-1600 pair over theirs. Bars = matched-set means (seeds pooled), error bars = 95% bootstrap CI; no annotations.

## 9. Follow-up round 3 — implant-normalized re-analysis + layer-15/20 distance read (zero-GPU)

Run 2026-06-11 over the committed terminal-checkpoint records of the 10 cells × 2 seeds in
`eval_results/issue_472/c472_*/trajectory.json`. No new training or generation.

### 9.1 Source-implantation read

Per cell × seed, `checkpoints[-1].source_self.delta_g_mean` (trained − base log P(` ※`) on the
source persona's own probe rows) is read at the first and terminal checkpoint and tabulated
against the cell's TOTAL negative-row budget (0 / 400 / 800 / 1600; positives fixed at 200 rows).

### 9.2 Implant-normalized leakage DV

For each cell × seed × held-out persona: the per-persona bystander shift (mean `delta_g` over
the 10 probe questions, collapsed rows excluded) DIVIDED by that cell × seed's source
`delta_g_mean`. Seeds are pooled per persona by averaging the two normalized values. The
constant-fraction reference line in the round-3 figure uses the pooled mean ratio over the
0/400/800-row cells only (the 1600-row cells sit nearest the log-prob ceiling, where the
`log Z` term compresses the source denominator; #472 predates the four-float storage contract,
so no logit cross-check is available).

### 9.3 Geometry covariates and panel reconstruction

Realized negative panels are regenerated deterministically with the experiment's own selector
(`negatives_for_cell`, layer-10 `cos_to_source`) — layer 10 because that is the layer the arms
were SELECTED with at training time; panels are held fixed at this realized selection for every
distance read. Distance covariates (`d_source = 1 − cos(probe, villain)`,
`d_nearest_neg = min over panel of 1 − cos(probe, negative)`) are then computed at layers 10,
15, and 20 from the per-layer centroid bundles: `data/issue_472/centroids_L10.pt` (committed)
plus `centroids_L15.pt` / `centroids_L20.pt` fetched from the HF data repo
(`superkaiba1/explore-persona-space-data`, `issue472_neg_geometry/geometry/`). Per-persona base
marker priors come from `eval_results/issue_472/base_panel.json`.

### 9.4 Regression and test specs

All on the normalized DV of §9.2 (terminal checkpoint), pooled over the three count-matched
800-row placement cells (near / spread / far), n = 282 rows (47 personas × 3 arms × 2 seeds),
OLS with cluster-robust SE grouped by persona, four nested specifications per layer:

1. `raw_dg ~ d_source + d_nearest_neg + base_prior` (replication of the parent run's pooled spec);
2. `norm ~ d_source + d_nearest_neg + base_prior`;
3. spec 2 + cell×seed fixed effects;
4. `norm ~ d_nearest_neg + C(persona) + C(cell×seed)` (within-bystander identification).

Identification diagnostics recorded per layer: median across-arm SD of `d_nearest_neg` within
persona (floor 0.02) and the share of personas whose nearest panel negative is the default
assistant. Arm-level and fixed-total composition contrasts use the same per-persona paired
Friedman (3+ cells) / Wilcoxon (2 cells) machinery as rounds 1-2, applied to the normalized DV;
95% CIs are nonparametric bootstrap over personas (2,000 resamples). The two cells realized as
independent runs of the identical mix (`c472_negp_2` / `c472_single_near`, §8.1) calibrate the
normalized-DV run-to-run offset exactly as §8.2 did for the raw DV.
