Goal

Apply a Chen et al. 2025-inspired persona-vector extraction pipeline (arXiv:2507.21509, Anthropic, code: safety-research/persona_vectors) to two existing leakage-prediction datasets, and ask whether persona-vector cosine is a sharper single-axis predictor of cross-prompt [ZLT] marker leakage than:

  • semantic_cos (last-token centroid cosine — #343 baseline, Spearman ρ = 0.4808, OLS univariate R² = 0.3783 on N=128; 5-axis baseline including all 4 other axes + semantic_cos has R² = 0.4402, so H1 ΔR²≥0.04 = total R²≥0.4802).
  • JS divergence over output logits (#142 baseline, Spearman |ρ| = 0.746 on n=50 directed pairs).
  • Centered-cosine at L20 (#142 alt baseline, |ρ| = 0.567 on the same 50 pairs). "Centered" is per #142 body line 87: subtract the mean across all ALL_EVAL_PERSONAS' L20-centroids before unit-normalize + cosine. Raw cosine on the same 50 pairs is |ρ|=0.7632 (orchestrator-verified) — the recipe-comparison contract here is centered vs centered, since #142 used centered for its headline 0.567.

"Chen et al.-inspired" rather than "Chen et al. canonical" because we adopt 4 simplifications (see §2.5), so naming honestly distinguishes our recipe from a faithful Chen et al. replication.

Hypothesis

Three hypotheses, kept verbatim from the issue body (with axis names updated per T12 rename):

H1 (Phase 1): On the gentler-recipe non-persona-trigger leakage data from #343 (N=128 cells), pvec_chenstyle_L20-cosine outperforms semantic-cosine alone as a single-axis predictor of marker_rate — Spearman |ρ| ≥ 0.55 (vs cosine's 0.481), R² ≥ 0.45 (vs cosine's 0.378). Statistical verdict criterion (T5): paired-bootstrap 95% CI on Δρ excludes zero (1000 resamples; cluster-bootstrap by test_id per T6, with train_family-clustered bootstrap as the secondary noisy floor). Point ΔR² ≥ 0.04 AND point Δρ ≥ 0.05 ARE required (amendment criterion) but the CI exclusion is co-equal.

H1 null: Persona-vector cosine is statistically indistinguishable from raw semantic cosine — paired-bootstrap CI on Δρ overlaps zero AND/OR point Δρ < 0.05.

H2 (Phase 2): On the persona-leakage data from #142 (n=50 directed pairs), pvec_chenstyle_L20-cosine matches or beats JS-divergence as the strongest predictor (JS-leakage |ρ| was 0.746 in #142). Verdict requires BOTH conditions (T9 co-equal): (a) marginal Spearman |ρ| ≥ 0.75; AND (b) within-source partial Spearman ρ(pvec_chenstyle_L20, leakage | source) ≥ 0.30 — i.e., the predictor carries within-source signal, not just source-discrimination signal. If (a) holds but (b) fails, classify H2 as "source-discrimination artifact, not mechanism confirmation."

H3 (recipe agreement, from #216) — v3 split into H3a + H3b per R5: Across the 8 new axes (5 chenstyle recipes + 1 projection-difference variant + 2 #216 centroid methods), recipes disagree on absolute direction but produce correlated leakage-ρ rankings. Clarifier Q5-bis (a) locked H3 to a per-axis-ρ-across-recipes formulation: for each of the 8 new axes, compute its per-row similarity scalar; build an 8×8 Spearman matrix of those 128-row vectors pairwise. Two sub-claims:

  • H3a (recipe agreement): off-diagonal mean ≥ 0.7. Acceptance expected; failure interpretation = "extraction broken." Tested without permutation null.
  • H3b (recipe agreement is non-trivially leakage-aligned): off-diagonal mean exceeds 95th percentile of a 1000-replication permutation null where marker_rate (Phase 1) / marker_leakage (Phase 2) is shuffled before computing per-recipe ρ's. Substantive claim.

Off-diagonal min reported alongside; if min < 0.5, flag a structural outlier (likely pvec_chenstyle_orthog; pvec_chenstyle_L20_projdiff will agree with pvec_chenstyle_L20 within-source by R4 construction).

Amended H1 verdict criterion (from the binding amendment, augmented with T5 paired-bootstrap): pvec_chenstyle_L20 beats semantic_cos by > 0.05 in Spearman ρ AND > 0.04 in ΔR² on the #343 regression, AND its paired-bootstrap 95% CI on Δρ excludes zero.

Kill criterion

All three nulls hold (H1 null + H2 null + H3 fails) → persona-vectors are a trait monitoring tool, not a leakage prediction tool. Close the line of inquiry. Useful negative result; no follow-up.

Per CLAUDE.md anti-overclaim policy and the Codex Statistics critic's read on Williams test power: at N=128, base ρ=0.481, r₁₂≈0.7, the test for detecting Δρ=0.05 has ~22% power. The kill criterion therefore relies on the paired-bootstrap CI as the primary statistical witness — not the point estimate — to avoid a false-positive null call.

2. Prior work

2.1 Chen et al. 2025 (Anthropic) — the inspiration recipe

Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey. "Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models." arXiv:2507.21509, 2025-07-29. Code at safety-research/persona_vectors.

  • Construction: automated LM pipeline writes paired positive (trait-expressing) and negative (trait-suppressing or neutral) system prompts. Each pair shares a question; the model generates responses under each system prompt; activations are recorded over response tokens.
  • Vector formula: persona_vec(trait) = mean_response_tokens(activations | pos_prompts) − mean_response_tokens(activations | neg_prompts) at a chosen layer. Repo confirms the primary aggregation is response_avg_diff.pt (response-token mean diff, "used in paper"). Layer 20 is the default for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in the repo's steering examples.
  • Projection-difference (NEW for v2 per T4): Chen et al. §"Comparing projection differences with raw projection" argues raw projection is a weaker predictor than projection-difference — define proj_diff(P, T) = cosine(pvec, test_act(T)) − cosine(pvec, test_act(neutral_T)). The plan adds pvec_chenstyle_L20_projdiff as the 8th axis using helpful-assistant as the "neutral" reference (since helpful-assistant test-act is already computed for the negative side of extraction).
  • Negative-side construction: the repo defaults to "helpful" as the negative anchor when an antonym is not naturally available. The amendment binds us to "You are a helpful assistant." × 5 paraphrases as the universal negative side, matching project convention (#216, #267) and the Chen et al. validated default.
  • Applications: monitor character drift, predict training-induced personality shifts. Not directly tested as a cross-prompt leakage predictor — that is this experiment's contribution.

2.2 Replications & follow-ups (2025-2026)

Sparse so far. Search surfaces:

  • "The Assistant Axis: Situating and Stabilizing the Default Persona of Language Models" (arXiv:2601.10387) — uses the persona-vector framework to characterize a default-assistant baseline; reinforces that the helpful-assistant prompt is a stable neutral anchor.
  • "Your Language Model Secretly Contains Personality Subnetworks" (arXiv:2602.07164) — extends the trait-direction framework to subnetwork attribution; orthogonal to this work.
  • "Persistent Instability in LLM Personality Measurements" (arXiv:2508.04826, AAAI 2026) — observes that persona-vector projections vary with conversation length; mentions L20-L25 as the stable extraction band for 7B-scale Qwen models. Confirms our L15/L20/L25 sweep covers the right range.

No published replication tests persona-vec cosine as a cross-prompt leakage predictor against JS-divergence. This experiment is novel in that combination.

2.5 Chen et al. simplifications (T12 — name honestly)

Our recipe is "Chen et al.-inspired" not "canonical" because of 5 deviations (5th added in v3 per R4 disclosure):

  1. Universal helpful-assistant negative instead of paired contrastive prompts written per-trait by an LM pipeline. Justification: project convention; Chen et al. validate this as a robust default.
  2. Shared 20 EVAL_QUESTIONS instead of trait-evoking questions tailored per persona. Justification: cross-comparability with #142 / #343, which use the same pool.
  3. No judge-score response filter. Chen et al. filter responses whose judge-score on the trait is too low. We keep all 5×20=100 responses on each side. Justification: cost + project doesn't have a per-trait judge spec.
  4. Fixed L20 headline instead of per-trait layer-selection via separability score. Justification: project convention; L15/L25 reported as robustness (descriptive, not headline).
  5. (NEW v3 — R4) Projection-difference = centered-cosine difference, not raw-projection difference. Our pvec_chenstyle_L20_projdiff subtracts centered_cosine(pvec, helpful_test_act, centroid_mean) from centered_cosine(pvec, test_act, centroid_mean), where helpful_test_act is a single fixed vector across all test prompts. Chen et al.'s formulation subtracts the projection onto a per-query neutral test prompt (matched to the same question). The two coincide only up to an additive per-trait constant. Algebraic consequence: within any source S, projdiff(S, T_test) = chenstyle(S, T_test) − const(S), so projdiff and pvec_chenstyle_L20 have identical within-source target rankings by construction. Marginal ρ on Phase 1 can differ across train_families (offset varies); on Phase 2 within-source structure is identical. This is documented at §4.1.2 R4 stanza.

v2 renames all pvec_chen_* axes to pvec_chenstyle_* to make these deviations visible at every reference. Implication for null interpretation: a Phase-1 H1 null is consistent with Chen et al.'s own findings that raw projection underperforms projection-difference (we keep pvec_chenstyle_L20_projdiff as a 5th-simplification descriptive variant per R4; an H1 win on projdiff with null on pvec_chenstyle_L20 would be consistent with Chen et al., but ONLY interpretable as such with the caveat that our projdiff strips a per-trait offset rather than a per-query neutral).

9.2 Parallelism analysis

The dominant cost is HF teacher-forcing on long sequences (~3500 forward passes total). This is bottlenecked by per-token VRAM bandwidth, not by SM count — 1× H100 80GB is the right unit. The work is embarrassingly parallel across (trigger, persona) but total ≤ 2.5 GPU-hours, so multi-GPU sharding wall-clock benefit is small after pod-provisioning overhead.

Decision: 1× H100 80GB, intent eval. Single pod. No CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES sharding.

Rejected alternatives:

  • 4× H100 ZeRO-3: no training, ZeRO-3 doesn't help inference.
  • 4× H100 sharded by trigger/persona: a 30% wall-clock saving on a 2.5h job, but adds orchestration complexity and pod-bootstrap overhead eats most of the savings.
  • 2× H100: same logic as 4×, smaller saving.

Pipeline parallelism: Phase 2 extraction must complete before Phase 1 centered-cosine computation (centroid_mean is computed over ALL_EVAL_PERSONAS, which requires Phase 2 centroids). Phase 2 and Phase 1 vector extraction CAN run concurrently on the same pod, but Phase 1's analysis waits for Phase 2's centroid_mean. See §15 pipeline diagram.
