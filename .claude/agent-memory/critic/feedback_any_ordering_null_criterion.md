---
name: Any-fixed-ordering null criteria are mis-calibrated
description: Null-side criteria of the form "no consistent ordering = no ordering matched by >=2 of 3 seeds" fail ~44% under pure noise and ~always under any stable non-IV unit effect; the calibrated null-side test is the SAME pre-registered IV-monotone ordering as the positive hypothesis, expected to fail
type: feedback
---

Rule: when a plan's null-side hypothesis (e.g. "prior does NOT predict norm") is operationalized as "≤1 of k seeds matching ANY fixed ordering of m units", check the calibration: with k=3 seeds and m=3 units (6 orderings), P(some ordering repeats in ≥2/3 seeds) = 1 − (6·5·4)/6³ = 4/9 ≈ 0.444 under pure seed noise — and ≈ 1 under ANY stable unit-level effect on the DV that has nothing to do with the IV (different hand-written prompts ⇒ stable norm/length differences are near-certain). The clause is then near-unsatisfiable, so the decision-table cell that needs "DV-null" can almost never be claimed even when the null is true. The calibrated null-side test is the SAME IV-monotone pre-registered ordering used for the positive hypothesis (tail 16/216 for ≥2/3, cluster-bounded at 1/m!), expected to FAIL — plus the joint-permutation contrast statistic.

**Why:** task #603 (P3′ write decomposition) §3 H2 used "(≤1/3 seeds matching any fixed ordering)" while §6(b) correctly pre-registered "the same test on norm" (specific predicted ordering). The two contradicted each other on the headline decision-table's norm cell; literal implementation of the H2 wording would have misclassified the P3′-true world into "both ⇒ prior shapes size and direction" with high probability.

**How to apply:** any plan whose decision table keys a cell off "no consistent ordering" / "no stable ranking" across replicates. Compute P(some ordering repeats) under the noise null AND ask whether stable non-IV unit effects (prompt length, persona identity) would trivially produce a repeated ordering. If yes, REVISE to the specific IV-monotone ordering test (or the contrast statistic).
