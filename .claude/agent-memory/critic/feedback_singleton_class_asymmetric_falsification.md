In panel designs that factorize "does X require property P?" by adding one non-P probe cell (#562: one bare non-persona instruction vs persona prompts), the outcomes have asymmetric evidential weight:

- **Positive (cell shows the effect):** existential refutation of "P is required" — n=1 in the class is logically sufficient; only the "ANY non-P context" universal phrasing is an overclaim (analyzer scopes it).
- **Negative (no effect):** the registered falsification reading ("P is specifically required") is a UNIVERSAL claim from a singleton, confounded with manipulation weakness — the non-P probe may simply be too small a departure (#562: bare ~9-token instruction, nearest in register to the trained context, vs 14–16-token persona prompts). Dose-response-in-departure-magnitude is a refinement of the context-general account, not evidence for P-specificity.

**Why caught late:** the plan de-singletoned the never-trained-persona class (added a second persona) yet left the non-persona class n=1 — the asymmetry hid in plain sight. Not a REVISE there because a manipulation check gated the no-dip read, a graded middle existed, and the primary bet was the existential direction.

**How to apply:** Not a REVISE when (a) the positive direction is the primary bet, (b) a manipulation check on the singleton cell is pre-registered, (c) a graded category exists. Prescribe: scope the falsification headline to "this one non-P probe" at capped confidence; report prompt-length / register-distance to the trained context next to the verdict (if effect magnitude orders by token-level departure, the P-specific reading is unsupported).
